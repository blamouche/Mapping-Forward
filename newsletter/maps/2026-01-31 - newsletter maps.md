# Hot on maps: When the Car Becomes the Map

January 2026 marked a defining moment for vehicle navigation. At CES, HERE Technologies argued that the era of phone-based mapping is ending. Dashboard-integrated systems now access sensor data that smartphones cannot: windshield wipers detecting rain, traction control sensing ice, and cameras identifying road closures. Combined with HERE UniMap's 24-hour update cycle, vehicle-native navigation is positioning itself as more capable than the apps that disrupted traditional systems a decade ago. The announcement of partnerships with Hyundai, Lucid Motors, and Qualcomm reinforced this shift toward treating vehicles as continuously evolving digital platforms.

The tooling ecosystem around open map data continued to mature. The Overture Maps Foundation named William Mortenson as its first dedicated executive director, signaling organizational growth beyond its founding tech consortium. Protomaps added Overture as an alternative data source to OpenStreetMap, using DuckDB queries against S3-hosted Parquet files for efficient regional extraction. TomTom joined Precisely's Data Link program, making authoritative map data available in a pre-linked ecosystem alongside Overture and other providers. HERE's January platform release introduced experimental layers for administrative hierarchies and enhanced truck routing APIs, while SDK 4.25.1.0 added traffic light visualization and multimodal routing profiles.

Practical applications of location intelligence expanded across sectors. TomTom's 15th annual Traffic Index revealed that the District of Columbia leads U.S. congestion at 42%, while Mexico City drivers cover only 3.4 km during rush hour versus 7.3 km in optimal conditions. CARTO identified Agentic GIS as a defining trend of 2025: AI agents embedded in dashboards now let non-technical users query spatial data through natural language. Esri demonstrated how GIS and AI are transforming urban forestry into data-driven infrastructure, with cities like Washington DC and Los Angeles using predictive modeling to place trees where they best mitigate heat islands. Urban forests now deliver quantifiable value: $5.4 billion in air pollution removal and $5.4 billion in reduced energy costs across the U.S.

Looking ahead, the integration of AI assistants into mapping applications is accelerating. Google expanded Gemini to walking and cycling navigation, positioning the AI as a personal tour guide for pedestrians and hands-free assistant for cyclists. The cartographic community will gather in Paris for State of the Map 2026 in August, hosted at Geodata Paris with support from France's National Geographic Institute. And in a reminder that mapping innovation has deep roots, a professor discovered a lost Mercator manuscript in Spain's El Escorial library, revealing the astronomical calculations behind the 1551 celestial globe that remained hidden for 450 years.
