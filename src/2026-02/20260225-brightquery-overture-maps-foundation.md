# BrightQuery Joins Overture Maps Foundation to Expand Open Places Data Coverage
**Source**: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/brightquery-joins-overture-maps-foundation-to-expand-open-places-data-coverage-302697160.html
**Date**: February 25, 2026
**Author**: Overture Maps Foundation
**Keywords**: BrightQuery, Overture Maps Foundation, open data, geospatial, places dataset, entity data, mapping infrastructure

## Elevator pitch
BrightQuery joins the Overture Maps Foundation as a General Member, contributing 324 million organizations and 512 million locations from government filings to strengthen open geospatial infrastructure.

## Takeaways
- BrightQuery's dataset covers 324 million organizations, 512 million locations, and 1.2 billion people across 222 countries
- The data is sourced from over 100,000 government agencies through official public filings
- The partnership expands the Overture Places dataset, which already covers 70+ million real-world locations
- BrightQuery will collaborate on schema alignment, entity resolution, and data quality frameworks
- Overture Maps Foundation was founded in 2022 by AWS, Meta, Microsoft, and TomTom, now with 40+ contributing companies

## Synthesis
BrightQuery's accession to the Overture Maps Foundation represents a significant expansion of the open mapping ecosystem's authoritative data layer. By contributing its global entity dataset — built from official government filings across 222 countries — the company addresses one of the persistent weaknesses in open geographic data: the accuracy and completeness of organizational and commercial location records.

The Overture Maps Foundation, launched in 2022 by AWS, Meta, Microsoft, and TomTom, is building a standardized, interoperable geospatial infrastructure designed to compete with and complement proprietary mapping platforms. Its Places dataset, covering over 70 million real-world locations, forms the foundation for developer applications requiring location intelligence. BrightQuery's contribution — drawn from official government sources rather than crowdsourced or commercially aggregated data — brings a different quality profile: high authority, high reliability, but also potentially less immediately current than real-time commercial data.

Will Mortenson, Overture's Executive Director, articulates the strategic rationale clearly: "High-quality places and entity data are essential building blocks for modern mapping." This framing positions BrightQuery's government-sourced data as infrastructure rather than a competitive feature — a foundational layer that other applications and datasets build upon.

The collaboration on schema alignment and entity resolution is technically important. As the Overture dataset grows through multiple member contributions, ensuring that different data sources describe the same real-world entity consistently — the same organization, building, or address — is a core data engineering challenge. BrightQuery's experience in this area, including its role as a primary government data supplier for leading AI companies, makes it a substantive technical contributor rather than a passive dataset provider.

The announcement reflects the broader trend of open geospatial infrastructure gaining seriousness as a category, with major technology companies and specialized data providers recognizing that collaborative standards serve the ecosystem more effectively than proprietary fragmentation.
