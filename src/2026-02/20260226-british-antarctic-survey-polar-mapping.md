## Elevator pitch
The British Antarctic Survey's MAGIC (Mapping and Geographic Information Centre) provides comprehensive geospatial services for polar research, from satellite-based wildlife monitoring to the Antarctic Digital Database that underpins UK polar operations.

## Takeaways
- BAS MAGIC (Mapping and Geographic Information Centre) provides maps, satellite imagery, and location data essential for UK polar research and operations
- The Antarctic Digital Database (SCAR ADD) offers seamless topographic data including coastlines, place names, and contours for Antarctica to 60°S, updated several times per year
- Wildlife from Space program uses sub-metre Very-High-Resolution satellite imagery to monitor remote penguin colonies, track icebergs, and observe vegetation changes
- Bedmap is a collaborative community project producing maps of Antarctic ice thickness and bed topography, critical for understanding ice sheet dynamics
- Operational services include real-time Assets Tracking (ships, aircraft, vehicles) and Polar View delivering sea ice information directly to ships in the Southern Ocean

## Synthesis
The British Antarctic Survey's Mapping and Geographic Information Centre (MAGIC) serves as the backbone of UK polar geospatial capabilities, providing the essential maps and data that make polar science and operations possible. From tracking penguin populations via satellite to ensuring ship safety through sea ice monitoring, MAGIC's work demonstrates how modern geospatial technology transforms our ability to study and work in Earth's most extreme environments.

At the core of MAGIC's offerings is the Antarctic Digital Database (ADD), maintained under the Scientific Committee on Antarctic Research (SCAR). This seamless compilation of topographic data covers Antarctica to 60°S latitude, providing researchers and operators with standardized coastline data, contours, and place names. The database is updated several times annually as new data becomes available, reflecting the dynamic nature of Antarctic geography where coastlines shift with ice dynamics and climate change.

The Wildlife from Space program represents a revolutionary approach to ecological monitoring. Many polar wildlife populations inhabit locations too remote or dangerous for traditional ground surveys. By leveraging Very-High-Resolution (VHR) satellite imagery with sub-metre resolution, researchers can now count emperor penguin colonies, track vegetation growth on the Antarctic Peninsula, and monitor ecosystem changes across vast areas. This satellite-based approach provides consistent, repeatable measurements impossible through field expeditions alone.

The Bedmap project addresses one of polar science's most fundamental questions: what lies beneath Antarctica's ice sheets? This collaborative international effort produces maps of ice thickness and underlying bed topography, data critical for understanding ice sheet stability and predicting sea level rise. The hidden landscape beneath the ice includes mountain ranges, valleys, and lake systems that influence how ice flows and responds to warming.

For operational support, MAGIC provides two critical real-time services. The Assets Tracking Service monitors the positions of BAS ships, aircraft, and vehicles, essential for coordination and safety in regions where communication is challenging. Polar View delivers sea ice information directly to vessels operating in the Southern Ocean, enabling safe navigation through one of Earth's most treacherous maritime environments.

MAGIC employs cutting-edge technologies including satellite imagery analysis, GPS positioning, laser scanning for 3D terrain modeling, drone surveys, and sophisticated GIS platforms. This technological toolkit supports not just science but also conservation efforts, policy decisions on Antarctic place names and territorial management, and public engagement through accessible maps and imagery. Most BAS maps are freely available, with an online catalogue providing both current products and historical mapping datasets documenting decades of polar exploration and research.
