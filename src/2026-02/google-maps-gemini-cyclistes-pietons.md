# Google Maps : Gemini arrive pour les cyclistes et pietons

**Source**: https://www.lesnumeriques.com/gps/google-maps-cyclistes-pietons-cette-nouvelle-mise-a-jour-est-enfin-pour-vous-n250729.html

**Date**: January 30, 2026

**Author**: Les Numeriques

**Keywords**: Google Maps, Gemini, cycling, walking, voice assistant, navigation, hands-free, AI, pedestrians

## Elevator pitch

Google extends Gemini AI capabilities to walking and cycling modes in Google Maps, enabling hands-free voice interaction for safer navigation and contextual information during trips.

## Takeaways

- Gemini AI is now available for pedestrians and cyclists in Google Maps, extending beyond its initial driving-only functionality
- For pedestrians, Gemini acts as a personal tour guide, answering questions about neighborhoods and suggesting nearby restaurants
- For cyclists, safety is the primary focus with hands-free voice commands for ETA checks, calendar queries, and sending messages
- Voice commands include technical navigation controls like "Add a stop", "Show alternative routes", "Mute guidance", and "What's my next turn?"
- The feature is rolling out globally on both Android and iOS platforms

## Synthesis

Google has announced the expansion of its Gemini AI assistant to walking and cycling navigation modes within Google Maps, marking a significant evolution of the platform's intelligent assistance capabilities. This update follows the initial rollout of Gemini for driving navigation and addresses the unique needs of pedestrians and cyclists.

For pedestrians, Gemini positions itself as a personal tour guide. Users can ask contextual questions directly within the navigation interface, such as "What neighborhood am I in?" or "What are the best restaurants nearby?" The AI assistant draws on Google Maps' extensive location data to provide relevant information about points of interest and suggest well-rated establishments along the user's route.

The cycling integration prioritizes safety as its core value proposition. By enabling hands-free voice interaction, Gemini allows cyclists to keep their hands on the handlebars and eyes on the road while staying connected. Common use cases include checking estimated arrival times, verifying upcoming calendar appointments, or sending text messages to contacts about delays without physically handling the phone.

Beyond basic queries, Gemini offers technical navigation controls through voice commands. Users can say "Add a stop" to modify their route, "Show alternative routes" to explore other options, "Mute guidance" to silence turn-by-turn instructions temporarily, or "What's my next turn?" to get immediate directional information.

The feature is accessible globally on both Android and iOS devices. Users can activate Gemini either by tapping the Gemini icon in the upper right corner of the navigation interface or by using the "Hey Google" voice command. This dual activation method ensures accessibility for different user preferences and situations.

This expansion reflects Google's strategy of making AI capabilities pervasive across its core products, recognizing that different transportation modes have distinct requirements for intelligent assistance.
