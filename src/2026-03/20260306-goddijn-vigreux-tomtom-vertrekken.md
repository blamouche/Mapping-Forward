# Goddijn en Vigreux, mede-oprichters TomTom, vertrekken
**Source**: https://www.computable.nl/2026/03/06/goddijn-en-vigreux-mede-oprichters-tomtom-vertrekken/
**Date**: March 6, 2026
**Author**: Rik Sanders
**Keywords**: TomTom, Harold Goddijn, Corinne Vigreux, Mike Schoofs, leadership transition, co-founders, automotive, AI maps

## Elevator pitch
TomTom co-founders Harold Goddijn and Corinne Vigreux are departing after 25+ years, with Mike Schoofs nominated as CEO to lead the company's next phase of AI-driven maps and mobility solutions.

## Takeaways
- Harold Goddijn will step down at the April 16, 2026 shareholder meeting after 25 years as CEO, transitioning to the supervisory board
- Corinne Vigreux, central to TomTom's commercial growth since 1991, is also leaving daily management
- Mike Schoofs, current chief revenue officer at TomTom since 2005, has been nominated as the new CEO
- The new management structure consists of CEO Schoofs and financial director Taco Titulaer
- TomTom believes it is positioned for "the next growth phase in AI-driven maps and mobility solutions"

## Synthesis
The simultaneous departure of TomTom's two founding executives marks one of the most significant leadership transitions in European mapping technology history. Harold Goddijn and Corinne Vigreux built TomTom from a software company founded in 1991 into a global navigation giant, navigating the company through its hardware dominance era, the smartphone disruption that eliminated most of its consumer business, and a subsequent pivot to automotive and enterprise data services.

Goddijn's 25-year tenure as CEO encompasses TomTom's entire arc as a public company. His exit to the supervisory board — rather than a complete departure — suggests a gradual transition designed to preserve institutional knowledge while enabling new leadership to operate with full authority. Vigreux's exit from daily management represents the more complete break; her role in shaping TomTom's commercial culture and growth strategy was formative.

Mike Schoofs, who joined TomTom in 2005 and built its global sales organization, brings continuity rather than disruption to the succession. His background in automotive and enterprise partnerships — the two growth vectors TomTom is actively pursuing — makes him a logical choice for the current strategic moment. The simplified management structure, reducing executive leadership to CEO and CFO, reflects the leaner operational model TomTom has been building following years of portfolio rationalization.

The company's stated confidence in being prepared for "the next growth phase in AI-driven maps and mobility solutions" reflects both genuine opportunity and competitive pressure. The automotive location data market remains robust, but TomTom faces intensifying competition from HERE Technologies, Google's automotive unit, and automotive OEMs building proprietary mapping capabilities. Schoofs' challenge is to accelerate revenue growth while the transition from legacy navigation revenue to data services contracts plays out.
