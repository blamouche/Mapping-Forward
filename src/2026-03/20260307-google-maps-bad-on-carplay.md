# I just realized how bad Google Maps is on CarPlay — why does it look so terrible?
**Source**: https://www.tomsguide.com/computing/mobile-apps/i-just-realized-how-bad-google-maps-is-on-carplay-why-does-it-look-so-terrible
**Date**: March 7, 2026
**Author**: Tom Pritchard
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Apple CarPlay, Android Auto, in-car navigation, mobile apps, UX

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps performs significantly worse on Apple CarPlay than on Android Auto, raising questions about why Google has failed to optimize its app for Apple's automotive platform.

## Takeaways
- There is a stark contrast in Google Maps quality between Android Auto and Apple CarPlay implementations
- The CarPlay version appears visually inferior and less polished than the Android counterpart
- The article includes screenshots documenting navigation, panning, and interface states on CarPlay
- Google's failure to optimize for CarPlay may reflect strategic prioritization of its own Android platform
- The gap matters increasingly as CarPlay adoption grows in new vehicles

## Synthesis
Tom Pritchard's piece in Tom's Guide highlights a persistent and largely unremarked quality disparity: Google Maps on Apple CarPlay underperforms compared to its Android Auto counterpart by a meaningful margin. The observation may surprise casual users who assume that major applications deliver consistent experiences across platforms, but it reflects a pattern in how cross-platform development priorities play out in practice.

The visual quality difference the article documents is notable because Maps is one of the most widely used navigation applications globally, and CarPlay has become a standard feature in new vehicles sold in markets where Apple's ecosystem dominates. Drivers investing in vehicles with CarPlay integration reasonably expect first-party apps from major developers to leverage the platform's capabilities fully.

The likely explanation, though the article does not state it explicitly, lies in Google's development priorities. As a company whose navigation platform is tightly integrated with Android, Google has both the engineering resources and the strategic motivation to maintain Android Auto as the superior implementation. Apple CarPlay, by contrast, represents a competitor's platform — one where Google participates but does not control the environment. This creates a disincentive to invest equivalent development resources in polish and optimization.

The practical consequence for users is that driving a CarPlay-equipped vehicle while preferring Google Maps means accepting a degraded experience relative to Android users in the same model car. The gap documented in this article is particularly relevant for iPhone users who constitute a significant share of Google Maps' user base, given the app's cross-platform popularity despite being a Google product.

This critique lands during a period when Google is simultaneously announcing major Maps feature expansions — Immersive Navigation, Ask Maps — raising the question of whether the CarPlay experience will receive proportionate investment alongside these flagship updates or continue to lag as a secondary platform concern.
