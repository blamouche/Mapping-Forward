# New Google Maps alternative has a feature that has everyone saying the underdogs might truly be taking over
**Source**: https://supercarblondie.com/tech/google-maps-alternative-underdog-could-take-over/
**Date**: March 7, 2026
**Author**: Henry Kelsall
**Keywords**: MapQuest, Google Maps, Apple Maps, Waze, navigation app, CarPlay, traffic data, lane guidance

## Elevator pitch
MapQuest is emerging as a credible Google Maps alternative after adding real-time traffic, lane guidance, Apple CarPlay support, and session-persistent satellite mode in a rapid feature expansion.

## Takeaways
- MapQuest added real-time traffic data, matching a long-standing Google Maps capability
- Lane guidance and enhanced satellite mode that remembers user settings between sessions are now available
- Full directions list is accessible via navigation header tap, improving trip planning usability
- Apple CarPlay compatibility expands its reach into vehicle-integrated navigation
- The pace of development suggests MapQuest is seriously competing for users beyond its nostalgic base

## Synthesis
MapQuest, once synonymous with early internet mapping and largely displaced by Google Maps after the smartphone era, is executing a meaningful product revival. The article documents a cluster of feature additions that, taken together, move the application from a curiosity to a credible competitor in the navigation app category.

The addition of real-time traffic data is the most strategically significant update. Traffic awareness has been a foundational Google Maps feature for years and a primary reason users have been reluctant to switch to alternatives. By closing this gap, MapQuest removes a core objection to adoption. The lane guidance feature similarly addresses a navigation quality requirement for complex highway interchanges, where imprecise guidance causes driver stress and missed exits.

The satellite mode memory — retaining user settings between sessions rather than reverting to defaults — demonstrates attention to the friction points that erode daily usability. Small UX improvements of this kind accumulate into meaningful quality-of-life differences for habitual users.

Apple CarPlay support is particularly important for penetrating the iPhone user segment, which represents a large share of MapQuest's potential audience given its US-centric history. Without CarPlay integration, a navigation app is effectively invisible to drivers whose vehicles use the platform as the primary interface.

The article frames MapQuest's trajectory using the phrase "underdogs might truly be taking over," which overstates the competitive threat in the near term but captures a real dynamic: the navigation market is becoming meaningfully more competitive. Google Maps retains overwhelming market share and infrastructure advantages, but the combination of MapQuest's updates with growing awareness of alternatives like Waze, Apple Maps, and TomTom GO Navigation suggests users are increasingly willing to evaluate options.
