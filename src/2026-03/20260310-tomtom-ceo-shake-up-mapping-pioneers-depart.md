# TomTom CEO shake-up as mapping pioneers depart
**Source**: https://www.automotiveworld.com/articles/tomtom-ceo-shake-up-as-mapping-pioneers-depart/
**Date**: March 10, 2026
**Author**: Megan Lampinen
**Keywords**: TomTom, Harold Goddijn, Corinne Vigreux, Alain De Taeye, Mike Schoofs, leadership, autonomous driving, automotive

## Elevator pitch
TomTom's founding generation exits as Harold Goddijn, Corinne Vigreux, and Alain De Taeye step down, leaving Mike Schoofs to lead a company navigating losses and betting on AI-driven automotive mapping.

## Takeaways
- Harold Goddijn departs April 16, 2026 after decades leading TomTom; retains a supervisory role
- Alain De Taeye, chairman and "Godfather of mapping," is also leaving alongside co-founder Corinne Vigreux
- TomTom reported €6.40 million losses in 2025 and expects flat 2026 revenue due to contract transitions
- The automotive location data backlog stands at €2.40 billion, indicating strong future pipeline
- AI and LLM integration into navigation services is described as "more defensive relevance strategy than revenue driver" currently

## Synthesis
The departure of three foundational executives — Harold Goddijn, Corinne Vigreux, and Alain De Taeye — represents the complete changing of TomTom's founding guard. Each brought a distinct contribution to the company's development: Goddijn as the strategic architect of TomTom's commercial and technical direction, Vigreux as a Forbes-recognized force in commercial growth and culture, and De Taeye as the technical cartographic authority whose work earned him the informal title "Godfather of mapping."

Chief Revenue Officer Mike Schoofs inherits a company in financial difficulty but with a substantial asset base. The €6.40 million loss in 2025 and flat revenue expectation for 2026 reflect the painful dynamics of a business transitioning revenue streams: automotive data contracts, which represent TomTom's growth thesis, take years to negotiate, implement, and begin generating recurring revenue. Meanwhile, legacy navigation revenue from hardware and consumer software continues to decline.

The €2.40 billion automotive backlog is the most important counterweight to the current financial picture. It represents committed future revenue that validates the strategic pivot to automotive location data services, even as the transition creates short-term income gaps. Schoofs' primary task is managing the cash flow dynamics of this transition while continuing to build the contract pipeline.

The analyst characterization of TomTom's AI and LLM navigation initiatives as "more defensive relevance strategy than revenue driver" is a sober assessment of where generative AI sits in the company's monetization roadmap. The initiatives serve to demonstrate that TomTom is technologically current and competitive with Google and HERE in AI-enhanced navigation — necessary for maintaining automotive OEM relationships — without yet contributing meaningful incremental revenue. Converting these capabilities into billable features at sufficient scale remains the key unsolved challenge for Schoofs' tenure.
