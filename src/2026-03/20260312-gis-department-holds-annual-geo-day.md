# GIS department holds annual Geo Day
**Source**: https://www.tropnews.com/post/gis-department-holds-annual-geo-day
**Date**: March 12, 2026
**Author**: Trop News
**Keywords**: GIS, land surveying, Troy University, Geo Day, geospatial careers, education

## Elevator pitch
Troy University's geospatial informatics department held its annual Geo Day, bringing high school students together with industry professionals and alumni to showcase careers in GIS and land surveying.

## Takeaways
- Troy University is the only Alabama school guaranteeing ABET certification for surveying graduates
- GIS and land surveying are low-visibility but high-demand career fields
- The event featured equipment demonstrations, alumni speakers, and industry presentations from ALDOT
- Students discover GIS careers through events like Geo Day, often changing their academic trajectory
- The field spans construction, real estate, emergency services, political campaigns, and weather mapping

## Synthesis
Troy University's geospatial informatics department held its annual Geo Day on March 12 in the Trojan Center, welcoming high school and community college students from across Alabama to explore careers in land surveying and geographic information science. The event highlights both the vitality of geospatial education and the persistent challenge of career visibility in a field that most people never encounter until they need it.

Geo Day featured a mix of hands-on equipment demonstrations — tripods, cameras, drones — alongside presentations from working professionals at surveying firms and the Alabama Department of Transportation (ALDOT). All guest speakers were Troy University alumni, which created a powerful testimonial loop: students could see real career paths traced from the same classrooms they were considering.

The event captures something important about the geospatial profession: despite being foundational to enormous sectors of the economy — construction, real estate, emergency management, utility infrastructure, environmental monitoring — GIS and surveying remain largely invisible to the general public. Few high school students spontaneously consider geospatial careers, making outreach events like Geo Day essential for pipeline development.

Troy University occupies a distinctive niche as the only Alabama school that guarantees ABET certification for surveying graduates upon completion — a quality signal that makes its graduates immediately employable as licensed surveyors. The combination of strong academic credentials and industry networking at events like Geo Day positions Troy well for students who discover the field through this kind of community engagement.

The testimonials from current students who attended Geo Day as high schoolers before choosing to enroll underscore the event's effectiveness as a recruitment and inspiration vehicle. For an industry facing workforce shortages as experienced surveyors retire, educational outreach of this kind is strategically essential.
