# Kapsch TrafficCom Selects TomTom Traffic to Enhance Its Global Traffic Management Solutions
**Source**: https://highways-news.com/kapsch-trafficcom-selects-tomtom-traffic-to-enhance-its-global-traffic-management-solutions/
**Date**: March 12, 2026
**Author**: Adrian Tatum
**Keywords**: Kapsch TrafficCom, TomTom, EcoTrafiX, floating car data, traffic management, intelligent mobility, Intertraffic 2026

## Elevator pitch
Kapsch TrafficCom's selection of TomTom Traffic for its EcoTrafiX Platform gives cities immediate access to rich real-time traffic intelligence while streamlining deployment for road authorities worldwide.

## Takeaways
- TomTom's Floating Car Data is embedded in Kapsch's EcoTrafiX Platform for end-to-end traffic management
- Cities and road authorities gain immediate access to traffic flow, incidents, congestion patterns, and multimodal movement data
- The standard integration removes customization complexity and accelerates deployment timelines
- Samuel Kapsch emphasized data helps cities understand congestion impacts and ensure equitable mobility
- The enhanced capabilities are being showcased at Intertraffic 2026 with live analytics demonstrations

## Synthesis
Highways News' coverage of the Kapsch-TomTom partnership complements the broader announcement with infrastructure sector context, placing the collaboration within the operational world of road authorities and highway agencies. Adrian Tatum's reporting captures the partnership's significance for the highways management community — an audience for whom data-driven traffic intelligence is transitioning from experimental to operational.

The article's framing around "immediate access to rich, real-time insights" addresses a specific gap in traditional traffic management infrastructure. Road authorities operating EcoTrafiX have historically relied on fixed sensor networks — induction loops embedded in asphalt, camera systems, and weather monitoring stations. These systems are accurate for monitored segments but create coverage gaps on unmonitored routes and between sensor points. TomTom's Floating Car Data fills these gaps continuously, providing a network-wide view that fixed infrastructure cannot economically provide.

The equity dimension introduced in Samuel Kapsch's statement — understanding congestion impacts and ensuring equitable mobility — reflects a shift in how urban traffic management is being articulated politically. Beyond efficiency metrics, modern traffic management is increasingly evaluated against questions of who bears the burden of congestion (often lower-income communities without alternative transport options) and whether traffic system design considers diverse users' needs.

For road authorities evaluating the platform, the combination of richer data and streamlined deployment creates a compelling operational and procurement case. The standard integration approach reduces the technical risk that often accompanies enterprise platform upgrades in public sector environments, where project failures carry significant political and financial consequences.
