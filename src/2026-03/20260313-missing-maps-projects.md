# Current Projects - Missing Maps
**Source**: https://missingmaps.org/projects/
**Date**: 2026-03-13
**Author**: Missing Maps / HOT
**Keywords**: Missing Maps, HOT Tasking Manager, MapSwipe, humanitarian mapping, volunteer cartography, field mapping, mapathon

## Elevator pitch
Missing Maps' projects hub connects volunteers of varying expertise to active humanitarian mapping campaigns through MapSwipe, the HOT Tasking Manager, and field mapping workflows.

## Takeaways
- Missing Maps organizes volunteer mapping through three pathways: MapSwipe (quick tasks), HOT Tasking Manager (detailed mapping), and Field Mapping
- Priority campaigns are regularly updated and matched to volunteer skill levels
- The platform provides resources for hosting mapathons and training materials for new contributors
- Contact is managed through HOT (Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team) at info@hotosm.org
- The initiative serves as a gateway for turning volunteer time into actionable humanitarian geographic data

## Synthesis
Missing Maps' projects page functions as the operational interface between the humanitarian mapping organization and its global volunteer community. The platform structures volunteer engagement around three progressively intensive participation modes, enabling contributors with varying technical skills and time availability to contribute meaningfully to humanitarian mapping goals.

MapSwipe serves as the entry point: a mobile application that presents satellite imagery tiles for volunteers to classify — marking areas containing buildings, roads, or populated areas that warrant more detailed mapping. This binary classification task requires no cartographic experience and can be completed in spare moments on a phone, making it the widest-funnel participation mode.

The HOT Tasking Manager represents the core mapping contribution workflow. Here, volunteers use OpenStreetMap tools to trace buildings, roads, and waterways in areas that humanitarian organizations identify as priority zones — typically regions affected by disaster, conflict, or disease outbreaks where accurate mapping directly informs response logistics. This requires more commitment and basic OSM skills, but the platform provides training materials to support new contributors.

Field Mapping involves on-the-ground data collection in the communities being mapped, typically conducted by local teams in coordination with international volunteers. This is the most impactful but also most resource-intensive form of contribution.

The mapathon resources — guides for organizations wanting to host community mapping events — reflect Missing Maps' strategy of building a distributed network of active contributors rather than relying solely on individual participation. Organized events concentrated in universities, companies, and community organizations have historically been significant drivers of contribution volume.

The humanitarian geography use case represented by Missing Maps — turning volunteer attention into life-saving logistical data — remains one of the clearest demonstrations of participatory cartography's practical value.
