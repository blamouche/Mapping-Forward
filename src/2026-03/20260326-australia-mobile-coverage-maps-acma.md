# New rules for mobile phone coverage maps in Australia
**Source**: https://www.acma.gov.au/articles/2026-03/new-rules-mobile-phone-coverage-maps
**Date**: March 26, 2026
**Author**: Australian Communications and Media Authority (ACMA)
**Keywords**: ACMA, mobile coverage maps, Australia, standardization, Optus, Telstra, TPG, consumer protection

## Elevator pitch
Australia's ACMA introduces mandatory standardized mobile phone coverage maps requiring carriers to display 4G/5G coverage in four categories by June 2026, enabling consumers to compare networks on equal terms.

## Takeaways
- New industry standard requires standardized coverage maps with four categories: good, moderate, basic, no coverage
- Carriers must publish maps by June 30, 2026 and update them every three months
- All three Australian mobile operators (Optus, Telstra, TPG) must comply and provide maps to their MVNOs
- Maps are based on predictive modelling with plain English descriptions of each coverage level
- Non-compliance may result in enforceable undertakings, remedial directions, and financial penalties

## Synthesis
The Australian Communications and Media Authority (ACMA) has introduced the Telecommunications (Mobile Network Coverage Maps) Industry Standard 2026, requiring mobile phone carriers to publish standardized coverage maps for the first time in Australia's history. This regulation addresses a persistent consumer frustration: existing coverage maps from different carriers used inconsistent methodologies and presentations, making meaningful comparison impossible.

The new standard mandates four coverage categories — good, moderate, basic, and no coverage — with plain English descriptions that consumers can actually understand. "Good" coverage means high-quality, seamless connectivity for voice, SMS, and data. "No coverage" areas acknowledge that some locations may still have very limited connectivity even where the map shows nothing, but service overall is expected to be "very limited, inconsistent or non-existent."

ACMA Chair Nerida O'Loughlin stated the core problem directly: "Mobile providers make available network coverage maps, but they are measured and presented differently. We know that consumers are frustrated that, as a result, they can't make any meaningful comparison between them." The new standard resolves this by imposing a uniform measurement and presentation methodology across all carriers.

Maps must be updated at least quarterly to reflect network changes. The three major operators — Optus, Telstra, and TPG — must also provide compliant maps to their Mobile Virtual Network Operators (MVNOs), ensuring that even customers of smaller resellers benefit from accurate coverage information.

This development is relevant to the mapping and GIS community because it represents an official recognition that geographic data presentation standards matter for consumer outcomes. The debate over how to represent uncertainty, coverage gradients, and edge cases in network maps mirrors challenges in many other mapping domains — from flood risk maps to wildfire risk maps — where the choice of categories and thresholds has direct public impact.
