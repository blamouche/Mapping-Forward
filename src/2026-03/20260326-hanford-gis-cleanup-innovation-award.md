# Hanford Contractor Recognized for Advancing Cleanup With Innovative GIS Tools
**Source**: https://www.energy.gov/em/articles/hanford-contractor-recognized-advancing-cleanup-innovative-gis-tools
**Date**: March 26, 2026
**Author**: U.S. Department of Energy
**Keywords**: Hanford, GIS, nuclear cleanup, Esri, FedGIS, HMIS, environmental management

## Elevator pitch
Hanford Mission Integration Solutions received Esri's "Making a Difference" award at the 2026 FedGIS conference for deploying innovative GIS tools that advance the cleanup of the Hanford nuclear site.

## Takeaways
- HMIS received the Esri "Making a Difference" award at the 2026 FedGIS conference in Washington D.C.
- GIS tools at Hanford streamline infrastructure operations, improve emergency response, and support environmental management
- Esri president Jack Dangermond presented the award, recognizing HMIS's exceptional GIS application
- Hanford is one of the most complex nuclear cleanup sites in the U.S.
- Award highlights GIS's critical role in large-scale government infrastructure management

## Synthesis
The U.S. Department of Energy announced that Hanford Mission Integration Solutions (HMIS), the essential services prime contractor at the Hanford nuclear site in Washington state, received Esri's "Making a Difference" award at the 2026 FedGIS conference. The award recognizes exceptional use of geographic information system technology to improve operations and benefit communities.

Hanford is among the most challenging nuclear remediation projects in the world — a former plutonium production complex whose cleanup involves decades of work, billions of dollars, and immense logistical complexity. In this context, GIS tools serve multiple critical functions: mapping contamination plumes, tracking remediation progress, managing underground infrastructure, coordinating emergency response, and supporting environmental compliance documentation.

HMIS has deployed GIS to streamline infrastructure operations, improve emergency response capabilities, and support environmental management practices at the site. The specific applications recognized by Esri likely include spatial tracking of underground storage tanks and piping systems (a major Hanford safety concern), coordination of construction and remediation activities across a geographically large and complex site, and real-time situational awareness for emergency response teams.

The FedGIS conference is the premier event for geospatial professionals in government, bringing together federal agencies, defense organizations, and contractors who rely on GIS for mission-critical operations. Recognition at this forum carries significant weight in the government GIS community.

This award highlights an often-overlooked dimension of GIS applications: their role in large-scale, long-duration government infrastructure projects where spatial data management is not merely a planning convenience but a safety-critical operational requirement. The Hanford example demonstrates that GIS technology, often associated with urban planning and consumer mapping, is equally essential in complex environmental remediation contexts.
