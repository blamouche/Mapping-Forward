# Cruisebound propose « Search by Map », la recherche de croisière par carte
**Source**: https://www.tom.travel/2026/03/27/cruisebound-propose-search-by-map-la-recherche-de-croisiere-par-carte/
**Date**: March 27, 2026
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: cruise search, map interface, travel tech, itinerary discovery, UX

## Elevator pitch
Cruisebound introduced a map-first search interface that lets users explore cruise ports and routes visually, aiming to make itinerary discovery faster and more intuitive than list-based filters.

## Takeaways
- The “Search by Map” feature centers cruise discovery on an interactive world map.
- Users can click ports to reveal connected itineraries and real departures.
- Cruisebound positions the feature as both a design and engineering challenge.
- The tool addresses customer uncertainty about available cruise routes.
- Map-first discovery differentiates Cruisebound in a competitive OTA market.

## Synthesis
Cruisebound announced “Search by Map,” a cartographic interface that puts a world map at the center of cruise discovery. Instead of relying on filters and lists, the feature allows travelers to explore ports of departure, arrival, and stopovers directly on the map, revealing routes and available itineraries linked to each location. The company positions this as an immediate, visual way to understand the structure of cruise options across regions.

The article frames the feature as both a user-experience and engineering challenge. Cruise itineraries are inherently complex, with multiple ports, route variations, and departure dates. Cruisebound’s approach aims to make that complexity legible at a glance by anchoring the exploration in spatial context. The result, according to the company, is a faster, more dynamic way to connect ports, routes, and departures, allowing users to visualize their trip before booking.

A key motivation is the discovery gap in cruise planning: many customers are not aware of the range of destinations accessible by cruise or how routes connect across regions. By using a map-first interface, Cruisebound tries to replace abstract itinerary lists with spatial exploration, turning the search into a browsing experience. This is aligned with the company’s “Cruising Made Simple” positioning and its effort to differentiate itself in a crowded online travel agency landscape.

The feature is live on Cruisebound’s website and integrates with its broader marketplace for comparing cruise offerings from major lines. Cruisebound describes its platform as offering transparent pricing, flexible payment options, and no booking fees. “Search by Map” is presented as an additional layer that accelerates route discovery and strengthens the platform’s value proposition.

Overall, the announcement highlights a trend in travel tech: replacing static, form-based search with spatial and visual interfaces that align with how travelers conceptualize destinations. For cruise planning, where routes are geographic by nature and port sequences matter, a map-first design offers a more intuitive way to explore and compare options, while also serving as a differentiator in the user experience.
