# USGS National Cooperative Geologic Mapping Program Announces 2026 EDMAP Funding Opportunity
**Source**: https://www.usgs.gov/programs/national-cooperative-geologic-mapping-program/news/usgs-national-cooperative-geologic
**Date**: March 27, 2026
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: geologic mapping, EDMAP, USGS, funding, education

## Elevator pitch
USGS opened the 2026 EDMAP call, offering geologic mapping grants to U.S. universities to train students through field-based mapping projects with proposals due May 26.

## Takeaways
- EDMAP funds geologic mapping projects led by faculty mentors at U.S. institutions.
- Awards support graduate and upper-level undergraduate students for 12–24 months.
- The program targets hands-on field mapping culminating in first-authored maps.
- Key dates include a March 27 webinar and May 26, 2026 proposal deadline.
- EDMAP supports workforce development and national geologic mapping priorities.

## Synthesis
The USGS National Cooperative Geologic Mapping Program announced the 2026 EDMAP funding opportunity, a grant program designed to train geoscience students in professional geologic mapping. The call is open to faculty and adjunct faculty at accredited colleges and universities in the United States and its territories, and it supports student-led projects under the guidance of a faculty mentor. These projects typically span one to two years and culminate in the production of a first‑authored geologic map by participating students.

EDMAP is positioned as the educational component of the National Cooperative Geologic Mapping Program. Its focus is on field-based experience and practical skill building, emphasizing mapping techniques that are foundational for careers in geologic surveying and broader geoscience roles. The announcement highlights that since 1996, the program has supported more than 1,500 student mappers across 177 universities in 46 states, Puerto Rico, and the District of Columbia, underlining its role as a pipeline for mapping expertise.

The notice lays out the key logistics for the 2026 cycle. Proposals are due by 5:00 PM EDT on May 26, 2026, and projects can run for 12 to 24 months, with a target earliest start date of August 1, 2026. USGS will host a Microsoft Teams webinar on March 27, 2026 to explain the proposal process and answer questions, followed by regular “office hours” sessions for additional guidance. The call also specifies geographic constraints: both the proposing institution and study areas must be in the United States or U.S. territories.

Beyond the administrative details, the announcement frames EDMAP as a program that generates tangible mapping outputs while strengthening collaboration between academia and government. Student maps can contribute to state and national mapping priorities, and in many cases are published through state geological surveys. The emphasis on mentorship and field experience positions EDMAP as a structured pathway for training the next generation of geologic mappers.

In summary, the announcement signals a new funding cycle for a long-running mapping education program, with clear timelines, eligibility criteria, and support structures designed to align university research, student training, and national geologic mapping goals.
