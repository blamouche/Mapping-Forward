# Mieux que Google Maps : cette appli GPS gratuite vous guide partout sans connexion Internet
**Source**: https://www.commentcamarche.net/applis-sites/applications/37469-comaps/
**Date**: Unknown
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: offline navigation, OpenStreetMap, privacy, open source, GPS

## Elevator pitch
CoMaps positions itself as a fully offline, privacy-first GPS app built on OpenStreetMap that trades real-time traffic for autonomy, transparency, and community governance.

## Takeaways
- CoMaps offers offline navigation with pre-downloaded maps and no account, ads, or tracking.
- The project emerged from a split with Organic Maps to preserve open-source transparency.
- It supports multi-mode routing, outdoor maps, and common GPS export formats.
- Privacy is central, with audits reporting zero trackers or abusive permissions.
- Lack of live traffic remains its key limitation versus Google Maps or Waze.

## Synthesis
CoMaps is presented as a navigation app that takes a deliberately different stance from mainstream GPS services: it is designed to be fully offline, community-run, and privacy-first. The article emphasizes the practical constraints of most navigation apps, which either require an active data connection or provide limited offline modes. CoMaps addresses those gaps by allowing users to download regional maps in advance and then navigate without any network access, a model that suits remote travel, foreign roaming situations, and “white zone” areas without coverage.

A key part of the story is governance and transparency. CoMaps is positioned as a fork from Organic Maps following a dispute around hidden proprietary components and broader concerns about stewardship. That origin story is not just background; it frames CoMaps as a community effort to restore trust in the open-source promise. The project leans on OpenStreetMap data, highlighting the role of volunteer mapping communities in enabling alternatives to commercial mapping platforms.

Functionally, the app aims to match everyday navigation needs while staying offline. It supports route planning, place search, saved journeys, and turn-by-turn guidance for walking, biking, and driving. For outdoor contexts, it brings forward trails, campgrounds, water sources, elevation lines, and bike routes, which are essential for hikers and cyclists. The inclusion of offline Wikipedia articles and metro maps signals a broader “offline travel companion” angle, while export/import via GPX, KML, and KMZ ensures compatibility with hiking devices and other navigation tools.

Privacy is the differentiator that the article highlights most. CoMaps allegedly collects no personal data, avoids advertising, and does not require accounts. An external audit by Exodus Privacy is cited to reinforce the claim of zero trackers and no abusive permissions. Additionally, project finances and development decisions are made public via Open Collective, which frames CoMaps as a civic-style infrastructure rather than a commercial service.

The main trade-off is real-time traffic. Without live congestion data, CoMaps cannot optimize for the constantly changing conditions that drive everyday commuting. The article notes that real-time traffic is in development, but until then, CoMaps is less suitable for heavy daily highway use and more appropriate for predictable trips, travel, or outdoor navigation. Overall, the piece positions CoMaps as an alternative mapping tool for users who value autonomy and privacy over convenience features tied to data collection.
