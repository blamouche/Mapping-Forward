# Google Maps simplifies battery predictions and trip planning for 350+ Android Auto EV models
**Source**: https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/google-maps-simplifies-battery-predictions-and-trip-planning-for-350-android-auto-ev-models/
**Date**: March 30, 2026
**Author**: Google
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Android Auto, EV trip planning, battery predictions, AI energy models

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps expands AI-powered EV charging features to 350+ Android Auto car models, recommending when and where to charge based on vehicle model, battery level, traffic, elevation, and weather.

## Takeaways
- Official Google announcement of AI-powered EV trip planning for 350+ Android Auto models
- Users add EV info in Maps, set destination, and see expected battery usage
- Adding current charge level generates recommended stops and updated ETA
- AI combines vehicle details (weight, battery size) with real-time traffic, road elevation, and weather
- Rolling out across 15+ brands in the US, with more vehicles and markets coming soon

## Synthesis
This is the official Google Blog announcement for the Android Auto EV trip planning feature in Google Maps. As the primary source, it provides the authoritative description of how the feature works and Google's strategic framing of the launch.

The core feature is described succinctly: "Maps will recommend when and where to charge based on your EV model and battery level, reducing range anxiety and the need to juggle multiple apps." Users configure their EV in the Maps app settings, set a destination in their car's Android Auto interface, and see projected battery consumption. Adding current charge level generates recommended charging stops, estimated arrival battery percentage, and an updated ETA that factors in charging time.

Google's technical description is notably precise: "To deliver accurate battery predictions for hundreds of vehicles, we combine AI with advanced energy models that analyze car details — like weight and battery size — alongside Maps' real-time information about traffic, road elevation and weather." This combination is what distinguishes Google's approach from static manufacturer data: the predictions adapt to real driving conditions.

The announcement positions this as a scalable platform play — rather than negotiating individual data integrations with each automaker (as some competitors do), Google built a general model that works across hundreds of vehicles. This approach trades some precision (no live battery readout from the car) for dramatically broader reach.

The rollout covers 15+ brands in the US including Audi, BMW, Chevrolet, FIAT, Genesis, Hyundai, Jaguar, Kia, Lexus, Lucid, Mercedes-Benz, Nissan, Porsche, Subaru, Toyota, and Volkswagen. Google indicates that additional models and markets will follow, signaling this as the beginning of a broader EV navigation strategy rather than a one-time feature launch.
