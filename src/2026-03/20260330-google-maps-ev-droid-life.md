# Google Maps Gets Feature Every EV Owner Has Dreamed Of
**Source**: https://www.droid-life.com/2026/03/30/google-maps-ev-trip-planning/
**Date**: March 30, 2026
**Author**: Droid Life
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Android Auto, EV trip planning, charging stops, battery predictions, electric vehicles

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps finally integrates EV road trip planning into Android Auto, letting drivers see battery consumption, recommended charging stops, and arrival estimates — for 350+ models across 16 brands.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps EV trip planning arrives for Android Auto users with 350+ models supported
- Feature accessible from in-car interface: set destination, add charging, input starting battery %
- System calculates when to stop, how long to charge, and battery percentage at arrival
- Users can specify desired arrival battery level for route adjustments
- AI-powered but not connected to vehicle — driving habits not captured for further accuracy

## Synthesis
Droid Life covers the Google Maps EV trip planning launch from an Android enthusiast perspective, with a focus on user experience and practical workflow. The article leads with the emotional resonance that EV owners will recognize: Google Maps has long been a go-to navigation app for everyday driving, but for road trips it has consistently been the wrong choice because it couldn't handle the complexity of EV range planning.

The article describes the specific UI flow in detail: connect to Android Auto, set a destination, click "Add charging," enter starting battery percentage. From there, Google Maps calculates when charging stops are needed, how long each stop should take, expected battery level at arrival, and overall ETA. The ability to specify desired arrival battery percentage is highlighted as a particularly useful feature — drivers who want to arrive with a comfortable buffer rather than running to the nearest charger can configure this preference.

One honest limitation noted: the system is "AI-powered and won't take into account your actual driving habits to get even more accurate, which is something your car's built-in systems can often do." Tesla's Autopilot routing, for example, learns individual driver behavior over time to refine predictions. Google's system starts fresh each time, relying on population-level efficiency models rather than personalized data.

Droid Life's enthusiasm is genuine but calibrated: this is a meaningful improvement for the majority of Android Auto EV users who lack quality built-in navigation, while acknowledging that it doesn't match the sophistication of Tesla's native routing or ABRP for users with OBD adapters. For the mainstream EV driver who just wants one app for everything, this is a significant quality of life improvement.
