# Google Maps Is Bringing EV Route Planning To Android Auto
**Source**: https://insideevs.com/news/791556/google-maps-ev-route-planning-android-auto/
**Date**: March 30, 2026
**Author**: Tim Levin, InsideEVs
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Android Auto, EV route planning, A Better Route Planner, charging stops, electric vehicles

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps brings native EV route planning to Android Auto for 350+ models, competing directly with specialized apps like A Better Route Planner and addressing a major gap for EVs without quality built-in navigation.

## Takeaways
- Feature competes with A Better Route Planner (ABRP) and other specialized EV routing apps
- Particularly valuable for Toyotas and other brands without integrated route planning
- Unlike ABRP, Google Maps cannot use an OBD reader for real-time battery data
- Feature does not currently extend to the Google Maps mobile app for non-Android Auto users
- No announcement yet on expanded coverage to Google Maps for iPhone/non-Android Auto users

## Synthesis
InsideEVs, as a dedicated electric vehicle publication, provides the most EV-market-specific analysis of Google's new route planning feature. Author Tim Levin correctly contextualizes the launch within the ecosystem of specialized EV tools that currently dominate this space.

The competitive framing is clear: A Better Route Planner (ABRP) has built a dedicated user base for exactly this use case — long-distance EV trip planning with charging stop recommendations. ABRP's key technical advantage is its ability to connect to a vehicle's OBD port via a Bluetooth adapter, reading real-time battery state of charge directly from the car's computer. This allows ABRP to adjust recommendations based on actual driving behavior and battery performance, rather than relying on AI predictions built from static vehicle specifications.

Google Maps cannot do this via Android Auto. The starting state of charge must be entered manually, and while Google says the system will update estimates as driving and charging progress, it does so through model inference rather than live data. This is a meaningful technical gap for power users who want maximum precision.

However, InsideEVs makes an important practical observation: the feature is particularly valuable for Toyota and other brands that historically have not offered quality integrated EV route planning. For owners of these vehicles, choosing between a clunky proprietary system and Google Maps' familiar interface is an easy decision — and Google's real-time traffic data advantage means that even without live battery data, route planning quality may exceed what these brands' native systems provide.

A notable gap flagged in the article: the feature is currently limited to Android Auto users. iPhone users and those who don't use Android Auto cannot access EV trip planning through Google Maps. No announcement has been made on when (or if) this will expand to the Google Maps mobile app for broader use.
