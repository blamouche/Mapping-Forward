# Google Maps adds AI-based EV route planning to Android Auto
**Source**: https://www.electrive.com/2026/03/31/google-maps-adds-ai-based-ev-route-planning-to-android-auto/
**Date**: March 31, 2026
**Author**: Electrive
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Android Auto, EV route planning, AI battery predictions, electric vehicles

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps introduces AI-based battery prediction and route planning for 350+ EV models on Android Auto, enabling charge stop recommendations without requiring direct vehicle data integration.

## Takeaways
- Feature rolls out initially in the US across 350+ EV models from 15+ brands
- Users manually input starting state of charge; system then updates projections dynamically
- AI and energy modeling combine vehicle data (weight, battery size) with real-time traffic, elevation, and weather
- Google positions Maps as a central EV navigation interface to reduce third-party app reliance
- Additional models and markets to follow

## Synthesis
Electrive, a publication focused on electric vehicle news, provides a concise technical overview of Google's new EV route planning feature for Android Auto. The article emphasizes the architectural choice Google made: enabling route planning without direct vehicle data integration, which allows the feature to scale across a broad vehicle base including models without native Android Auto battery reporting.

The user flow is straightforward: enter vehicle details in the Google Maps app, set a destination in the car, manually input current battery percentage. The system then generates recommended charging stops with timing, duration, and estimated battery level at arrival, plus an updated ETA. Users can also specify a desired arrival battery level.

The technical engine behind this feature combines AI models trained on vehicle efficiency data with real-time Maps information — traffic conditions, road elevation, and weather. Google describes this as combining "AI with advanced energy models that analyze car details — like weight and battery size — alongside Maps' real-time information." The result is dynamic rather than static battery consumption estimates.

The strategic framing is significant: Google explicitly positions this as reducing "range anxiety and the need to juggle multiple apps." By integrating EV planning natively into Maps, Google is competing directly with specialized apps like A Better Route Planner and PlugShare that currently serve this market. Google's advantage is its unmatched real-time traffic data; its limitation is the manual state-of-charge input compared to systems that read live vehicle data.

The rollout beginning in the US with expansion to additional markets reflects a typical Google Maps launch cadence. Electrive notes this is particularly relevant for EV owners whose vehicles lack quality built-in navigation — a common pain point with many non-Tesla EVs that offer subpar native route planning.
