# Google Maps Can Now Plan EV Road Trips With Charging Stops via Android Auto
**Source**: https://www.cnet.com/home/electric-vehicles/google-maps-android-auto-ev-roadrip-charge-plan-update/
**Date**: March 30, 2026
**Author**: Antuan Goodwin, CNET
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Android Auto, EV road trips, charging stops, AI routing, electric vehicles

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps adds native EV road trip planning to Android Auto for 350+ models across 16 brands, bringing route intelligence to older EVs and those who prefer Google Maps over built-in navigation.

## Takeaways
- Feature brings EV charging stop suggestions to Android Auto for 350+ models from 16 brands
- Google positions this as a convenience upgrade for owners of older EVs or those without quality built-in navigation
- System uses AI plus vehicle weight, battery size, EPA range, real-time traffic, elevation, and weather
- Limitation: Android Auto cannot pull live battery data, so starting charge must be entered manually
- Manual updates required during trip if driving differs significantly from AI predictions

## Synthesis
CNET's automotive-focused review of Google Maps' new EV trip planning feature provides a practically grounded perspective that addresses both the genuine value proposition and the real limitations of the system.

The context CNET sets is historically accurate: early EV road trips required "multiple mapping and EV charging network apps and careful planning" — a fragmented, stressful experience. Today, nearly all modern EVs include connected navigation with automatic charging stop suggestions. But "Google Maps' new EV route planning function brings some of that new EV convenience to older models and to drivers who prefer the Google Maps and Android Auto ecosystem."

The technical limitation is addressed directly and honestly: "phone-based Android Auto is currently unable to automatically pull live battery information from most cars." Users must manually enter their starting battery percentage, and the system may not automatically adjust if real-world efficiency differs from the AI model's predictions. Factors like a heavy payload, roof rack, trailer, or under-inflated tires that reduce range faster than expected won't be captured automatically.

CNET's assessment is appropriately measured: "this new automatic charge plan generation sounds like a promising tool to boost convenience and reduce range anxiety at the beginning of a long journey." The use case is particularly relevant for owners of older EVs without smart in-car navigation and for drivers who prefer Maps' interface over their vehicle's built-in system.

The review notes that the Google Maps support page allows users to check vehicle compatibility — an important practical detail for readers who want to know if their specific EV and trim level is covered among the 16 makes and 350+ models supported at launch.
