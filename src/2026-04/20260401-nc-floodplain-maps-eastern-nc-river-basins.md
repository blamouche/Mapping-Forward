# New Advisory Floodplain Maps Available for Five Eastern North Carolina River Basins
**Source**: https://www.deq.nc.gov/news/press-releases/2026/04/01/new-advisory-floodplain-maps-available-five-eastern-north-carolina-river-basins
**Date**: April 1, 2026
**Author**: NC Department of Environmental Quality
**Keywords**: North Carolina, floodplain maps, Flood Resiliency Blueprint, FEMA, flood risk, river basins, climate resilience

## Elevator pitch
North Carolina's DEQ releases enhanced advisory floodplain maps for five Eastern NC river basins using 2D rain-on-grid modeling that reveals 90,000+ buildings at flood risk outside traditional FEMA flood zones.

## Takeaways
- New maps cover the Neuse, Lumber, Tar-Pamlico, White Oak, and Cape Fear river basins
- UNC Chapel Hill research found 90,000+ buildings in Eastern NC flooded at least once from 1996-2020, with 43% outside mapped FEMA floodplains
- 2D rain-on-grid modeling simulates precipitation and surface runoff, capturing flood risk beyond traditional stream-based FEMA boundaries
- The interactive Flood Resiliency Blueprint Tool enables communities to assess vulnerabilities and identify funding opportunities
- French Broad River Basin mapping expected in fall 2026; comprehensive River Basin Action Strategies anticipated for summer 2026

## Synthesis
North Carolina's Department of Environmental Quality has released updated advisory floodplain maps for five Eastern North Carolina river basins as part of its Flood Resiliency Blueprint initiative. The release addresses a significant and well-documented gap in flood risk communication: traditional FEMA flood zone maps systematically undercount flood-vulnerable properties.

The scope of this undercount is substantial. Research from UNC Chapel Hill found that more than 90,000 buildings in Eastern North Carolina flooded at least once between 1996 and 2020 — and critically, 43 percent of those buildings were located outside the mapped FEMA floodplain. This means that nearly half of flood-affected properties were in areas officially categorized as low-risk, leaving property owners, local governments, and insurers without adequate risk information.

The new maps use two-dimensional rain-on-grid modeling to address this gap. Unlike traditional FEMA floodplain mapping, which primarily models flooding based on stream overflow patterns, rain-on-grid modeling simulates precipitation landing across the entire landscape and tracks surface runoff as it flows across terrain. This approach captures flooding from intense rainfall events even in areas miles from streams — a particularly relevant capability for a region like Eastern North Carolina that experiences high-rainfall hurricane and tropical storm events.

The five river basins covered — Neuse, Lumber, Tar-Pamlico, White Oak, and Cape Fear — include some of the most flood-affected areas in North Carolina's history, having experienced repeated major flood events from hurricanes Floyd (1999), Matthew (2016), and Florence (2018).

The interactive Flood Resiliency Blueprint Tool built around these maps enables practical decision-making: local governments and community organizations can use it to assess specific flood vulnerabilities, prioritize resilience projects, evaluate future flooding scenarios under different climate assumptions (including sea level rise and intensified storms), and identify applicable funding sources for resilience investments. The tool's forward-looking climate scenario modeling represents a significant advance over static historical flood mapping.
