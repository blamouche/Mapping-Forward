# Location data for a greener future
**Source**: https://www.tomtom.com/newsroom/behind-the-map/location-intelligence-making-a-greener-future
**Date**: April 1, 2026
**Author**: TomTom Editorial Team
**Keywords**: TomTom, location intelligence, sustainability, EV routing, traffic analytics, CO2 emissions, smart cities

## Elevator pitch
TomTom outlines five ways location intelligence — from smart routing to EV infrastructure planning — is helping reduce transportation CO2 emissions and build more sustainable mobility systems.

## Takeaways
- Transportation is one of the largest contributors to global greenhouse gas emissions
- TomTom's eco-routing helps drivers avoid congestion and reduce fuel consumption
- EV navigation with charging infrastructure data reduces range anxiety and accelerates EV adoption
- Traffic analytics tools enable cities to model infrastructure changes before implementation
- Real-time traffic management tools reduce idling and optimize signal timing

## Synthesis
This editorial from TomTom presents a structured argument for how location intelligence can serve as a practical lever in reducing transportation-related greenhouse gas emissions — one of the most significant contributors to global climate change. The piece is framed around five concrete use cases where TomTom's technology provides measurable environmental benefit.

The first use case is eco-routing: by combining detailed road maps with real-time traffic data, TomTom's navigation systems guide drivers away from congested routes. Stop-and-go traffic generates significantly higher CO2 per kilometer than steady-speed travel, so routing intelligence that smooths traffic flow has direct emissions benefits even when it adds marginal distance.

Second, TomTom addresses EV adoption support. Its navigation products include detailed charging infrastructure data — station locations, availability, compatibility — to reduce "range anxiety" and make EV ownership more practical. The company also notes that aggregated mobility data helps charging operators identify hotspots for station deployment, accelerating infrastructure expansion where it is most needed.

Third, TomTom's geospatial datasets support smarter road network planning. Urban planners and road authorities use maps, traffic flow data, and scenario modeling tools to evaluate proposed infrastructure changes — new roads, redesigned junctions — before physical implementation. This data-driven planning reduces costly mistakes and optimizes outcomes for both congestion and emissions.

Fourth, traffic analytics — including the TomTom Traffic Index and Origin-Destination Analysis — give decision-makers visibility into where congestion occurs, when it peaks, and how travel patterns are shifting. The company's MOVE platform aggregates these insights for environmental impact assessment and infrastructure investment decisions.

Fifth, real-time traffic management tools enable cities to dynamically optimize signal timing and lane management, reducing idling at intersections — a known emissions hotspot. Junction Analytics provides granular intersection-level data to support these optimizations.

Together, these five domains represent a comprehensive argument that mapping and location intelligence are not merely convenience technologies but essential infrastructure for the global decarbonization agenda.
