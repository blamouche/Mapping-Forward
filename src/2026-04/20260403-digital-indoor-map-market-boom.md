## Synthesis
This market research report from Coherent Market Insights covers the Digital Indoor Map sector, projecting strong growth from 2026 to 2033. Digital indoor maps differ from 3D wayfinding products in that they focus more broadly on the mapping data layer — the floor plans, spatial metadata, and point-of-interest databases that power navigation, facility management, and spatial analytics in interior environments.

The competitive landscape is notable for its diversity: technology giants (Google, Apple, Microsoft) compete with specialized indoor mapping companies (Navvis, IndoorAtlas) and enterprise infrastructure firms (Siemens, Smappee). This heterogeneity reflects the reality that indoor mapping touches multiple industry verticals simultaneously, each with different technical requirements and deployment models.

Google's Indoor Maps product and Apple's Indoor Maps initiative represent the consumer-facing layer, where users benefit from seamless transitions between outdoor navigation and interior wayfinding in major venues. However, enterprise deployments — facility management in hospitals, inventory tracking in warehouses, visitor guidance in museums — are handled by specialized providers with deeper integrations into building management systems.

Key technical trends include the use of LiDAR scanning (via companies like Navvis) to create high-precision 3D floor plans automatically, replacing manual drafting workflows. AI-powered occupancy tracking and heat mapping are emerging use cases that transform indoor maps from static wayfinding tools into dynamic operational intelligence platforms.

The smart city dimension is relevant here: as urban centers invest in digital twin infrastructure and IoT sensor networks, indoor mapping data becomes a critical layer that completes the spatial picture of city operations — connecting building interiors to outdoor environments in a unified spatial model.
