# National Geospatial Policy Strengthening India's Mapping Ecosystem

## Elevator pitch
India's National Geospatial Policy 2022 is transforming the country's mapping ecosystem by democratizing spatial data access, targeting a complete Digital Elevation Model by 2030, and establishing the Geospatial Data Promotion and Development Committee as a national coordinating body.

## Takeaways
- National Geospatial Policy 2022 aims to make India a global geospatial leader
- Targets include a Digital Elevation Model for all of India by 2030 and a National Digital Twin for major urban centers by 2035
- Two key platforms: National Geospatial Data Registry (NGDR) and Unified Geospatial Interface (UGI)
- The National Geospatial Mission 2025-26 focuses on foundational infrastructure
- BISAG-N and the Survey of India (est. 1767) are key implementing institutions

## Synthesis
India's National Geospatial Policy 2022 represents a significant policy shift toward democratizing access to spatial data and positioning India as a major global player in geospatial technology. This article from Usthadian provides an educational overview of the policy's objectives, institutional framework, and key targets.

The policy's core ambition is to remove the restrictions that previously limited access to geospatial data in India — restrictions rooted in historical security concerns that hampered both commercial innovation and government efficiency. By opening access to mapping data for government agencies, private firms, researchers, and startups, the policy seeks to unlock innovation across sectors from smart cities to climate monitoring.

Concrete targets demonstrate the policy's ambition: a high-resolution Digital Elevation Model covering the entire country by 2030, providing accurate terrain data for planning and disaster mitigation; and a National Digital Twin for major urban centers by 2035. The digital twin target is particularly significant — it represents a commitment to creating comprehensive spatial replicas of Indian cities that can support urban planning simulation, infrastructure management, and emergency response coordination.

The institutional infrastructure is equally important. The Geospatial Data Promotion and Development Committee (GDPDC) serves as the apex national body for coordination. Two digital platforms — the National Geospatial Data Registry (NGDR) and the Unified Geospatial Interface (UGI) — will provide centralized data access and seamless cross-platform integration. The Continuously Operating Reference Stations (CORS) network, built by the Survey of India, provides the high-precision positioning foundation that underpins the entire ecosystem.

Supporting initiatives include the National Geospatial Mission 2025-26 and Operation Dronagiri, a pilot project demonstrating practical geospatial applications in governance. BISAG-N (Bhaskaracharya National Institute for Space Applications and Geo-informatics) provides technical implementation capacity for GIS-based government projects. This combination of policy ambition, institutional infrastructure, and technical capability positions India for significant geospatial sector growth in the coming decade.
