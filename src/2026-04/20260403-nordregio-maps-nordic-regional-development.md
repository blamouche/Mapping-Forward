## Elevator pitch
Nordregio's maps collection provides over 200 spatial analyses covering demography, labour markets, migration, and regional development across Nordic, Arctic, and Baltic regions — a reference resource for evidence-based regional policy.

## Synthesis
Nordregio, the Nordic Council of Ministers' research center for regional development and planning, maintains an extensive public collection of spatial analyses and cartographic work focused on the Nordic, Arctic, and Baltic regions. This maps portal serves as a reference resource for researchers, policymakers, and journalists working on Northern European regional development.

The collection is organized around research themes reflecting Nordregio's core mandate: demography leads with 201 maps covering population aging, fertility trends, and population change by municipality; labour market analysis follows with 78 maps; migration and integration research accounts for 60 maps; and regional development analysis contributes 29 maps. Smaller thematic collections cover digitalisation, governance, rural development, sustainability, and urban planning.

Recent additions to the collection include analyses of population aged 80 and over as a share of total population (2025 data), total population change by municipality from 1990 to 2024, total fertility rates for women (2024), and green patent applications per 10,000 inhabitants covering 2021 to 2023. The temporal depth of some analyses — spanning multiple decades of demographic and economic change — makes the collection particularly valuable for tracking long-term regional trajectories.

The Arctic dimension of Nordregio's mapping work is increasingly relevant in a context of rapid environmental change and growing geopolitical attention to the region. The 60 migration and integration maps reflect another area of current policy relevance across Nordic countries, providing spatial context for policy debates around settlement patterns and integration outcomes.

From a cartographic perspective, Nordregio's maps demonstrate the value of institutional, thematic map publishing: maintaining a coherent, long-running series of comparable regional analyses enables evidence-based comparison across time and place — a function that ad hoc mapping projects cannot easily replicate.
