# La dernière mise à jour de Google Maps va tout changer pour les trajets en voiture électrique
**Source**: https://www.automobile-magazine.fr/toute-l-actualite/article/51488-la-derniere-mise-a-jour-de-google-maps-va-tout-changer-pour-les-trajets-en-voiture-electrique
**Date**: 2026-04-04
**Author**: David Lefevre
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Android Auto, EV routing, charging infrastructure, battery prediction, automotive UX

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps is bringing EV-aware trip planning to Android Auto, reducing the need for separate charging apps while still relying on user-entered battery levels rather than live vehicle telemetry.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps can now insert charging stops automatically for EV road trips in Android Auto.
- The system combines vehicle attributes with traffic, relief, and weather to estimate consumption.
- Drivers still need to enter the starting battery percentage manually on smartphone-based setups.
- The feature initially supports about 350 models from 16 brands in the US rollout.
- It positions Google more centrally in the in-car digital ecosystem versus carmaker-native systems.

## Synthesis
Automobile Magazine looks at Google Maps’ latest EV-routing update through an automotive usability lens. The article argues that the real value is not simply the addition of another feature, but the reduction of friction for electric-vehicle owners who currently have to patch together route guidance and charging logistics across several apps.

The update enables Google Maps, via Android Auto, to propose charging stops automatically when planning a trip. The user enters a destination and the system integrates recharge stages into the journey while estimating consumption and arrival battery level. The article presents this as a meaningful simplification for drivers whose cars do not already include a capable built-in route planner.

A key nuance is the distinction between smartphone-based Google Maps on Android Auto and deeply integrated in-car systems such as Android Automotive or certain manufacturer-native navigation stacks. In the Android Auto version, Google Maps cannot directly read live battery telemetry from the vehicle, so the driver must manually provide the initial charge level. That means the model can be smart, but it is still partly blind to real-world deviations such as extra cargo, a roof box, aggressive driving, or adverse weather. The article rightly treats this as a practical limitation rather than a minor detail.

The rollout details reinforce the platform story. The feature launches first in the United States for roughly 350 EV models from 16 brands. Configuration requires the user to declare vehicle type, model, and charging connector details in the app. Once set up, the route guidance displays projected consumption, battery level on arrival, and recommended charging durations directly in the itinerary.

The broader implication is competitive. By making Maps more vehicle-aware, Google strengthens its role inside the automotive software stack and pushes closer to the territory traditionally occupied by OEM infotainment systems and specialized EV route-planning apps. The article therefore captures both the consumer benefit and the strategic platform move behind the update.
