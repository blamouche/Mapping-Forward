# TomTom integrates traffic info with Locus platform
**Source**: https://www.itsinternational.com/news/tomtom-integrates-traffic-info-locus-platform
**Date**: Unknown
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: TomTom, Locus, traffic analytics, location intelligence, mobility planning

## Elevator pitch
TomTom is feeding its traffic data into Locus so agencies and operators can use a broader location-intelligence platform for transport planning, operations and infrastructure decisions.

## Takeaways
- TomTom and Locus have partnered to integrate TomTom Traffic into the Locus analytics platform.
- Locus says the new input will strengthen transportation modelling, traffic operations, parking, venue management and infrastructure use cases.
- The companies frame the integration as a way to support faster and more proactive decisions.
- The pitch also links better traffic intelligence to lower waste and reduced emissions.
- The partnership targets both public- and private-sector mobility stakeholders in North America.

## Synthesis
This short industry note reports a data partnership rather than a new consumer product, but it reflects a familiar pattern in the mobility stack: traffic information is becoming an embedded input for broader analytics platforms instead of a standalone product category. TomTom is integrating its traffic feed into Locus, a company focused on mobility analytics and location intelligence. The promise is that clients will be able to combine traffic conditions with other operational and planning workflows rather than treating congestion data as a separate dashboard.

The article highlights the practical domains where this matters: transportation modelling, traffic operations, parking, venue management and infrastructure. That list is useful because it shows how location intelligence is widening from traditional navigation and routing toward decision support for agencies and operators. Traffic data has value when it helps answer planning questions such as where bottlenecks persist, how demand patterns shift over time, or which interventions are worth funding. In that context, TomTom is acting as a supplier of situational data while Locus packages it into higher-level analytical workflows.

Another takeaway is the continued convergence between AI-forward messaging and geospatial products. Locus describes its platform as AI-forward, while TomTom emphasizes the quality and real-time character of its traffic information. Even without much technical detail, the combination suggests a market where raw mobility data is increasingly expected to support forecasting, simulation and operational prioritization. The article’s language about proactive decision-making and reduced wasted spending points to the business case: better traffic intelligence is supposed to improve resource allocation, not just describe network conditions after the fact.

For mapping and transport watchers, the significance is modest but clear. TomTom continues to expand beyond navigation interfaces and automotive use cases by placing its data inside third-party planning platforms. That is strategically important because it broadens the company’s relevance to public agencies, infrastructure managers and urban operators. It also reinforces a wider industry trend in which the competitive edge lies in making traffic and map data interoperable with the software environments where planning decisions are actually made.
