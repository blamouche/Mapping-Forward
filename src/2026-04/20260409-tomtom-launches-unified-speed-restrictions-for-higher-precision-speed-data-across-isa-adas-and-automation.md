# TomTom launches Unified Speed Restrictions for higher-precision speed data across ISA, ADAS and automation
**Source**: https://www.tomtom.com/newsroom/press-releases/general/163629779976706/tomtom-launches-unified-speed-restrictions-for-higher-precision-speed-data-across-isa-adas-and-automation/
**Date**: April 9, 2026
**Author**: TomTom
**Keywords**: TomTom, ADAS, ISA, speed limits, automated driving

## Elevator pitch
TomTom is packaging its static, live, and sensed speed-limit data into one continuously refreshed service for safer and more automation-ready driving systems.

## Takeaways
- TomTom launched Unified Speed Restrictions to improve the precision and freshness of speed-limit data.
- The service combines unsigned speed limits, sign recognition, probe data, and real-time variable speed limits into one output.
- TomTom is targeting both minimum ISA compliance and the higher reliability needed for driver trust.
- The product is designed to support more advanced ADAS and automated-driving functions that depend on accurate speed context.
- It is available both as an API and as a pre-integrated component within the TomTom ADAS SDK.

## Synthesis
TomTom’s new Unified Speed Restrictions service is aimed at a specific but strategically important layer of the automotive stack: speed-limit intelligence. The company is positioning the product as more than a compliance tool for Intelligent Speed Assistance regulations. Instead, it argues that accurate speed data must become a dependable foundation for a wider range of advanced driver assistance and automated-driving features. That is an important distinction, because regulatory compliance can tolerate occasional imperfections, while driver trust and automated decision-making require much more consistent performance.

The technical proposition is data fusion. TomTom says the service combines several categories of speed information into a single continuously refreshed output: conventional mapped limits, unsigned restrictions, roadside sign recognition, probe data, and real-time variable speed limits. In theory, that should reduce the mismatch between what a map says, what the vehicle senses, and what is actually in force on the road at any moment. For OEMs, this matters because speed-limit errors can undermine both safety systems and user confidence. A system that regularly shows the wrong limit may satisfy a rulebook threshold but still feel unreliable in day-to-day driving.

TomTom also ties the launch to the evolution of ADAS and vehicle automation. Accurate speed context supports smoother maneuver planning, better predictions, and more comfortable intervention logic across a variety of driving situations. In other words, speed data is being reframed as a dynamic control input rather than a passive informational overlay. That aligns with the broader industry move toward richer environment models for automated functions, where mapping, sensor fusion, and live traffic context all contribute to how vehicles interpret the road.

Commercially, the service is meant to reduce integration friction. TomTom is offering it as both a standalone API and as part of the TomTom ADAS SDK, which lets automakers and Tier 1 suppliers consume the data within existing software stacks. The pitch is clear: improve ISA readiness today, while building a more scalable technical base for predictive assistance and higher levels of automation tomorrow. For mapping and location-tech watchers, this release shows how map providers are moving deeper into operational vehicle intelligence, not just navigation interfaces.
