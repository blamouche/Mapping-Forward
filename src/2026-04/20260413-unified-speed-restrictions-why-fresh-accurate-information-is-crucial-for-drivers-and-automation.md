# Unified Speed Restrictions: Why fresh, accurate information is crucial for drivers and automation
**Source**: https://www.webwire.com/ViewPressRel.asp?aId=353194
**Date**: April 13, 2026
**Author**: WebWire / TomTom
**Keywords**: TomTom, speed restrictions, ADAS, automation, real-time maps

## Elevator pitch
TomTom argues that dynamic speed-limit data is becoming a core layer for both safer driving and better automation.

## Takeaways
- TomTom’s Unified Speed Restrictions combines permanent and temporary speed-limit information into one feed.
- The company positions the product as useful both for human drivers and for ADAS or automated systems.
- Temporary restrictions such as roadworks, weather, or congestion account for a meaningful share of real-world speed changes.
- TomTom relies on multi-source fusion, including sensors, probe data, and government feeds, to maintain freshness.
- The product is framed as a building block in TomTom’s larger push toward a real-time map.

## Synthesis
This WebWire release outlines TomTom’s pitch for Unified Speed Restrictions, a service designed to deliver the “effective” speed limit at any given moment by merging permanent and temporary restrictions into a single feed. The company argues that traditional map speed data is no longer enough because a meaningful share of real-world limits are dynamic, changing in response to roadworks, incidents, congestion, reroutes, weather, or variable message signs.

The release makes two commercial claims at once. First, accurate speed information improves the driver experience by reducing frustration and preserving trust in the vehicle when displayed restrictions match what people actually see on the road. Second, it is critical for driver assistance and automated systems. ADAS functions and automated driving stacks need more than a legal baseline; they need timely, context-rich speed information so the vehicle can anticipate transitions smoothly rather than reacting abruptly once a sensor spots a sign.

TomTom positions the service as going beyond minimum compliance with Intelligent Speed Assistance rules. The company says its standard map data already exceeds the required threshold for regulatory accuracy, but Unified Speed Restrictions extends that capability with live and temporary data. That matters because compliance is one thing, whereas a comfortable and credible automated driving experience requires fresher, more granular information.

Technically, the release emphasizes multi-source fusion. TomTom combines sensor-derived observations from vehicles, probe data that reveals how cars are actually moving, and government feeds where road authorities publish live restrictions. By cross-validating these sources, TomTom claims it can update the map fast enough to reflect temporary conditions while still maintaining precision about where a restriction begins and ends. That positional accuracy is especially relevant for automation because harsh or mistimed braking degrades both safety perception and user trust.

More broadly, the article shows TomTom continuing to redefine the map as a real-time, modular software service for the automotive stack. Unified Speed Restrictions is presented not as a standalone feature but as one integration-ready component within a wider architecture that includes ADAS SDKs and real-time map ambitions. In that sense, the product is part of TomTom’s effort to turn mapping from a static background layer into a live operational feed for software-defined vehicles.
