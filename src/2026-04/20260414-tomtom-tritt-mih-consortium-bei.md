**Date**: March 31, 2022
**Author**: Irina Hübner
**Keywords**: TomTom, MIH Consortium, EV navigation, digital cockpit, automotive platforms

## Elevator pitch
TomTom’s entry into the MIH Consortium shows how map providers seek influence upstream in software-defined EV ecosystems.

## Takeaways
- TomTom joined the Foxconn-backed MIH Consortium as the first global mapmaker in the alliance.
- The consortium aims to accelerate EVs, autonomous driving, and mobility services through shared industry collaboration.
- TomTom’s contribution centers on EV navigation, digital cockpits, and its IndiGO platform.
- The article frames mapping as a strategic layer inside software-defined electric vehicles.
- Even though the article is older, it reflects the long arc of TomTom’s platform strategy in automotive ecosystems.

## Synthesis
Elektroniknet reports that TomTom joined the MIH Consortium, the Foxconn-initiated alliance intended to coordinate technology and automotive partners around electric mobility, autonomous driving, and related mobility services. The article presents TomTom as the first global mapmaker and navigation provider in the consortium, which gives the company a role in shaping shared platforms rather than simply supplying downstream components.

TomTom’s value to the consortium is framed around digital cockpit experience and EV navigation. In particular, the article highlights the company’s routing capabilities for electric vehicles, which combine map, traffic, and vehicle-context data to calculate routes that account for range and charging constraints. That is significant because EV navigation is not just a nicer interface layer; it is a core system for making electric driving feel reliable, especially when range anxiety and charging uncertainty are still barriers to adoption.

The piece also mentions TomTom IndiGO, the company’s open digital cockpit platform, as part of what it brings into the partnership. That detail matters because it shows TomTom pursuing a broader automotive software strategy. Rather than competing only on consumer navigation or map licensing, the company is trying to embed itself deeper into the software-defined vehicle stack, where interfaces, route intelligence, charging logic, and map-based services all converge.

Membership in the MIH Consortium also has a strategic governance dimension. By participating alongside carmakers and technology firms earlier in the ecosystem, TomTom gains influence over priorities, interfaces, and integration patterns that may shape future EV platforms. The article quotes executives stressing collaboration and the need to close gaps between different actors in the automotive ecosystem. In practice, that means mapping data is being treated as infrastructure for platform coordination, not just a vendor add-on.

Although the article dates from 2022, it remains relevant because it captures the direction TomTom has continued to pursue: positioning maps as an essential input for EV user experience, automation, and intelligent mobility services. It shows how the company’s automotive strategy has long been tied to industry alliances and open platforms, anticipating the current push toward software-centric vehicle architectures.
