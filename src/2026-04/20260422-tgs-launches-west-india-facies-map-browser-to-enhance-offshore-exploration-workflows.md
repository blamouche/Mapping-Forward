# TGS Launches West India Facies Map Browser to Enhance Offshore Exploration Workflows
**Source**: https://www.euro-petrole.com/tgs-launches-west-india-facies-map-browser-to-enhance-offshore-exploration-workflows-n-i-29983
**Date**: Unknown
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: TGS, facies map browser, offshore exploration, India, seismic interpretation, basin analysis

## Elevator pitch
TGS has launched a basin-scale facies map browser for India’s west coast that packages seismic, well, and sequence-stratigraphic interpretations into a ready-to-use exploration dataset aimed at accelerating offshore screening and reducing subsurface uncertainty.

## Takeaways
- The product covers about 642,825 square kilometers along India’s west coast.
- It combines regional seismic interpretation, well data, gross depositional environment maps, and stratigraphic correlations.
- TGS positions the dataset as a way to cut time spent sourcing, cleaning, and aligning subsurface information.
- The company says the browser supports the full workflow from basin screening to prospect evaluation.
- The launch is timed to help exploration teams assess opportunities around India’s ongoing licensing rounds.

## Synthesis
This industry announcement describes the launch of TGS’s West India Facies Map Browser, a new interpretation dataset designed to streamline offshore exploration work along India’s west coast. Although the product is framed as a commercial geoscience offering rather than a public mapping initiative, it still reflects an important mapping trend: the growing value of integrated, workflow-ready spatial intelligence products that package interpretation, not just raw data.

The dataset covers a very large offshore area, roughly 642,825 square kilometers, and combines multiple layers of subsurface information into a single framework. According to the release, the browser is built from 65 wells with full sequence-stratigraphic interpretation and includes gross depositional environment maps, seismic horizon interpretations, and detailed stratigraphic correlations. That combination matters because exploration decisions often stall not from lack of data, but from the cost and time required to reconcile inconsistent sources across scales and formats.

TGS’s value proposition is therefore about compression of analytical effort. Instead of asking exploration teams to source regional seismic data, gather well control, align horizons, and build a coherent basin model themselves, the company offers a pre-integrated interpretation layer that can be used immediately for screening and prospect evaluation. In operational terms, that shifts the product from being a passive archive to being an active decision-support environment.

The article emphasizes uncertainty reduction. In offshore exploration, geologic ambiguity is expensive, and each stage of prospect maturation depends on a consistent understanding of basin evolution, reservoir distribution, and depositional context. Facies maps and stratigraphic frameworks help narrow that uncertainty by clarifying where potential reservoir intervals may be present and how they relate across the basin. By turning these interpretations into a browser delivered in multiple technical formats, TGS is effectively offering a map-based interface to geologic reasoning.

The timing is also strategic. The release links the product to India’s ongoing and recent licensing rounds, implying that better regional understanding can improve competitive positioning when assessing open blocks. That makes the browser more than a static geoscience compilation. It is positioned as a tool for faster commercial judgment in a live exploration market.

From a broader mapping perspective, this announcement highlights how specialized sectors increasingly expect maps to function as integrated analytical platforms. In this case, the “map” is not a cartographic end product for public communication, but a structured representation of subsurface space designed to support exploration workflows. The business advantage lies in combining multiple interpretive layers into a format that reduces friction between data acquisition and decision-making.

Overall, the West India Facies Map Browser exemplifies how mapping products in energy are evolving from reference materials into operational intelligence systems. The core promise is speed with confidence: give teams a coherent geologic picture earlier, reduce the effort spent harmonizing inputs, and improve the quality of decisions made under uncertainty.
