# TomTom、自動運転やISA向けに高精度な速度データを提供する新サービスを発表
**Source**: https://ligare.news/story/tomtom_20260422/
**Date**: April 22, 2026
**Author**: LIGARE編集部T.M
**Keywords**: TomTom, Unified Speed Restrictions, ADAS, ISA, autonomous driving, speed data

## Elevator pitch
A member-only LIGARE article reports that TomTom has launched Unified Speed Restrictions, a service designed to deliver more timely and accurate speed-limit data for ADAS, ISA, and automated driving systems.

## Takeaways
- TomTom says the new service is built to provide more timely and accurate speed information.
- The product is named Unified Speed Restrictions.
- TomTom positions it for next-generation ADAS and Intelligent Speed Assistance systems.
- The announcement links speed-data quality directly to safety-critical automated driving use cases.
- Even the preview reflects how map vendors are productizing regulatory road data as a standalone capability.

## Synthesis
Although the accessible text is only a preview, it conveys the core point clearly: TomTom has introduced a new service called Unified Speed Restrictions to improve the timeliness and precision of speed-limit data used in advanced driver assistance and automated driving systems. That is significant because speed data is no longer just a convenience layer for navigation. It is increasingly treated as safety-critical infrastructure.

For ISA and ADAS applications, errors in posted speed limits create more than a poor user experience. They can undermine driver trust, trigger incorrect vehicle behavior, and weaken compliance with regulations that increasingly require assistance systems to interpret road rules correctly. By presenting Unified Speed Restrictions as a dedicated service, TomTom is signaling that the market now values regulatory map attributes as a product category in their own right.

This move also reflects a broader shift in automotive mapping. Traditional navigation products focused on route calculation, traffic, and consumer usability. The new emphasis is on machine-readable road intelligence that can support semi-automated and automated driving functions. That means higher expectations for freshness, consistency, and geographic coverage, especially where regulatory or physical signage changes frequently.

The article preview suggests TomTom is aiming beyond minimum compliance. It frames the service as helping customers exceed the requirements of next-generation ISA and ADAS systems, which implies that map accuracy is becoming a competitive differentiator for vehicle platforms and suppliers. In practice, better speed data can improve comfort, reduce false interventions, and help vehicles align more closely with real road conditions.

From a mapping perspective, this matters because it shows how digital road networks are evolving from descriptive maps into operational control layers. Attributes such as speed restrictions, lane guidance, and traffic rules are increasingly expected to feed automated decision-making systems directly. That raises the value of providers that can maintain those layers at scale and with sufficient reliability.

Overall, even in preview form, the article points to a simple but important trend: the future of automotive maps depends less on visual cartography and more on precise, continuously updated rule data that vehicles can trust. Unified Speed Restrictions fits squarely into that transition.
