# GEO Business 2026 opens registration with expanded programme
**Source**: https://www.gim-international.com/content/news/geo-business-2026-opens-registration-with-expanded-programme
**Date**: April 23, 2026
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: GEO Business, conferences, geospatial industry, AI, location intelligence

## Elevator pitch
GEO Business 2026 is positioning itself as a practical showcase for how AI, digital twins, and location intelligence are moving into mainstream geospatial operations.

## Takeaways
- Registration is open for GEO Business 2026, scheduled for 3 and 4 June at ExCeL London.
- The event is expanding to more than 150 CPD-accredited sessions across eight stages with over 200 speakers.
- The programme spans AI, climate resilience, digital twins, emergency response, and broader location intelligence themes.
- Named organizations, including BT, Esri UK, DEFRA, Mastercard, NHS, Ordnance Survey, TomTom, and WGIC, signal a broad practitioner audience.
- The exhibition component remains central, with more than 100 exhibitors and free attendance aimed at converting interest into applied adoption.

## Synthesis
The announcement frames GEO Business 2026 as a growing industry convening point for geospatial professionals who want practical exposure to the latest tools, workflows, and use cases rather than a narrowly academic event. By emphasizing the scale of the programme, more than 150 accredited sessions, eight stages, and over 200 speakers, the article signals that the conference has matured into a broad marketplace for ideas and operational practice across the UK geospatial sector.

A key theme is the widening scope of the geospatial conversation. The programme is not presented as being only about surveying, GIS software, or mapping production. Instead, it spans artificial intelligence, climate resilience, digital twins, and emergency response, showing how location intelligence is increasingly embedded in cross-functional decision environments. The list of speaker organizations reinforces that point. Telecoms, payments, healthcare, national mapping, consulting, and global geospatial institutions are all represented, suggesting that geospatial work is being treated as shared infrastructure rather than a specialist niche.

The article gives special attention to sessions focused on AI, especially Will Cadell’s session on how AI is reshaping the mapping industry and Mastercard’s perspective on AI coding agents in software engineering. That positioning is notable because it treats AI not as a side topic but as one of the main forces reconfiguring geospatial practice. At the same time, the programme still values grounded craft, with examples such as a Humanitarian OpenStreetMap mapathon, an in-depth survey-focused session on the Barbican, and regional behavioural analysis led by BT. In other words, the event is balancing automation themes with field, data, and analytical expertise.

The commercial layer of the event is equally important. More than 100 exhibitors will showcase geospatial technologies, and the article presents that as a practical benefit for attendees who want to evaluate solutions, not just hear talks. This reinforces the idea that GEO Business functions as a bridge between strategic trends and procurement or implementation choices. Overall, the piece presents GEO Business 2026 as a sign of a sector that is growing more interdisciplinary, more AI-aware, and more directly tied to real operational decisions.
