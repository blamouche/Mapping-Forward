# Building clarity across the road network with AtkinsRéalis and TomTom
**Source**: https://www.tomtom.com/customers/atkinsrealis-and-tomtom/
**Date**: April 25, 2026
**Author**: Unknown
**Keywords**: TomTom, AtkinsRéalis, National Highways, journey time reliability, traffic data, road performance

## Elevator pitch
TomTom and AtkinsRéalis helped National Highways replace segment-based traffic reporting with an end-to-end journey reliability metric that is easier for the public to understand and more useful for operations.

## Takeaways
- National Highways wanted a journey reliability metric that made sense to ordinary road users rather than specialists.
- TomTom and AtkinsRéalis redesigned the metric around end-to-end journeys instead of road-section averages.
- The new framework reports the share of trips that fall within a predictable time range.
- TomTom’s Traffic Stats data was used to provide full-trip travel time coverage across the network.
- The solution has been live since April 2025 and is positioned as both publicly intelligible and operationally actionable.

## Synthesis
This customer story focuses on a common weakness in transport analytics: the gap between what agencies measure and what citizens actually understand. National Highways had been using a reliability metric based on differences between observed and profile travel times in seconds per vehicle per mile. While technically precise, that measure was poorly aligned with how drivers think. People care about whether an entire trip will be predictable, not about abstract variability on isolated segments. The article argues that this mismatch created a need for a clearer and more human-centered metric.

TomTom and AtkinsRéalis addressed that problem by shifting the framework from road sections to complete journeys. The new measure expresses reliability as the percentage of journeys that fall within an expected time range around the most common travel time for a given period. That reframing is important because it turns a specialist transport indicator into something closer to a service-quality signal. It also makes the metric more compatible with public accountability, since it can be communicated in plain terms rather than technical formulas.

TomTom’s role in the project centers on data coverage. The article says its Traffic Stats product could deliver end-to-end journey data across the network, where other providers were limited to segment-level travel times. That distinction is not trivial. If the policy goal is to understand actual trip reliability, then fragmented segment data can obscure the user experience. Full-traversal information makes it easier to build metrics that match how journeys are lived, not just how networks are modeled.

The broader takeaway is that location intelligence is becoming as much about governance and communication as about raw measurement. Better transport data is useful, but better-framed transport data can also change how agencies manage performance, justify interventions, and build trust with the public. This case positions TomTom less as a navigation brand and more as a provider of network-level decision infrastructure for public road operations.
