# Mapping the Arctic Explorations of Sir John Ross

**Date:** 29 April 2026
**Source:** Library of Congress (Worlds Revealed Blog)
**Author:** Cynthia Smith
**Keywords:** Arctic exploration, Sir John Ross, Northwest Passage, historical cartography, Library of Congress, 19th century

---

For hundreds of years, European explorers sought the Northwest Passage through the Canadian Arctic. This Library of Congress blog post features maps related to the Arctic expeditions of Sir John Ross, a British naval officer born in Scotland in 1777 who joined the Royal Navy at age nine.

Ross's first Arctic exploration in 1818 led a British Admiralty expedition on the ships Isabella and Alexander. He sailed counterclockwise around Baffin Bay before entering Lancaster Sound, but ended the expedition after mistaking a mirage for mountains blocking the route — which he named "Croker Mountains." His subordinate William Edward Parry disproved their existence the following year, leading to Ross facing ridicule.

In 1829, Ross and his nephew James Clark Ross began a second attempt aboard the Victory, funded by gin distiller Felix Booth. The Victory became trapped in ice at Felix Harbour. Ross led his final expedition in 1850 at age seventy-two.
