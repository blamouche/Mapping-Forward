# US-Indian Space Mission Maps Extreme Subsidence in Mexico City

**Date:** 29 April 2026
**Source:** NASA / Jet Propulsion Laboratory
**Author:** Sally Younger
**Keywords:** NISAR, NASA, ISRO, SAR, subsidence, Mexico City, radar, satellite, land deformation

---

One of the most powerful radar systems ever launched into space, the NISAR (NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar) satellite, has mapped ground subsidence beneath Mexico City — one of the fastest-sinking capitals in the world. The findings demonstrate the satellite's ability to track real-time changes across Earth's surface unhindered by clouds or vegetation.

Home to some 20 million people, the Mexico City area is built atop an aquifer. Extensive groundwater pumping and urban development have caused the ancient lakebed to compact for over a century. By the 1990s and 2000s, parts of the metropolitan area were sinking by around 14 inches (35 cm) per year, damaging infrastructure including the Metro transit system.

The new analysis, based on measurements between October 2025 and January 2026, shows parts of the region subsiding by more than half an inch (2+ cm) per month. Launched in July 2025 from India, NISAR is the first satellite carrying two SAR instruments at different wavelengths (L-band by NASA, S-band by ISRO), monitoring Earth's land and ice surfaces twice every 12 days with a 12-meter-wide antenna reflector.
