# Huawei će na novim telefonima umjesto Google Mapa koristiti TomTom mape
**Source**: https://bljesak.info/magazin/tehnologija/huawei-ce-na-novim-telefonima-umjesto-google-mapsa-koristiti-tomtom-mape/299119
**Date**: January 21, 2020
**Author**: Bljesak.info
**Keywords**: Huawei, TomTom, Google Maps, US sanctions, HMS, navigation, Android

## Elevator pitch
Following US sanctions that cut Huawei off from Google services, the Chinese manufacturer signed a deal with TomTom to replace Google Maps on its future smartphones.

## Takeaways
- Huawei lost access to Google Mobile Services (GMS) after US sanctions took effect in May 2019
- The company developed its own Huawei Mobile Services (HMS) and app store as Android alternatives
- TomTom maps will power navigation on future Huawei devices, with Huawei building its own navigation app on top
- The deal was confirmed by TomTom spokesperson Remco Meerstra but financial details were not disclosed
- This partnership represents a key piece of Huawei's strategy to reduce dependency on American technology

## Synthesis
Following the US government's addition of Huawei to the Entity List in May 2019, the Chinese tech giant was cut off from using American technology in its devices, including Google's Android operating system and the full suite of Google Mobile Services. This meant future Huawei phones would ship without Gmail, Google Maps, YouTube, or the Google Play Store — a significant competitive disadvantage in international markets.

In response, Huawei accelerated development of its own ecosystem: the HarmonyOS operating system, Huawei Mobile Services (HMS) to replace Google Mobile Services, and the Huawei AppGallery as an alternative app store. The mapping piece of this puzzle was solved through a partnership with Dutch navigation company TomTom, whose map data would underpin navigation on Huawei devices.

The arrangement is notable for its division of responsibilities: TomTom provides the underlying map data and technology, while Huawei builds the user-facing navigation application. This mirrors the approach of other smartphone manufacturers who license map data rather than maintaining their own global mapping infrastructure.

TomTom spokesperson Remco Meerstra confirmed the deal to Reuters but provided no financial details. The timing was significant — it demonstrated that Huawei could assemble a viable alternative to Google's ecosystem by combining European mapping technology with its own software development capabilities.

The Huawei-TomTom partnership illustrates how geopolitical tensions can reshape technology supply chains. For TomTom, it opened a new distribution channel through one of the world's largest smartphone manufacturers. For Huawei, it was an essential piece of restoring competitiveness in global markets where Google Maps had become the default navigation standard.
