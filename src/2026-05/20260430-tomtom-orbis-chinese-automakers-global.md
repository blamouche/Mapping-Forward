# TomTom：以位置智能为航图，驶向全球——推动自动驾驶系统规模化落地
**Source**: https://i.gasgoo.com/news/70456153.html
**Date**: April 30, 2026
**Author**: Gasgoo (盖世汽车) / Leo Sei (TomTom CPO keynote summary)
**Keywords**: TomTom, Orbis, autonomous driving, ADAS, Chinese automakers, global expansion, location intelligence, Overture Maps, GDPR

## Elevator pitch
TomTom CPO Leo Sei presented at the 4th China Auto Parts Export Ecosystem Conference, positioning Orbis Maps as the unified location layer for Chinese automakers expanding globally, combining open-source data with proprietary precision for ADAS and autonomous driving.

## Takeaways
- Sei argued that Chinese automakers waste resources rebuilding base map data from scratch — a unified Orbis platform eliminates this duplication
- Orbis combines OSM open-source base with TomTom's proprietary lane geometry, topology, speed limits, and traffic data
- The platform is "ADAS-first": designed for safety-critical applications with lane-level world representation from multiple sensor sources
- Three global automakers (including VW Group) have adopted Orbis for their global ADAS systems
- TomTom positions itself as a conflict-free independent provider — no hardware, no SoC, no competition with automakers

## Synthesis
Gasgoo's coverage of Leo Sei's keynote at the 4th China Auto Parts Export Ecosystem Conference reveals TomTom's increasingly focused pitch to Chinese automakers. The core argument is straightforward: Chinese car companies waste massive resources duplicating base map infrastructure that should be shared. TomTom's Orbis platform solves this by providing both a common open-source foundation (via Overture Maps, co-founded with Microsoft, Amazon, and Meta) and proprietary precision layers (lane geometry, curvature, speed limits, traffic events) on top.

The China-specific angle is critical. Sei acknowledges China's dominance — producing 40% of global EVs and leading autonomous driving innovation — but notes that expansion into Southeast Asia, the Middle East, Latin America, Europe, and North America requires a global location platform that handles local compliance automatically. TomTom's European roots give it GDPR and EU AI Act compliance "designed in from the start."

The technical argument centers on sensor-map fusion: sensors alone are like "a tourist exploring passively" while map-enhanced systems have "local cognition" — anticipating speed limit changes kilometers ahead, understanding school zone time windows, and compensating for sensor degradation in rain, fog, and glare. Three global automakers including Volkswagen Group have adopted Orbis, and Counterpoint Research has recognized TomTom's leadership position. The independent positioning — TomTom sells no hardware, competes with no automaker — is framed as a trust advantage, particularly relevant for Chinese firms navigating complex international business relationships.
