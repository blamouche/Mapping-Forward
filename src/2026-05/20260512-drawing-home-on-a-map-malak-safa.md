# Drawing Home on a Map: Malak Safa's Story of Loss and Resilience
**Source**: https://www.un.org/ar/delegate/drawing-home-map-malak-safa%E2%80%99s-story-loss-and-resilience
**Date**: May 2026 (estimated)
**Author**: United Nations (OCHA)
**Keywords**: humanitarian mapping, Lebanon, displacement, OCHA, maps as identity, Tyre, conflict

## Elevator pitch
An OCHA humanitarian officer from southern Lebanon shares how maps represent more than geography — they are identity and belonging — after being displaced for the sixth time amid renewed conflict.

## Takeaways
- Malak Safa, Associate Humanitarian Affairs Officer at OCHA, has been displaced six times from her home in southern Lebanon (between the border and Litani River)
- Her first displacement as a family was in 2023 when her youngest child was three months old; she fled to Congo
- She served as national staff in Tyre before the escalation, and notes the experience deepened her understanding of why protection standards for mothers and children matter
- Maps represent identity, memory, and belonging — not just geography — for displaced people
- The story illustrates how humanitarians are not separate from the crises they respond to

## Synthesis
This brief but powerful UN interview highlights a dimension of mapping rarely discussed in technical circles: for people displaced by conflict, maps are not navigation tools but repositories of identity and memory. Malak Safa, an Associate Humanitarian Affairs Officer with OCHA, describes southern Lebanon — "the area between the border and the Litani River" — as "not just geography to me — it is identity, memory, and belonging."

The practical and emotional toll is stark. Safa has been displaced six times. Her first displacement as a family came in 2023, when her youngest was just three months old and she was still on maternity leave. She fled to Congo with both children, turning the experience into "an adventure" for her older child while grappling with the vulnerability that humanitarian reports describe in abstract terms. "It really deepened my understanding of why global standards matter," she notes — the experience of breastfeeding and caring for young children during emergencies gave her a visceral understanding of what protection frameworks are meant to address.

Now displaced again to Batroun, she finds solace in the town's resemblance to her hometown Tyre — "the sea, the light, the feeling of home." But her six-year-old and three-year-old have "already lived through more uprooting than many adults," and the emotional toll is accumulating. The article connects the personal to the institutional: humanitarians are not separate from the crises they respond to, and maps — as Safa's title "Drawing home on a map" suggests — carry meanings that transcend coordinates and boundaries. For displaced people, a map is a document of what was lost and a promise of what might be regained.
