## Elevator pitch
The MENA GIS market is entering a high-growth phase driven by smart city megaprojects, AI-powered digital twins, cloud-based SaaS adoption, and aggressive government digital transformation across Saudi Arabia, UAE, and North Africa.

## Takeaways
- NEOM in Saudi Arabia advanced its AI-powered GIS digital twin platform for smart city planning and autonomous mobility as of March 2026.
- Esri Middle East expanded ArcGIS analytics across UAE government entities for urban planning and disaster management.
- Key M&A activity includes VertiGIS acquiring 1Spatial for ~£87M and Enverus acquiring Kayrros for satellite-driven GIS intelligence.
- Cloud-based GIS and SaaS platforms are lowering infrastructure costs and improving accessibility across MENA enterprises.
- Egypt, Morocco, and Algeria are accelerating national GIS infrastructure upgrades for land governance, climate monitoring, and resource mapping.

## Synthesis
DataM Intelligence's 2026 market report on the Middle East and North Africa GIS market paints a picture of a region in the midst of a sustained geospatial investment boom. Saudi Arabia leads the charge with its Vision 2030-aligned megaprojects, most notably NEOM, which in March 2026 advanced an AI-powered GIS digital twin platform integrating smart city planning, infrastructure monitoring, and autonomous mobility. The UAE follows closely, with Esri Middle East expanding ArcGIS-based geospatial analytics across federal and municipal government entities to strengthen urban planning and disaster management capabilities.

The report documents a notable wave of consolidation and strategic partnerships across the sector. VertiGIS completed its acquisition of 1Spatial for approximately £87 million in May 2026, strengthening integrated GIS workflow and spatial data governance capabilities for utilities and government clients. Enverus agreed to acquire Kayrros to expand its satellite-driven GIS intelligence for energy and financial markets. On the ground, Schneider Geospatial and Spatial Business Systems made complementary acquisitions to expand parcel mapping, utility design, and field service software offerings — signaling that GIS is increasingly treated as core operational infrastructure rather than a niche tool.

Technology trends underscore the shift toward software-as-a-service and AI integration. Cloud-based GIS platforms are improving accessibility and reducing infrastructure costs, while the integration of machine learning with GIS tools enables predictive analytics for urban planning, traffic management, and environmental monitoring. 3D mapping, digital twin technologies, and high-resolution satellite imagery are expanding the range of applications from oil and gas pipeline monitoring to cadastral modernization in Morocco and climate risk assessment in Egypt. The convergence of IoT, AI, and GIS is particularly visible in the Gulf's smart city initiatives, where real-time spatial analytics are becoming standard infrastructure requirements.

North Africa represents an emerging frontier, with Egypt's NARSS upgrading national GIS infrastructure for land use and climate monitoring, Morocco modernizing its cadastral systems, and Algeria expanding satellite-based GIS for natural resource mapping. The report segments the market across hardware (GNSS antennas, LiDAR, total stations), software (desktop, server, mobile, remote sensing), and services, with end-user demand spanning electricity, oil and gas, water, telecom, automotive, and agriculture. The recurring theme is that GIS is no longer a back-office tool but a foundational layer for national digital transformation strategies across the region.
