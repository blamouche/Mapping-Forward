# A 30 m Vegetation Map for the Source Regions of the Yangtze and Yellow Rivers from 2020–2025 Ground Surveys
**Source**: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-07438-2
**Date**: May 14, 2026
**Author**: Defu Zou, Xiaodong Wu, Guojie Hu et al. (Chinese Academy of Sciences)
**Keywords**: vegetation mapping, Yangtze River, Yellow River, Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, random forest classification, Landsat, alpine swamp meadow, alpine shrubland, permafrost, ecohydrology

## Elevator pitch
Chinese Academy of Sciences researchers release a 30-meter vegetation map of the Yangtze-Yellow River source regions on the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, built from 1,168 ground survey sites (2020–2025) and achieving 79.56% accuracy — explicitly improving delineation of alpine swamp meadows and shrublands missed by broader plateau-wide products.

## Takeaways
- The dataset covers the source regions of the Yangtze and Yellow Rivers (SRYYR) at 30 m resolution, filling a gap between coarse plateau-wide products and local studies.
- 1,168 georeferenced ground survey sites collected over five years (2020–2025) provide the training data, with sample representativeness verified against environmental predictor distributions.
- A random forest classifier using Landsat imagery and environmental predictors achieved 79.56% overall accuracy with Cohen's kappa of 0.74.
- The map explicitly delineates alpine swamp meadows (ASMs) and improves spatial representation of alpine shrublands (ASHs) — vegetation types overlooked by existing products.
- The dataset targets permafrost-vegetation coupling studies and ecohydrological process modeling in one of the world's most climate-sensitive regions.
- Published in Scientific Data under open access (CC BY 4.0), making it directly usable by the global research community.

## Synthesis
A team from the Chinese Academy of Sciences' Cryosphere Research Station has published a new 30-meter resolution vegetation type map for the source regions of the Yangtze and Yellow Rivers (SRYYR) in Nature's Scientific Data. The work addresses a specific cartographic gap: existing plateau-wide vegetation products, while useful at macro scales, fail to capture fine-grained vegetation patterns in the headwater regions of China's two most important rivers — an area acutely sensitive to climate change and permafrost degradation.

The methodological approach is systematic and well-documented. Researchers collected 1,168 georeferenced ground survey sites between 2020 and 2025, then trained a random forest classifier on Landsat imagery combined with environmental predictors (topography, climate, soil). They verified sample representativeness by comparing predictor distributions between survey sites and the broader study area, reporting Euclidean distances of 2.99% to 8.85% and Pearson correlations of 0.59 to 0.97. The final map achieved 79.56% overall accuracy with a Cohen's kappa of 0.74 — respectable for vegetation classification in complex high-altitude terrain.

Two vegetation types receive particular attention: alpine swamp meadows (ASMs) and alpine shrublands (ASHs). These are ecologically significant but poorly mapped in existing products. ASMs play a crucial role in water regulation and carbon storage on the plateau; ASHs represent important habitat and permafrost-protection zones. By explicitly delineating these types, the dataset provides an updated baseline for permafrost-vegetation coupling studies — a research priority as the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau experiences accelerated warming.

The dataset enters the public domain under CC BY 4.0, meaning it can be freely used, adapted, and redistributed by the global scientific community. For the broader mapping community, this work exemplifies the value of systematic ground truth campaigns in an era dominated by satellite-derived products. Five years of field surveys produced a map more accurate than what remote sensing alone could achieve — and revealed vegetation patterns invisible to coarser products. The tradeoff between spatial coverage and classification detail is real, and this dataset chooses detail for a region where it matters most.
