# 10 Google Maps settings I always change to greatly improve its usability - and you should, too
**Source**: https://www.zdnet.com/article/google-maps-settings-i-immediately-change-on-new-phone/
**Date**: May 14, 2026
**Author**: Elyse Betters Picaro
**Keywords**: Google Maps, privacy, settings, location history, navigation, usability

## Elevator pitch
A senior tech journalist shares the ten Google Maps settings she changes on every new phone to tighten privacy, disable notifications and ads, and enable genuinely useful features like offline maps and privacy controls.

## Takeaways
- Disable location history and Timeline to prevent Google from keeping a running diary of every place visited.
- Turn off notifications and personalized ads to reduce intrusiveness without losing navigation quality.
- Enable offline maps and Wi-Fi-only mode before traveling to avoid massive roaming data charges.
- Use privacy settings like incognito mode for sensitive searches and auto-delete for location data.
- Configure navigation preferences (avoid tolls/highways, satellite view toggle) to match personal driving style.

## Synthesis
Elyse Betters Picaro, a senior contributing editor at ZDNET, outlines a personal checklist of settings she applies to Google Maps on every new device. Her approach balances two priorities: reducing Google's data collection footprint and activating features that genuinely improve the day-to-day navigation experience.

The most impactful privacy step is disabling location history entirely. Google Maps Timeline otherwise records every store, doctor's office, and restaurant visited, building a detailed personal travel log. Betters Picaro argues this is unnecessary for the core navigation experience and disables it immediately. She also recommends turning off location-based notifications and personalized ads, which add noise without utility.

On the usability side, she highlights several underused features. Offline maps, downloaded over Wi-Fi before travel, eliminate the risk of massive roaming charges — she cites a case where a US traveler received a $10,000 bill for 4.25 GB used in Europe. The Wi-Fi-only mode goes further by cutting all cellular data while preserving GPS functionality. She also recommends configuring navigation preferences such as avoiding tolls and highways, toggling satellite view, and enabling auto-delete for location history data.

The article covers additional quality-of-life improvements: enabling incognito mode for private searches, customizing the explore tab to match personal preferences, and adjusting voice guidance volume. These small tweaks collectively transform Maps from a data-hungry default into a more private, more efficient navigation tool without sacrificing any of the core functionality that makes it indispensable.
