# BH tem 27º pior trânsito do mundo e supera SP, diz TomTom
**Source**: https://www.otempo.com.br/autotempo/2026/5/15/bh-tem-27-pior-transito-do-mundo-e-supera-sp-diz-tomtom
**Date**: May 15, 2026
**Author**: Igor Veiga
**Keywords**: TomTom Traffic Index, Belo Horizonte, congestion, traffic, Brazil, urban mobility

## Elevator pitch
Belo Horizonte now ranks 27th in the world for traffic congestion according to the TomTom Traffic Index 2025, surpassing São Paulo with a 58.6% congestion rate and costing drivers 130 hours per year stuck in peak-hour traffic.

## Takeaways
- Belo Horizonte ranked 27th among nearly 160 cities worldwide with a 58.6% average congestion level in 2025.
- The city edged past São Paulo (58.5%) in the national ranking, now sitting behind only Recife (64.7%) and Porto Alegre (59.6%) among Brazilian capitals.
- Drivers in BH lose an average of 130 hours per year in peak-hour congestion, equivalent to more than five full days.
- Average speed across the city is just 21.3 km/h, with drivers covering only 5.3 km in 15 minutes.
- Worst congestion peaks on Tuesday and Wednesday mornings (8 AM, 97%) and Wednesday evenings (6 PM, 103%), while weekends remain significantly lighter.

## Synthesis
Igor Veiga reports for O Tempo on the TomTom Traffic Index 2025 results for Belo Horizonte, revealing that the capital of Minas Gerais has climbed to the 27th position globally among nearly 160 cities with over 800,000 inhabitants. The city recorded a 58.6% average congestion rate, narrowly overtaking São Paulo's 58.5% — a difference of just 0.1 percentage points but symbolically significant for residents.

Within the Brazilian ranking, Belo Horizonte trails only Recife (64.7%) and Porto Alegre (59.6%), placing it third nationally. The data shows average speeds of 21.3 km/h citywide, with the typical 15-minute journey covering just 5.3 km. During rush hour, the situation worsens: drivers in BH lose 130 hours annually to peak-hour congestion, matching Recife and exceeding Porto Alegre's 125 hours.

The TomTom data, derived from onboard navigation systems, shows congestion patterns follow predictable rhythms. The worst periods cluster around 7-9 AM and 5-7 PM on weekdays. Tuesday and Wednesday are the most severe, with Wednesday evenings reaching a peak of 103% congestion — meaning a trip takes more than twice as long as in free-flow conditions. Thursday and Friday are also heavy but slightly less intense. Weekends bring considerable relief, with Saturday and Sunday showing lower congestion throughout the day.

The article contextualizes these numbers beyond raw statistics. The 0.1-point margin over São Paulo, while small, reflects a structural issue: Belo Horizonte's road network is operating with very little spare capacity, approaching the saturation levels of a city with triple its metropolitan population. The practical implication — 130 hours lost per driver per year — translates into tangible economic and quality-of-life costs for the city's residents.
