# Court denies Virginia's request to reinstate congressional map that would benefit Democrats
**Source**: https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/05/court-denies-virginias-request-to-reinstate-congressional-map-that-would-benefit-democrats/
**Date**: May 2026
**Author**: SCOTUSblog
**Keywords**: redistricting, Supreme Court, Virginia, congressional map, Democrats

## Elevator pitch
The U.S. Supreme Court denied Virginia's emergency request to reinstate a voter-approved congressional map that would have favored Democrats in the upcoming elections.

## Takeaways
- The Supreme Court rejected Virginia's application to reinstate a Democratic-drawn congressional map
- The map had been approved by Virginia voters in a referendum before being voided by the Virginia Supreme Court
- The decision effectively leaves the existing congressional districts in place
- This ruling is part of a broader pattern of Supreme Court emergency redistricting decisions this term
- The outcome has significant implications for the balance of power in the U.S. House of Representatives

## Synthesis
The U.S. Supreme Court declined to grant Virginia's emergency application seeking to reinstate a congressional redistricting map that would have conferred a distinct electoral advantage to Democratic candidates. The map in question had been drafted by Democratic lawmakers and ratified by Virginia voters through a referendum process, but was subsequently struck down by the Virginia Supreme Court on state constitutional procedural grounds.

This denial represents the latest in a sequence of high-stakes redistricting rulings from the Supreme Court this term. The Court has repeatedly been called upon to adjudicate emergency requests involving electoral maps, with outcomes that have alternately favored Republican and Democratic interests depending on the specific circumstances of each case. The pattern reveals a Court that is handling redistricting disputes on a case-by-case basis rather than articulating a clear federal doctrine.

For Virginia, the practical effect is that the state will proceed with its next congressional elections using the existing district boundaries — maps that voters had explicitly rejected at the ballot box. The decision underscores the profound role courts play in shaping American electoral geography and raises ongoing questions about the tension between direct democracy through referendums and constitutional procedural requirements.
