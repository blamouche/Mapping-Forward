# Morador de BH perde quase 1 semana por ano no trânsito
**Source**: https://rede98.com.br/noticias/bh-regiao/horario-de-pico-faz-belo-horizontino-perder-quase-1-semana-por-ano-no-transito/
**Date**: May 15, 2026
**Author**: Larissa Reis
**Keywords**: TomTom Traffic Index, Belo Horizonte, congestion, peak hours, mobility, Brazil

## Elevator pitch
Residents of Belo Horizonte lose nearly one full week per year to traffic jams, with the TomTom Traffic Index 2025 ranking the city as Brazil's 3rd worst and the 27th worst globally with a 58.6% congestion rate.

## Takeaways
- BH residents lose an average of 130 hours per year in peak-hour traffic, equivalent to roughly 5.4 full days.
- The city ranks 3rd in Brazil behind Recife and Porto Alegre, and 27th worldwide among nearly 160 large cities.
- Average speed is 21.3 km/h, covering just 5.3 km in a 15-minute interval.
- The TomTom Traffic Index evaluates cities based on average travel times and congestion levels using data collected throughout the year.
- The study provides freely available mobility data aimed at helping cities understand and address congestion patterns.

## Synthesis
Larissa Reis of Rede 98 FM reports on the TomTom Traffic Index 2025 findings for Belo Horizonte, emphasizing the human cost of congestion: 130 hours lost per year in peak-hour traffic, equivalent to nearly an entire week spent immobile in gridlock. The data places Belo Horizonte as the third most congested city in Brazil and 27th globally.

The article highlights key metrics from the TomTom study: a 58.6% average congestion level, an average speed of 21.3 km/h, and a 15-minute travel distance of only 5.3 km. Within Brazil, Recife leads the ranking, followed by Porto Alegre, with Belo Horizonte edging out São Paulo for third place.

Reis notes that the TomTom Traffic Index methodology evaluates cities based on average travel time and congestion levels using data aggregated across the full year. The study is designed to allow cross-regional comparison of urban mobility performance and makes its data freely available, positioning it as a tool for both public awareness and policy planning. The article frames the findings as a call for attention to Belo Horizonte's mobility infrastructure challenges, noting that what might seem like a statistical ranking carries a tangible, daily cost for every resident who commutes by car.
