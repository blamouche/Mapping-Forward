# 10 000 € de facture mobile pour 4 Go : le piège de Google Maps en voyage
**Source**: https://www.lesnumeriques.com/mobilite-c17/10-000-de-facture-mobile-pour-4-go-le-piege-de-google-maps-en-voyage-n255867.html
**Date**: May 15, 2026
**Author**: Alexandre Nardo
**Keywords**: Google Maps, roaming, data charges, offline maps, travel, mobile data

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps can silently consume hundreds of megabytes in the background while traveling abroad, leading to bills as high as €10,000 — but five minutes of offline preparation before departure eliminates the risk entirely.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps consumes 3-5 MB/hour in standard view and 15-20 MB/hour in satellite view, staying active even when not visibly navigating.
- Outside the EU roaming zone, operators charge up to €9/MB, meaning two hours of satellite navigation can cost several hundred euros.
- The solution is straightforward: download offline maps over Wi-Fi, activate Wi-Fi-only mode, and the GPS chip continues working passively via satellite without any cellular data.
- Airplane mode with offline maps works fully — GPS reception is passive and doesn't require any cellular network.
- For long trips outside the EU, an eSIM with a local data plan (Saily, Airalo, Holafly) costs a few euros and provides a safer connectivity option.

## Synthesis
Alexandre Nardo of Les Numériques details a widely misunderstood risk for travelers: Google Maps continues consuming mobile data in the background even when navigation appears to be working normally. The article opens with a striking example — a US customer received a €10,000 bill for only 4.25 GB used during a European trip, triggered by routine Maps usage, photo sharing, and email checking.

The technical reality is that Maps never truly goes idle. To display real-time traffic, recalculate routes dynamically, and load map tiles when zooming or panning, the cellular modem remains active continuously. In the European Economic Area, roaming is covered under the "roam like at home" principle (extended to Ukraine and Moldova as of January 2026). Outside this zone, rates vary dramatically: Free charges €3/MB in Morocco, €9/MB in Japan, and €6.50/MB in Thailand.

The article prescribes a three-step preparation routine. First, download the destination area as an offline map over Wi-Fi (100-500 MB for a large city, up to 2 GB for a region). Second, enable automatic offline map updates set to Wi-Fi only. Third, and most critically, activate Wi-Fi-only mode in Maps settings, which blocks all cellular data requests while preserving full GPS navigation on cached maps. An additional tip is switching to airplane mode, which keeps the passive GPS receiver operational via direct satellite communication without any cellular tower contact.

For extended trips, Nardo recommends purchasing a local eSIM (Saily, Airalo, Holafly, Ubigi) offering multiple gigabytes for a few euros. He also suggests disabling background data refresh on iOS or restricting Android background data, blocking automatic app updates over mobile data, and pre-saving important locations (hotels, hospitals, meeting points) in a Google Maps list so they appear as markers on offline maps. The core message: five minutes of preparation prevents a minimum €60 surprise — which is the Arcep-mandated automatic ceiling in France, triggered only after you've already spent that amount.
