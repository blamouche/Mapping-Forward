# Supreme Court rejects bid to restore Virginia congressional map favoring Democrats
**Source**: https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/supreme-court-rejects-bid-to-restore-virginia-congressional-map-favoring-democrats
**Date**: May 15, 2026
**Author**: Mark Sherman, Associated Press
**Keywords**: redistricting, Supreme Court, Virginia, congressional map, Voting Rights Act, gerrymandering

## Elevator pitch
The U.S. Supreme Court rejected Virginia Democrats' bid to reinstate a voter-approved congressional map that would have flipped four House seats, marking another blow in the mid-decade redistricting battle.

## Takeaways
- The Supreme Court refused to restore a Virginia map that voters had approved, which would have given Democrats four additional House seats
- The Virginia Supreme Court voided the map on procedural grounds, ruling the Democratic legislature improperly put the amendment on the ballot
- This rejection comes as the Court has recently sided with Republicans in Alabama and Louisiana redistricting cases
- The mid-decade redistricting war was kicked off by Trump urging Republican states to redraw lines, and supercharged by a Supreme Court ruling weakening the Voting Rights Act
- Virginia will hold 2026 elections under the old 2021 district lines; the window for a court-ordered change has now closed

## Synthesis
The U.S. Supreme Court declined to intervene in Virginia's redistricting dispute, rejecting an emergency application from Democratic lawmakers and the state attorney general to restore a congressional map approved by voters in an April referendum. The map would have given Democrats a path to pick up four seats in the closely divided House of Representatives, serving as a counterweight to Republican redistricting gains in Texas, Missouri, North Carolina, Ohio, and Florida.

The Virginia Supreme Court had voided both the referendum and the map in a 4-3 ruling, finding that the Democratic-controlled legislature violated the state constitution by initiating the amendment process after early voting had already begun for the 2025 general election. Democrats argued this misread federal law, but the U.S. Supreme Court declined the appeal without explanation — consistent with its general reluctance to second-guess state courts on state constitutional matters.

The rejection is the latest development in an unprecedented mid-decade redistricting competition. President Trump's call for Republican-controlled states to redraw their congressional lines, combined with the Supreme Court's April ruling severely weakening the Voting Rights Act, has unleashed a wave of map redrawing across the country. In recent days alone, the justices sided with Republicans in Alabama and Louisiana seeking to produce more GOP-leaning districts.

For Virginia, the practical deadline has already passed. The state elections commissioner had indicated a court order was needed by Tuesday, May 12 to set district lines for the August 4 primary elections. Governor Abigail Spanberger's office confirmed the state will proceed under the 2021 district boundaries. The broader implication is that the Supreme Court's approach to redistricting — intervening in some states but not others, based on narrow procedural distinctions rather than consistent doctrine — is reshaping American electoral geography in ways that increasingly appear to favor one party.
