# TomTom Traffic Index 2025年度版：世界の交通トレンドと通勤行動を分析
**Source**: https://car.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/2109242.html
**Date**: May 16, 2026
**Author**: 佐久間 秀 (Car Watch)
**Keywords**: TomTom, Traffic Index, 交通渋滞, 都市交通, モビリティデータ, Area Analytics

## Elevator pitch
TomTom held a press briefing in Japan for the 15th edition of its Traffic Index, revealing that Tokyo congestion rose 7.3% in 2025 as return-to-office and tourism increase, while introducing a new Area Analytics tool for deeper traffic pattern exploration.

## Takeaways
- TomTom Traffic Index 2025 edition covers 500+ cities globally using 3.65 trillion km of GNSS driving data
- Tokyo saw a 7.3% increase in average congestion level (44.1%) driven by return-to-office trends and inbound tourism recovery
- Kumamoto ranked as Japan's most congested city (56.7%) largely due to TSMC's new semiconductor plant creating localized traffic concentration
- London congestion rose 1% while New York saw a 1% reduction, partly attributed to congestion pricing and cycling infrastructure
- New Area Analytics tool launched, allowing users to create custom traffic analysis reports with GeoJSON/CSV export

## Synthesis
TomTom held a press briefing in Tokyo on May 15, 2026, to present the findings of the 2025 edition of its TomTom Traffic Index — the 15th annual update of the global traffic analysis since its 2011 launch. The Index leverages 3.65 trillion kilometers of driving data collected from vehicles and smartphones via GNSS, covering over 500 cities worldwide. Three TomTom Japan executives — Hideaki Tanaka (Country Sales Head), Noriaki Ishibashi (Senior Account Manager), and Atsushi Nagao (Project Manager) — presented the methodology, key findings, and new tools.

The data reveals nuanced urban mobility patterns. London's congestion level increased 1% year-over-year, with its worst day (September 12) reaching 138% congestion — meaning a 10-minute drive took 23.8 minutes. The city's historical lack of highway infrastructure creates cumulative delays. New York, by contrast, saw travel times decrease 1%, with congestion pricing and cycling infrastructure improvements contributing to a 3.3% reduction in the first half of 2025 — though the improvement attracted more traffic later in the year, illustrating the rebound effect challenge.

Tokyo's data tells a story of post-pandemic normalization. Average congestion rose 7.3% to 44.1%, with every month in 2025 showing higher congestion than 2024. The return of office commuting and a surge in inbound tourism (boosted by the weak yen) are driving the increase, though levels remain below pre-pandemic peaks. Tokyo's worst congestion day was August 8 (Friday before Obon holidays) with 105% congestion at 5 PM, meaning 15 minutes of driving covered only 3.6 km.

Japan's domestic analysis revealed Kumamoto as the country's most congested city at 56.7% average congestion, with rush-hour commuters losing 154 hours annually — ranking 13th in all of Asia. The primary cause is the 2024 activation of TSMC's JASM semiconductor plant, which created intense localized traffic with limited public transit alternatives. Kyoto ranked 4th nationally but featured the lowest average speed (17.5 km/h) among Japanese cities and ranked 8th slowest globally, attributed to its grid-pattern streets, high intersection density, tourist buses, and constrained road widths.

TomTom also introduced Area Analytics, a new traffic analysis tool within its TomTom Move platform. The GUI-based tool allows users to define custom areas, date ranges, road classes, and time periods to generate congestion reports, visualized as color-coded maps with comparative graphs. Reports can be exported as GeoJSON or CSV for integration with other analysis software. The company demonstrated the tool using Golden Week congestion data for Hakone, showing peak congestion on May 5 at 6 PM with conditions clearing by May 6.

Beyond traffic analysis, TomTom highlighted disaster response applications: during heavy rain events, probe data detecting zero-traffic underpasses and tunnels identifies flooded roads; after the 2024 Noto Peninsula earthquake, the data helped analyze traffic disruptions and plan relief supply routes. The Index and its tools are publicly available on TomTom's website for the most recent two years, with historical data available commercially.
