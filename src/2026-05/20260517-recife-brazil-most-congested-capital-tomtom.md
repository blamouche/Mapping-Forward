# Recife Is Brazil's Most Congested Capital City, TomTom Study Finds
**Source**: https://autoranking.com.br/recife-e-a-capital-com-transito-mais-congestionamento-do-brasil-aponta-estudo/
**Date**: May 17, 2026
**Author**: Mariane Magno
**Keywords**: Recife, Brazil, TomTom Traffic Index, congestion, urban mobility, Sao Paulo, Belo Horizonte, Porto Alegre

## Elevator pitch
Recife tops Brazil's capital cities for traffic congestion with a 64.7% average index, where a 10 km trip takes over 26 minutes and drivers lose 130 hours per year in peak-hour traffic — surpassing even São Paulo.

## Takeaways
- Recife leads Brazilian capitals with 64.7% average congestion index, up 3.8 points from 2024, with 130 hours lost per year in peak traffic.
- A 10 km trip in Recife averages 26 minutes 26 seconds; during evening peak it stretches to nearly 35 minutes, with speeds dropping to 17.2 km/h.
- Porto Alegre ranks second (59.6%), followed by Belo Horizonte (58.6%) and São Paulo (58.5%).
- Despite a lower congestion percentage, São Paulo tops total lost time at 132 hours/year; Curitiba ranks highest nationally at 135 hours/year.
- Rio de Janeiro rounds out the top 5 at 57.8% congestion, 92 hours lost.
- Brazil ranks 4th in South America for average congestion at 25%, behind Colombia (48.1%), Peru (36%), and Chile (29%).
- The study draws from TomTom's global dataset covering 3.65 trillion km of driving data.

## Synthesis
The TomTom Traffic Index 2025 has delivered a stark finding for Brazilian urban mobility: Recife now claims the title of most congested capital city in the country. Drawing from over 3.65 trillion kilometers of global driving data, the Dutch company's analysis paints a picture of a city where drivers lose an average of 130 hours — over five full days — stuck in peak-hour traffic annually.

Recife's 64.7% congestion index represents a 3.8 percentage point increase from 2024, pushing it ahead of traditional congestion heavyweights. The time penalty is concrete: a 10-kilometer trip averages 26 minutes and 26 seconds under normal conditions, but balloons to nearly 35 minutes during evening peak. Speeds plummet accordingly — vehicles average just 18 km/h during busy periods and 17.2 km/h during the evening rush.

The ranking reshuffles established assumptions about Brazilian traffic. Porto Alegre takes second at 59.6%, Belo Horizonte third at 58.6%, and São Paulo — despite its reputation — comes in fourth at 58.5%. However, São Paulo claims the highest total time lost at 132 hours per year due to its vastly larger road network, while Curitiba tops the national ranking for hours lost at 135. Rio de Janeiro rounds out the five most affected capitals at 57.8%, albeit with significantly fewer hours lost at 92.

On a South American scale, Brazil's 25% average congestion places it fourth behind Colombia (48.1%), Peru (36%), and Chile (29%). The disparity between Recife's 64.7% and Brazil's national average of 25% underscores just how concentrated the country's congestion problem is in specific metropolitan areas — and how much worse it's becoming year over year.
