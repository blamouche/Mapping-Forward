## Synthesis
TomTom held a press briefing on May 15, 2026 to present regional analyses from its 15th annual Traffic Index, covering 500 cities worldwide and drawing on 3.65 trillion kilometers of driving data sourced from connected vehicles, fleet operators, and smartphone location signals.

The briefing compared three global megacities. London, constrained by historic street layouts and lacking an urban expressway network, saw its congestion level tick up 1 point — with the worst day (September 10) reaching 138% congestion at 5 PM, meaning a 15-minute trip covered only 2.6 km. New York showed the opposite trend: average travel time improved 1%, attributed to Manhattan's congestion pricing scheme and expanded bike lanes, demonstrating that policy interventions can measurably shift urban traffic patterns.

Tokyo presented a more concerning picture for Japan. Average congestion rose 7.3% to 44.1%, with the worst day (August 8, just before Obon) hitting 105% at 5 PM — 3.6 km in 15 minutes, or under 15 km/h. The drivers are structural: the post-pandemic return to office work and surging inbound tourism are both long-term trends that suggest further increases ahead.

Among Japan's 12 major cities, Kumamoto surprisingly topped the congestion rankings at 56.7% with 154 hours lost per year — ranking 13th across all of Asia. The catalyst is unmistakable: TSMC's JASM semiconductor fabrication plant has drawn an influx of logistics, construction, and commuter traffic that the region's road infrastructure and public transit were not designed to handle. Kyoto, at 17.5 km/h average speed and 8th slowest globally, illustrates a different pattern — a historic grid-based city center, dense with traffic signals, inadequate parking, and tour bus volumes that strain streets built for a different era.

The seasonal analysis revealed three congestion patterns in Japan: major holidays (Obon, New Year), leisure seasons (spring/autumn), and business-driven spikes (large retail or factory openings). The least congested days were January 1 and New Year's Eve, when commercial traffic nearly disappears.

Beyond the index itself, TomTom highlighted its Area Analytics tool, which allows users — from city planners to corporate site selectors — to query traffic conditions by geography, date, and time slot, from individual road segments to metropolitan areas. TomTom also emphasized the disaster-response value of its real-time traffic data, capable of identifying "zero traffic" zones where roads become impassable, enabling targeted rerouting and relief coordination.
