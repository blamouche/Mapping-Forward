## Elevator pitch
TomTom's Chief Product Officer Léo Sei explains on BFM Business how AI is now at the core of the company's next-generation mapping platform, transforming TomTom from a navigation company into a location intelligence provider.

## Takeaways
- TomTom is repositioning around AI-powered maps that go beyond turn-by-turn navigation.
- The company's mapping platform now serves automotive, enterprise, and public sector use cases including ADAS and autonomous driving.
- Sei emphasises that map freshness and real-time updates are critical differentiators enabled by AI processing of sensor data.
- TomTom's strategy involves building a unified map platform that serves multiple industries rather than siloed navigation products.
- The interview highlights TomTom's shift from consumer GPS devices to B2B location intelligence.

## Synthesis
In this BFM Business interview, TomTom CPO Léo Sei outlines the Dutch mapping company's strategic transformation from consumer navigation to AI-driven location intelligence. The discussion centres on how artificial intelligence has become the engine of TomTom's new map-making approach — processing vast streams of sensor data from vehicles, mobile devices, and infrastructure to create maps that are continuously updated rather than periodically refreshed.

Sei frames TomTom's competitive advantage around map quality and freshness. In an era where autonomous driving and advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS) require centimetre-level precision and real-time awareness of road conditions, speed limits, and hazards, traditional map-making cycles are obsolete. AI enables TomTom to ingest, validate, and integrate changes at scale, turning the map into a living data layer.

The business shift is equally significant. TomTom is moving decisively toward B2B, positioning its map platform as infrastructure for automotive OEMs, logistics companies, smart cities, and public authorities. Rather than competing head-to-head with Google Maps and Waze on consumer navigation, TomTom is betting on being the underlying location intelligence layer that powers multiple industries. This is the "location intelligence" pivot — from selling maps to selling location understanding as a service.
