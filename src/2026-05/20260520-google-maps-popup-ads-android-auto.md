# Google Maps affiche des publicités pop-up pendant la navigation Android Auto, les conducteurs s'agacent
**Source**: https://speedme.ru/fr/posts/id53681-google-maps-affiche-des-publicites-pop-up-pendant-la-navigation-android-auto-les-conducteurs-s-agacent
**Date**: May 20, 2026
**Author**: Dmitry Novikov
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Android Auto, pop-up ads, navigation, driver safety, advertising, Dunkin Donuts

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps users on Android Auto report intrusive pop-up ads for sponsored locations appearing mid-navigation and occupying nearly half the screen, raising significant driver distraction and safety concerns.

## Takeaways
- Android Auto users report Google Maps displaying pop-up ads for sponsored locations like Dunkin' Donuts during active navigation.
- The ads occupy nearly half the screen and propose adding the sponsored stop to the current route.
- The format is dangerous: drivers must determine whether the overlay is a navigation instruction or an advertisement while driving.
- Reports suggest the behaviour is not limited to beta versions of Android Auto.
- While Google Maps has long featured promoted locations in search results, pop-up ads overlaying active navigation represent a new level of intrusion.
- Users argue a navigation app should function as a safety instrument, not a recommendation feed.

## Synthesis
This SpeedMe report documents growing user frustration with a new advertising format in Google Maps on Android Auto. According to multiple user reports, the navigation app is now displaying pop-up ads for sponsored locations — such as Dunkin' Donuts — during active navigation, with the ad overlay occupying roughly half the screen and proposing to reroute to the advertised business.

The safety implications are stark. Drivers must visually parse whether an on-screen overlay is a legitimate navigation update (a turn instruction, a hazard warning, a reroute) or a paid advertisement, all while operating a vehicle. One user described being directed away from their airport route toward a coffee shop without immediately realising it — exactly the kind of attention-splitting that traffic safety guidelines explicitly warn against.

The report is careful to note that screenshots are rare because, as users point out, they were driving. While Google has long incorporated promoted locations into Maps search results, the shift to pop-up ads overlaying active navigation marks a qualitative escalation in the platform's monetisation strategy. The core tension is between Google's advertising business model and the safety-critical function of a navigation app — a tension that regulators, particularly in Europe where driver distraction rules are tightening, may find hard to ignore.
