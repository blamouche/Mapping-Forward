# Pourquoi Waze et Google Maps ne vous donnent plus les trajets les plus rapides
**Source**: https://www.presse-citron.net/cest-inutile-pourquoi-waze-et-google-maps-ne-vous-donnent-plus-les-trajets-les-plus-rapides/
**Date**: May 20, 2026
**Author**: Jean-Yves Alric
**Keywords**: Waze, Google Maps, navigation, environmental routing, CO2 emissions, French decree, traffic policy

## Elevator pitch
French government decree n°2022-1199 now forces Waze and Google Maps to prioritise the least polluting routes over the fastest ones, prompting a shift from speed-optimised to emissions-optimised navigation that is drawing mixed reactions from users.

## Takeaways
- French decree n°2022-1199 requires navigation apps to highlight routes with lowest greenhouse gas emissions, not shortest travel times.
- Routes on roads with ≥110 km/h speed limits must offer an alternative with a 20 km/h speed reduction.
- Users can still manually select faster routes in app settings but eco-routes are now the default.
- Île-de-France region had already pushed Google Maps toward alternative routing to reduce congestion during the Paris 2024 Olympics.
- The Île-de-France Mobilités app was specifically designed to optimise collective traffic flow, not individual journey times.

## Synthesis
Jean-Yves Alric reports on a regulatory shift reshaping how navigation apps operate in France. Under decree n°2022-1199, services like Waze and Google Maps are now required to prioritise routes with the lowest environmental impact rather than the shortest travel times. The text explicitly mandates that "digital services aimed at facilitating multimodal travel shall highlight route proposals with the lowest impact in terms of greenhouse gas emissions."

The practical consequence is that users increasingly see routes that are slower but greener. On highways where speed limits reach or exceed 110 km/h, the apps must also propose an alternative involving a 20 km/h speed reduction — a more fuel-efficient pace that extends journey time. The change has not been universally welcomed, with some users calling it "useless" in online comments. However, users retain the ability to switch back to fastest-route mode in settings.

The article traces the broader policy context: Île-de-France authorities had already pressured Google Maps ahead of the Paris 2024 Olympics to favour alternative routing over direct paths, prioritising traffic flow across the network rather than individual journey optimisation. Laurent Probst, director general of Île-de-France Mobilités, framed this as a public safety issue, going so far as to warn that the State could demand app shutdowns if platforms refused to comply.

This marks a significant inflection point where sovereign regulatory power is actively reshaping the routing logic of global navigation platforms — moving them from user-centric speed optimisation toward collective environmental and traffic management goals. It raises questions about whether other jurisdictions will follow France's lead, fundamentally altering how hundreds of millions of drivers experience navigation.
