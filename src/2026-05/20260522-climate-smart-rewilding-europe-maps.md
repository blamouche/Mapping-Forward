# New maps show where European landscapes can advance climate and biodiversity goals together
**Source**: https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1129319
**Date**: May 22, 2026
**Author**: German Centre for Integrative Biodiversity Research (iDiv)
**Keywords**: Climate-smart rewilding, biodiversity, climate mitigation, Europe, spatial mapping, ecosystem restoration

## Elevator pitch
A new climate-smart rewilding framework maps Europe's landscapes to identify where ecosystem restoration can simultaneously advance climate mitigation, climate adaptation, and biodiversity goals with low socio-economic risk.

## Takeaways
- Eastern and southern Europe show highest overall suitability for climate-smart rewilding
- Northern Europe stands out for climate adaptation potential, eastern Europe for mitigation
- Western Europe is more constrained due to landscape fragmentation
- The framework addresses trade-offs between carbon-first strategies (e.g., monoculture forests) and biodiversity
- All data, code, and maps are publicly available on Zenodo and the WildE Knowledge Hub

## Synthesis
Researchers from the German Centre for Integrative Biodiversity Research (iDiv), Martin Luther University Halle-Wittenberg, and the EU Horizon project WildE have published a spatial framework in One Earth that maps Europe's potential for "climate-smart rewilding" — an approach that goes beyond traditional rewilding by explicitly incorporating climate mitigation, climate adaptation, and socio-economic benefits.

Rather than identifying a single optimal region, the analysis reveals regional complementarity. Eastern and southern Europe show the highest overall suitability scores. Northern regions — particularly the Baltic States, Finland, and parts of Sweden — stand out for climate adaptation, with connectivity hotspots where restoring ecological corridors would allow wildlife to migrate in response to climate change. Eastern Europe offers strong carbon storage potential through vegetation recovery on abandoned farmland, though this can increase wildfire risk. The framework suggests managing this trade-off through natural grazing by reintroduced herbivores or controlled livestock.

The researchers explicitly address the tension between carbon-first strategies and biodiversity. Fast-growing monoculture plantations may sequester carbon quickly but support far fewer species than diverse, naturally regenerating forests. The climate-smart rewilding framework harmonizes these objectives by identifying where they naturally reinforce each other and where targeted interventions are needed.

Senior author Prof. Henrique Pereira emphasized that the framework helps practitioners and decision-makers see which interventions have the most impact when implemented in the right regions. The approach is context-dependent, requiring adjustment to local conditions and appropriate spatial scales. All data and code are publicly available, enabling practitioners, researchers, and policymakers to adapt the analyses to their own planning contexts.
