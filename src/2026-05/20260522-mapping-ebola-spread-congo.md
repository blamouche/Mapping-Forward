# Mapping Ebola's Spread in a Vulnerable Region
**Source**: https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/05/22/world/africa/ebola-outbreak-congo-map.html
**Date**: May 22, 2026
**Author**: Samuel Granados, Adina Renner, Amy Schoenfeld Walker
**Keywords**: Ebola, Bundibugyo, Congo, outbreak mapping, Ituri, WHO, disease surveillance, cartography

## Elevator pitch
The New York Times maps the spread of a rare Bundibugyo Ebola strain across eastern Congo's conflict-ridden Ituri Province, where population displacement, limited testing capacity, and the near-absence of US support have complicated containment.

## Takeaways
- The rare Bundibugyo Ebola strain has only appeared in two prior outbreaks (Uganda 2007, Congo 2012), with no approved vaccines
- Outbreak likely originated in Mongwalu, a remote gold-mining area almost inaccessible during rainy season
- Cases have spread to major transit hubs including Bunia and across borders to Uganda (Kampala)
- Testing delays occurred because local equipment only tested for the common Zaire strain
- This outbreak has already surpassed the combined toll of the two prior Bundibugyo episodes

## Synthesis
The New York Times employs detailed interactive cartography to track the spread of a rare Bundibugyo-strain Ebola outbreak centered in Ituri Province, eastern Democratic Republic of Congo. The mapping reveals how geography, conflict, and infrastructure deficits have shaped the epidemic's trajectory.

The outbreak likely began in Mongwalu, a remote gold-mining town that becomes nearly inaccessible during the rainy season. Years of militia conflict have displaced over a million people in the region and stripped health infrastructure, allowing the virus to circulate undetected for weeks. When people fell ill, many traveled to urban centers like Bunia — a major transit point for displaced populations and migrant mine laborers — accelerating spread to densely populated areas.

The maps highlight cross-border transmission risks, with Ituri bordering South Sudan and Uganda. Uganda has reported two cases in Kampala, including one death. The regional population density makes the area one of Africa's most challenging environments for disease containment.

A critical failure in the response was testing capacity. Local equipment in Ituri, over 1,000 miles from the capital Kinshasa, only tests for the Zaire strain — the most common Ebola variant. Early samples returned negative, delaying recognition of the rare Bundibugyo species. It was only when samples reached Kinshasa that the true pathogen was identified.

The Times contextualizes the current outbreak within Ebola's historical geography: the Bundibugyo strain has caused only two prior outbreaks (Uganda 2007, Congo 2012), and this episode has already exceeded their combined toll. The US, historically a major funder of disease surveillance and emergency response in the region, is notably absent. The WHO assesses high regional spread risk but does not currently classify this as a global threat.
