# Le ministère du Numérique entend exiger aux fournisseurs commerciaux de cartes satellites en ligne de masquer les sites militaires
**Source**: https://www.rti.org.tw/fr/news?uid=3&pid=210156
**Date**: May 22, 2026
**Author**: La Rédaction (Radio Taiwan International)
**Keywords**: Taiwan, Google Maps, satellite imagery, military sites, national security, digital sovereignty, cartography

## Elevator pitch
Taiwan's Ministry of Digital Affairs met with Apple, Google, and Microsoft to require commercial satellite map providers to blur or mask sensitive military installations, citing security risks demonstrated by Iran's use of such imagery for precision strikes.

## Takeaways
- DPP legislator Lin Chun-hsien cited Iran's use of commercial satellite imagery to locate and strike US military bases
- Deputy Minister Isabel Hou confirmed meetings with Apple, Google, and Microsoft on May 22 to establish emergency communication channels
- Operators will be required to cooperate with the Ministry of Defense under vital zone regulations and surveying/cartography laws
- The initiative reflects growing global concern about dual-use risks of commercial satellite mapping platforms
- Taiwan's specific geopolitical vulnerability to China makes this a particularly urgent national security issue

## Synthesis
Taiwan's government is taking regulatory action to address national security vulnerabilities exposed by commercial satellite mapping platforms. DPP legislator Lin Chun-hsien held a press conference on May 22 flagging how platforms like Google Maps reveal sensitive military installations, citing the case of Iran using commercial satellite imagery to identify and precision-strike US military bases in the Middle East.

In response, Deputy Minister of Digital Affairs Isabel Hou confirmed that her ministry had met with the three major international providers — Apple, Google, and Microsoft — on the very same day. The objective is to establish emergency communication channels between the government and platform operators, enabling rapid response to security concerns. Operators would be required to cooperate with the Ministry of Defense to adjust satellite imagery revealing sensitive sites, under existing regulations governing vital zones and the national surveying and cartography law.

The initiative sits at the intersection of digital sovereignty and national defense. Taiwan's particular geopolitical situation — facing continuous military pressure from China — makes the visibility of its military infrastructure on commercial mapping platforms an acute vulnerability. The demand for blurring or masking sensitive locations follows a pattern seen in other countries (France, South Korea, Israel) that have long restricted high-resolution imagery of military sites.

The meetings represent a shift from reactive complaints to proactive regulatory frameworks, establishing government-to-platform coordination mechanisms rather than relying on individual takedown requests. This model could become a template for other nations concerned about the dual-use nature of commercial geospatial data in an era where satellite imagery is increasingly weaponized for intelligence and targeting purposes.
