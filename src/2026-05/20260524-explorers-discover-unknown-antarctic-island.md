# Explorers discover unknown Antarctic island labeled as 'danger zone' on maps

**Source:** [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/05/24/lifestyle/explorers-discover-unknown-antarctic-island-labeled-as-danger-zone-on-maps/) (originally Fox News)  
**Date:** 2026-05-24  
**Author:** Andrea Margolis (Fox News)  
**Keywords:** Antarctica, island discovery, Weddell Sea, Alfred Wegener Institute, Polarstern, bathymetry, nautical charts, exploration, SCAR

## Elevator Pitch

Researchers aboard the German icebreaker Polarstern discovered a previously unknown island in the northwestern Weddell Sea — an area long marked simply as a "danger zone" on nautical charts. The island, roughly 426 ft long × 164 ft wide and protruding ~16m above water, was surveyed for the first time using echo sounders and drone imaging. With less than a quarter of the Weddell Sea fully charted, the discovery highlights how much remains unknown in Antarctica.

## Key Points

- **Location:** Northwestern Weddell Sea, spotted while sheltering near Joinville Island during bad weather
- **Discovery context:** Research team was studying rapid sea ice decline when harsh weather forced a pause
- **Simon Dreutter** (AWI bathymetry data engineer) first noticed what looked like a "dirty iceberg" — upon closer inspection, it was rock
- **Dimensions:** ~426 ft (130m) long, ~164 ft (50m) wide, 16m high above water
- **Survey methods:** Echo sounder and drone imaging — first time this area has been surveyed and recorded
- **Why "danger zone"?** Nautical charts marked area as "unexplored dangers to navigation" — likely due to uncharted shoals and hummocky seafloor mounds where depths change abruptly over short distances
- **Naming process:** Island will go through formal naming via proposal to the Scientific Committee on Antarctic Research (SCAR)
- **Only ~25% of Antarctic waters are charted** — satellite data is low resolution, especially inshore/nearshore
