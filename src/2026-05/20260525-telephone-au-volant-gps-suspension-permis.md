# VRAI OU FAUX. Téléphone au volant : Waze, Google Maps… utiliser son GPS sur son smartphone peut-il entraîner une suspension du permis ?
**Source**: https://www.lejournaltoulousain.fr/lifestyle/automobile/vrai-ou-faux-telephone-au-volant-waze-google-maps-utiliser-son-gps-sur-son-smartphone-peut-il-entrainer-une-suspension-du-permis-382637/
**Date**: May 25, 2026
**Author**: Antoine Bressan
**Keywords**: GPS, smartphone, driving, license suspension, France, road safety, Waze, Google Maps, law enforcement

## Elevator pitch
A viral TikTok rumor claiming GPS apps on smartphones are now banned while driving is false — but in four French departments, holding your phone to check GPS can now result in immediate license suspension under new prefectoral decrees.

## Takeaways
- Using GPS on a properly mounted smartphone is still legal across all of France; only handheld phone use is prohibited under Article R412-6-1 of the Code de la route.
- Since May 1, 2026, four departments (Landes, Lot-et-Garonne, Pas-de-Calais, Charente-Maritime) apply immediate administrative license suspension for handheld phone use while driving.
- The suspension relies on prefectoral decrees, not a new national law, with durations ranging from 15 days to 6 months depending on circumstances.
- Standard penalties nationwide remain a €135 fine and 3 points off the license; in Charente-Maritime, an additional €200 citizen contribution to road victims' associations is levied.
- Even a quick gesture to zoom or change a destination while stopped at a red light constitutes a full infraction; police can also seize the device even if the screen was off during the stop.

## Synthesis
The article from Le Journal Toulousain fact-checks a viral rumor spreading on TikTok and automotive forums claiming that GPS applications on smartphones — including Waze, Google Maps, and Apple Plans — are now banned while driving across France, with immediate license suspension supposedly in effect since May 1, 2026. Antoine Bressan's investigation reveals that the rumor is largely false at the national level but contains a kernel of truth at the local level.

The core legal framework remains unchanged: Article R412-6-1 of the French Code de la route prohibits holding a phone in hand while driving, but a smartphone properly fixed on an approved mount displaying a GPS app without requiring manipulation remains legal throughout France. For vehicles equipped with Android Auto or Apple CarPlay, the risk of infraction is effectively zero since no physical phone handling is involved. The real trap for drivers is the reflexive gesture — grabbing the phone for "just two seconds" to zoom on a route or change a destination, which legally constitutes an infraction even when stopped at a red light.

The significant evolution is that four French departments now enforce immediate administrative suspension of driving licenses for handheld phone use. The Landes pioneered this approach in November 2025, followed by Lot-et-Garonne and Pas-de-Calais in February 2026, and Charente-Maritime since May 1, 2026. These measures are based not on new national legislation but on prefectoral decrees leveraging existing Code de la route provisions. Suspension durations range from 15 days to 6 months depending on circumstances, and early data from Landes shows approximately 350 licenses were suspended between November 2025 and March 2026, accompanied by a notable decrease in phone-related infractions on departmental roads.

The article provides a clear verdict: FALSE for a total GPS ban at the national level — Waze and Google Maps remain authorized on fixed mounts across France. TRUE for the suspension risk — in these four departments, holding a phone while driving, even to consult GPS, now exposes drivers to immediate suspension. An often-overlooked detail is that even if the phone screen was turned off during a stop, Article R412-6-2 of the Code de la route explicitly allows police to seize the device. The golden rule remains unchanged: phone on the mount, route programmed before departure.
