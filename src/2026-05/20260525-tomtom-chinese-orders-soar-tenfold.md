## Elevator pitch
TomTom executives reveal that Chinese-related orders have surged tenfold from 2024 to 2025, positioning the European map provider as a critical enabler for Chinese automakers' intelligent driving expansion into global markets.

## Synthesis

TomTom, the Dutch location technology company with over three decades of history, is riding a wave of Chinese automotive globalization. In an interview with 36Kr, Chief Revenue Officer Mike Schoofs disclosed that China-related orders skyrocketed tenfold between 2024 and 2025, and in Q1 2026, 100,000 vehicles equipped with TomTom services were already exported overseas.

The interview frames Chinese automakers' overseas expansion as a definitive industry trend rather than an optional growth path. While the first wave of Chinese automotive exports leveraged cost-effectiveness and electrification advantages, the current phase centers on intelligence — smart cockpits and advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS) have become the differentiating factors for Chinese brands competing in premium overseas markets. Navigation, the most frequently used and perceptible in-vehicle function, depends entirely on locally adapted map data for lane-level guidance, real-time traffic, EV charging planning, and multilingual POI search. Without professional overseas maps, cockpit intelligence experiences significant gaps that undermine product competitiveness.

For ADAS, maps play a role across the full R&D chain: training data for intelligent driving models requires massive real-world local road scenarios; maps provide over-the-horizon positioning in sensor-degraded environments like tunnels and overpasses; and EU markets impose clear compliance requirements mandating certified map data for vehicle type approval. TomTom positions itself as the "overseas version of AutoNavi" — a comparison the company embraces — offering full-stack map data, flexible cooperation models (raw data, services, or turnkey solutions), and an independently controlled global data base covering 235 countries.

The company's technical architecture follows a layered, modular approach: a base geographic map with superimposed layers for 3D buildings, ADAS functions, EV services, and real-time traffic. AI agent technology is being integrated to push dynamic traffic conditions, speed limits, road hazards, extreme weather, and charging station availability in real time. TomTom counts Toyota, Volkswagen, Honda, and Stellantis among its core automotive partners, plus long-term relationships with BMW and Daimler, and collaborations with Microsoft and major mobility platforms.

Notably, TomTom's Greater China team achieved six-month mass production delivery on a cooperation project, and traffic light countdown recognition co-developed with Huawei Petal Maps has completed a pilot in Madrid with large-scale European deployment planned for H2 2026. Schoofs stated two core goals: improving user experience to demonstrate product leadership, and securing more orders to help additional Chinese automakers go global — particularly through ADAS partnerships being discussed with all major algorithm companies.

On Tesla, Schoofs noted the company's strict disclosure policies but acknowledged "many contacts," sidestepping whether Tesla uses TomTom data. He acknowledged competitors in the ADAS export space but cited TomTom's vetting by automakers like Volkswagen as validation of its market position. The article underscores a broader strategic reality: as Chinese automotive exports accelerate, the underlying geospatial data infrastructure required for intelligent vehicles creates a growing dependency on established global map providers like TomTom, who hold the regulatory certifications, local data, and integration capabilities that Chinese OEMs cannot replicate overnight in foreign markets.
