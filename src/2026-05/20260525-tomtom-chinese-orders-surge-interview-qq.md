## Elevator pitch
TomTom reports a 10x surge in China-related orders and positions itself as the overseas map backbone for Chinese automakers expanding globally with intelligent driving capabilities.

## Takeaways
- TomTom's China-related orders grew 10x between 2024 and 2025, with Q1 2026 seeing 100,000 exported vehicles equipped with TomTom services.
- Chinese automotive exports have shifted from cost and electrification advantages to intelligence (smart cockpit + ADAS) as the core differentiation in overseas markets.
- TomTom covers 235 countries with independently controlled base map data, partnering flexibly with Chinese tech firms including Huawei Petal Maps and Tencent.
- EU compliance requires certified map data for vehicle type approval, making established providers like TomTom essential for Chinese OEMs entering European markets.
- TomTom's six-month mass production record for a Chinese client and Madrid pilot of traffic light countdown recognition with Huawei demonstrate rapid implementation capabilities.

## Synthesis

This 36Kr article, syndicated on Tencent News, presents an interview with TomTom's executive team led by Mike Schoofs, detailing how the European mapping company has become indispensable to Chinese automakers' global ambitions. The core revelation is quantitative: China-related orders exploded tenfold from 2024 to 2025, and 100,000 vehicles carrying TomTom services were already exported in the first quarter of 2026 alone.

The narrative situates Chinese automotive globalization as a structural shift. BYD, Chery, XPeng, NIO, Li Auto, and Xiaomi are all pushing into high-end overseas markets, but the competitive edge has moved from hardware cost advantages to software intelligence. Navigation — the most-used in-car function — cannot function properly in foreign markets without locally adapted map data supporting lane-level routing, real-time traffic, EV charger planning, and multilingual search. Similarly, ADAS requires maps across the full value chain: model training needs real local road data, safety-critical over-the-horizon positioning fills sensor gaps in tunnels and urban canyons, and EU regulations mandate certified map data outright.

TomTom characterizes itself as the "overseas AutoNavi" — full-stack map data plus complete software services, with a layered modular architecture supporting 3D buildings, ADAS, EV services, and real-time traffic on top of a global base map. AI agents are being integrated for dynamic information push and proactive route optimization. Partnerships with Huawei Petal Maps (navigation UI) and Tencent (intelligent driving co-creation) illustrate TomTom's flexible engagement model: pure data licensing, standalone services, or full tier-1 solution delivery.

The interview reveals pragmatic operational metrics: a six-month mass production achievement, Madrid pilot completion with Huawei for traffic light countdown recognition, and European scale-up planned for H2 2026. Schoofs identifies two future goals — superior user experience and more orders — with ADAS partnerships being actively discussed across the algorithm company landscape. On the competitive landscape, he acknowledges rivals but points to Volkswagen's vendor qualification process as market validation. The subtext is clear: Chinese automakers racing to globalize their intelligent vehicles are converging on TomTom as the infrastructure layer they cannot afford to build independently.
