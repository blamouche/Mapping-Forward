# Geo Week Seeks Impactful Presentations; Accepting Speaker Submissions Through July 17

**Date**: May 26, 2026
**Author**: Unknown

## Elevator pitch
Geo Week 2027 has opened its Call for Speakers, inviting practitioners and innovators to share real-world case studies on lidar, reality capture, photogrammetry, and related geospatial technologies for the February 2027 event in Salt Lake City.

## Takeaways
- Geo Week 2027 will take place February 23-25, 2027 in Salt Lake City, Utah, with speaker submissions open through July 17.
- The conference emphasizes practitioner-led sessions: "The most impactful sessions come from people who've dealt with real challenges and found ways to move forward."
- Key topics span aerial lidar, AI/ML, digital twins, SLAM/mobile mapping, UAV applications, bathymetry, Gaussian splatting, and workforce development.
- Accepted speakers are matched to tracks hosted by Geo Week, ASPRS, or Reality Capture Network based on content fit.
- The event brings together three organizations — Geo Week, ASPRS, and RCN — under one conference pass, covering geospatial, photogrammetry, and reality capture programming.

## Synthesis
Organizers of Geo Week, one of the premier geospatial and mapping industry events, have announced their Call for Speakers for the February 23-25, 2027 edition in Salt Lake City. The submission window runs through July 17, and the conference is explicitly seeking practitioner-led presentations rather than purely academic or vendor-driven content.

Carla Lauter, Senior Content Manager at Geo Week News, framed the call around practitioners doing the work: people who have dealt with real challenges and found ways to move forward. The conference wants professionals at every level to share case studies on workflow transformations, complex projects, and innovative applications of geospatial technology. The list of key topics is extensive, covering aerial lidar and data acquisition, AI and machine learning, GIS applications, digital twins, photogrammetry, point cloud processing, reality capture, SLAM and mobile mapping, bathymetry and coastal mapping, UAV/UAS applications, field-to-finish workflows, workforce development, and the emerging technique of Gaussian splatting.

A distinctive feature of Geo Week is its three-track structure. Accepted speakers are matched to sessions hosted by Geo Week, the American Society for Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing (ASPRS), or the Reality Capture Network (RCN) based on content fit, and all full conference pass holders have access to programming across all three tracks. This combined approach reflects the convergence happening across geospatial disciplines — lidar specialists, photogrammetrists, surveyors, GIS professionals, and reality capture practitioners increasingly work with overlapping toolsets and face similar challenges in data integration and workflow optimization.

The announcement signals an industry that continues to professionalize its knowledge-sharing mechanisms while maintaining a focus on practical, field-tested insights over theoretical presentations. Registration opens in September 2026.
