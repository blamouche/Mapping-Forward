## Synthesis
Cartes.gouv.fr is the French government's ambitious sovereign mapping platform, launched on June 25, 2026 as the successor to Géoportail — the historical portal of the Institut national de l'information géographique et forestière (IGN). With Géoportail becoming obsolete in September 2026, Cartes.gouv.fr aims to rival commercial platforms like Google Maps and Waze while offering a fundamentally different value proposition rooted in public data sovereignty and open access.

The platform boasts over 1,100 cartographic layers, far exceeding the depth of commercial mapping services. Key features include aerial photography at 20 centimeter resolution, the IGN vector plan, cadastre data, INSEE demographic data, and thematic maps covering urbanism, environment, and forests. A Street View-like feature has been integrated with 110 million photographs already available, providing a free and open alternative to Google's proprietary street-level imagery.

Beyond basic mapping, Cartes.gouv.fr offers practical tools including surface and distance measurement, isochrone calculation to visualize areas accessible within a given time, and public contribution capabilities. The LiDAR HD program stands out as a particularly advanced feature, providing a terrain model with centimetric precision. This data serves applications in hydrology, archaeology, and natural risk prevention — use cases that go well beyond consumer navigation.

The platform builds on existing IGN services like macarte.ign.fr and geoservices.ign.fr, consolidating them into a unified public-facing portal. While it may not match Google Maps or Waze in real-time traffic navigation, Cartes.gouv.fr differentiates through its depth of authoritative geographic data, open access model, and specialized features for public administration, research, and citizen information.
