# Casablanca Digitizes Road Network Management with GIS-Based System

## Synthesis
The Commune of Casablanca has initiated a major digital overhaul of its road network management, centered on the deployment of a Geographic Information System (GIS) — referred to as SIG in French — to create a comprehensive geo-referenced database of the city's road infrastructure. This initiative represents a fundamental shift from reactive road maintenance to proactive, data-driven planning.

The first phase of the project consists of an exhaustive technical inventory of the entire road network, encompassing structural roads, secondary roads, and all pathways within the municipality. This inventory will feed into the geo-referenced database, creating a digital twin of Casablanca's road assets that can be queried, analyzed, and updated continuously.

The GIS database serves as the foundation for defining both annual and multi-year road maintenance plans. Rather than responding to road failures as they occur, the municipality will be able to prioritize maintenance based on data-driven assessments of road conditions, traffic patterns, and infrastructure aging. This approach enables more efficient allocation of maintenance budgets and longer-term infrastructure planning.

The project aligns with Casablanca's broader digital transformation efforts, which aim to modernize municipal infrastructure management through technology. By grounding road management in geospatial data, the city joins a growing number of municipalities worldwide adopting GIS-based asset management systems to improve service delivery and optimize infrastructure investments. The initiative reflects a broader trend in North African and Middle Eastern cities of leveraging geospatial technology for urban governance.
