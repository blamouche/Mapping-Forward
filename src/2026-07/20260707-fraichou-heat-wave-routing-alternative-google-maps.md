## Synthesis
As heat waves become more frequent and intense, a French engineer has created Fraichoù, a web application that helps pedestrians find the coolest walking routes through cities. Currently available in three French cities — Paris, Lille, and Rennes with their surrounding areas — Fraichoù addresses a specific need that mainstream navigation apps like Google Maps do not cover: thermal comfort for walkers.

The app's interface is straightforward. Users enter a departure point, a destination, and a departure time. Fraichoù then proposes three route options: the fastest (equivalent to standard navigation), a balanced route, and the coolest route. The "coolest" option calculates paths that maximize shade from buildings, trees, and water features, minimizing exposure to direct sun during hot weather. Toggle buttons allow users to visualize these shade sources on the map for a better understanding of why a particular route was chosen.

This approach fills a genuine gap in the navigation market. Google Maps and similar apps optimize primarily for speed and distance, with some consideration for elevation and eco-routing. However, none factor in the microclimate of urban walking routes — the difference between walking along a tree-lined boulevard versus an exposed asphalt corridor can be significant during a heat wave, particularly for vulnerable populations.

The app's current limitation to three cities reflects the data-intensive nature of shade modeling, which requires detailed 3D building data, tree canopy maps, and knowledge of water features. As urban heat island effects worsen with climate change, tools like Fraichoù point toward a new category of thermally-aware pedestrian navigation that could become increasingly relevant.
