## Synthesis
Montana State University hosted GIS Day, an educational event designed to introduce rural Montana students to Geographic Information System (GIS) technology, mapping skills, and potential career paths in the geospatial industry. The event aimed to bridge the opportunity gap that rural students often face in accessing technology education and exposure to STEM career pathways.

GIS — the technology that powers navigation devices, smartphone searches for local services, and spatial analysis tools — is a foundational technology increasingly critical across industries including agriculture, urban planning, environmental management, and emergency response. For rural Montana students, understanding GIS opens doors to careers that are particularly relevant to their communities, from precision agriculture to natural resource management.

The event provided hands-on exposure to mapping skills, allowing students to understand how geographic data is collected, analyzed, and applied to real-world problems. By connecting mapping technology to familiar contexts — navigation apps they use daily, weather maps, and local planning decisions — the program made GIS accessible and relevant.

MSU's GIS Day is part of a broader workforce development effort in the geospatial sector, which faces growing demand for skilled professionals. By reaching rural students who might not otherwise have exposure to GIS technology, the university aims to diversify the pipeline of talent entering the field while also providing students with awareness of career opportunities that combine technology, geography, and data analysis. The initiative reflects the growing recognition that geospatial literacy is a critical 21st-century skill.
