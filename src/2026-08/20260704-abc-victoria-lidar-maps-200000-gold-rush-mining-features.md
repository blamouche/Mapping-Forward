# Geological Survey of Victoria Maps 200,000 Unrecorded Gold Rush Mining Features
**Source**: https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-07-04/mineshafts-lidar-map-gold-rush-geological-survey-victoria/106860926
**Date**: 2026-07-04
**Author**: ABC News (Australia)
**Keywords**: LiDAR mapping, Geological Survey of Victoria, gold rush, mining features, remote sensing, 3D mapping, historical mining, Crown land

## Elevator pitch
A four-year LiDAR mapping project by the Geological Survey of Victoria has discovered an estimated 230,000 historic mining features across Crown land, dramatically revising previous records of just 30,000 and revealing the hidden landscape impact of 175 years of gold rush activity.

## Takeaways
- LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) surveying increased mapped mining features from 30,000 to an estimated 230,000 in Victoria
- A light aircraft equipped with LiDAR surveyed the Golden Plains, Greater Melbourne, Central Goldfields, and the High Country
- The technology sends laser beams that bounce back to sensors, measuring return time to create 3D point clouds of the ground surface

## Synthesis
The Geological Survey of Victoria has completed a four-year LiDAR mapping project that has drastically revised official records of historic mining features across the state, taking the number of mapped features from 30,000 to an estimated 230,000. The project, led by Geological Survey director Louise Goldie Divko, focused on areas around the Golden Plains, Greater Melbourne, Central Goldfields, and the High Country — regions at the heart of Victoria's gold rush history that began in 1851.

The technology works by equipping a light aircraft with LiDAR sensors that send out tiny laser beams, which hit objects and bounce back. The sensor measures how long each beam takes to return, producing a dense 3D point cloud of the surface. Software then identifies and removes objects like trees and buildings, "revealing" the ground below with remarkable precision. Sinks, divots, and other surface irregularities in this bare-earth model indicate the presence of mining features — mineshafts, surface workings, pits, adits (horizontal tunnels), open stopes (steplike excavations), and mullock heaps (mounds of leftover soil or rock).

The automated detection program achieved 90% accuracy in locating mining features, though it was less successful in identifying specific feature types. Field teams verified a sample of detected features to validate the methodology. The results confirm what prospectors and historians have long suspected: the physical legacy of 175 years of gold mining is far more extensive than existing records indicate.

The mapping has practical importance for public safety — holes opening up after heavy rain are frequently attributed to historic mining activity — and for land-use planning on Crown land. It also provides a comprehensive archaeological record of one of Australia's most transformative historical periods, demonstrating the power of airborne LiDAR remote sensing to reveal hidden landscapes at a scale that traditional ground surveys could never achieve.
