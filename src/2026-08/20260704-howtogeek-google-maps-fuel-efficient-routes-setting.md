# This Google Maps Setting Helps Me Save Gas, But You Might Have It Configured Wrong
**Source**: https://www.howtogeek.com/this-google-maps-setting-can-help-you-save-gas-but-most-people-have-it-configured-wrong/
**Date**: 2026-07-04
**Author**: Joe, How-To Geek
**Keywords**: Google Maps, fuel-efficient routes, gas saving, navigation settings, engine type, route preferences, fuel consumption, driving settings, eco-routing

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps now defaults to fuel-efficient routing for everyone, but since the app doesn't know what kind of car you drive, the "efficient" route may actually be slower and longer — unless you configure your engine type and route preferences correctly.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps rolled out fuel-efficient routes in 2022, originally as an optional route choice rather than the default
- At some point Google made fuel-efficient routes the default for everyone, causing some users to notice slower, longer routes
- The app's "fuel-efficient" calculation depends on your engine type — gas, diesel, hybrid, or electric — which most users never configure
- Without knowing your engine type, Google Maps applies generic assumptions that may not match your vehicle's actual efficiency profile
- Users can set their engine type in Settings > Navigation > Route options to get more accurate fuel-efficient routing
- To always get the fastest route regardless of fuel efficiency, disable "Prefer fuel-efficient routes" in Settings > Navigation
- Fuel efficiency in Google Maps comes down to two factors: the type of car you drive and the terrain in your location

## Synthesis
Google Maps introduced fuel-efficient routes in 2022 as one of several route options, selected as the default only when they were also the fastest. Since then, Google has made fuel-efficient routing the default for everyone — a change that has caused some users to notice Maps deliberately taking them on slower, longer routes in the name of fuel savings. The problem, as How-To Geek explains, is that Google Maps doesn't know what kind of car you drive, and "fuel-efficient" isn't the same for every vehicle.

The fuel-efficient routing algorithm considers two main factors: the type of car you drive and the terrain in your location. A route that is fuel-efficient for a gas-powered sedan may not be efficient for a diesel truck, a hybrid, or an electric vehicle. Yet most users never configure their engine type in Google Maps, leaving the app to apply generic assumptions that may not reflect their actual fuel consumption profile. Users can fix this by navigating to their profile icon > Settings > Navigation > Route options and selecting their engine type (gas, diesel, hybrid, or electric).

For users who find that fuel-efficient routing consistently produces slower routes and would prefer the fastest option regardless, the solution is straightforward: in Settings > Navigation, disable "Prefer fuel-efficient routes." This restores the previous behavior of always selecting the fastest route. The trade-off is between fuel savings and time savings, and the correct choice depends on individual priorities — but the key insight is that most users are unknowingly accepting Google's default without understanding what it optimizes for or how to adjust it.

This article highlights how a well-intentioned default — eco-routing — can create friction when users don't understand the underlying assumptions. Google Maps' fuel-efficient routing is a powerful feature, but only when the app has accurate information about your vehicle. Without it, the "efficient" route may be neither fast nor genuinely efficient for your specific car.
