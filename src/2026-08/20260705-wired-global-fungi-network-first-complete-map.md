## Synthesis
Beneath the Earth's surface lies an extraordinary underground fungal network of almost unimaginable scale. An international team of researchers has, for the first time, produced a global map of this vast mycorrhizal network — the system of fungal filaments that forms mutually beneficial partnerships with plants across the planet. They estimate that the network stretches for roughly 110 quadrillion kilometers in total, nearly 1 billion times the distance between the Earth and the sun.

The findings, published in the journal Science, represent a breakthrough in spatial ecology. Mycorrhizal fungi form symbiotic relationships with plant roots, exchanging nutrients and water for carbon compounds produced through photosynthesis. These underground networks are fundamental to terrestrial ecosystem function, influencing plant health, soil structure, carbon cycling, and drought resilience. Until now, mapping these networks has been limited to local or regional studies, leaving the global picture largely unknown.

The researchers combined extensive field sampling data with global geospatial datasets — including climate variables, soil properties, and vegetation patterns — to model and predict the distribution of mycorrhizal fungal networks worldwide. This approach allowed them to estimate the total extent of the network and identify geographic patterns in fungal diversity and abundance. The resulting map reveals how fungal network distribution varies across biomes, from boreal forests to tropical rainforests to grasslands.

The implications extend beyond academic interest. Mycorrhizal networks play a crucial role in carbon sequestration and climate regulation, making them relevant to climate modeling and conservation policy. Understanding their global distribution could inform ecosystem restoration efforts, agricultural practices, and biodiversity protection strategies. The map provides a baseline for monitoring how these underground networks respond to environmental change, including climate warming, land-use conversion, and soil degradation.

This first complete global fungal map underscores the importance of subsurface ecosystems that are invisible but vital to life above ground, and demonstrates how advanced geospatial modeling can illuminate ecological systems at planetary scale.
