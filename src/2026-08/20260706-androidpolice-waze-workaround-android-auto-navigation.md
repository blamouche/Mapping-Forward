# This Little-Known Waze Workaround Solved My Biggest Android Auto Navigation Issues

**Author**: Jade Bryan, Android Police
**Keywords**: Waze, Android Auto, navigation glitches, app crash, unresponsive screen, app reinstall, Google Maps alternative, crowdsourced navigation, troubleshooting, connected car

## Elevator pitch
A simple uninstall-and-reinstall of Waze proved to be the most effective fix for persistent Android Auto glitches including frozen screens, unresponsive touch inputs, and mid-drive crashes — problems that affect both wired and wireless connections across multiple devices and car models.

## Synthesis
Android Police writer Jade Bryan documents a thorough investigation into persistent Waze glitches on Android Auto that affect navigation reliability and, in some cases, driving safety. The problems range from frequent Waze crashes when launching on the dashboard to complete touch-input lockouts where the infotainment screen refuses to acknowledge taps while the rest of the Android Auto interface functions normally. The most dangerous scenario is a mid-drive screen freeze that prevents route changes, trip cancellation, or zoom adjustments — particularly hazardous at highway speeds or on tight corners.

These issues are not isolated to specific hardware. Bryan experienced them across a Samsung Galaxy A54, Google Pixel 9 Pro XL, and Galaxy Z Flip 7, paired with vehicles including a Toyota Vios sedan and Innova SUV. Both wired and wireless Android Auto connections are affected, though wireless appears more prone to display hangs.

Several workarounds were tested. Force-closing Waze on the phone (which also closes it on Android Auto) is a one-time fix that usually resolves unresponsiveness. Fiddling with physical or capacitive dials on the car's head unit can sometimes refresh the app. Using Gemini voice commands to reroute or close the drive works if available. Yanking and reinserting the USB cable forces a connection reboot. But the most effective permanent solution was a complete uninstall and reinstall of Waze from the Play Store, followed by a device reboot. Since doing this, total screen unresponsiveness vanished, though some sluggishness in place search and navigation persisted — likely attributable to the vehicle's display rather than the app.

Another useful workaround is removing Waze from Android Auto's default startup apps, forcing manual launch each drive. This bypasses the initial boot-up bottleneck in the Android Auto launcher. Bryan argues that Google pours more effort into polishing Google Maps while treating Waze as a secondary concern, and calls for the same level of basic stability to be brought to Waze — preferring a reliable app over new features.
