## Synthesis
The Massachusetts government has launched a statewide housing data visualization portal that enables residents and policymakers to track home construction progress across the state. Developed by the Center for Data Innovation's David Kertai, the portal draws on U.S. Census address records to provide a granular, geographically referenced view of housing production.

The portal's key finding: 34,500 homes were added in 2025, representing approximately 15 percent of the state's housing production goal for 2035. This benchmark framing transforms raw construction data into a progress metric, making it immediately actionable for policy discussions about whether the state is on track to meet its housing targets.

The interactive map employs intuitive cartographic design — darker blue shades indicate areas with more homes built, lighter shades show fewer — making construction progress easy to compare across municipalities and regions. This approach demonstrates how effective data visualization can make complex policy metrics accessible to non-technical audiences, from concerned residents to city planners.

The portal exemplifies a growing trend in open government data initiatives: using interactive maps to communicate progress on policy goals. By grounding the visualization in authoritative Census address records and presenting it through an accessible geographic interface, Massachusetts has created a tool that supports both transparency and informed decision-making in housing policy. Similar approaches could be replicated by other states and municipalities seeking to track and communicate their own housing production metrics.
