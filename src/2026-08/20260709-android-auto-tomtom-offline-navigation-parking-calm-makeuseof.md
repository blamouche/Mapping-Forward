# 3 Android Auto apps I never knew existed — and now I can't drive without them

**Date**: 2026-07-09
**Author**: Shimul Sood (MakeUseOf)
**Keywords**: Android Auto, TomTom, offline navigation, parking, Calm, driving apps, speed camera alerts

## Elevator pitch
A MakeUseOf reviewer highlights three Android Auto apps that transformed their driving experience, with TomTom's offline navigation, parking reservations, and the Calm meditation app ranking as indispensable dashboard companions.

## Takeaways
- TomTom's Android Auto app offers full 3D offline map downloads, eliminating dependence on cellular connectivity
- TomTom provides real-time traffic updates, road closures, construction zones, speed camera alerts, and lane guidance
- The app costs $20/year subscription and is ad-free with Android Auto and Apple CarPlay compatibility
- A parking reservation app integrates with Android Auto to display booking details directly on the dashboard
- Calm's Android Auto interface offers distraction-free, curated driving content with large controls

## Synthesis
A MakeUseOf contributor shares a personal account of three Android Auto apps that evolved from experimental downloads to essential driving tools. The standout is TomTom's navigation app, which impressed the reviewer with its offline capabilities — full 3D maps can be downloaded directly to the phone, enabling navigation without relying on a stable internet connection.

TomTom's real-time features go beyond basic routing: traffic updates proved impressively accurate, with road closures and construction zones appearing quickly and routes recalculating on the fly. The inclusion of speed camera alerts and lane guidance added practical value that became surprisingly indispensable once the reviewer started paying attention to them. At $20 per year, the subscription model positions TomTom as a premium but affordable alternative to free options like Google Maps and Waze.

The second app addresses parking anxiety by enabling reservations through Android Auto, with booking details and notifications accessible directly on the dashboard. The third app, Calm, brings meditation and mindfulness content to the car in a driving-optimized format — simple interface, large controls, and content curated for moments when attention should remain on the road.

The review underscores how Android Auto's app ecosystem has matured beyond navigation and music, offering practical utilities that address common driving pain points from routing to parking to stress management.
