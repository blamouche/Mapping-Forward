# Benarkah Kemacetan di Jakarta Menurun (Has Jakarta's Traffic Congestion Decreased?)
**Source**: https://www.tempo.co/data/data/benarkah-kemacetan-di-jakarta-menurun-2274768
**Date**: 2026-07-09
**Author**: Faisal Javier (Tempo.co)
**Keywords**: TomTom Traffic Index, Jakarta, traffic congestion, urban mobility, Indonesia

## Elevator pitch
Jakarta's governor claims progress on reducing congestion, but TomTom Traffic Index data shows the city's congestion rate actually rose to 59.8% in 2025, with motorcycle numbers increasing by 200,000 units year-over-year.

## Takeaways
- TomTom Traffic Index recorded Jakarta's congestion level at 59.8% in 2025, up over 1% from the previous year
- Key corridors like Jalan Casablanca and Jalan Prof. Dr. Satrio (Kuningan) require 9 minutes 34 seconds for just 3.8 km
- Motorcycle registrations in Jakarta increased by 200,000 units compared to the previous year
- Governor Pramono Anung acknowledged that eliminating congestion is impossible; the goal is reduction
- The data contradicts official claims of improving traffic conditions

## Synthesis
An investigation by Tempo.co using TomTom Traffic Index data challenges the narrative that Jakarta's traffic congestion is improving. According to TomTom's measurements, the Indonesian capital's congestion rate reached 59.8% in 2025, representing an increase of more than one percentage point over the previous year — moving in the opposite direction of the reduction goals stated by Governor Pramono Anung.

The TomTom data provides granular detail: on two major arteries, Jalan Casablanca and Jalan Prof. Dr. Satrio in the Kuningan business district, vehicles require 9 minutes and 34 seconds to cover just 3.8 kilometers. This level of detail, characteristic of TomTom's floating car data methodology, enables precise corridor-level analysis that aggregate statistics often obscure.

A key driver of worsening congestion is vehicle fleet growth. Motorcycle registrations in Jakarta increased by 200,000 units year-over-year, adding to the already dense traffic mix. The governor acknowledged in a July 2026 interview that eliminating congestion is impossible and that government efforts can only aim to reduce it.

This case illustrates how TomTom Traffic Index data serves as an independent, data-driven accountability tool for urban mobility policy, providing objective measurements against which official claims can be validated. The index's methodology — based on GPS probe data from millions of vehicles — offers a consistent, comparable benchmark across global cities.
