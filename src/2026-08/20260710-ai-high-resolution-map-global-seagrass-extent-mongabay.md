# Scientists use AI to produce first high-resolution map of global seagrass extent

## Synthesis
Scientists at Arizona State University's School of Ocean Futures, led by assistant professor Jiwei Li, have achieved a milestone in marine cartography by producing the first high-resolution global map of seagrass ecosystems. Published in Nature in July 2026, the study identified 148,506 km² of seagrass globally — an area larger than England — with the majority located in subtidal areas.

The team trained a deep-learning AI model on satellite imagery to distinguish seagrass from other coastal features, a task that proved fundamentally different from mapping coral reefs. Collaborators worldwide provided ground-truth validation through research dives and underwater cameras, ensuring the model's accuracy across diverse coastal environments.

The map's most striking finding is that 70% of global seagrass cover is concentrated off the coasts of just five countries, underscoring both the uneven distribution of these critical blue carbon ecosystems and the conservation priorities that emerge from high-resolution mapping. The dataset was compiled over two periods — 2019-2020 and 2023-2024 — enabling change detection analysis.

This work represents a significant advance in applying AI to environmental mapping, demonstrating how machine learning can fill gaps in global ecosystem monitoring that traditional field survey methods alone cannot address at scale. The publicly available map supports conservation planning by identifying both existing seagrass meadows and areas with restoration potential.
