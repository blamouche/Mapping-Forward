## Elevator pitch
A satellite image on Google Maps captures France's nuclear-powered aircraft carrier Charles-de-Gaulle transiting the Suez Canal, complete with Rafale Marine fighters on deck — illustrating how public mapping tools can inadvertently reveal military positions.

## Takeaways
- The Charles-de-Gaulle is visible at coordinates 30.542777, 32.340328 on Google Maps, in the heart of the Suez Canal
- The 261-meter carrier with its distinctive angled flight deck, central island, and delta-wing Rafale Marine fighters is clearly identifiable, accompanied by an escort frigate
- The image is a frozen snapshot from a satellite pass, not real-time tracking — it may persist for months before the next imagery update
- The Suez Canal route is the most logical and economical path for the carrier to return to its home port of Toulon
- The discovery highlights how public satellite imagery services can inadvertently expose sensitive military assets, similar to past exposures via fitness tracking apps

## Synthesis
Published on July 10, 2026, by AquitaineOnLine, this article reports on the discovery of France's flagship aircraft carrier Charles-de-Gaulle visible on Google Maps satellite imagery while transiting the Suez Canal. At coordinates 30.542777, 32.340328, the carrier's distinctive silhouette — a 261-meter platform with an angled flight deck, central island, and delta-wing Rafale Marine fighters aligned on the deck — is clearly identifiable. A thinner vessel to the north, likely an escort frigate, accompanies it.

The Charles-de-Gaulle is the only nuclear-powered aircraft carrier in the European Union, with a displacement of approximately 42,000 tons. Based at Toulon, it projects French air power and participates in NATO and coalition operations. The Suez Canal transit represents the most logical and economical route between the Indian Ocean or eastern Mediterranean and the carrier's home port, typically occurring after operational missions, diplomatic port calls, or crew rotations.

The article explains why such a sensitive military asset can appear on a public mapping service: Google Maps satellite images are not live feeds but periodic captures from satellites or aerial photography, integrated into a mosaic updated at intervals. If a vessel is present during the sensor's pass, it appears frozen on the image, sometimes for months. This is not geolocated real-time tracking but a coincidental snapshot.

The discovery is not isolated. Military positions have been inadvertently revealed through other channels, including fitness tracking apps used by crew members. The article notes that even personal geolocation data can become sensitive when it concerns military zones or personnel. The anecdote illustrates both the power and the limits of public cartographic tools: a simple zoom on a portion of the Suez Canal is enough to stumble upon one of the French fleet's most strategic vessels — a reminder that in an era of ubiquitous satellite imagery, true operational secrecy is increasingly difficult to maintain.
