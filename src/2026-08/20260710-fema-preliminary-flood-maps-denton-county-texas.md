# Preliminary Flood Maps for Denton County, Texas, Ready for Public View
**Source**: https://www.fema.gov/press-release/20260601/preliminary-flood-maps-denton-county-texas-ready-public-view
**Date**: 2026-06-01
**Author**: FEMA Region 6
**Keywords**: FEMA, flood maps, FIRM, Denton County, Texas, flood risk, Special Flood Hazard Areas

## Elevator pitch
FEMA has released preliminary Flood Insurance Rate Maps for Denton County, Texas, enabling residents and community leaders to review updated flood risk designations and participate in the public appeal process.

## Takeaways
- Preliminary Flood Insurance Rate Maps (FIRMs) cover Denton County including cities of Denton, Fort Worth, Haslet, Justin, New Fairview, and Roanoke
- The maps define Special Flood Hazard Areas (SFHAs) — zones at high risk for flooding
- Residents and business owners can review the maps and participate in appeal and comment periods
- Maps are accessible via the FEMA Flood Map Changes Viewer and FEMA Map Service Center online portals
- Cost-saving insurance options are available for properties newly mapped into high-risk flood zones

## Synthesis
FEMA Region 6 announced the availability of preliminary Flood Insurance Rate Maps (FIRMs) for Denton County, Texas, marking a significant update to the area's flood risk mapping infrastructure. The Physical Map Revision covers portions of multiple municipalities including Denton, Fort Worth, Haslet, Justin, New Fairview, and Roanoke, as well as the towns of Dish, Northlake, and Ponder, plus unincorporated areas of Denton County.

The updated FIRMs serve critical functions: they define Special Flood Hazard Areas (SFHAs) that identify zones at high risk for flooding, providing the foundation for local building standards, development decisions, and flood insurance requirements. Community leaders and residents can use these maps to make informed decisions that enhance resilience and reduce the impact of flood events.

FEMA emphasizes that flooding can and does occur outside of designated high-risk areas, a reminder that flood maps represent probabilistic risk rather than definitive boundaries. The agency has established multiple channels for public access: residents can review the preliminary maps through their local floodplain administrator, via the online Flood Map Changes Viewer at msc.fema.gov/fmcv, or through the FEMA Map Service Center.

The release initiates a public participation process where residents can identify concerns, submit comments, and file appeals during designated periods. FEMA also highlights cost-saving insurance options available for properties newly classified into high-risk zones, encouraging affected property owners to consult insurance agents or visit floodsmart.gov for guidance.
