## Elevator pitch

## Synthesis
Published on July 11, 2026, by Futura-Sciences, this article (in Dutch) describes a practical trick for French drivers who want speed camera warnings while using Google Maps. The solution is TomTom AmiGo, a free app developed by the Dutch navigation company TomTom that overlays a floating widget on top of Google Maps, displaying real-time speed camera alerts and the current speed limit.

The legal context is important: in France, article R.413-15 of the traffic code strictly prohibits traditional radar detectors. Only apps that indicate 'danger zones' are legal. TomTom AmiGo operates within this framework, making it a lawful alternative for French motorists who want both Google Maps navigation and speed camera warnings simultaneously.

The article provides a step-by-step installation guide: download from Google Play Store, activate speech synthesis for audio alerts, and grant overlay permissions so the widget displays correctly over Google Maps. The setup takes minutes but significantly enhances the navigation experience.

Compared to Waze (owned by Google), TomTom AmiGo offers several advantages according to experienced users. It distinguishes between different types of speed cameras — fixed and mobile — whereas Waze primarily focuses on fixed radars. This broader coverage is particularly useful for drivers who want to match their speed to current limits. However, the app's reliability depends on community input, meaning warnings may be less accurate on rural roads with fewer active users.

A key limitation is platform availability: the floating widget feature is exclusive to Android, as iOS does not allow floating widgets over other apps. iPhone users can still use AmiGo as a standalone navigation app and with Apple CarPlay, but cannot overlay it on Google Maps. The article also warns drivers to check local regulations when crossing borders, as radar detectors are strictly prohibited in Germany and other countries.
