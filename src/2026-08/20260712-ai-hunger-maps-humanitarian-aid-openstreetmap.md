# From space rovers to hunger maps: How AI is reshaping humanitarian aid
**Source**: https://www.euronews.com/next/2026/07/12/from-space-rovers-to-hunger-maps-how-ai-is-reshaping-humanitarian-aid
**Date**: 2026-07-12
**Author**: Roselyne Min (Euronews, with AP)
**Keywords**: AI, humanitarian aid, HungerMap Live, OpenStreetMap, satellite imagery, disaster mapping, WFP, DLR, remote delivery

## Elevator pitch
Humanitarian organizations are using AI to predict hunger, map disaster zones, and deliver aid remotely — adapting space rover technology to take workers out of danger while accelerating response times in crisis areas.

## Takeaways
- Project AHEAD, a collaboration between WFP, DLR, Red Cross, and tech partners, is developing remotely operated vehicles adapted from planetary rover technology to deliver aid through conflict zones and minefields
- HungerMap Live, a WFP platform, uses machine learning and near-real-time data to track food insecurity across 95+ countries, with plans to forecast 90 days into the future
- The Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team used AI and satellite imagery with 600+ volunteers to map earthquake damage in Venezuela within four days via the MapSwipe app
- AI-generated maps prioritize speed over precision: manual mapping still provides the best quality, but ML models can quickly estimate building counts and population in affected areas
- Despite advances, AI systems remain far from routine integration into emergency protocols in most countries, with only isolated exceptions like India's AI early-warning system

## Synthesis
Published on July 12, 2026, by Euronews Next, this article by Roselyne Min examines how humanitarian organizations are leveraging AI and space technology to transform aid delivery in dangerous environments. The report highlights three key applications: remote physical delivery, hunger prediction, and disaster mapping.

Project AHEAD, a collaboration between the World Food Programme, Germany's aerospace center DLR, the Red Cross, and technology partners, is developing remotely operated vehicles capable of carrying supplies through areas too dangerous for conventional trucks. The system draws on DLR's expertise building planetary rovers, including the MMX rover designed to explore Phobos, one of Mars's moons. Footage from a DLR test site shows a SHERP all-terrain vehicle wading into water and climbing rough terrain while an operator controls it remotely.

HungerMap Live, publicly available and developed by the WFP, uses machine learning to track food insecurity across more than 95 countries. It combines data on conflict, weather, climate hazards, and economic conditions to identify emerging crises. Bernhard Kowatsch, director of WFP's Global Accelerator, notes the platform is exploring forecasting food security 90 days into the future.

The Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team demonstrated the power of AI-assisted mapping after earthquakes struck northern Venezuela in June 2026. Using ML to extract building information from satellite imagery and the MapSwipe app, over 600 volunteers swiped through images marking damaged structures within four days. Leen D'hondt, the team's technology director, emphasizes that while manual mapping still provides the best quality, AI's speed advantage is critical in emergencies where knowing approximate building locations and population counts enables faster response.

Despite these advances, Fraunhofer Institute's Monique Kuglitsch notes that AI systems are still far from routine integration into emergency protocols worldwide, with only isolated exceptions in India and Europe. The article underscores both the potential and the gap between experimental AI tools and operational humanitarian response.
