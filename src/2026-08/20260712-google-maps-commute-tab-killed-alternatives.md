# Google Maps killed the Commute tab—here's what to use instead
**Source**: https://www.howtogeek.com/google-maps-best-feature-for-commuters-isnt-on-every-phone/
**Date**: 2026-07-12
**Author**: HowToGeek
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Commute tab, navigation, commuting, Android Auto, alternatives

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps has quietly killed its dedicated Commute tab, leaving commuters who relied on the feature scrambling for alternatives — including Android Auto, pinned trips, and third-party apps.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps removed the Commute tab, a feature introduced nearly eight years ago that provided quick access to home and work route information
- The Commute tab was one of Maps' most popular features, giving users a one-tap view of their daily drive with live traffic and ETA
- Users can still pin home and work addresses, but the streamlined single-tab experience is gone
- Alternatives include Android Auto integration, Google Assistant voice commands, and dedicated navigation apps like Waze
- The removal reflects Google's broader strategy of consolidating features and pushing users toward AI-assisted navigation

## Synthesis
Published on July 12, 2026, by HowToGeek, this article reports on Google Maps' decision to remove the Commute tab — a feature introduced nearly eight years ago that had become one of the app's most beloved tools for daily commuters. The Commute tab provided a streamlined, one-tap interface showing live traffic conditions, ETA, and alternate routes between home and work, making it indispensable for millions of users.

The removal has sparked frustration among commuters who relied on the feature's simplicity. While users can still pin home and work addresses within the app, the dedicated tab's consolidated view is no longer available. The article suggests several workarounds, including using Android Auto for in-vehicle navigation, leveraging Google Assistant voice commands to start navigation, and switching to alternative apps like Waze (also owned by Google) or third-party options.

The decision reflects Google's broader strategy of streamlining the Maps interface and pushing users toward AI-assisted navigation features, potentially integrated with Gemini. The article notes that while the feature may not have been heavily promoted in recent years, its quiet removal highlights how Google can deprecate popular functionality with little warning, leaving users to adapt to new workflows or seek alternatives in an increasingly consolidated mapping ecosystem.
