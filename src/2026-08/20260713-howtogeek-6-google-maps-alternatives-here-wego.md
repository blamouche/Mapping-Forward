# I tested 6 Google Maps alternatives—this is the one I'm sticking with
**Source**: https://www.howtogeek.com/i-tested-google-maps-alternativesthis-is-the-one-im-sticking-with/
**Date**: 2026-03-19
**Author**: Andy Betts (How-To Geek)
**Keywords**: Google Maps alternatives, navigation apps, TomTom, OsmAnd, Waze, Sygic, Organic Maps, HERE WeGo, OpenStreetMap, privacy, offline maps, Android Auto

## Elevator pitch
After testing six Google Maps alternatives — TomTom, OsmAnd, Waze, Sygic, Organic Maps, and HERE WeGo — a How-To Geek reviewer settles on HERE WeGo as the only app that matches Google Maps' feature set while offering better privacy and a cleaner interface.

## Takeaways
- TomTom, despite its heritage, is described as a "pale imitation" of its former self — basic, driver-only, with limited POI search and few options
- OsmAnd is a solid open-source option using OpenStreetMap data, but suffers from a robotic voice, basic search, cluttered menus, and requires payment for unlimited map downloads
- Waze (owned by Google) excels at crowdsourced real-time traffic but is limited to driving only, with basic maps and no satellite view
- Sygic is feature-rich (including what3words and dash cam mode) but most features are behind a paywall; it's driver-only and works offline
- HERE WeGo — originally Nokia's mapping product, now owned by a consortium of car makers — is the winner: closest to Google Maps with online/offline use, multiple map styles, multi-modal routing, business info, parking suggestions, and Android Auto support

## Synthesis
Published by How-To Geek in March 2026, this hands-on review by Andy Betts systematically tests six Google Maps alternatives across criteria including mapping quality, routing, features, privacy, and multi-modal support. The article is motivated by two factors: Google Maps' increasing clutter and bloat with unused features, and growing privacy concerns about the amount of data Google collects.

The review is candid about each app's strengths and weaknesses. TomTom, once a navigation pioneer, is found to be very basic compared to modern apps — driver-only with good maps and routing but hit-and-miss POI search. OsmAnd, the open-source OpenStreetMap-based option, offers good maps and multi-modal routing but lacks polish, with a robotic voice, basic search, and overly complex settings. Waze, while excellent for crowdsourced traffic alerts, is limited to driving and lacks satellite view. Sygic is the most feature-rich, with unique offerings like what3words integration and dash cam mode, but most useful features require a premium subscription.

Organic Maps is highlighted as the ultimate privacy choice — completely free, open-source, using OpenStreetMap data, with good maps and multi-modal routing. However, its privacy focus means no user tracking, resulting in no traffic information and poor business/POI data. It's described as a solid basic navigation tool but missing the extras users take for granted.

HERE WeGo emerges as the winner because it's the only app that has all the features the author uses daily. Originally created by Nokia and now owned by a consortium of car makers, it works online or offline with downloadable country maps, offers satellite and 3D views, comprehensive business information, multi-modal routing (driving, cycling, walking, public transport), parking suggestions, and Android Auto support. While it does track users for traffic data (with opt-out available), it's more private than Google. The author concludes: "I set out to reduce my reliance on Google without feeling like I was downgrading. With HERE WeGo, I'm not."
