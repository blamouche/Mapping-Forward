## Elevator pitch
Haropa Port's GIS team at Le Havre has transitioned to 3D GIS using Esri's ArcGIS, improving exploitation, maintenance, and administration of France's largest port complex by integrating BIM models and enterprise systems.

## Takeaways
- Haropa Port — combining Le Havre, Rouen, and Paris river port — is France's largest port, with its Le Havre GIS team using ArcGIS to manage the complex infrastructure
- The team transitioned from 2D to 3D GIS in recent years, enabling better visualisation and management of port infrastructure in perpetual evolution

## Synthesis
Published on July 13, 2026, in Les Cahiers Techniques du Bâtiment, this article by François Ploye examines how Haropa Port's GIS team at Le Havre has leveraged 3D geographic information systems to optimise the administration of France's largest port infrastructure. Haropa Port regroups the ports of Le Havre, Rouen, and the river port of Paris, making it a complex multi-site operation requiring sophisticated spatial management.

The GIS team, led by Mathieu Larue, uses Esri's ArcGIS and has progressively transitioned to 3D for its data and maps. This transition to 3D has been key to improving both exploitation and maintenance of the port infrastructure. The 3D SIG serves two major business domains: exploitation (managing port rights and client leases) and development/aménagement. The integration with SAP allows the team to compare expenses and revenue by geographic zone, providing a spatial dimension to financial analysis that would be impossible with traditional accounting systems alone.

For maintenance, the GIS is primarily interfaced with Coswin GMAO (Siveco) and other specialised maintenance management software, including railway-specific systems. A critical prerequisite is organising consistent references between the GIS and GMAO systems, ensuring that assets tracked in maintenance software correspond to the same objects in the spatial database. The system also supports BIM integration, with Revit models consultable within ArcGIS, placing building models in their geographic context — a bridge between BIM and GIS that is increasingly important for infrastructure management.
