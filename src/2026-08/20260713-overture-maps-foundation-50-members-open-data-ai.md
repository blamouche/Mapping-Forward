# Overture Maps Foundation Reaches 50 Members as Industry Converges on Open Data to Ground AI
**Source**: https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/overture-maps-foundation-reaches-50-members-as-industry-converges-on-open-data-to-ground-ai
**Date**: 2026-07-13
**Author**: Overture Maps Foundation / Linux Foundation
**Keywords**: Overture Maps Foundation, open spatial data, GERS, AI grounding, GeoParquet, open mapping, Linux Foundation, TomTom, AWS, Meta, Microsoft, Uber

## Elevator pitch
Overture Maps Foundation nearly doubles its membership to 50 organizations as industries increasingly adopt open spatial data and the Global Entity Reference System (GERS) to provide reliable, vendor-neutral ground truth for AI systems that need to reason about the physical world.

## Takeaways
- Overture Maps Foundation has reached 50 members, nearly doubling its 2024 count
- New members include Uber, Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team, Maxar, Wherobots, Grab, and UC Santa Cruz
- The Global Entity Reference System (GERS) assigns stable, unique IDs to every place, road, and boundary, enabling AI pipelines to anchor outputs to real-world entities
- The June 2026 data release refreshed "operating status" fields for US points of interest using aggregated, anonymized member signals
- Overture's data is delivered in cloud-native GeoParquet format with a machine-readable schema, purpose-built for AI-native workflows

## Synthesis
The Overture Maps Foundation's growth to 50 members signals a significant shift in how the geospatial industry is organizing around open data to solve a critical AI problem: without a shared, stable reference for physical places, large language models and AI agents risk hallucinating locations, missing real-world entities, or mislabeling places entirely. Founded in 2022 by AWS, Meta, Microsoft, and TomTom, Overture provides open base map layers complemented by GERS, which assigns every place, road, and boundary a persistent, resolvable identifier.

Executive Director Will Mortenson emphasized that AI cannot reason about the physical world without an open, verifiable foundation beneath it. GERS gives AI builders that ground truth — shared, vendor-neutral, and free from lock-in. This is driving Overture's rapid growth as organizations race to operationalize AI and need reliable spatial data to ground their systems.

The June 2026 data release demonstrated the collaborative model's value, refreshing operating status fields for US points of interest using aggregated, anonymized signals contributed by members. New members span technology (Uber, Grab, Maxar), public sector (Fresno County), academia (UC Santa Cruz), and non-profits (Humanitarian OpenStreetmap Team, Global Earthquake Model Foundation). Uber's membership is particularly notable, as the ride-hailing giant contributes real-time mobility data for open mapping used in AI and autonomous systems.

The combination of open base data, GERS unique IDs, GeoParquet cloud-native format, and a machine-readable schema makes Overture's data purpose-built for AI-native workflows, positioning it as the de facto open foundation for spatial data in the age of AI.
