# The people behind the map: How TomTom teams keep cities moving during the Football World Championship

**Date**: 2026-07-13
**Author**: TomTom Editorial Team
**Keywords**: TomTom, Football World Championship, traffic management, real-time mapping, Map Experts, Traffic Quality, AI, human judgment, mobility, host cities, road closures, fan zones

## Elevator pitch
TomTom reveals the behind-the-scenes human effort of keeping maps accurate during the Football World Championship, where 16 host venues across North America see road closures, fan zones, and shifting transport patterns requiring constant map updates that AI alone cannot manage.

## Takeaways
- During the 39-day, 104-match tournament across 16 host venues in Canada, Mexico, and the US, TomTom's Map Experts and Traffic Quality teams work continuously to keep maps accurate
- Approximately 10% of roads change every year, meaning even familiar infrastructure requires fresh verification before major events
- AI helps surface signals at scale (speed drops, congestion, possible closures from local news) but cannot understand context — human judgment determines the "why" and "so what"
- Map Experts ensure the map starts accurate; Traffic Quality helps it stay accurate as conditions change minute by minute, creating a continuous loop of preparation, monitoring, validation, and correction
- Communication quality varies by city: some share detailed fast updates, others require teams to rely on local news, social media, public webcams, or sudden traffic pattern changes

## Synthesis
Published on July 13, 2026, on the TomTom Newsroom blog, this article provides an inside look at the human and technological effort required to maintain map accuracy during the Football World Championship. With 16 host venues across Canada, Mexico, and the United States, 39 days of competition, 104 matches, and millions of fans moving through cities that temporarily stop behaving normally, the tournament presents an extreme test of real-time mapping capabilities.

The article features two team perspectives. Luca Comi from the Map Experts team focuses on foundational accuracy: airports, stadiums, transportation hubs, major POIs, and the road network connecting them. With roughly 10% of roads changing every year, even familiar infrastructure requires fresh verification — one-way systems, new ramps, updated intersections, public transportation projects, and last-minute changes. The team builds relationships with host cities to receive reliable updates directly from the source.

Christian Cruz from the Traffic Quality team describes the work as "a mix of planning ahead and responding in real time." The challenge extends far beyond stadium gates: each host city has official fan events running for the full tournament, while pop-up gatherings and watch parties create new movement patterns far from the pitch. On match days, fan zones stretch across neighbourhoods, security perimeters reshape districts, shuttle routes replace normal traffic, and downtown streets become pedestrian-only.

The article is particularly interesting for its nuanced stance on AI's role in mapping. While AI helps teams move faster by surfacing signals at scale — sudden speed drops, unusual congestion, possible closures mentioned in local news — it cannot understand context on its own. A slowdown could be normal rush hour, a last-minute fan zone setup, or construction equipment. As Luca Comi states: "AI can flag the 'what,' but humans still determine the 'why' and the 'so what.'" The partnership between Map Experts and Traffic Quality creates a continuous loop of preparation, monitoring, validation, and correction, ensuring that fans can navigate unfamiliar cities with confidence.
