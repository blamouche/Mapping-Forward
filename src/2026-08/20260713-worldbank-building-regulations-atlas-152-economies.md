# Mapping the world's building regulations: new platform for smarter reforms
**Source**: https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/mapping-the-world-s-building-regulations--new-platform-for-smart
**Date**: 2026-07-13
**Author**: Keiko Sakoda, Jayashree Srinivasan (World Bank Group)
**Keywords**: World Bank, building regulations, interactive map, urban resilience, spatial planning, GFDRR, policy benchmarking, disaster risk reduction

## Elevator pitch
The World Bank Group launches the Building Regulations Atlas, a free interactive web-based tool consolidating building regulatory data across 152 economies into a single map, enabling countries to benchmark performance and identify reform priorities.

## Takeaways
- The Building Regulations Atlas covers 152 economies with 373 indicators organised across three regulatory pillars: Spatial Planning, Building Regulations, and Compliance Mechanisms
- The platform draws on four methodologically rigorous World Bank datasets, each traceable to its original source, providing varying coverage levels clearly signalled on the map
- Building regulations are critical for urban resilience: they determine whether buildings are safe, disaster-resilient, energy-efficient, and accessible — yet the global picture has never been visible in one place before
- The Atlas serves as both a benchmarking and diagnostic tool, allowing countries to compare their regulatory framework against regional peers and identify critical reform areas in minutes rather than months
- Country data can be exported directly, feeding into existing analytical work and providing a shared, citable reference for framing technical assistance

## Synthesis
On July 13, 2026, the World Bank's Data Blog introduced the Building Regulations Atlas, a free, open, interactive web-based tool that consolidates building regulatory data across 152 economies into a single map with consistent country profiles. The platform addresses a long-standing challenge: building regulatory systems — codes, planning rules, energy standards, inspection regimes — have been studied in fragments across different reports, methodologies, and country samples, making it impossible for policymakers to see the global picture or compare their country's status against peers.

The Atlas organises 373 indicators across three regulatory pillars that mirror the building value chain. Spatial Planning covers zoning rules governing where building is permitted, accounting for land use, hazard maps, and environmental impacts. Building Regulations form the technical core, spanning structural design, fire safety, green buildings, and universal accessibility. Compliance Mechanisms ensure rules on paper are applied in practice, covering building control and inspection, professional registration, transparency, and dispute resolution.

The tool draws on four complementary datasets produced by the World Bank Group: two analytical reports from GFDRR's Building Regulation for Resilience thematic area (global and Sub-Saharan Africa regional), the Business Ready 2025 report, and the Building Green Global Dataset 2025. Coverage varies by economy, and the map transparently signals how thoroughly each country has been studied. The platform is designed to grow over time, with the potential to incorporate additional datasets and expand country coverage.

The Atlas serves a practical purpose: as a benchmarking tool, it helps countries understand their regulatory status, learn from regional peers or aspirational comparators, identify reform areas, and translate findings into actionable roadmaps. The World Bank Group positions itself ready to support countries through policy dialogues, stakeholder consultations, and technical and financial resources. The tool is particularly relevant given that the world adds floor space equivalent to a Paris-sized city every five days, mostly in countries facing steep combinations of climate risk and rapid urban growth.
