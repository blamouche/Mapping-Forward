# New Maps Show Unprecedented Cuts to Bears Ears and Grand Staircase-Escalante
**Source**: https://www.backpacker.com/news-and-events/news/new-maps-show-unprecedented-cuts-to-bears-ears-and-grand-staircase-escalante-this-is-what-were-losing/
**Date**: 2026-07-15
**Author**: Adam Roy, Backpacker
**Keywords**: Bears Ears, Grand Staircase-Escalante, national monuments, boundary maps, conservation, Trump administration, public lands, mining

## Elevator pitch
Newly released maps from conservation groups reveal the dramatic scale of boundary cuts to Bears Ears and Grand Staircase-Escalante National Monuments, showing that iconic landscapes, cultural sites, and backpacking routes now lie outside protected boundaries.

## Takeaways
- Maps from SUWA and The Wilderness Society show Bears Ears reduced by 91% and Grand Staircase-Escalante by 90%
- The cuts go far beyond the 2017 reductions, removing protection from popular canyons, cultural sites, and hiking routes
- The White House has not released official mapping data; the maps were digitized from a BLM press copy
- Mining claims from a 2018 Grand Canyon Trust map now mostly lie outside monument boundaries
- Conservation groups warn excluded lands could open to drilling and mining within 60 days

## Synthesis
Newly released maps are providing the clearest picture yet of the drastic reductions to two of Utah's most significant national monuments. Created by the Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance (SUWA) and The Wilderness Society (TWS) from maps obtained via the White House press office and BLM data, the maps show that the Trump administration's latest executive order has stripped roughly 91% of Bears Ears and 90% of Grand Staircase-Escalante from protected status — reductions that go far beyond the administration's earlier 2017 cuts.

At Bears Ears, the southern Shash Jáa Unit appears largely unchanged, but the northern Indian Creek Unit has been reduced to roughly a fifth of its previous size. Five small exclaves protect specific sites including the Moon House, Citadel, and Rig Canyon. At Grand Staircase-Escalante, the monument has been slashed to a contiguous stretch in the northeast (the Escalante Canyons Unit) plus three small areas collectively named the Kaipirowits Horizon Unit. Iconic features now outside the boundaries include Fish Canyon, Grand Gulch, Valley of the Gods, Peek-a-Boo Canyon, Spooky Gulch, Coyote Gulch, and Bull Valley Gorge.

The mapping data reveals a particularly troubling detail for conservationists: a 2018 map of active mining claims from the Grand Canyon Trust shows that the vast majority of mineral deposits now lie outside the monuments' reduced boundaries. Should the proclamation stand, excluded lands could open to mining and other extractive uses within 60 days. SUWA's legal director Steve Bloch described the cuts as having "eviscerated" the monuments, with the administration indicating it intends to manage remaining lands primarily for grazing, off-road vehicles, and resource extraction. The maps from SUWA and TWS appear to match an interactive map of the reductions shared by the State of Utah.
