# What the leaf icon on Google Maps really means (and why it confuses so many users)
**Source**: https://www.engadget.com/2209466/what-the-little-leaf-symbol-on-google-maps-means/
**Date**: 2026-07-15
**Author**: Dave Meikleham, Engadget
**Keywords**: Google Maps, eco-routing, fuel-efficient routes, leaf icon, navigation, eco-friendly

## Elevator pitch
The leaf icon on Google Maps indicates a fuel-efficient route, but many users find the eco-routing feature confusing because it can add time to journeys in exchange for marginal fuel savings.

## Takeaways
- The leaf symbol appears next to routes that Google Maps has identified as fuel-efficient
- Google Maps defaults to the most fuel-efficient route when arrival times are close to the standard route
- The algorithm considers traffic data, road types, junctions, topography, and motorway vs. local road trade-offs
- Fuel-efficient routes can take more time, which frustrates some users
- Users can disable the feature via Settings > Navigation > Prefer fuel-efficient routes

## Synthesis
Google Maps' leaf icon has become a familiar but often misunderstood feature for millions of drivers. The symbol indicates that the selected route is fuel-efficient, meaning Google's algorithm has determined it will consume less fuel than alternative paths — even if it takes slightly longer. The feature, which aligns with growing environmental awareness and regulatory pressure for eco-routing in jurisdictions like France, uses a sophisticated multi-factor analysis to determine optimal routes.

The algorithm behind the leaf icon pulls live traffic data to predict congestion patterns, while also considering road types, junction layouts, and even topography. The inclusion of elevation data is notable: charting a flatter route can save significant energy, as driving uphill requires more power than cruising on even roads. Google Maps also weighs the benefits of motorway driving — with its consistent cruising speeds — against alternative routes that might avoid congestion but involve more stops via traffic lights and roundabouts.

The feature has generated mixed reactions. Some users appreciate the environmental and cost benefits, while others find it counterintuitive that a "better" route takes more time. The key tension is that fuel efficiency and speed are not always aligned: a route that avoids stop-and-go traffic may be more fuel-efficient even if it covers more distance. Google Maps filters to keep the time gap manageable, but there is no guarantee that the eco-friendly option will not cause delays. Users who prefer speed over fuel savings can disable the feature in settings, though doing so means opting out of a system that, in many cases, delivers meaningful reductions in fuel consumption and emissions.
