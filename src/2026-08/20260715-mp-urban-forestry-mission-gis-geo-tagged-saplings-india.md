# MP To Launch ₹100-Crore Urban Forestry Mission With Geo-Tagged Saplings
**Source**: https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/bhopal/mp-to-launch-100-crore-urban-forestry-mission-with-geo-tagged-saplings
**Date**: 2026-07-15
**Author**: Staff Reporter, Free Press Journal
**Keywords**: GIS mapping, geo-tagging, QR codes, urban forestry, Madhya Pradesh, India, environmental monitoring, Amrit Harit Maha-Abhiyan

## Elevator pitch
Madhya Pradesh launches a ₹100-crore Urban Forestry Mission using GIS mapping, geo-tagging, and QR codes to monitor every planted sapling, aiming to dramatically improve survival rates that have historically been as low as 30-40% in urban areas.

## Takeaways
- Madhya Pradesh's UADD is launching a ₹100-crore Urban Forestry Mission to create theme-based urban forests over five years
- Every sapling will be monitored through geo-tagging, QR codes, and GIS mapping
- The initiative, named 'Namo Harit-Nagar Yojana', will be implemented under the 'Amrit Harit Maha-Abhiyan'
- Historical survival rates of urban saplings have been only 30-40% due to poor monitoring and maintenance
- Indore's Revati Range achieved over 90% survival in 2024 through continuous monitoring — a model for the new initiative
- Theme-based parks include Panchvati, Nakshatra Van, Aushadhi Vatika, Oxygen Park, Butterfly Park, and Yoga-Wellness Park

## Synthesis
Madhya Pradesh's Urban Administration, Development and Housing Department (UADD) is deploying GIS mapping and geo-tagging technology to address one of India's most persistent environmental challenges: the poor survival rate of urban tree-planting campaigns. The ₹100-crore Urban Forestry Mission, named 'Namo Harit-Nagar Yojana', will monitor every sapling planted during the monsoon through geo-tagging, QR codes, and GIS mapping — a technology-driven approach to improve accountability and survival rates.

The problem is well-documented: environmental expert Dr Subhash C Pandey noted that despite numerous plantation drives, poor monitoring and maintenance have limited average sapling survival in urban areas to just 30-40%. Bhopal's own 2025 "Ek Ped Maa Ke Naam 2.0" campaign achieved only about 17% of its 12-lakh target. The contrast with Indore's Revati Range, where over 12 lakh saplings planted in 2024 reportedly achieved over 90% survival through continuous monitoring, demonstrates that the problem is tractable when monitoring is taken seriously.

The mission will focus on five major cities — Bhopal, Indore, Gwalior, Jabalpur, and Ujjain — developing theme-based urban forests with walking tracks, nature trails, and public amenities. The 50:50 funding model requires urban local bodies to mobilize matching funds through public participation, NGOs, self-help groups, and CSR contributions, with up to ₹5 crore per major municipal corporation.

This initiative represents a growing trend in Indian governance: using GIS and geo-tagging not just for mapping infrastructure but for environmental monitoring and accountability, transforming tree-planting from photo-op politics into measurable, data-driven environmental action.
