# The World Cup Final: What to expect on New York's roads
**Source**: https://www.tomtom.com/newsroom/explainers-and-insights/world-cup-final-what-to-expect-on-new-yorks-roads
**Date**: 2026-07-15
**Author**: Tom Wilkinson, TomTom Newsroom
**Keywords**: TomTom, World Cup 2026, traffic predictions, MetLife Stadium, congestion data, mobility intelligence, New York

## Elevator pitch
TomTom analyzed traffic data from seven World Cup matches at MetLife Stadium to predict three traffic patterns that will shape the World Cup Final, finding that post-match congestion is more persistent and more disruptive than pre-match spikes.

## Takeaways
- The highest single congestion spike was 198.4%, recorded before Brazil vs. Morocco — travel took triple the free-flow time
- Post-match congestion averaged 108.1% vs. 87% pre-match, and was slower to dissipate
- Five of seven matches recorded higher post-match than pre-match congestion
- Kick-off time and day of the week significantly influence traffic patterns: weekends produce stronger pre-match spikes, weekdays higher post-match congestion
- TomTom published an interactive dashboard for exploring the data

## Synthesis
TomTom leveraged its traffic data infrastructure to analyze congestion patterns around seven World Cup 2026 matches held at MetLife Stadium, producing three key predictions for the World Cup Final on July 19. The analysis uses TomTom's congestion level metric — the percentage increase in travel time compared to free-flow conditions — the same methodology used in the company's annual Traffic Index report.

The first prediction is that traffic will spike two to three hours before kick-off. The highest congestion level across all seven matches was 198.4%, recorded before the Brazil vs. Morocco match, meaning spectators needed triple the normal travel time. This pre-match concentration allows traffic management teams to plan distinct phases of demand, adjusting signal timings and implementing variable speed limits proactively.

The second, and perhaps more significant prediction, is that the longest disruption will begin after the final whistle. While the sharpest spike occurred pre-match, post-match congestion was both higher on average (108.1% vs. 87%) and slower to dissipate. The highest post-match reading reached 153.2% after France vs. Senegal — the only match sharing the Final's 15:00 kick-off time. More than an hour after Norway vs. Senegal ended, congestion still stood at 125.1%. This persistence makes managing spectator departures as critical as managing arrivals, as roads, public transport, and ride-hail services all come under simultaneous pressure at the final whistle.

The third prediction is that kick-off time and day of the week significantly influence patterns. Weekend fixtures produced stronger pre-match spikes (aligned with leisure/tourism travel), while weekday matches recorded higher post-match congestion (interacting with evening rush hour). TomTom has published an interactive dashboard allowing users to explore the underlying data, including travel times, average speeds, and congestion comparisons across matches.
