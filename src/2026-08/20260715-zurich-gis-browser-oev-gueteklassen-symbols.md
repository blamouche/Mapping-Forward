## Elevator pitch
The Canton of Zürich has updated its GIS-Browser with a new map layer showing population and employment density by public transport accessibility class, plus a new symbol annotation tool for customizing maps.

## Takeaways
- New map layer "Einwohner und Beschäftigte nach ÖV-Güteklassen" combines structural data with public transport accessibility ratings
- The dataset uses hectare grid cells with population/employment data and ÖV-Güteklassen (A–F) at each cell's center point
- Users can switch between "residents" and "employees" layers and filter by transport quality class or spatial planning zone
- Cells with 0–3 residents/employees are excluded for privacy protection
- New symbol tool allows users to add custom markers to maps for marking important locations

## Synthesis
The Canton of Zürich has enhanced its GIS-Browser platform with two significant updates that demonstrate the practical value of combining spatial data with transportation accessibility metrics. The first is a new map layer titled "Einwohner und Beschäftigte nach ÖV-Güteklassen" (Residents and Employees by Public Transport Quality Classes), which answers a fundamental spatial planning question: how well are residential and employment locations served by public transport, and where do population and employment concentrate?

The map is the result of collaboration between the Canton's Office of Statistics and Data and the Office of Mobility. It combines annual structural data (population and employment counts) with the cantonal ÖV-Güteklassen — a public transport accessibility rating system specifically developed for Canton Zürich with categories A through F. The dataset consists of center points representing hectare grid cells, each containing aggregated structural data and the quality class at the cell's center. For privacy protection, cells with 0–3 residents or employees are excluded.

Users can toggle between "residents" and "employees" layers, filter by individual ÖV-Güteklassen, and filter by Handlungsräume (functional zones) as defined in the canton's spatial planning concept. This enables rapid, intuitive exploration of spatial relationships — for example, identifying areas where high population density coincides with poor transport accessibility, or where employment centers lack adequate public transport connections.

The second update adds a symbol annotation tool to the GIS-Browser's drawing capabilities. Users can now select from a variety of symbols and place them on their maps — marking important locations such as bus stops, creating understandable visual guides for colleagues, or customizing personal maps for orientation. The tool is accessible via the drawing tools panel on the right side of the GIS-Browser interface.
