# Apple Maps va afficher de la publicité, mais certaines sont déjà interdites
**Source**: https://www.mac4ever.com/divers/197141-apple-maps-va-afficher-de-la-publicite-mais-certaines-sont-deja-interdites
**Date**: 2026-07-16
**Author**: Mac4Ever
**Keywords**: Apple Maps, Apple Plans, advertising, sponsored results, local services ads, Google Maps comparison, ad policy, privacy

## Elevator pitch
Apple is preparing to launch advertising on Apple Maps in the US and Canada, publishing ad policies that notably ban home services (plumbers, electricians), bail bonds, and cryptocurrency ATMs — categories that represent significant revenue on Google Maps.

## Takeaways
- Apple Maps ads will appear in search results and the new Suggested Places module introduced with iOS 26.5
- Three categories are specifically excluded: home services, bail bond services, and cryptocurrency ATMs
- Medical services will be evaluated on a case-by-case basis
- The home services ban is a sharp contrast with Google Maps, where Local Services Ads for tradespeople are a major revenue stream
- Apple will display only one ad per search result, with clear signaling around the map pin and in suggested places
- Privacy protections: ad interaction data stays on-device, not collected by Apple or shared with third parties
- Initial launch covers US and Canada only; no timeline for Europe or other regions

## Synthesis
Apple's forthcoming launch of advertising on Apple Maps represents a significant moment in the mapping platform's commercial evolution, but with a deliberately different approach from Google. After the App Store, News, and Stocks, Apple Maps is the next service to receive sponsored content, with ads appearing in two locations: at the top of search results and in the new Suggested Places module introduced with iOS 26.5.

The most striking aspect of Apple's policy is what it excludes. Home services — plumbers, electricians, locksmiths, HVAC technicians, pest control, roofers — are entirely banned from advertising. This category is one of Google Maps' most important local advertising revenue streams through its Local Services Ads program. Apple's decision to exclude these categories appears motivated by a desire to avoid the verification complexity that Google manages through initial checks and regular audits of service providers. Bail bond services and cryptocurrency ATMs are also excluded.

Apple's approach to ad presentation is deliberately restrained: only one ad per search result, clearly identifiable with signaling around the map pin. Privacy is emphasized — ad interaction data remains on-device and is not collected by Apple or shared with third parties. Medical services will face case-by-case evaluation, and additional restricted categories include tobacco, drugs, religion, politics, and adult products.

The launch is imminent in the US and Canada, with no announced timeline for other regions. Apple's restrictive ad policy positions Apple Maps as a more curated, less commercially saturated alternative to Google Maps, where advertising has become increasingly prominent and, according to some users, harder to distinguish from organic results.
