# Where in the world is biogas? A new IEA tool maps its potential
**Source**: https://www.iea.org/commentaries/where-in-the-world-is-biogas-a-new-iea-tool-maps-its-potential
**Date**: 2026-07-16
**Author**: Peter Zeniewski, Jeanne-Marie Hays, Pierre Demey — IEA
**Keywords**: IEA, biogas, biomethane, BioGRAM, interactive map, renewable energy, spatial data, feedstock

## Elevator pitch
The International Energy Agency has launched BioGRAM, an interactive online map that provides detailed country-level estimates of global biogas and biomethane production potential, using high-resolution spatial datasets to inform investment and policy decisions.

## Takeaways
- BioGRAM is the IEA's new interactive online map of global biogas and biomethane potential
- The tool leverages high-resolution spatial datasets including GAEZ v5, GLW4, WorldPop, and the Global Human Settlement Layer
- Users can define specific areas and generate estimates of biogas potential by feedstock, alongside development cost estimates
- Country-level estimates include regional indicators such as proximity to transmission grids
- The tool is designed to inform investment decisions, support policy development, and accelerate biogas value chains

## Synthesis
The International Energy Agency has introduced a new mapping tool called BioGRAM (Biogas Resource and Assessment Model) that for the first time provides a comprehensive, spatially explicit view of global biogas and biomethane production potential. As governments increasingly seek to develop domestic energy sources and reduce dependence on fossil fuels, the lack of accessible, verified data on biogas potential has been a significant barrier to investment and policy development. BioGRAM addresses this gap by combining multiple high-resolution spatial datasets into an interactive online platform.

The tool integrates agricultural output data from FAO's GAEZ v5 and GLW4 databases, population density from WorldPop and the Global Human Settlement Layer, and wastewater treatment facility data from HydroWaste. These are combined with aggregated datasets including the OECD-FAO Agricultural Outlook and the World Bank's What a Waste 3.0. The result is a granular, location-specific assessment of biogas potential that goes far beyond previous country-level estimates. Users can define a specific geographic area and generate estimates of production potential broken down by feedstock type, along with development cost estimates.

The inclusion of proximity-to-grid data is particularly valuable for investment decisions, as the economic viability of biogas projects depends heavily on the distance to gas transmission infrastructure. By making this data freely accessible, the IEA aims to improve transparency, support policymakers in developing targeted biogas strategies, and help investors identify the most promising opportunities. The agency plans to continue updating BioGRAM with new data and analytical capabilities, and is actively inviting contributions from the research community.
