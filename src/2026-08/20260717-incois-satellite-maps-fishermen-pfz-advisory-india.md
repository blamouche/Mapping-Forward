# How satellite maps are leading fishermen to bigger catches

**Date**: 2026-07-17

## Elevator pitch
Hyderabad-based INCOIS has developed a Potential Fishing Zone advisory system using satellite data that has benefited nearly 1.4 million Indian fishermen over two decades, now evolving to provide species-specific advisories and promote sustainable fishing practices.

## Takeaways
- INCOIS's Potential Fishing Zone (PFZ) advisory uses satellite data to identify waters where fish are likely to aggregate
- Nearly 1.4 million fishermen have benefited from the system over the past two decades
- PFZ-guided fishing generates net annual benefits worth several billion dollars according to INCOIS estimates
- The system is evolving beyond catch improvement to include species-specific advisories and ecological safeguards
- The technology traces its origins to an experimental service from the late 1990s

## Synthesis
For generations, Indian fishermen read the sea by instinct, relying on experience and knowledge passed through families. Today, many head out guided by satellites orbiting hundreds of kilometers overhead. The Potential Fishing Zone (PFZ) advisory, developed by Hyderabad-based INCOIS (Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services), has transformed how nearly 1.4 million fishermen find productive fishing grounds.

What began as an experimental service in the late 1990s has evolved into a sophisticated forecasting system that uses satellite remote sensing to identify oceanographic features where fish are likely to aggregate — areas where temperature gradients, chlorophyll concentrations, and other indicators signal productive waters. A recent INCOIS-led assessment estimates that PFZ-guided fishing generates net annual benefits worth several billion dollars.

The system is now being redesigned to address sustainability concerns. Rather than simply maximizing catches, the next generation of advisories will include species-specific guidance and ecological safeguards to protect the very fish stocks the technology helps exploit. This evolution reflects a broader shift in how satellite-based mapping and oceanographic remote sensing are being applied not just for economic efficiency but for long-term ecological stewardship.

The PFZ advisory represents one of the most successful practical applications of satellite mapping technology for direct human benefit in the developing world, demonstrating how remote sensing data, when translated into actionable advisories, can transform traditional livelihoods at massive scale.
