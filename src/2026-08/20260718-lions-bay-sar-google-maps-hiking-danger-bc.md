# Lions Bay search-and-rescuers warn against dangers of Google Maps
**Source**: https://vancouversun.com/news/lions-bay-search-and-rescuers-warn-against-dangers-of-google-maps
**Date**: 2026-07-18
**Author**: Vancouver Sun
**Keywords**: Google Maps, hiking safety, Lions Bay SAR, Howe Sound Crest Trail, backcountry navigation, trail mapping, AllTrails, search and rescue

## Elevator pitch
Lions Bay Search and Rescue issued a warning after rescuing hikers who used Google Maps directions for the Howe Sound Crest Trail, which estimated 5 hours for a hike that typically takes experienced hikers 12-14 hours, underscoring the dangers of using road-navigation apps for backcountry terrain.

## Takeaways
- Hikers were rescued after using printed Google Maps directions for the Howe Sound Crest Trail
- Google Maps estimated 5 hours 13 minutes for a hike that takes elite trail runners ~9 hours and experienced hikers 12-14 hours
- Google Maps does not account for alpine terrain, route finding, scrambling sections, loose rock, and elevation changes
- Lions Bay SAR emphasizes that Google Maps was designed for roads and city streets, not mountain terrain
- Recommended alternatives include AllTrails, Fatmap/Gaia GPS, and Avenza Maps for backcountry navigation

## Synthesis
Lions Bay Search and Rescue's warning about Google Maps for backcountry navigation highlights a persistent and dangerous gap between road-oriented mapping applications and the realities of wilderness terrain. The incident involved hikers who planned their trip using printed Google Maps directions that estimated the Howe Sound Crest Trail at 5 hours 13 minutes — wildly optimistic for a route that even elite trail runners typically complete in about 9 hours and experienced hikers tackle in 12 to 14 hours, often splitting across multiple days.

The core problem is that Google Maps' routing algorithm is designed for roads and city streets, not mountain terrain. It does not account for the 1,300+ meters of elevation gain, alpine terrain, route-finding challenges, scrambling sections, loose rock, and the countless ups-and-downs that characterize the Howe Sound Crest Trail. As Lions Bay SAR noted, "when your planning tool gets the fundamentals wrong, your entire trip is built on a false assumption" — affecting everything from departure time to food and water allocation.

This is the second time in July 2026 that Lions Bay SAR has issued a reminder about underprepared hikers on the HSCT. The group recommends purpose-built backcountry navigation tools: AllTrails for trail maps and crowdsourced difficulty information, Fatmap (developed in Squamish, BC) for backcountry skiing and hiking, Gaia GPS for advanced topographic maps, and Avenza Maps for professional-grade offline topographic data.

The warning underscores a broader issue: as consumer mapping apps like Google Maps become ubiquitous, users increasingly apply them to use cases far beyond their designed scope, with potentially life-threatening consequences in wilderness settings.
