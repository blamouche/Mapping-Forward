# Waze Vs. Google Maps: Which App Is Best For Older Drivers?
**Source**: https://www.bgr.com/2215773/waze-vs-google-maps-which-app-best-older-drivers/
**Date**: 2026-07-19
**Author**: Chris Smith, BGR
**Keywords**: Waze, Google Maps, older drivers, senior drivers, navigation apps, Gemini AI, Android Auto, CarPlay, accessibility, user interface

## Elevator pitch
BGR compares Waze and Google Maps for older drivers, concluding that Google Maps' simpler interface, Gemini voice interactions, and broader transit features make it the safer choice for most seniors, while tech-savvy older users may still benefit from Waze for daily commuting.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps offers a less cluttered, more predictable interface better suited for most older drivers
- Waze's real-time incident reports can be confusing, distracting, or overwhelming for seniors with cognitive or sensory age-related changes
- Gemini-powered natural language voice control in Google Maps benefits older drivers who may struggle with touchscreen input
- Tech-savvy seniors who have used smartphones for years can effectively use both apps, switching to Waze for traffic avoidance on familiar routes
- Google Maps' support for public transit, walking, and cycling directions gives it broader utility beyond driving

## Takeaways (cont.)
- The article recommends installing both apps and using each for its strengths rather than choosing one permanently

## Synthesis
BGR's Chris Smith examines the relative merits of Google Maps and Waze for older drivers, a demographic where age-related changes in vision, hearing, and cognition can significantly impact the usability of navigation applications. The analysis concludes that Google Maps is the better default choice for most senior drivers, primarily due to its less cluttered interface, predictable main-road routing, and broader feature set that extends beyond driving to include public transit, walking, and cycling directions.

Waze's strengths — real-time community-generated incident reports, aggressive traffic avoidance rerouting, and fast point-to-point navigation — can paradoxically become liabilities for older drivers. The unpredictable stream of accident, police, and roadwork alerts may confuse or overwhelm seniors, diverting attention from driving to interpreting the app's interface. When Waze reroutes a driver to an unfamiliar road, an aging motorist may experience anxiety about getting lost rather than appreciating the time savings. Google Maps' recently integrated Gemini AI offers a compensating advantage: natural language voice interactions allow older drivers to control navigation, alter routes, and find places of interest without needing to interact with a touchscreen display — a meaningful accessibility improvement for users with dexterity or vision limitations.

The article identifies an important exception: tech-savvy seniors who have used smartphones for years can navigate both apps effectively. For these users, Waze remains valuable for daily commuting on familiar routes where incident reports and traffic avoidance provide genuine benefits. The recommendation is pragmatic: install both apps and use each according to context — Google Maps for general navigation and city exploration, Waze for traffic-sensitive road trips. This dual-app approach maximises utility while acknowledging that no single navigation app perfectly serves all use cases and user profiles.
