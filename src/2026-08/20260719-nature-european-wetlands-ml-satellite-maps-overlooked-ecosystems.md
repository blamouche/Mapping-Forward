## Synthesis
A study featured in Nature's News & Views section describes how researchers have used machine-learning algorithms to analyse satellite imagery and produce detailed, high-resolution maps of European wetland environments. These maps reveal previously overlooked patches of wetland ecosystems that had been missed by earlier, lower-resolution mapping efforts, providing conservationists and policymakers with a more complete picture of these critical and threatened habitats.

Wetlands are among Europe's most biodiverse and most endangered ecosystems, providing essential services including water filtration, flood mitigation, carbon sequestration, and habitat for numerous species. However, they have historically been difficult to map at scale due to their variable size, seasonal dynamics, and the limitations of traditional survey methods. The application of machine learning to satellite imagery represents a significant methodological advance, enabling automated identification and classification of wetland features across large geographic areas with a level of detail that was previously unattainable.

The high-resolution maps produced through this approach are described as a boon for conservation efforts, as they allow for more accurate assessment of wetland extent, connectivity, and degradation. By revealing overlooked wetland patches, the maps can inform targeted conservation actions and help identify priority areas for restoration. The study underscores the growing importance of AI-powered remote sensing in environmental monitoring and the potential for similar approaches to be applied to other ecosystem types across the globe.
