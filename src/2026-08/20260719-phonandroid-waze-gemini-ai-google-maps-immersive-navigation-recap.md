## Elevator pitch

## Takeaways
- Waze is integrating Google's Gemini AI to analyse driving habits and adapt future routes to user preferences
- The Gemini-powered personalisation in Waze is fully optional and can be disabled by users
- Waze's voice command system has been improved to handle more varied situation reports and vaguer destination descriptions
- An AI-optimised Motorcycle mode has been added to Waze with road condition alerts, though initially limited to select countries
- Google Maps' Immersive Navigation, announced in spring 2026, is slowly rolling out to Android Auto with detailed 3D route visualisation

## Synthesis
Phonandroid's weekly technology recap, written by Clio Leonard, highlights two significant developments in the navigation app ecosystem. The first is Waze's accelerated integration of Google's Gemini AI, which now powers route calculation by memorising users' driving habits and creating tailored itineraries adapted to individual preferences. The AI personalisation feature is designed to be non-intrusive — it can be fully deactivated by users who prefer traditional routing. Beyond route personalisation, Gemini enhances Waze's voice command capabilities, allowing drivers to report a wider variety of road situations orally and to search for destinations using more natural, less precise descriptions. A new AI-optimised Motorcycle mode has also been introduced, featuring specific alerts about road conditions, though the feature is initially restricted to a limited number of countries.

The second major development is the gradual deployment of Google Maps' Immersive Navigation to Android Auto. First announced in spring 2026, the feature was initially available only to CarPlay users. The new interface offers highly detailed 3D route visualisation, modelling the trajectory with enhanced environmental context. The slow rollout to Android Auto suggests Google is taking a phased approach to ensure the feature performs adequately across the diverse Android Auto ecosystem, which spans many vehicle manufacturers and head unit configurations.

The broader trend captured in this recap is the deepening integration of generative AI into both of Google's navigation properties. Waze, acquired by Google in 2013, has historically maintained a distinct product identity focused on community-driven traffic alerts and fast routing. The introduction of Gemini into Waze's core routing engine represents a significant convergence with Google Maps' AI-powered features, while the two apps continue to target different user segments — Waze for traffic-sensitive drivers and Google Maps for broader navigation and exploration use cases.
