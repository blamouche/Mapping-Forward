## Synthesis
On July 20, 2026, Phys.org reported on a new open-access web tool called PACA (Paleocoordinates Calculator) developed by the BIOST3 Research Group at the University of Barcelona's Faculty of Biology. The tool, presented in Scientific Reports, addresses a long-standing challenge in paleontology and related disciplines: calculating a fossil's geographic position throughout geological history, a process that traditionally required programming skills or complex specialized software.

PACA transforms current geographic positions into paleocoordinates using state-of-the-art plate tectonics models. The interface is remarkably simple: users upload a CSV file containing current coordinates and the geological age of the find, and the tool returns the exact paleocoordinates in seconds. This accessibility removes the methodological barrier that has prevented many researchers and educators from accessing high-quality paleogeographic reconstructions.

The mathematical core of PACA is built on the R package palaeoverse, which connects directly to the GPlates web service—a widely used platform for plate tectonics reconstruction. The tool allows users to compare their data simultaneously with up to five global plate models (GPM) that are widely used by the scientific community: PALEOMAP, GOLONKA, MERDITH2021, TorsvikCocks2017, and MATTHEWS. This multi-model capability enables researchers to assess the sensitivity of their paleogeographic reconstructions to different tectonic frameworks.

Professor Antonio Monleón-Getino, head of the BIOST3 Research Group and member of the Bioinformatics Barcelona (BIB) platform, explained that the tool removes methodological barriers by enabling anyone, regardless of computing background, to trace locations back through geological time. The authors—Noa Scholz-Murcia, Alejandro Rodríguez-Mena, Víctor Madarnás-Gómez, and Monleón-Getino—designed PACA in line with open science principles.

Both the PACA source code and the 3D conversion scripts are publicly available on the Zenodo repository, promoting transparency and reproducibility in Earth sciences research. The tool offers an efficient way to process large volumes of data, making it valuable for both research and education in paleontology, biogeography, and related fields that require understanding of Earth's ancient geography.
