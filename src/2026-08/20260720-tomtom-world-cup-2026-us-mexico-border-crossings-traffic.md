# Football World Championship 2026 Puts Pressure on US-Mexico Border Crossings
**Source**: https://www.webwire.com/ViewPressRel.asp?aId=357891
**Date**: 2026-07-20
**Author**: TomTom / WebWire
**Keywords**: TomTom, traffic data, US-Mexico border, Football World Championship 2026, border crossings, Otay Mesa, Calexico, congestion, mobility intelligence, defense and government

## Elevator pitch
TomTom traffic data reveals that the Football World Championship 2026 caused a 15.6% year-over-year increase in congestion across six major US-Mexico border crossings, with Otay Mesa surging 37.3% as fan movements, team logistics, and regional activity reshaped mobility patterns far from match venues.

## Takeaways
- Aggregate border congestion rose 15.6% YoY across six crossings in June 2026, with 16.9% increase during the June 11-30 tournament window
- Otay Mesa averaged 37.3% YoY growth during the tournament, spiking to 66% on Sundays versus the 2025 baseline
- Calexico jumped 28.6% YoY, with Sunday congestion doubling (+106.6%), the largest weekday shift in the dataset
- Sunday, historically the quietest border day, started behaving like a Wednesday due to match schedules
- TomTom positions the data as demonstrating the value of location-based intelligence for mobility planners and border authorities

## Synthesis
TomTom has released proprietary traffic data analysis revealing how the Football World Championship 2026, hosted across the United States, Mexico, and Canada, significantly reshaped mobility patterns at US-Mexico land border crossings. The study compared traffic conditions during the tournament's opening weeks with the same period in 2025 across six major gateways: Calexico, Otay Mesa, San Ysidro, Santa Teresa, El Paso, and Laredo.

The aggregate picture shows a 15.6% year-over-year increase in border congestion during June 2026, accelerating to 16.9% during the June 11-30 tournament window. Otay Mesa, the Tijuana-San Diego commercial gateway and closest major crossing to Estadio Caliente and Iran's team base camp, emerged as the clearest hotspot with a 37.3% YoY daily average congestion increase. The crossing recorded its highest June congestion on record, with daily averages climbing from 42.5% to 58.4%. The Belgium vs Iran match on Sunday, June 21, triggered a 54% jump in Sunday congestion at Otay Mesa, which typically sits in the low 30s.

Calexico, approximately 110 miles east of Tijuana without a nearby host city, provided perhaps the clearest signal of broader tournament-driven travel demand. Its 28.6% YoY increase and 106.6% Sunday surge — the largest weekday shift in the dataset — demonstrated that the tournament's mobility impact extended far beyond host-city boundaries. Notably, Calexico's congestion remained elevated even after Iran's camp disbanded, suggesting a sustained, non-team-specific tournament tourism effect.

TomTom frames the analysis as evidence that global sporting events can serve as temporary stress tests for border mobility systems, underlining the need for timely location-based intelligence to anticipate demand surges, allocate resources, and maintain operational resilience. The company positions its Orbis Global Basemap and traffic data solutions as decision-critical tools for defense, government, and C4ISR/GEOINT applications.
