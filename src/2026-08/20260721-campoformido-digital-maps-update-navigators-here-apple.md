## Synthesis
The municipality of Campoformido, in the Friuli-Venezia Giulia region of Italy, has launched an unprecedented initiative to address the growing problem of heavy truck traffic being misrouted through residential areas by digital navigation systems. The town's administration has formally contacted the world's leading mapping and navigation providers — Google Maps, Waze, TomTom, HERE Maps, and Apple Maps — requesting that they verify and update road data for the centre of Basaldella, a frazione where permanent restrictions on heavy vehicle traffic are in place but consistently ignored by truck drivers following GPS directions.

Mayor Massimiliano Petri explained that the initiative grew out of a recurring experience: drivers stopped by local police repeatedly claim "the navigator brought me here." This observation led the administration to recognise that navigation technology now influences driving behaviour more than traditional cartography or road signage. The municipality submitted its requests through both official map update channels and institutional communications, accompanied by photographs of the existing road signage and an offer to provide any additional information needed for database corrections.

The problem is not unique to Campoformido. Many Italian towns face similar situations where heavy traffic is directed through residential centres rather than along more appropriate routes. Petri emphasised that the initiative is not about finding a responsible party but about finding a concrete solution, noting that permanent police presence at every town entrance is impractical, especially at night. The mayor described digital maps as "a true infrastructure" that guides millions of routing decisions daily, meaning that even a simple digital error can become a real-world problem for an entire community. The municipality plans to monitor the evolution of its requests and assess the impact on heavy traffic in Basaldella over the coming months.
