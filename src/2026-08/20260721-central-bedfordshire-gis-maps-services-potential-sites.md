## Elevator pitch
Central Bedfordshire Council used digital mapping to analyze local services and infrastructure capacity across all land parcels, creating an infrastructure opportunity score that enables proactive site identification for development beyond the traditional call-for-sites process.

## Synthesis
On July 21, 2026, the UK Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government published a case study on how Central Bedfordshire Council used digital mapping to transform its approach to identifying potential development sites. The pilot project, funded through the PropTech Innovation Fund round 4 in 2024, tested a digital approach to assessing submitted sites and identifying additional development opportunities using the PlaceMaker platform.

The planning challenge was significant. During the previous local plan process, infrastructure had been a key concern for residents, ward members, and town and parish councils. The council wanted infrastructure mitigation considered earlier in plan-making, but at the start of the new local plan, it lacked a clear view of infrastructure capacity across the area. This made it difficult to judge whether the call-for-sites process reflected areas where infrastructure capacity actually existed. The council also wanted to reduce reliance on sites submitted through the call for sites alone and to identify potential sites that landowners had not put forward.

The solution involved using PlaceMaker to assess all land parcels across the authority based on access to local services and infrastructure capacity. The council developed an Infrastructure Opportunity Score (IOS) that could be applied to every Land Registry parcel in Central Bedfordshire. The score combined information on the presence of local services including public transport, education, health, retail and services, employment, community facilities, and leisure and open space. Access to infrastructure was assessed using walking or driving catchments, depending on the service or facility, with specialist officers helping develop and refine weightings and scores.

As part of the pilot, the council also used the platform to run a brownfield call for sites and a subsequent greenfield call for sites, creating an evidence base to support proactive site identification and assessment. This approach represented a shift from reactive—waiting for landowners to submit sites—to proactive, using digital mapping to identify suitable locations based on infrastructure capacity and service access.

The case study demonstrates how GIS-based tools can improve transparency in how infrastructure informs site identification and assessment, addressing a key concern in local planning processes. By making infrastructure data spatially explicit and scored, the council could better communicate to stakeholders why certain sites are preferred and what mitigation might be needed.
