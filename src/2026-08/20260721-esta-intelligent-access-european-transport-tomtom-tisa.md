## Elevator pitch
The European association of abnormal road transport and mobile cranes (ESTA) has endorsed the use of Intelligent Access systems in the European transport sector, joined by representatives from TomTom/TISA, the Dutch ministry of infrastructure, the European Commission's DG Move, and POLIS to push for regulatory reforms and investment.

## Synthesis
The European association of abnormal road transport and mobile cranes (ESTA) has formally endorsed the adoption of Intelligent Access systems in the European transport sector, marking a significant step toward digital integration of oversized and overweight vehicle routing across the continent. The announcement was accompanied by a photograph showing key stakeholders: Ana Pereira de Miranda from the European Commission's DG Move, Arjan van Vliet from the Dutch ministry of infrastructure, İffet Türken from ESTA, Marcel Otto from Rijkswaterstaat, Ivo Cré from POLIS, and Stephanie Leonard representing TomTom and TISA (Traveller Information Services Association).

Intelligent Access systems use digital map data, real-time traffic information, and vehicle-specific dimension and weight data to enable compliant routing for abnormal transport vehicles. Rather than relying on static permit systems and manual route planning, Intelligent Access allows transport operators to dynamically identify viable routes that accommodate their vehicles' specific characteristics while respecting infrastructure constraints such as bridge load limits, height restrictions, and weight-limited roads.

The breadth of stakeholders involved — spanning an industry association (ESTA), national government (Dutch ministry), EU institutions (DG Move), city networks (POLIS), and mapping/traffic data providers (TomTom/TISA) — reflects the cross-jurisdictional nature of the challenge. Abnormal transport in Europe must navigate varying national regulations, infrastructure standards, and permit procedures. ESTA's endorsement is strategically aimed at applying pressure on regulators and politicians to back reforms and investment in digital infrastructure that would streamline the process, reduce delays, and improve safety for both abnormal transport operators and other road users.
