# Liberaware and Pasco Agree on Joint GIS-Based Sewer Infrastructure Inspection Using IBIS Drone Data
**Source**: https://digital-construction.jp/news/3785
**Date**: 2026-07-21
**Author**: デジコン編集部 (Digital Construction)
**Keywords**: Liberaware, Pasco, GIS, IBIS drone, sewer inspection, No Entry inspection, infrastructure maintenance, spatial information, Japan, drone survey

## Elevator pitch
Japanese drone maker Liberaware and spatial information firm Pasco have signed a basic agreement to jointly develop GIS-integrated inspection solutions for sewer infrastructure, combining the IBIS micro-drone's imaging data with Pasco's GIS platform to enable "No Entry" inspections of confined spaces.

## Takeaways
- Liberaware and Pasco signed a basic agreement for joint verification of sewer pipe inspection using drones and GIS
- The collaboration covers three areas: No Entry inspection advancement, IBIS data GIS integration, and maintenance planning utilisation
- Pasco provides the GIS platform and municipal submission formatting; Liberaware handles IBIS drone operations and image capture
- The solution targets Japan's aging infrastructure and workforce shortages in confined-space inspections
- Future expansion planned for bridges, tunnels, plants, and port facilities

## Synthesis
Liberaware, a Japanese developer of compact drones for confined-space inspection, and Pasco, a spatial information and GIS specialist, have signed a basic agreement to collaborate on advancing sewer infrastructure inspection through the integration of drone imaging and GIS technology. The partnership addresses two pressing challenges in Japanese infrastructure management: the accelerating deterioration of facilities built during the high-growth era, and the severe workforce and fiscal constraints facing municipalities responsible for maintenance.

The collaboration focuses on three key areas. First, the advancement and efficiency of "No Entry" inspections — a concept promoted by Japan's Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT) — for sewer pipes and other narrow, enclosed spaces where human entry is hazardous due to toxic gases, oxygen deficiency, high temperatures, and bacterial exposure. Second, the assignment of positional information to data captured by Liberaware's IBIS series micro-drones and its integration with Pasco's GIS platform, enabling structured management of inspection results. Third, the development of methodologies for using inspection data in screening, condition assessment, and maintenance planning.

The role division is clear: Pasco provides the GIS platform, manages inspection result integration, handles mapping and municipal submission formatting, and develops inspection specifications and screening methodologies. Liberaware operates the IBIS drones and successor/derivative models for image data capture in confined spaces, provides piloting expertise and operational know-how, and handles data transmission to Pasco's GIS system. Both companies plan to extend the solution beyond sewer infrastructure to buildings, tunnels, bridges, plant facilities, and port installations, building a comprehensive "No Entry inspection solution" powered by drone and spatial information technology.
