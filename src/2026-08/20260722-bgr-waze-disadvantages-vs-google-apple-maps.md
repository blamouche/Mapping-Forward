# 5 Disadvantages Of Using Waze Over Google Maps Or Apple Maps
**Source**: https://www.bgr.com/2211550/disadvantages-using-waze-navigation/
**Date**: 2026-07-22
**Author**: Chris Smith / BGR
**Keywords**: Waze, Google Maps, Apple Maps, navigation apps, offline maps, public transit, walking directions, city exploration, AI integration, Gemini

## Elevator pitch
Despite its popularity for community-driven traffic alerts, Waze lacks five critical features that Google Maps and Apple Maps offer: offline maps, public transit navigation, walking and biking directions, city exploration tools, and AI-assisted navigation.

## Takeaways
- Waze does not support offline maps, making it unusable in areas with poor connectivity or when traveling abroad without data, while both Google Maps and Apple Maps allow downloading maps for offline navigation
- Public transit navigation is entirely absent from Waze, as community-driven traffic rerouting has limited utility for passengers on fixed-route buses or trams, whereas Google Maps excels at multi-modal transit planning
- Walking and biking directions are not available in Waze, while Google Maps offers cycling-friendly routes with bike path overlays and Apple Maps supports cycling directions with elevation and traffic considerations
- City exploration and discovery features are minimal in Waze compared to the rich place discovery, reviews, photos, street-level imagery (Street View/Look Around), and 3D experiences (Immersive View/Flyover) in Google Maps and Apple Maps
- Google Maps' Gemini AI integration enables natural-language search, route modification, and AI-assisted navigation features that Waze lacks entirely, with no comparable AI experience on the horizon for Waze

## Synthesis
On July 22, 2026, BGR published a comprehensive comparison highlighting five key disadvantages of using Waze as a sole navigation app compared to Google Maps and Apple Maps. While Google owns both Waze and Google Maps, the two apps serve fundamentally different purposes: Waze offers a bare-bones, community-driven driving navigation experience, while Google Maps and Apple Maps provide richer, multi-modal navigation and discovery platforms.

The first major gap is offline maps support. Waze requires an active cellular connection and GPS to function, making it unsuitable for travel abroad or in areas with spotty coverage. Both Google Maps and Apple Maps allow users to download maps for offline use, with Apple Maps even supporting offline public transit, walking, and cycling navigation. This limitation is particularly significant because it also means Waze's core feature—real-time incident reports—is inherently dependent on connectivity.

Public transit navigation is the second area where Waze falls short. The app offers no support for buses, trams, or other public transportation, as community-driven traffic rerouting has limited utility for passengers on fixed routes. Google Maps provides real-time transit updates, including how busy services are and which transit cars to board for faster exits, while Apple Maps offers transit schedules and directions in supported regions.

Walking and biking directions represent the third disadvantage. Waze is exclusively designed for driving, while both Google Maps and Apple Maps offer walking directions with obstacle information, cycling routes with bike path data, and AR-enhanced navigation. Google Maps' Lens in Maps feature provides augmented reality walking directions, and Apple Maps supports hiking routes and custom walking routes.

City exploration and discovery is the fourth area. Waze's simple design caters to driving rather than exploration. Google Maps and Apple Maps offer detailed place information, reviews, photos, and street-level imagery—Google's Street View and Apple's Look Around—plus immersive 3D experiences that help users understand a location before visiting.

Finally, AI integration represents a growing gap. Google Maps features Gemini-powered AI assistance for natural-language search, route modification, and in-app help. Apple Maps is evolving its Siri integration. Waze has no comparable AI experience, and its community-dependent model means functionality is limited in areas with small user bases.
