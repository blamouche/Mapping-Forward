## Synthesis
On July 22, 2026, Esri published reflections from its 2026 User Conference in San Diego, focusing on the intersection of AI, GIS, and the mining industry. The conference, held July 13-17, brought together GIS practitioners from around the world, with several themes consistently emerging around AI, geospatial technology, and mining.

The relationship between AI and GIS was the dominant theme. Jack Dangermond and Esri's leadership team articulated a clear message: "GIS enables AI and AI enhances GIS." The shift in the GIS community's approach to AI was notable—organizations have moved from evaluating risks and wondering about AI's impact to actively implementing solutions. A refreshingly realistic discussion emerged about the future workforce: while some jobs will be automated, new opportunities are emerging, and demand for GIS professionals who understand geography, data quality, and business context continues to grow.

A critical lesson repeated throughout the conference was that "AI is only as good as the data behind it." Mining companies are actively seeking ways to implement AI, but data readiness remains one of the largest hurdles. Data cleanup, governance, integration, and standardization are essential prerequisites that organizations must invest in before AI can deliver returns.

One of the conference's standout moments was Jack's simple statement: "GIS is a Digital Twin." Rather than viewing digital twins as something organizations need to build, Esri's perspective is that many already possess digital twin foundations through their GIS. The conference showcased examples combining spatial data, operational systems, sensor feeds, imagery, and 3D content into living representations of environments.

The Mining Track featured practical examples across several themes. Stantec's Alex Harrison demonstrated GeoAI transforming reclamation workflows, using deep learning models to automate tree counts for bond release and monitor landslide conditions. Vale's Veronica Silva showcased a GIS-enabled environmental monitoring platform integrating field inspections, sensor networks, and predictive analysis. Skeena Gold + Silver demonstrated drone imagery and 3D visualization for near real-time project awareness, while Teck's MetaTwin platform combined geoscience and engineering data into a VR-enabled environment. Freeport-McMoRan received a 2026 Special Achievement in GIS Award for their Townsite Information & Assessment System, which reduced information retrieval from days to seconds.

A new Esri Mining User Group webpage was launched to foster community connections, featuring newsletters, customer spotlights, datasets, and community-driven content for mining GIS professionals.
