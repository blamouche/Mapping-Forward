# Google Maps déraille sur Android Auto, les utilisateurs réclament une mise à jour urgente
**Source**: https://www.autoplus.fr/actualite/insolite/google-maps-deraille-sur-android-auto-les-utilisateurs-reclament-une-mise-a-jour-urgente-1457275.html
**Date**: 2026-07-22
**Author**: Etienne Villaret / Auto Plus
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Android Auto, traffic bug, grey roads, color-coded traffic, workaround, Android Auto 17.3 beta, navigation

## Elevator pitch
Since mid-July 2026, Google Maps on Android Auto has been displaying all roads in grey instead of showing color-coded traffic information, with users discovering a workaround by switching to "North Up" orientation while awaiting an official fix.

## Takeaways
- The bug only affects Google Maps on Android Auto: traffic colors disappear on the car screen while functioning normally on the smartphone
- Roads appear entirely grey even during peak hours, despite traffic data working correctly on the phone's Google Maps app
- Standard troubleshooting—clearing cache, reinstalling Android Auto, rebooting—does not resolve the issue, pointing to a software-side bug
- A workaround exists: switching the map to "North Up" orientation (fixing north rather than following the vehicle) causes traffic colors to reappear on the car display
- The bug may be linked to Android Auto's major ongoing redesign including immersive navigation, 3D views, and interface overhaul, with version 17.3 in beta and stable expected by month's end

## Synthesis
On July 22, 2026, Auto Plus reported a growing bug affecting Google Maps on Android Auto that has left drivers without color-coded traffic information since mid-July. The issue manifests as all roads appearing grey on the car's infotainment screen, even in typically congested areas during peak hours, while the same traffic data displays normally on the smartphone version of Google Maps.

The bug specifically targets the traffic overlay layer on Android Auto—the green, orange, and red route segments that indicate traffic density. Without these visual indicators, drivers who rely on at-a-glance traffic assessment before choosing a route are left without a key navigation tool. The problem appears to be software-related rather than a connectivity or vehicle issue, as standard troubleshooting steps including clearing the cache, reinstalling Android Auto, and rebooting the phone and car system all fail to resolve it.

Reddit users have documented the extent of the problem. One driver reported: "I suddenly started having the same problem. Everything works perfectly on my Pixel but traffic doesn't display on AA in my RAV4 or in my wife's Forester. I tried clearing the cache and even reinstalled AA on my phone. Really annoying." The bug affects various device and vehicle combinations, though some users with Pixel 10 Pro XL running Android Auto 17.3 beta report normal display.

A workaround has been discovered by affected users: switching the map orientation to "North Up"—which fixes the map to show north rather than rotating to follow the vehicle's heading—causes the color-coded traffic roads to reappear immediately on the car screen. This suggests the bug is related to the map rendering engine's handling of the traffic layer in the default vehicle-following orientation mode.

The bug emerges against the backdrop of significant changes to Android Auto and Google Maps, including more immersive navigation, new 3D views, interface redesign, and the announced arrival of widgets and video applications. Android Auto version 17.3 is currently in beta with a stable release expected by the end of July. Several users speculate that this major visual overhaul may have introduced the traffic display bug, though Google has not acknowledged the issue or listed it on the official "Android Auto Known Issues" page updated in early July.
