## Synthesis
On July 22, 2026, the Las Vegas Optic reported that a GIS map documenting the 2022 Hermit's Peak/Calf Canyon (HPCC) Fire and its aftermath received international recognition at the Esri User Conference in San Diego, California. The map, designed by Dana Heusinkveld, a GIS research specialist with the New Mexico Forest and Watershed Restoration Institute (NMFWRI) based at Highlands University, was awarded first place in the Place Perception category of the conference's map gallery competition.

The HPCC Fire was one of the most devastating wildfires in New Mexico history, burning more than 340,000 acres in northern New Mexico, destroying hundreds of houses and other buildings, and triggering damaging post-fire floods. Heusinkveld's map combined cartographic design with text describing the events leading up to and following the wildfire, creating a visual narrative of the disaster's progression and consequences.

The Esri User Conference map gallery attracted over 600 entries from around the globe across a dozen categories. In their comments, the Esri judges noted that "this map delivers a powerful sense of progression through its skillful use of color and composition. The growth of the fire unfolds naturally across the page while the chart colors are beautifully coordinated with the map, reinforcing the story and creating a unified design." This recognition highlights how effective cartographic design can communicate complex environmental narratives to broad audiences.

Beyond the award, NMFWRI's GIS team—comprising program manager Patti Dappen, Katie Withnall, and Heusinkveld—presented to more than 100 GIS colleagues at the conference on two initiatives. The first was the HPCC Post-fire Resource Hub, a platform for organizing post-fire recovery resources. The second was ReSHAPE, a program and online tool designed to collect and analyze data on fuel treatment projects and wildfires at the national level. ReSHAPE is a joint effort under the Southwest Ecological Restoration Institutes, of which NMFWRI is a member, and is accessible at reshapewildfire.org.

The award underscores the growing role of GIS in wildfire response, recovery, and communication, demonstrating how maps can serve not only as technical tools but also as powerful storytelling instruments that convey the human and environmental toll of natural disasters.
