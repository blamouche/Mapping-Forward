## Synthesis
On July 22, 2026, L'Express Maurice reported on the launch of a professional Street View 360° imagery campaign on Google Maps for Mauritius. Until this initiative, Mauritius was among the rare tourist destinations worldwide that lacked professional Street View coverage on Google Maps—a gap that limited the country's digital presence at a time when travelers increasingly rely on online tools to prepare trips and choose itineraries.

The project is led by World Travel in 360, a certified Google partner for Street View image collection, with logistical support and authorization facilitation from the Mauritius Tourism Promotion Authority (MTPA). The official mapping campaign began on July 19 and will run through August 21. Four specially equipped vehicles mounted with ultra-high-definition 360° cameras are traversing the island to capture imagery of the public road network, major tourist attractions, historical sites, and select coastal routes. Once processed and integrated into Google Maps and Google Earth, these images will allow users worldwide to virtually explore Mauritius.

For tourism professionals, the platform represents a new promotional tool. Hotels, guesthouses, restaurants, cultural sites, and other operators stand to gain visibility as potential visitors can explore their surroundings before traveling. The technology also enables travelers to better prepare their trips, explore points of interest, and get a more precise preview of destinations.

Benoît Harter, director of the MTPA, emphasized that "the journey begins long before boarding a plane. Today, people explore destinations online, compare experiences, and seek reassurance before making a decision. Street View allows Mauritius to present itself as it truly is—beautiful, welcoming, authentic, and easily accessible."

Beyond tourism, the imagery data has broader applications. Road users will benefit from better visual references for navigation and route assessment. Long-term, the data could support urban planning, infrastructure management, environmental monitoring, and emergency response preparation. The MTPA confirmed that all image collection complies with Google's privacy standards, with automatic blurring of faces and license plates before publication.
