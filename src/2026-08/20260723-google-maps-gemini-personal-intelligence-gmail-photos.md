# Google Maps s'apprête à piocher dans vos e-mails et vos photos : ce qui va changer
**Source**: https://www.01net.com/actualites/google-maps-sapprete-piocher-dans-vos-e-mails-photos-ce-qui-va-changer.html
**Date**: 2026-07-23
**Author**: Gabriel Manceau
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Gemini, Personal Intelligence, AI, privacy, navigation

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps is integrating Gemini's "Personal Intelligence" to cross-reference data from Gmail, Google Photos, Contacts, and Workspace, enabling personalized navigation based on users' flight tickets, hotel reservations, and saved locations.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps beta code references "Personal Intelligence" — Gemini's cross-app data integration system
- The feature will allow Ask Maps to access flight tickets, hotel reservations, and restaurant bookings from Gmail
- Google Photos locations and memories will be used to enrich search results and recommendations
- Two new menu items — "Connected app content" and "Personalization" — have appeared in the profile settings
- This follows Google's broader strategy of connecting all its services through Gemini AI

## Synthesis
Google Maps is on the verge of its most significant AI integration yet, as code in the latest beta version reveals explicit references to Gemini's "Personal Intelligence" framework. This development signals a fundamental shift in how the mapping service will operate, moving from isolated queries to a holistic, context-aware experience that draws on a user's entire Google ecosystem.

The Personal Intelligence system, already deployed across Contacts, Google Photos, Google Workspace (Gmail, Drive, Calendar), Search, and YouTube, enables Gemini to cross-reference information scattered across a single Google account. With Maps joining this interconnected web, the navigation app will be able to consult flight tickets stored in Gmail, hotel and restaurant reservations, and locations saved in Google Photos to generate personalized responses rather than generic ones.

The primary vehicle for this integration appears to be Ask Maps, the Gemini-powered feature that already helps users find places and answer questions about destinations. With Personal Intelligence, Ask Maps will transcend its current generic capabilities, potentially suggesting restaurants near a user's hotel reservation or navigating to an airport using flight details pulled from Gmail.

Two new menu items — "Contenu des applications connectées" (Connected app content) and "Personnalisation" — have been manually activatable in the profile menu, though Google has not yet officially detailed the functionality. As with all APK-discovered features, the final implementation may evolve before official deployment.

This integration raises important privacy considerations, as it requires users to grant Maps access to data across multiple Google services. However, it also represents Google's competitive response to Apple's ecosystem integration, where the Maps app leverages system-level data for a seamless experience. The move underscores Google's strategy of using its AI capabilities to create deeper lock-in across its service ecosystem, making each app more valuable when used in conjunction with the others.
