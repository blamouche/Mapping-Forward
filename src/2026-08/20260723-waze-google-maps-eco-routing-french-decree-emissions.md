# C'est fini : pourquoi Waze et Google Maps ne donnent plus les trajets les plus rapides en France
**Source**: https://www.presse-citron.net/cest-fini-pourquoi-waze-et-google-maps-ne-donnent-plus-les-trajets-les-plus-rapides-en-france/
**Date**: 2026-07-23
**Author**: Presse-citron
**Keywords**: Waze, Google Maps, France, eco-routing, decree, emissions, navigation policy

## Elevator pitch
A French government decree now requires navigation apps like Waze and Google Maps to prioritize lowest-emission routes over fastest routes, fundamentally changing how millions of French drivers experience GPS navigation.

## Takeaways
- French decree n° 2022-1199 mandates that digital mobility platforms highlight routes with the lowest greenhouse gas emissions
- For roads with speed limits ≥110 km/h, apps must offer alternatives with speeds reduced by 20 km/h
- Users can still manually switch back to fastest routes in settings, but eco-routing is now the default
- The policy echoes similar changes made for the Paris 2024 Olympics in Île-de-France
- The shift represents a regulatory approach to reducing transportation emissions through software design

## Synthesis
French drivers using Waze and Google Maps are experiencing a fundamental shift in how their navigation apps recommend routes. Rather than defaulting to the fastest itinerary, both platforms now prioritize routes with the lowest environmental impact — a change driven not by corporate sustainability initiatives but by French government regulation.

The policy stems from decree n° 2022-1199, which obliges digital mobility assistance platforms to "highlight route proposals with the lowest impact in terms of greenhouse gas emissions." The decree includes a specific provision for high-speed roads: when a route includes sections with speed limits of 110 km/h or higher, apps must propose an alternative with a speed reduced by 20 km/h. This represents a significant behavioral intervention, as it subtly encourages drivers to travel more slowly on highways without explicitly mandating it.

The change has proven controversial. While environmentally conscious users appreciate the carbon footprint visualization and the ability to make eco-friendly choices, many drivers have expressed frustration that their habitual routes are now longer. The apps do not block users from selecting fastest routes — this can be toggled in settings — but the default has shifted, which is where the regulatory power truly lies.

The Île-de-France region had already pioneered a similar approach ahead of the Paris 2024 Olympics, requesting that Google Maps modify its routing to favor alternative itineraries and prevent traffic congestion. The Île-de-France Mobilités app was designed around this principle, prioritizing routes in the general interest of all motorists rather than individual optimization.

Laurent Probst, director general of the Syndicat des transports d'Île-de-France, has framed this as a public safety issue, warning that if platforms do not comply voluntarily, the state should take necessary measures — including potentially forcing apps to close. This regulatory stance positions navigation software as a tool of public policy, not merely a consumer service, and may set a precedent for other jurisdictions seeking to reduce transportation emissions through software design.
