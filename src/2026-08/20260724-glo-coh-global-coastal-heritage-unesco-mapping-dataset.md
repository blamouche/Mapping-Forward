# Mapping the Global Distribution of Coastal World Heritage Sites: Glo-CoH Dataset
**Source**: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-07758-3
**Date**: 2026-07-24
**Author**: Nature Scientific Data
**Keywords**: UNESCO, World Heritage Sites, coastal mapping, sea-level rise, climate change, remote sensing, GIS dataset

## Elevator pitch
A new global dataset called Glo-CoH digitizes the boundary extents of all 1,250 UNESCO coastal World Heritage Sites under 20m above sea level, enabling precise spatial assessment of climate-induced flood and erosion risks.

## Takeaways
- The Glo-CoH dataset contains digitized boundary extents of 1,250 individual serial UNESCO World Heritage sites situated along the global coastline under 20m AMSL
- The dataset covers over 235 million hectares across cultural, natural, and mixed heritage sites
- Site digitization used extant UNESCO site plans combined with Google Earth satellite imagery
- A validation procedure on a stratified sub-sample shows 92% of digitized sites achieve optimal digitization accuracy
- The dataset fills a critical gap: no previous global-scale high-resolution digital dataset of coastal heritage site boundaries existed

## Synthesis
The Global Coastal Heritage (Glo-CoH) dataset, published in Nature Scientific Data, represents a significant advancement in the spatial documentation of UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Produced by the Global Coastal Heritage Mapping Project between January 2022 and April 2024, the dataset digitizes the spatial extent of all coastal World Heritage Sites up to and including 2023 inscriptions that are located at or under 20 meters Above Mean Sea Level — the Extended Low-Elevation Coastal Zone.

The dataset comprises 1,250 individual serial sites covering cultural, natural, and mixed heritage designations, encompassing over 235 million hectares. This fills a critical gap in geospatial data: previously, no global-scale high-resolution digital dataset of coastal heritage site boundary extents existed, limiting the spatial precision of modelled sea-level rise estimates and risk assessments.

The digitization methodology combined extant site plans submitted as part of the UNESCO inscription process with Google Earth worldwide satellite imagery. This hybrid approach leveraged both authoritative documentation and freely available high-resolution imagery, making the project replicable and transparent. The quality of the resulting dataset was assessed through a mixed qualitative/quantitative validation procedure applied to a stratified sub-sample, evaluating source material quality, site identification, visibility, and digitizing accuracy. The validation suggests 92% of digitized sites achieve scores indicative of optimal digitization accuracy.

The dataset is designed to support assessment of coastal flood and erosion risks, including the effects of climate-induced sea-level rise. This is particularly urgent given that coastal heritage sites face compounding threats from flooding, coastal erosion, and development pressure. The data enables researchers and practitioners to assess environmental, climatic, economic, infrastructural, and societal dimensions relating to coastal heritage at scales ranging from individual sites to global analyses.

The Glo-CoH dataset is made available for geo-spatial analysis, representing a valuable resource for climate adaptation planning, heritage conservation, and disaster risk reduction in some of the world's most culturally and naturally significant coastal areas.
