## Elevator pitch
Google Maps on Android Auto is finally rolling out a real-time speedometer display, catching up to Apple CarPlay which has had the feature since 2024 — and highlighting the often superior precision of GPS-derived speed over mechanical speedometers.

## Synthesis
After years of absence, Google Maps on Android Auto is finally receiving a real-time speedometer display, bringing the feature in line with Apple CarPlay, which has offered it natively since 2024. The deployment is gradual, currently appearing for select Canadian and American users on the beta version of the application, with server-side activation controlling the rollout.

The functionality is straightforward: the navigation interface now displays the vehicle's current speed directly adjacent to the posted speed limit. If the driver exceeds the limit, the indicator changes color — first to yellow, then to red — providing a clear visual cue without requiring the driver to look away from the navigation screen. This centralizes driving information on a single display, allowing speed verification with a quick glance.

The two-year delay behind CarPlay is notable, especially given that Google Maps' mobile versions (both iOS and Android) have featured speedometers since 2019. Even more curiously, Waze — acquired by Google in 2013 — has offered a similar speed indicator since its early days. The gap highlights the often-glacial pace of feature parity between Google's Android Auto projection system and Apple's CarPlay equivalent.

There is also a technical dimension to this feature that goes beyond mere convenience. GPS-calculated speed is often more accurate than a vehicle's mechanical speedometer. The instrumentation in cars depends on physical variables like tire wear and pressure, and manufacturers deliberately calibrate speedometers to slightly overestimate speed for legal safety. In contrast, GPS speed is calculated mathematically by dividing distance traveled by elapsed time, providing a raw, objective measurement that is typically closer to the actual speed — assuming adequate satellite signal.

An important distinction: this feature applies to Android Auto (the smartphone projection system), not Android Automotive (the native operating system integrated into modern vehicles), which already displayed speed data. The rollout continues Google's broader effort to bring Android Auto to feature parity with competitors, though the pace suggests the platform remains a lower priority for the company.
