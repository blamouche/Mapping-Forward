# Turning Raw Data into Spatial Intelligence: Google Maps Platform New Features
**Source**: https://mapsplatform.google.com/resources/blog/turning-raw-data-into-spatial-intelligence/
**Date**: 2026-07-24
**Author**: Google Maps Platform
**Keywords**: Google Maps Platform, Places Insights, Solar API, Air Quality, BigQuery, spatial intelligence, datasets

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps Platform has expanded its Places Insights dataset with brand presence data, added a new Detected Arrays feature for solar panel identification via satellite imagery, and introduced four years of historical pollen and air quality data for predictive environmental intelligence.

## Takeaways
- Places Insights now includes brand presence data for US, UK, Canada, and Australia, and has expanded to 50 countries including Argentina, Finland, and South Korea
- New Detected Arrays feature in the Solar API uses Google Earth AI foundation models to visualize existing solar panel installations from satellite imagery
- Air Quality Insights and Pollen Insights now offer four years of historical data, enabling predictive forecasting beyond real-time monitoring
- All datasets integrate with BigQuery, allowing businesses to join location data with their own sales, customer, and operational data
- Solar detection enables clean energy vendors to prospect customers and energy providers to make infrastructure decisions without site visits

## Synthesis
Google Maps Platform has unveiled a suite of new spatial intelligence features that transform its mapping service from a navigation tool into a comprehensive business intelligence platform. The updates span three core areas — places analytics, solar energy mapping, and environmental health data — all designed to help enterprises make data-driven decisions about physical locations.

The expanded Places Insights dataset now includes brand presence information, allowing businesses to analyze commercial density, identify competitive and complementary businesses, and optimize site selection. This expansion covers 50 countries, with new additions including Argentina, Finland, and South Korea. The data is available in BigQuery, enabling companies to break down data silos by joining location intelligence with their own sales, customer, and demographic data for comprehensive market analysis.

Perhaps the most technically impressive addition is the Detected Arrays feature in the Solar API. Using Google Earth AI's geospatial foundation models, the system can automatically identify and visualize existing rooftop solar panel installations from satellite imagery. This eliminates the need for manual satellite imagery inspection, allowing clean energy vendors to prospect for electrification customers, enhance site assessments, and identify expansion areas — all from a desktop. Energy providers can use this data for infrastructure planning, load balancing, and virtual power plant programs.

The Air Quality and Pollen Insights datasets have been significantly expanded with four years of historical data. This temporal depth moves the platform from reactive monitoring to predictive forecasting, enabling pharmaceutical and healthcare companies to anticipate seasonal environmental triggers, optimize supply chains for allergy medications, and inform R&D. The data includes granular information on particulate matter, allergens, and pollen types — available for the US, Canada, and Australia.

Together, these features position Google Maps Platform as a spatial intelligence engine that bridges the gap between raw geographic data and actionable business strategy, with applications spanning retail expansion, clean energy, and public health.
