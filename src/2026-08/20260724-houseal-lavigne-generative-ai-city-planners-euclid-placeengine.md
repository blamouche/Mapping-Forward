# Houseal Lavigne: Generative AI for City Planners
**Source**: https://www.geoweeknews.com/articles/houseal-lavigne-generative-ai-for-city-planners/
**Date**: 2026-07-24
**Author**: Abigail Hart, Geo Week News
**Keywords**: GeoAI, generative AI, city planning, ArcGIS, Esri, Euclid, PlaceEngine, zoning

## Elevator pitch
At the Esri User Conference, Houseal Lavigne demonstrated two AI-powered GIS workflows — Euclid and PlaceEngine — that turn city planning data into conversationally accessible knowledge and automatically generate finished deliverables like reports, maps, and presentations.

## Takeaways
- Euclid is a jurisdictional knowledge platform making community plans, codes, and data conversationally available via ArcGIS Online, launched commercially in January 2026
- PlaceEngine turns GIS data into finished deliverables (reports, maps, PDFs, StoryMaps) directly inside ArcGIS Pro and CityEngine, currently in alpha
- PlaceEngine generated 14 different deliverables from a single 60-acre Colorado site plan, including a pro forma, marketing prospectus, and coloring book
- The system is human-in-the-loop by design, stopping at decision points and asking for human input
- Both tools form what the firm calls "Place Intelligence" — a knowledge layer plus a production layer for GIS

## Synthesis
At the Esri User Conference, urban planning firm Houseal Lavigne showcased what it calls "Place Intelligence" — a two-platform system that uses generative AI to bridge the persistent gap between GIS data and the finished deliverables that planners actually need. The demonstration highlighted how AI is moving from a novelty in geospatial workflows to a practical production tool.

The first platform, Euclid, serves as the knowledge layer. Launched commercially in January 2026 as one of the first AI-native solutions in the Esri ecosystem, Euclid makes a jurisdiction's adopted plans, codes, and operational data conversationally available. Every inquiry is logged back to the jurisdiction's own ArcGIS Online, ensuring traceability. A planner can ask questions about zoning regulations and get answers sourced directly from the municipality's own documents.

The second platform, PlaceEngine, is the production layer. Currently in alpha testing, it works directly inside ArcGIS Pro and CityEngine to transform GIS data into finished work products — reports, maps, visuals, narratives, presentations, PDFs, InDesign files, websites, and ArcGIS Hub sites. The tool evolved from a project that used AI to enhance CityEngine renderings into photorealistic scenes. The team realized that the rich data behind 3D models could feed an AI pipeline to generate entire deliverables, not just prettier images.

To demonstrate PlaceEngine's range, the team fed it a 2022 plan for a 60-acre site in Colorado and had it produce 14 different deliverables: a pro forma, a marketing prospectus, and even a children's coloring book. The system also produced a four-page wedding-hosting guide for Madera County, California, sourced entirely from the county's zoning code via Euclid's knowledge base.

A critical design principle is human-in-the-loop. PlaceEngine interprets questions and drafts content but stops and asks a person when it hits a real decision point. Zoning answers are always sourced back to the jurisdiction's code. This careful approach addresses concerns about AI overreach in government planning contexts, where errors could have significant legal and community consequences.
