## Synthesis
On July 27, 2026, Digital Trends reported that Google was widening the rollout of two of the most anticipated Google Maps features for Android Auto: Immersive Navigation and a built-in live speedometer. After months of limited availability that left many users without access, both features were now reaching a broader group of users, including those running stable versions of Google Maps rather than beta builds.

Immersive Navigation represents Google's effort to modernize the Android Auto navigation experience. The feature makes Google Maps easier to follow while driving by displaying richer road layouts, clearer lane guidance, and more detailed intersections. The updated interface is designed to present navigation information in a way that's easier to interpret with a glance—critical for driving safety—rather than requiring drivers to study the map. This is particularly valuable at complex interchanges where multiple lanes and exits converge.

The live speedometer feature addresses a different but equally practical need. Unlike speed limit warnings, which show the legal limit, the live speedometer displays the vehicle's current speed directly within Google Maps on the car's infotainment screen. This reduces the need for drivers to switch attention between the infotainment display and the instrument cluster, particularly useful in vehicles where the speedometer isn't readily visible or when using aftermarket head units.

A notable interface change accompanying the rollout is the replacement of the Google Assistant shortcut with the Gemini icon in the top-right corner of Google Maps on Android phones. This reflects Google's broader ecosystem transition from Assistant to Gemini as its primary AI interface.

The server-side nature of the rollout means that patience is still required. Installing the latest version of Google Maps or Android Auto does not guarantee immediate access, as Google controls feature availability from its servers. However, the fact that features are appearing on stable builds rather than remaining exclusive to beta users signals that Google is becoming more confident about deploying the new experience at scale. Availability may still vary depending on region and device.
