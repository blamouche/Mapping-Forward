# It's not just you: Google Maps traffic lines have disappeared for many users
**Source**: https://www.androidauthority.com/google-maps-traffic-lines-bug-3691411/
**Date**: 2026-07-27
**Author**: Aamir Siddiqui / Android Authority
**Keywords**: Google Maps, traffic lines, bug, color-coded traffic, navigation, Android, iOS, desktop

## Elevator pitch
A server-side Google Maps bug caused color-coded traffic lines (green, yellow, orange, red) to vanish from the map interface across Android, iOS, and desktop, leaving commuters without visual traffic density indicators until Google deployed a fix.

## Synthesis
On July 27, 2026, Android Authority reported a growing Google Maps bug that caused color-coded traffic lines to completely disappear from the map interface for many users worldwide. The bug affected the visual traffic overlay—the green, yellow, orange, and red route lines that indicate traffic density—while leaving underlying traffic data, road closures, and route calculations intact. This meant that users could still receive accurate ETAs and route suggestions, but the at-a-glance traffic overview that commuters rely on before departure was gone.

The issue was first raised by Reddit user Czerwony150, who reported that traffic colors on Google Maps had "suddenly stopped working out of nowhere." Android Authority confirmed the bug across multiple platforms and devices: Android (Xiaomi 17 Ultra running Hyper OS 3.0 based on Android 16), iOS (iPhone 17 Pro running iOS 27 public beta), and desktop web browsers. The cross-platform nature pointed to a server-side rendering issue rather than a client-side bug.

Notably, some traffic layer elements remained visible—road closures and road maintenance work still appeared on the map—but the color-coded traffic density lines were completely absent. Route calculations would bring traffic data back into view for that specific route, but the rest of the map overview remained devoid of traffic colors. Standard fixes like toggling the traffic layer, toggling incognito mode, changing the Google account, and clearing the app's cache and data all failed to resolve the problem.

The bug was particularly frustrating for users who depend on Google Maps to gauge commute conditions before heading out. Without traffic colors, users had to start navigation to see traffic conditions for a specific route, losing the ability to compare multiple routes visually. The incident highlighted how essential basic UI elements like traffic density indicators have become to daily commuting decisions.

Google confirmed to Android Authority that a fix was available by July 28, 2026, though full restoration took several hours. The bug came at a time when Google is actively developing more complex Maps features, including Gemini's Personal Intelligence integration and live speedometers on Android Auto, raising questions about the stability of core features amid rapid innovation.
