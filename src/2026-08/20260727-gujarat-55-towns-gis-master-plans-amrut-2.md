# 55 Gujarat Towns to Get GIS-Based Development Plans Under AMRUT 2.0

## Elevator pitch
The Gujarat government has launched a ₹40 crore project to prepare GIS-based development plans for 55 towns with populations between 50,000 and 100,000, using drone surveys and Survey of India geo-databases under the Centre's AMRUT 2.0 mission.

## Takeaways
- The Gujarat government is preparing GIS-based development plans (master plans) for 55 towns with populations between 50,000 and one lakh (100,000)

## Synthesis
On July 27, 2026, DeshGujarat reported that the Gujarat government had initiated a major GIS-based urban planning project under the Centre's AMRUT 2.0 mission. The project targets 55 towns across Gujarat with populations between 50,000 and 100,000, aiming to create data-driven master plans that leverage geographic information systems for more accurate and comprehensive urban development planning.

The project, estimated at approximately ₹40 crore, represents a significant investment in geospatial infrastructure for smaller urban centers. The Union Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs will provide ₹70 lakh per town, making this a centrally supported initiative with state-level execution. The 55 selected towns span the state from Anjar in the west to Umargam in the south, including towns like Bilimora, Bardoli, Dahod, and Visnagar.

The technical workflow involves multiple stages of geospatial data collection and analysis. Drone surveys will be conducted to accurately map land features, including elevation and terrain—data that is critical for flood risk assessment, infrastructure planning, and zoning decisions. The Survey of India, the nation's principal mapping agency, is preparing the geo-database that will serve as the foundational spatial layer. Gujarat Town Planning Consultancy Limited, the state's nodal agency, will then use this data to prepare individual development plans for each town.

The initiative also includes a human capacity-building component: approximately 60 officers and employees of the Urban Development Department have been trained to build skilled manpower for preparing GIS-based master plans. This addresses a common bottleneck in GIS adoption—the gap between technology availability and the expertise needed to use it effectively.

The project's March 2027 completion deadline gives agencies roughly eight months to survey 55 towns, build geo-databases, and produce development plans. If successful, the model could be replicated across other Indian states, demonstrating how GIS can transition from a specialized tool to a standard component of urban governance, particularly in mid-sized towns that have historically lacked comprehensive master plans.
