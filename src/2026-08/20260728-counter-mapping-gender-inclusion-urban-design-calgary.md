# Expanding the Urban Design Toolbox: Counter-Mapping for Gender Inclusion
**Source**: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41289-026-00343-5
**Date**: 2026-07-28
**Author**: Springer Nature (Urban Design International)
**Keywords**: counter-mapping, feminist GIS, gender inclusion, urban design, University of Calgary, accessibility, safety, care infrastructure, participatory mapping

## Elevator pitch
A University of Calgary study introduces a gender-inclusive urban design toolkit using feminist GIS and counter-mapping to identify, map, and analyze features that mainstream mapping overlooks—translating everyday experiences of safety, accessibility, and care into actionable urban design knowledge.

## Synthesis
Published in Urban Design International (Springer Nature) in July 2026, this study addresses a critical gap in urban design evidence: the systematic underrepresentation of women's everyday mobility needs and care infrastructures in mainstream mapping. While maps are foundational to making spatial conditions visible, navigable, and actionable in urban design, the authors argue that conventional mapping practices privilege standardized, easily obtainable data—traffic counts, land use, parcel boundaries—while the spatial needs associated with care, safety, comfort, and representation remain invisible.

The study adopts a counter-mapping approach grounded in feminist GIS, applied to the University of Calgary campus. Counter-mapping, as the authors frame it, is not merely about adding new data layers to existing maps but about challenging whose experiences count as legitimate geographic knowledge. The researchers introduce a gender-inclusive urban design toolkit that includes a geospatial data typology for categorizing gender-relevant urban features, mapping practices for collecting this data through participatory methods, and analysis methods for translating lived experience into spatial evidence.

The project's tangible outputs include the "UCalgary Map for All," an interactive map application for campus navigation that incorporates gender-inclusive features alongside a spatial assessment of campus gender inclusivity. The mapping-driven design recommendations provide actionable guidance for campus transformation, demonstrating how feminist GIS can bridge the gap between abstract equity principles and concrete spatial interventions.

The study's theoretical contribution is its argument that feminist GIS can inform counter-mapping in urban design by moving beyond conventional notions of efficient mapping and design. Rather than optimizing for the fastest route or the most economically productive land use, feminist GIS asks what spatial conditions support care, safety, accessibility, and representation. This shift requires what the authors describe as "more careful, creative, and inclusive practices" that make overlooked spatial needs visible, spatially legible, and actionable.

The research was funded by the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council (SSHRC) of Canada and draws on a rich interdisciplinary literature spanning feminist geography (D'Ignazio and Klein's Data Feminism), urban design (Carmona's Public Places Urban Spaces), and cartographic theory (Wood and Fels' The Power of Maps), positioning counter-mapping as both a methodological innovation and a political intervention in how cities are designed and governed.
