# Google Maps : comment afficher les feux de forêt pour les suivre en temps réel

## Elevator pitch
As devastating wildfires tore through France and Spain in July 2026, Frandroid published a practical guide showing how to activate Google Maps' wildfire tracking layer to monitor active fires in real time, including evacuation information and emergency contacts.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps includes a "Wildfires" layer introduced in 2020 that allows users to track active forest fires in real time on their smartphone
- The feature is activated via the map type selector button, then navigating to "Map details" and tapping "Wildfires"
- Each active fire is marked with a flame icon, and zooming in reveals the affected area highlighted in red
- Each fire icon includes text specifying the region and the recency of the last information update
- Clicking on a fire provides practical information including emergency numbers, official social media accounts, evacuation center websites, and relevant press articles

## Synthesis
On July 28, 2026, as France faced unprecedented wildfires—particularly in the Gironde region around Bordeaux where more than 200,000 people were evacuated—Frandroid published a timely tutorial on how to use Google Maps' wildfire tracking feature. The feature, originally introduced in 2020, had become critically relevant as users sought real-time information about fire spread and evacuation zones.

The tutorial outlines a simple three-step process: opening the Google Maps app, tapping the map type selector button (represented by two overlapping cards with a plus sign) on the right side of the screen, and then selecting "Wildfires" from the "Map details" section. Once activated, the wildfire layer displays active fires as flame icons on the map.

When users zoom into the map, each fire's affected area is highlighted in red, providing a visual sense of the fire's scope. Each flame icon is accompanied by text specifying the region and indicating when the information was last updated, which is critical for users assessing the freshness of wildfire data during rapidly evolving situations.

Beyond the visual overlay, clicking on a specific fire opens an information panel containing practical emergency resources: emergency phone numbers, official social media accounts to follow for updates, websites for evacuation centers, and links to relevant press coverage. This transforms Google Maps from a navigation tool into an emergency information hub during wildfire crises.

The article's timing was significant—published during the worst wildfire season France had experienced since World War II, with President Macron calling the Gironde blaze "completely unprecedented." The fires had created their own fire thunderstorms and approached within 15 kilometers of Bordeaux. The Frandroid guide demonstrated how mainstream mapping applications have evolved beyond navigation to become critical public safety tools, particularly as climate change intensifies wildfire seasons across Europe.
