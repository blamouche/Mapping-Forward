# Apple Maps in iOS 27 makes Suggested Places feature even better
**Source**: https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/29/apple-maps-in-ios-27-makes-my-favorite-recent-feature-even-better/
**Date**: 2026-07-29
**Author**: Ryan Christoffel (9to5Mac)
**Keywords**: Apple Maps, iOS 27, Suggested Places, recommendations, search screen, personalization

## Elevator pitch
Apple Maps' Suggested Places feature, introduced in iOS 26.5, gets a significant upgrade in iOS 27 with up to 10 scrollable recommendations instead of just two.

## Takeaways
- Suggested Places was introduced in iOS 26.5, offering recommendations on the Maps search screen based on trending locations and recent user searches.
- The feature's main limitation was showing only two recommendations at a time—iOS 27 fixes this with horizontal scrolling revealing up to 10 suggestions.
- Personalization uses recent search history to contextualize recommendations, e.g., suggesting vacation-relevant places when researching a trip.
- The improvement is part of broader Apple Maps enhancements in iOS 27.
- The feature makes the Maps search screen a go-to destination for place discovery, not just navigation.

## Synthesis
Apple Maps continues to evolve its discovery capabilities with a meaningful upgrade to the Suggested Places feature in iOS 27. Originally launched in iOS 26.5, Suggested Places introduced personalized recommendations directly on the Maps search screen, combining trending locations with the user's recent search history to surface relevant places.

The initial implementation had one significant limitation: only two recommendations were visible at any time. For a feature designed to help users discover new places, this constraint felt unnecessarily restrictive. iOS 27 addresses this by maintaining the two initial suggestions but adding horizontal scrolling that reveals up to 10 recommended places. This simple change transforms the search screen from a limited suggestion strip into a proper discovery carousel.

The personalization logic is particularly clever. By analyzing recent searches, Apple Maps can infer user intent—if someone has been researching restaurants in a vacation destination, the feature will surface relevant suggestions for that location rather than generic local recommendations. This contextual awareness makes the suggestions more useful than a simple "popular near you" list.

This upgrade signals Apple's broader strategy for Maps: moving beyond pure navigation to become a location discovery platform. By making the search screen a destination in itself—where users can browse interesting places before even entering a query—Apple is competing more directly with Google Maps' Explore features. The incremental improvement from two to ten suggestions may seem minor, but it represents a fundamental shift in how users interact with Apple Maps: from tool-driven ("take me here") to discovery-driven ("what should I check out?").

The feature sits alongside other iOS 27 Maps improvements, continuing Apple's multi-year investment in building a competitive mapping platform that challenges Google's long-standing dominance in location discovery.
