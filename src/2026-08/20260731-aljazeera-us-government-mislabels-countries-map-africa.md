# US Government Mislabels Countries on Map of Africa at Global Conference
**Source**: https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/31/us-government-mislabels-countries-on-map-of-africa-at-global-conference
**Date**: 2026-07-31
**Author**: Reuters / Al Jazeera
**Keywords**: US State Department, Africa map, AI-generated content, AIDS 2026, OpenAI, PEPFAR, cartographic error, diplomacy

## Elevator pitch
US officials at the AIDS 2026 conference in Brazil displayed an AI-generated map of Africa with grossly mislabeled countries — Nigeria in the Sahara, Mozambique in the Horn of Africa — prompting international ridicule and an apology from the State Department.

## Takeaways
- The map appeared during a State Department presentation at the AIDS 2026 conference in Rio de Janeiro, during a discussion of controversial new US health agreements with African countries
- Nigeria was shown as landlocked in the Sahara Desert, Mozambique was moved to the eastern Horn of Africa, and Ivory Coast was placed on the opposite side of the continent
- Reuters found the map contained an OpenAI watermark, indicating it was created using AI tools; OpenAI said it was investigating
- The map was presented by Jeff Graham, head of PEPFAR (President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief), whose past deployments included Ghana and Ethiopia
- The State Department acknowledged "an unfortunate error" caused by a team member who "hastily modified the slide deck before the event" and took "full responsibility"

## Synthesis
Al Jazeera's coverage of the US government's cartographic blunder at the AIDS 2026 conference adds important geopolitical context to what might otherwise be dismissed as a simple mistake. The map was displayed during a presentation about new US health agreements with African countries — agreements that have themselves been controversial, following the Trump administration's unprecedented and deadly aid cuts to HIV/AIDS programs. In this context, displaying a map that mangles African geography carries symbolic weight: it reinforces perceptions of American disregard for African nations and their distinct identities.

The map's specific errors are breathtaking in their scope. Nigeria — Africa's most populous nation, a regional powerhouse — was placed landlocked in the Sahara. Mozambique, a southeastern coastal nation, was relocated to the Horn of Africa. Ivory Coast, in West Africa, appeared on the opposite side of the continent. Boundaries were "mere blobs" that did not represent any real countries. This was not a minor misplacement but a wholesale geographic failure.

The OpenAI watermark discovery is particularly damaging. It means a US government official, preparing for a major international diplomacy event, used an AI tool to generate a map of an entire continent and did not verify the result before presenting it to an audience including African partners. This joins a growing pattern of AI-generated content being used in official contexts without adequate verification, and it underscores the risks of replacing professional cartographic expertise with quick AI generation. The incident also highlights the geopolitical dimensions of AI: tools trained predominantly on Western data produce adequate results for Western geographies but fail catastrophically for regions underrepresented in training data.
