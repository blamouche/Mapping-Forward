# I raced Waze against Google Maps, and there was a clear winner
**Source**: https://www.howtogeek.com/raced-waze-against-google-maps/
**Date**: 2026-08-03
**Author**: Adam Davidson, How-To Geek
**Keywords**: Waze, Google Maps, navigation comparison, routing, user experience, driving apps

## Elevator pitch
A How-To Geek writer conducted four real-world races between Waze and Google Maps, finding Waze superior for local driving due to better route choices, while Google Maps won on long-distance estimated travel time.

## Takeaways
- Four races tested: local grocery store, parking lot avoiding a bad bend, dash to the beach, and full-length country drive
- Waze won races 1 and 2 by choosing better local routes — Google Maps suggested a dirt track shortcut and a route through a 180-degree bend
- Race 3 (beach trip) was a tie, with similar routes and estimated times (Waze 2h34m vs Google Maps 2h38m)
- Google Maps won race 4 (cross-country) with a 44-minute shorter estimated time (15h02m vs Waze 15h46m), though the author hadn't driven the route to verify accuracy
- Overall verdict: Waze wins for local/known routes due to smarter local routing, while Google Maps may be better for long-distance estimated times

## Synthesis
This head-to-head comparison provides practical empirical data on the ongoing Waze vs Google Maps debate. Despite both apps now being owned by Google and sharing traffic data, they maintain distinct routing algorithms that produce meaningfully different results. The key finding is that Waze excels at local routing — avoiding dirt tracks, impossible bends, and impractical shortcuts that Google Maps sometimes suggests. This aligns with Waze's community-driven model, where users actively report road conditions and route quality issues.

The test also reveals the limitations of estimated travel time as a metric. Google Maps won the long-distance race on paper with a faster ETA, but the author acknowledged he couldn't verify whether that route was actually faster in practice — and given Google Maps' tendency to suggest problematic local roads, the estimate may be misleadingly optimistic. Commenters reinforced this point, noting that Waze sometimes directs to wrong positions near water bodies while Google Maps handles off-road destinations better.

The broader takeaway for the mapping community is that despite consolidation under Google's umbrella, the two services maintain distinct character: Waze remains the community-powered, driver-focused tool, while Google Maps prioritizes comprehensive geographic data and multimodal navigation. The experiment validates the common advice to use Waze for daily driving and Google Maps for exploration and trip planning. The 44-minute difference on the cross-country route also highlights that routing algorithm differences can have significant real-world time implications over long distances.
