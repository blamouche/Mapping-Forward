## Synthesis
Published on August 3, 2026, in Bladi.net, this article examines how Google Maps' cartographic representation of the Sebta (Ceuta) and Melilla border using dashed lines continues to spark controversy in Spain. On Google Maps, these Spanish enclave cities on the Moroccan coast are not separated from Morocco by a solid gray line — their land borders appear as dashed lines, the symbol the platform uses when "parties have not reached an agreement concerning the border," according to Spanish newspaper La Razón.

The dashed-line representation has persisted through multiple political events. It was already visible in 2020 and provoked a new controversy in 2023 when the Observatory of Sebta and Melilla publicly contested Google's cartographic choice. The topic resurfaced in August 2026 amid renewed debate over sovereignty of the two cities, particularly following the recent migration crisis and renewed Moroccan claims.

The article explains the political stakes: Madrid considers Sebta and Melilla as fully integral parts of Spain, while Morocco claims the cities as occupied territories that must return to the Kingdom's territorial integrity. Google's map doesn't change their actual legal status, but it gives global visibility to the existence of the dispute — unlike Spanish maps, which typically represent the separation from Morocco as an ordinary, definitively established international border.

Google has not specified from what exact date this treatment has been applied, nor why the two cities are classified in this manner. Despite Spanish criticism, the platform maintains Sebta and Melilla behind dashed borders, making the cartographic representation of this territorial dispute visible to anyone who zooms in on the area.
