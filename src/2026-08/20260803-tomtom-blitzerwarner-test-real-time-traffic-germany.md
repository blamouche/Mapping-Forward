# TomTom-Blitzerwarner im Test: Stau, Gefahren und Baustellen in Echtzeit – was er wirklich kann

**Date**: 2026-07-11
**Author**: Eike Cramer, IMTEST
**Keywords**: TomTom, Blitzerwarner, real-time traffic, speed camera warning, connected devices, navigation safety

## Elevator pitch
IMTEST reviews TomTom's "Tom" dedicated speed camera warning device, finding it a well-built but imperfect companion that leverages real-time data from 600 million connected devices to warn drivers about hazards, construction zones, and traffic jams.

## Takeaways
- "Tom" by TomTom is a compact dedicated device (~80€) that uses real-time data from TomTom's network of 600 million connected devices to detect speed cameras, construction zones, accidents, and wrong-way drivers
- The device uses color-coded LED warnings (blue=cameras, yellow=hazards, red=traffic jam, orange=overspeed) to keep the driver's eyes on the road
- No subscription required — all warning and traffic data is free, making it attractive for frequent drivers and commuters
- The system integrates with the TomTom Navi app, supporting Apple CarPlay, but some hazards (like construction zones on the autobahn) were missed during testing
- The device competes against apps like Blitzer.de and hardware like Ooono Co-Driver, with varying degrees of accuracy and data completeness

## Synthesis
TomTom's entry into the dedicated Blitzerwarner (speed camera warning) market with "Tom" represents an interesting bet on hardware in an era where smartphone apps dominate. The device taps into TomTom's extensive traffic data network — built from 600 million connected devices worldwide — to provide real-time hazard warnings that go well beyond simple speed camera alerts. Drivers receive visual and acoustic warnings for speed cameras, construction zones, broken-down vehicles, objects on the road, and even wrong-way drivers (Geisterfahrer).

The IMTEST review reveals both strengths and weaknesses. On the positive side, the device is easy to install, has long battery life, requires no subscription, and integrates well with the TomTom Navi app including Apple CarPlay support. The color-coded LED system is designed to minimize distraction. However, the test also found gaps: a construction zone on the autobahn was overlooked, the data basis for speed cameras is less comprehensive than competitors like Ooono or Blitzer.de, and the user reporting mechanism (pressing buttons to report or cancel camera reports) is unintuitive.

The review highlights a broader tension in the navigation ecosystem: dedicated hardware offers focused, distraction-free experiences, but struggles to match the crowd-sourced data density of smartphone apps. TomTom's massive connected device network gives it a data advantage in traffic conditions, but for speed camera accuracy, community-driven apps still appear to have an edge. The device's success will likely depend on whether TomTom can grow its user base to improve data coverage — a chicken-and-egg problem for any new entrant in this space.
