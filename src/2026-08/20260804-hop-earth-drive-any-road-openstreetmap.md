# Google Maps x Need for Speed : ce site gratuit vous permet de conduire sur n'importe quelle route du monde
**Source**: https://www.lesnumeriques.com/societe-numerique/conduisez-et-faites-la-course-sur-n-importe-quelle-route-du-monde-depuis-votre-navigateur-avec-hop-earth-n260059.html
**Date**: 2026-08-04
**Author**: Antoine Roche, Les Numériques
**Keywords**: Hop Earth, OpenStreetMap, satellite elevation data, real-time terrain generation, browser-based driving, multiplayer racing, Need for Speed, virtual exploration

## Elevator pitch
Hop Earth is a free browser-based platform that uses OpenStreetMap data and satellite elevation to generate real-world roads in real time, letting users drive or race anywhere in the world — a step toward the "Need for Speed + Google Maps" promise.

## Takeaways
- Hop Earth generates terrain and roads in real time from OpenStreetMap data and satellite elevation sources, directly in the browser
- Users choose a starting point on a map, spawn on location, and can call a vehicle to drive existing real-world roads
- The platform supports multiplayer racing alongside solo exploration
- The tool is still in early development: visuals are rudimentary, bugs are frequent, and only a handful of countries (including France) have high-resolution data
- The creator acknowledges using AI tools to build the project but keeps it proprietary to prevent AI-driven appropriation of the work

## Synthesis
Published on August 4, 2026, in Les Numériques, this article by Antoine Roche introduces Hop Earth, a free web-based platform that enables users to drive on any real road in the world from their browser. The project, created by the developer account @DVLPLONDON on X, combines OpenStreetMap data with satellite elevation data to generate terrain in real time as the user drives or walks through it.

The concept evokes the promise of "Need for Speed + Google Maps" — the ability to virtually drive anywhere on actual roads. After selecting a departure point on a map, users are parachuted to the location and can summon a vehicle to navigate the roads as they exist in reality. Each road and intersection is generated locally in real time. The platform also supports multiplayer racing, allowing users to compete against others on the same roads.

However, the article is candid about the project's current limitations. The visual experience is basic — there are intentionally no buildings, and only a few countries including France currently have high-resolution terrain data. Bugs are numerous, and controls can be approximate, with a recommendation to switch to a QWERTY keyboard for better driving. The creator appears active in improving the tool over time.

An interesting note is the creator's stance on AI: while AI tools were likely used to build Hop Earth (including AI-generated music during races), the creator has chosen to keep the project proprietary specifically to prevent AI from appropriating the work, citing concerns that AI "steals everything and doesn't credit authors." For those seeking aerial virtual exploration, the article suggests Microsoft Flight Simulator remains the best option for traversing real-world locations from the sky.
