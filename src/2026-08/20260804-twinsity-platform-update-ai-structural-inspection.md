## Synthesis
Published on August 4, 2026, in Geo Week News, this article covers the largest platform update from Twinsity, a Kassel, Germany-based structural inspection platform. The release sharpens the platform's focus on what inspection work actually needs — not just visualization of 3D models or drone surveys, but the harder task of turning captured data into structured, defensible observations and reports in one place.

The update rebuilds the interface around the way inspection teams work, so moving from an open model to a shareable report takes fewer clicks and fewer steps. The most-used tools sit where users expect them, and the path from inspection to report is significantly shorter. A key new feature is the Observation Profile Builder, which addresses a common pain point: every inspection team has its own set of questions it asks about findings, whether set by a client standard or internal quality assurance. The Profile Builder lets teams replicate that structure — the observation classes they record and the fields they record against — inside the platform. Their existing standards transfer directly rather than being reshaped to fit the software.

Alongside the Profile Builder, Twinsity's AI provides a head start by surfacing potential observations for an inspector to review and confirm. The division of labor is explicit: the AI does the detecting, the inspector does the deciding, and every image is reviewed. This approach maintains human oversight while accelerating the detection phase.

The update serves three distinct groups: drone service providers deliver more professional, credible results faster; engineering and consulting firms spend less time turning models into reports; and asset owners and managers maintain a single trusted record of an asset over its lifetime. Co-CEO Brock Ryder summarizes the philosophy: "Anyone can visualise a project. The value is in what comes next, turning that data into something your teams and clients can rely on."
