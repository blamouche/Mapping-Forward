## Elevator pitch
Woolpert and Southern California Edison demonstrate how high-definition, high-altitude airborne LiDAR (ZEUS) is being applied to support one of the utility industry's most technically complex challenges, helping prioritize risk and allocate resources for power infrastructure.

## Synthesis
Published on Geo Week News on August 5, 2026, this webinar announcement describes how Woolpert's ZEUS high-altitude airborne LiDAR system is being applied to utility infrastructure challenges in partnership with Southern California Edison (SCE). The webinar, scheduled for September 10, 2026, serves as a precursor to a broader conversation at Geo Week 2027.

The application of high-altitude LiDAR to utility infrastructure represents a growing trend in geospatial intelligence for the energy sector. Traditional ground-based inspections of power infrastructure are labor-intensive, time-consuming, and often incomplete. Airborne LiDAR systems like ZEUS can capture high-definition 3D point clouds of power lines, transmission towers, vegetation encroachment, and terrain from aircraft flying at higher altitudes than traditional LiDAR surveys, covering larger areas more efficiently.

The partnership between Woolpert, a geospatial engineering firm, and Southern California Edison, one of the largest electric utilities in the United States, demonstrates the practical adoption of advanced LiDAR technology by major utility operators. SCE serves approximately 15 million people across a 50,000-square-mile service area in Southern California, making infrastructure risk management a significant operational challenge. The region's wildfire risks, in particular, have heightened the urgency of vegetation management and power line inspection programs.

The ZEUS system's high-altitude capability is significant because it enables wider area coverage per flight line compared to traditional low-altitude LiDAR, reducing the cost and time required to survey large utility service territories. The resulting point clouds can be analyzed to identify vegetation encroachment on power lines, assess structural integrity of transmission infrastructure, and prioritize maintenance and hazard mitigation activities based on risk levels.

This webinar highlights the broader trend of utilities adopting advanced geospatial technologies for proactive, data-driven operations, moving from reactive maintenance to risk-based resource allocation informed by high-resolution 3D mapping.
