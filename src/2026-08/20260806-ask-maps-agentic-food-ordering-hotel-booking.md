# Ask Maps Gets More Helpful with Food Ordering, Hotel Booking, and Personal Intelligence
**Source**: https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/order-food-in-ask-maps/
**Date**: 2026-08-06
**Author**: Miriam Daniel, VP & GM, Google Maps
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Ask Maps, Gemini, agentic AI, food ordering, hotel booking, Personal Intelligence, Gmail integration, real-time transit, conversational contributions

## Elevator pitch
Google announced major agentic upgrades to Ask Maps in Google Maps, including the ability to order food, book hotels, discover events, connect Gmail for personalized suggestions, access real-time transit delays, and contribute map updates conversationally.

## Takeaways
- Ask Maps now handles multi-step agentic tasks: users can order food from restaurants along their route via partners like Square, Toast, and Uber Eats, with items automatically added to cart
- Hotel discovery and event ticket purchasing are built in — users can ask for hotels matching specific vibes and walking-distance requirements, with real-time price comparison
- Personal Intelligence connects Ask Maps to Gmail (off by default) to factor in upcoming flights, dinner reservations, and travel plans for personalized recommendations
- A new live transit widget provides up-to-the-minute delays for buses, trains, subways, and ferries, updating minute by minute
- Conversational contributions let users suggest map edits and share local tips through natural language, including uploading photos of storefront signs from which Maps detects business hours
- Ask Maps is rolling out to Australia, Brazil, Canada, Indonesia, Japan, Mexico, and 150+ countries in English

## Synthesis
Published on the Google Keyword blog on August 6, 2026, by Miriam Daniel, VP & GM of Google Maps, this announcement marks the most significant expansion of Ask Maps since its launch. Google is positioning Google Maps not just as a navigation tool but as an agentic AI assistant capable of completing real-world tasks on behalf of users.

The food ordering capability is particularly noteworthy for its integration depth. Users can ask Maps to "order spicy pad kee mao with seafood for me to pick up on my way home," and the tool will find open restaurants along the route that serve the requested cuisine, consider saved places and dietary needs, and automatically add the dish to the cart. Google is co-developing the Universal Commerce Protocol for Food with partners, indicating a broader ambition to standardize conversational commerce interactions within mapping applications.

Personal Intelligence represents a significant privacy-conscious integration of personal data into mapping. By connecting Gmail (with user opt-in), Ask Maps can automatically factor in upcoming flights, hotel reservations, and dinner plans to provide contextual recommendations without users having to re-explain their itinerary. The feature remembers past conversations, allowing users to resume planning sessions — asking "What activities did you suggest for my trip to Seattle?" pulls up previous recommendations.

The live transit widget transforms Ask Maps into a real-time departure board, with delays updating in real-time. A ferry running late will have its wait time adjust minute by minute. Conversational contributions democratize map editing: users can upload a photo of a storefront and Maps will detect new business hours from the image, asking for confirmation before submitting. These contributions are reviewed by Maps' built-in protection systems for accuracy and policy compliance.

The global rollout to 150+ countries signals Google's confidence in the Gemini-powered Ask Maps platform. Food ordering, hotel discovery, and conversational contributions are initially U.S.-only, with broader international availability planned. This launch reflects Google's broader strategy to embed agentic AI capabilities across its product ecosystem, transforming passive tools into proactive assistants.
