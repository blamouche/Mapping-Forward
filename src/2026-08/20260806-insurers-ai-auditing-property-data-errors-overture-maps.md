## Synthesis
Published in Digital Insurance on August 6, 2026, by Michael Shashoua, this article examines how the insurance industry is applying AI to resolve property data discrepancies, with insights from Amy Rose, CTO of the Overture Maps Foundation. The article highlights the critical intersection of geospatial data quality and insurance risk assessment.

The core problem is fundamental to property insurance: where a property is located according to its address may differ from where it appears on a map when plotted by coordinates. Rose explains that understanding property data means figuring out whether data patterns are real or reflect problems with the data itself — for instance, detecting when address points are systematically "flipped" so that coordinates plot to the wrong location. The key, she argues, is auditability: as long as these issues and the data are communicated transparently, the end user — whether human or machine — can perform the proper validations.

Franklin Manchester, principal global insurance advisor at SAS, describes how insurance companies apply a variety of techniques to reconcile differing information about property risks, terrain, and historic claims. Computer vision and synthetic data generation are the most frequent tools used to "smooth over" differences between data sources. Synthetic data, itself an AI capability, enables insurers to augment datasets to respond to emerging risks and secondary peril activity that worsens with climate change.

Ben Brammer, head of product at Agentech, describes how data-sourcing rules help address discrepancies. These include "source of truth" rules (specifying which data source supercedes others in conflicts) and recency rules (the most recent data takes precedence). AI can also normalize unstructured data by interpreting documents and reports — understanding that "pipe jack" and "pipe boot" typically refer to the same thing, or inferring structural attributes from descriptions.

The article is notable for featuring the Overture Maps Foundation's CTO in the context of insurance data quality, demonstrating how open map data infrastructure is becoming critical to industries far beyond traditional GIS. The auditability framework Rose describes is directly relevant to the Overture Maps mission of providing interoperable, high-quality map data that can be trusted and validated by downstream users.
