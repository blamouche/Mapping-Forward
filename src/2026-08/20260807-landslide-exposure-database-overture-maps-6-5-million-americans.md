# New Database Maps 6.5 Million Americans Facing Landslide Risks Using Overture Maps Data
**Source**: https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/08/07/339318.htm
**Date**: 2026-08-07
**Author**: Newswise / Claims Journal
**Keywords**: landslide risk, Overture Maps Foundation, building footprints, USGS, exposure database, disaster mapping, socioeconomic vulnerability, University of Washington

## Elevator pitch
University of Washington researchers created the first Landslide Exposure Database mapping building-level landslide risk for the entire US, using Overture Maps Foundation building footprints and USGS data to identify 6.5 million Americans at risk.

## Takeaways
- First systematic nationwide assessment of building-level landslide risk in the US
- Rates 128 million buildings by landslide susceptibility using Overture Maps Foundation footprints
- Roughly 6.5 million people live in landslide-prone regions, concentrated in Appalachia and the West Coast
- 80% of at-risk residents live on the West Coast or in Appalachia; West Virginia has the largest share
- Urban at-risk populations tend to be more affluent; rural at-risk populations are more vulnerable
- Combines USGS terrain data, Overture Maps building footprints, Army Corps occupancy data, and Census socioeconomic data

## Synthesis
The Landslide Exposure Database (LED), developed by University of Washington researchers led by Joseph Wartman, represents the first systematic, building-level mapping of landslide risk across the entire United States. Published in Earth's Future on July 29, the database rates 128 million buildings by landslide susceptibility and integrates socioeconomic data to assess residents' vulnerability to landslide impacts.

The research team blended multiple massive datasets: USGS terrain and landslide susceptibility maps, building footprints from the Overture Maps Foundation, occupancy information from the Army Corps of Engineers, and socioeconomic data from the Census Bureau including income, disability, vehicle access, and housing condition. The Overture Maps Foundation's building footprint data was a critical component, demonstrating how open map data enables risk assessment at unprecedented granularity.

The analysis reveals that while 20% of US land is landslide-prone, only 2% of the population — approximately 6.5 million people — lives in these areas. Of those at highest risk, 80% are concentrated on the West Coast or in Appalachia, with West Virginia having the largest share. An unexpected urban-rural divide emerged: urban landslide-prone areas tend to be affluent hillside neighborhoods with desirable views, while rural at-risk communities face greater economic constraints and are less resilient. The researchers recommend region-specific mitigation strategies rather than one-size-fits-all approaches, and have made the full database publicly available with user-friendly tools for non-scientists.
