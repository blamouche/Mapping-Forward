# This Tiny Google Maps Tweak Has Saved Me Hours of Driving Over the Past Year
**Source**: https://www.howtogeek.com/google-maps-tiny-tweak-saved-hours-of-driving/
**Date**: 2026-08-08
**Author**: How-To Geek
**Keywords**: Google Maps, fuel-efficient routing, route options, navigation settings, EV charging, route optimization, driving time

## Elevator pitch
A How-To Geek writer discovered that Google Maps' "Prefer fuel-efficient routes" setting was quietly prioritizing fuel savings over speed, adding minutes to every trip, and that disabling it recovered hours of driving time over the course of a year.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps' "Prefer fuel-efficient routes" setting (Settings > Navigation > Route options) can recommend slower routes to save fuel, even when faster alternatives are available
- The feature considers traffic conditions, hill steepness, stop-and-go patterns, road types, and specified engine type when calculating fuel-efficient routes
- In one example, Maps recommended a 51-minute fuel-efficient route when a 48-minute faster route was available—a 3-minute penalty per trip
- Google does not publicly specify the exact threshold for when arrival times are considered "roughly the same" for fuel-efficient routing
- The default vehicle type is gas-powered; EV users should manually specify their vehicle type, as energy-efficient routes don't include charging stops

## Synthesis
Published on August 8, 2026, in How-To Geek, this article describes a personal discovery about Google Maps' fuel-efficient routing feature that had been quietly adding minutes to the author's trips. The setting, located at Settings > Navigation > Route options under "Prefer fuel-efficient routes," was introduced by Google with the intention of recommending routes that use less fuel when the estimated arrival time is roughly the same as the fastest route.

The article explains that Google doesn't publicly specify the exact threshold for when arrival times are considered "roughly the same," but the author found that the feature can accept routes that are several minutes slower if the fuel savings are deemed sufficient. In one documented example, Maps recommended a 51-minute fuel-efficient journey from Sandy Springs to McDonough, Georgia, even though a 48-minute alternative was available. After disabling the feature, the faster 48-minute route became the default recommendation.

The feature takes multiple factors into account: traffic conditions, steepness of hills, stop-and-go traffic patterns, road types (local vs. highway), and the vehicle's engine type if specified in Settings > Your vehicles. The author notes that Google uses a gas-powered car as the default unless the user manually selects their vehicle type—a potential issue for EV drivers, since energy-efficient routes don't include charging stops and EV efficiency characteristics differ from gas vehicles.

The article's practical takeaway is that most users don't compare every available route before starting a journey—they simply tap Start. The fuel-efficient routing default means they may unknowingly accept slower routes. The author found that those extra minutes added up faster than expected over a year of driving. The solution is simple: disable "Prefer fuel-efficient routes" in navigation settings if you prioritize speed over fuel savings, or manually compare routes before starting each journey.
