# Comment Google Maps prédit-il les embouteillages avant qu'ils ne se produisent ?
**Source**: https://www.vietnam.vn/fr/google-maps-du-bao-ket-xe-truoc-khi-tac-duong-xay-ra-the-nao
**Date**: 2026-08-09
**Author**: Vietnam.vn / VietNamNet
**Keywords**: Google Maps, traffic prediction, AI, machine learning, geolocation, real-time traffic, ETA, congestion forecasting

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps combines real-time geolocation data from millions of phones with AI and machine learning to predict traffic congestion before it happens, using anonymized speed data and historical traffic patterns to suggest alternative routes proactively.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps collects anonymized speed data from users' phones (with location permission) to build a real-time picture of traffic conditions across entire road networks
- When hundreds of vehicles on a 95 km/h road suddenly travel at 19 km/h, Maps automatically identifies the zone as a traffic jam
- Traffic conditions are displayed with a color code: green (smooth), orange (slowed), red (congestion), with the entire process automated and requiring no manual user intervention
- Real-time data alone only reflects current conditions; AI and machine learning analyze historical traffic patterns for each route at different times of day to predict future conditions
- Google anonymizes all location and speed data by region and automatically strips start and end points from each trip to prevent personal route tracing

## Synthesis
Published on August 9, 2026, on Vietnam.vn (via VietNamNet), this article explains how Google Maps predicts traffic congestion before it occurs, combining real-time crowd-sourced data with artificial intelligence. Rather than relying on surveillance cameras or field personnel, Google Maps collects traffic information directly from users' phones, with their location permission. When a user is traveling with location enabled, their device continuously sends anonymized speed data to Google's systems.

The article explains that individual signals provide little information, but when speed data from thousands of phones is collected simultaneously in a given area, the system can build an accurate picture of traffic conditions. For example, if a road segment where the speed limit is 95 km/h suddenly records hundreds of vehicles traveling at only 19 km/h, Google Maps automatically identifies this zone as a traffic jam. The data is continuously updated and displayed with a color code: green for smooth traffic, orange for slowed traffic, and red for congestion. This entire process is automated, requiring no manual user intervention.

However, real-time data alone only reflects current conditions and cannot predict what traffic will look like in 10 or 50 minutes. To address this limitation, Google Maps uses artificial intelligence and machine learning. The system analyzes the historical traffic patterns of each route at different times of day, combining this historical data with real-time data from active devices. This allows the app to predict traffic density at the time a user plans to travel—which is why Google Maps often suggests alternative routes proactively, helping drivers avoid congestion before it occurs.

On privacy, Google states that all location and speed data is anonymized and aggregated by region. The system also automatically strips the start and end points of each trip to ensure that personal routes cannot be traced back to individual users.
