**Source**: https://www.tf1info.fr/transports/video-tf1-waze-google-maps-apple-plans-sur-la-route-des-vacances-quelle-application-de-navigation-est-la-meilleure-2457537.html
**Date**: 2026-08-09
**Author**: TF1 Info
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Waze, Apple Plans, navigation comparison, vacation routing, traffic alerts, toll pricing, speed cameras

## Elevator pitch
TF1 conducted a head-to-head road test of Google Maps, Apple Plans, and Waze over a 50+ km route, finding that Waze excels at real-time traffic alerts and toll pricing transparency, while Google Maps offers the richest in-city detail and Apple Plans impresses with visual clarity but lacks speed camera reporting.

## Takeaways
- TF1 tested the three navigation apps over a 50+ km route from Boulogne-Billancourt to Meaux, with Waze predicting 58 minutes, Apple Plans 59, and Google Maps 62
- Waze was the only app to announce toll prices upfront (€2.40), while Google Maps and Apple Plans could not display toll amounts
- Waze excelled at real-time hazard reporting, alerting the team to accidents before patrol vehicles arrived on scene
- Google Maps uniquely displayed all traffic lights in urban areas, a feature neither competitor offered
- Apple Plans impressed with clear graphics and intuitive direction selection but cannot display or report speed cameras—a significant limitation for drivers

## Synthesis
Published on August 9, 2026, by TF1 Info, this article reports on a comparative road test of France's three most popular navigation apps—Google Maps, Apple Plans, and Waze—conducted during the summer vacation chassé-croisé. Three teams departed from the TF1 tower in Boulogne-Billancourt toward the Meaux train station, over 50 kilometers away, with each team using a different app on identical itineraries.

The test revealed distinct strengths for each app. Waze predicted the fastest arrival at 58 minutes and was the only app to announce toll prices upfront, showing €2.40 for the route. Neither Google Maps nor Apple Plans could display toll amounts, a practical disadvantage for budget-conscious drivers. Waze also dominated real-time traffic reporting, alerting the team to an accident before patrol vehicles arrived, leveraging its community-driven hazard reporting system.

Google Maps stood out in urban driving, uniquely displaying all traffic lights in the city—a detail that, while seemingly small, adds useful contextual awareness. The app also showed police presence on the roadside, which Waze and Apple Plans did not. Apple Plans impressed journalist Romain Chemoul with its clear, visually intuitive plan and easy direction selection, noting "big efforts" in the app's development. However, Apple Plans has a critical limitation: it cannot display or report speed cameras, making it less suitable for drivers who rely on this feature.

The test underscores that the three apps serve different priorities: Waze remains the favorite for real-time traffic information and driving-focused functionality, Google Maps offers the richest in-city experience, and Apple Plans provides the cleanest visual interface. The article notes that Waze was designed for navigation from the start, while the other two are mapping apps that added navigation tools afterward.
