# Google Rolls Out AI-Powered Ask Maps Feature in Singapore
**Source**: https://www.straitstimes.com/singapore/google-rolls-out-updated-ai-powered-ask-maps-feature-in-singapore
**Date**: 2026-08-11
**Author**: The Straits Times
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Ask Maps, AI assistant, agentic capabilities, natural language, navigation, Singapore

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps' AI-powered Ask Maps feature rolls out across Asia-Pacific including Singapore, enabling users to ask natural-language questions, get real-time traffic and crowd updates, plan trips, and even purchase event tickets directly from the mapping app.

## Takeaways
- Ask Maps was rolled out in Asia-Pacific including Singapore on August 7, 2026, expanding from its initial US and India launch in March
- The feature answers natural-language questions like "What time should I leave to get to Wisma Atria at 2pm?" with real-time traffic and crowd status
- Ask Maps acts as a travel planner: viewing real-time hotel prices, planning routes by preference, and creating itineraries for exploring cultural sites within a timeframe
- A "conversation memory" feature lets users revisit earlier recommendations and continue planning trips from previous conversations
- The feature is gradually rolling out to all users on iOS, Android, and desktop, and is also available in Australia, Brazil, Canada, Indonesia, Japan, Mexico, and 150+ countries in English

## Synthesis
Google's rollout of Ask Maps to the Asia-Pacific region marks a significant evolution of Google Maps from a navigation tool toward an AI-powered spatial assistant. The feature, first launched in the US and India in March 2026, uses agentic AI capabilities to answer conversational queries about real-world logistics, combining mapping data with real-time transit information, crowd statuses, and personalized recommendations based on users' past reviews.

The feature's scope extends well beyond navigation. Users can search for nearby halal eateries open past 10pm, find comedy shows with direct ticket purchase links, identify sunset vantage points, and plan multi-stop cultural itineraries within specified timeframes. The "conversation memory" feature creates a persistent planning context, allowing users to revisit earlier recommendations or continue trip planning from where they left off—a capability that positions Google Maps as a competitor to dedicated travel planning tools.

Google's August 6 blog post described the update as making Ask Maps "more helpful with new agentic capabilities, real-time transit information, personal intelligence and conversational contributions." The expansion to Australia, Brazil, Canada, Indonesia, Japan, Mexico, and 150+ English-speaking countries and territories represents a rapid global rollout. The gradual delivery across iOS, Android, and desktop platforms indicates Google is scaling infrastructure to support the AI workloads required for real-time natural-language spatial queries at global scale.
