# India Counters China's Map War: 27 Arunachal Pradesh Locations Standardised on Survey of India Maps
**Source**: https://asianews.network/india-counters-chinas-map-war-names-of-27-arunachal-pradesh-locations-standardised-on-survey-of-india-maps/
**Date**: 2026-08-11
**Author**: Asia News Network
**Keywords**: Survey of India, border mapping, cartographic sovereignty, Arunachal Pradesh, India-China border, territorial claims, toponymy

## Elevator pitch
India's Home Ministry formally identified and standardised 27 geographic locations in Arunachal Pradesh—including mountain passes, a lake, and a memorial—on official Survey of India maps, a cartographic assertion of sovereignty amid ongoing border tensions with China.

## Takeaways
- 27 locations and geographical features in Arunachal Pradesh have been formally identified on official Survey of India maps after Centre-state consultations
- The 27 entries include 21 land areas (towns and villages), 4 mountain passes (Dzo La, Riza La, Pukur La, Thag La), 1 lake (Sambho Sarovar), and 1 memorial (Sher-e-Thapa Memorial)
- The mapping exercise comes amid renewed scrutiny of the India-China border after social media reports alleged Chinese incursion in Arunachal Pradesh
- Chief Minister Pema Khandu, Home Minister Mama Natung, and the Indian Army have all rejected incursion claims as "rumours"
- China has slammed India for naming sites in the disputed border region, escalating what analysts call a "map war" between the two nations

## Synthesis
India's formal standardisation of 27 Arunachal Pradesh locations on Survey of India maps represents a deliberate act of cartographic sovereignty in the ongoing territorial dispute with China. The Home Ministry's announcement—made in consultation with the Arunachal Pradesh state government—covers 21 land areas including Longju, Maja, Bisa, and Jairampur; four mountain passes (Dzo La, Riza La, Pukur La, Thag La); Sambho Sarovar lake; and the Sher-e-Thapa Memorial. The ministry stated the identification is "aimed at facilitating their accurate recognition and better awareness among the public at large."

The timing is significant. The announcement comes amid renewed scrutiny of the border situation following social media reports alleging a Chinese incursion along the Line of Actual Control in Arunachal Pradesh. Chief Minister Pema Khandu rejected the reports, stating he would consult local communities and the Indian Army. Home Minister Mama Natung dismissed the claims as "rumours" and urged media to verify reports before publication. The Indian Army issued a separate rebuttal in June, calling reports of PLA encroachment and camp construction "incorrect and without any basis."

China has responded by criticising India for naming sites in what it considers a disputed border region, illustrating how toponymy—the formal naming of geographic features—has become a tool of territorial assertion. The inclusion of Longju, a historically significant border area, is particularly notable. This standardisation exercise demonstrates how national mapping agencies like the Survey of India serve as instruments of state sovereignty, transforming informal local place names into officially recognised geographic data that reinforces territorial claims on the global cartographic stage.
