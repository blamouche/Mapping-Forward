## Elevator pitch
The August 12, 2026 solar eclipse will drive millions of visitors to rural Spain where cellular networks will saturate, making GPS navigation unreliable—article recommends downloading offline maps on Google Maps or Apple Plans before traveling.

## Takeaways
- The total eclipse path crosses rural Spain (Galicia, Burgos, Balearic Islands) where cell tower infrastructure is minimal and expected to saturate under massive visitor influx
- During the 2024 US total eclipse, mobile networks saturated in multiple cities, forcing operators to deploy temporary towers and downgrade calls to SMS
- The ionospheric electron density drop during totality can degrade GPS precision by several meters, compounding the network saturation problem
- Google Maps offline maps can be downloaded via profile > "Offline Maps" > select area; a 50km radius zone typically requires 100–300 MB
- Apple Plans offers the same offline download capability through profile settings with area selection

## Synthesis
The August 12, 2026 solar eclipse presents a unique cartographic challenge: the path of totality traverses some of Spain's least densely populated areas, including Burgos (~175,000 inhabitants) and Soria (~40,000), whose cellular infrastructure was never designed for mass events. The French Association of Astronomy launched an "Éclipse Info" portal to prepare the public, warning that the combination of network saturation and ionospheric GPS perturbation could render smartphone navigation completely unusable during the event.

The article draws a direct parallel with the April 8, 2024 total eclipse in the United States, where thousands of spectators simultaneously attempted to share photos and videos, causing network collapse in multiple cities. Operators had to deploy temporary mobile towers and prioritize SMS over voice calls. The ionospheric effect—where the sudden disappearance of solar radiation alters electron density and degrades GPS signal precision by several meters—adds a second layer of disruption that returns once sunlight reappears.

The practical solution is straightforward: download offline maps before departure. On Google Maps, users access "Plans hors connexion" via their profile, select a perimeter, and download. Apple Plans offers an equivalent feature through profile settings. The article recommends targeting the specific travel area rather than downloading an entire region, and deleting unused maps to free storage. This preparation takes less than a minute but ensures navigation continues even when the network fails.
