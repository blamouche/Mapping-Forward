## Elevator pitch
HERE WeGo Maps version 4.21.000 adds real-time road hazard reporting and community confirmation of existing disruptions, bringing crowd-sourced traffic incident reporting to the offline-first navigation app.

## Takeaways
- HERE WeGo version 4.21.000 enables users to report and confirm road disruptions in real time during navigation
- Users can tap on existing disruptions to confirm they are still active, or report new incidents including accidents, roadworks, and hazards
- The feature makes HERE WeGo a more competitive alternative to Google Maps and Waze for community-driven traffic reporting
- The app requires Android 9.0 or iOS 17.0 and higher, and is available free on both app stores
- HERE WeGo provides offline navigation in over 1,900 cities worldwide with public transit support

## Synthesis
HERE WeGo's 4.21.000 update represents a significant step forward for the offline-first navigation app, adding real-time community reporting capabilities that have long been the domain of Waze and Google Maps. Users can now actively contribute to map data quality by reporting new road disruptions—accidents, roadworks, hazards—during navigation, and by confirming whether previously reported disruptions are still active.

This dual functionality addresses a key limitation of crowd-sourced traffic data: the freshness problem. By allowing users to confirm or deny existing reports, HERE WeGo creates a self-correcting system where stale incident reports can be cleared quickly, improving route accuracy for the entire user community. The feature description emphasizes that user contributions help keep "Karten- und Verkehrsinformationen für alle Nutzer aktuell und zuverlässig" (map and traffic information current and reliable for all users).

HERE WeGo's positioning remains distinct from its competitors: it offers genuine offline navigation with downloadable maps, public transit directions in over 1,900 cities worldwide, and pedestrian-specific turn-by-turn guidance. The addition of real-time reporting brings it closer to feature parity with Waze while maintaining its offline-first advantage—an important differentiator for users in areas with unreliable connectivity or those concerned about data usage.
