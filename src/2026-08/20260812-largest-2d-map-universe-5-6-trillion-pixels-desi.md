## Synthesis
A team of astronomers has unveiled the largest 2D map of the universe ever created, charting nearly 4 billion cosmic objects across an extraordinary 5.6 trillion pixels. The map is the culmination of 13 years of observations and data processing as part of the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys, conducted using telescopes at Kitt Peak National Observatory in Arizona and Cerro Tololo Inter-American Observatory in Chile. The facilities are operated by NSF's NOIRLab.

The Legacy Imaging Surveys were originally designed to support the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) survey, which is measuring the expansion history of the universe by collecting spectra from tens of millions of galaxies and quasars. The 2D imaging map serves as the foundational layer for DESI's spectroscopic observations, providing precise positions and photometric information that allow the spectrograph to target individual objects.

Beyond DESI, the dataset has broad scientific utility. Previous versions have already been referenced in more than 1,800 scientific papers, and the latest release could help astronomers search for rare objects and phenomena, study galaxy evolution, and train artificial intelligence systems to analyze the enormous volumes of data expected from next-generation observatories. As co-lead Arjun Dey of NSF NOIRLab noted, the survey gives everyone the chance to marvel at the wonders of the night sky, representing both a fundamental research tool and a public resource for cosmic exploration.
