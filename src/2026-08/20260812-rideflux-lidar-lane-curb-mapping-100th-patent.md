# RideFlux Registers 100th Patent for LiDAR-to-Lane-and-Curb Map Conversion Technology

**Date**: 2026-08-12
**Author**: VentureSquare
**Keywords**: RideFlux, LiDAR, autonomous driving, high-precision mapping, lane mapping, curb mapping, HD maps, patent, Level 4 autonomy, South Korea

## Elevator pitch

## Synthesis
RideFlux, a South Korean autonomous driving software company, has secured its 100th registered patent for technology that automatically converts LiDAR data collected by autonomous vehicles into high-precision maps displaying road geometry elements such as lanes and curbs. This technology significantly reduces the time and cost required for producing high-definition maps by automating a labeling process that was previously performed manually.

The patent was registered after examination confirmed its novelty and inventiveness, comparing it against prior art from China's Baidu and the US company DeepMap. It received the highest AAA rating on the Korea Invention Promotion Association's SMART5 patent evaluation system, which evaluates patents across five major countries (Korea, US, Europe, China, Japan) on a nine-grade scale from AAA to C. Additionally, RideFlux's safety-focused algorithm for verifying driving decisions received an A rating, and its road network map generation technology for real-time path planning received an AA rating.

As of July 2026, RideFlux holds 148 patent applications and 100 registrations with a 95.06% registration rate, exceeding the Intellectual Property Office's overall rate of 74.6%. The company's internal analysis shows 49.25% of its patents are rated SMART5 A or higher, more than double the industry average. RideFlux is currently conducting unmanned Level 4 autonomous driving test runs in Korea without a driver in the seat, implementing paid freight transportation connecting logistics hubs with autonomous vehicles. The company plans to submit a preliminary review application to the Korea Exchange this month for KOSDAQ listing, aiming for fully driverless operations by 2027.
