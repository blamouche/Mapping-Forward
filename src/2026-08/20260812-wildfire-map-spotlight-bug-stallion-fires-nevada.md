# Wildfire Map Spotlight: Bug and Stallion Fires, Nevada
**Source**: https://www.iqair.com/ca-fr/newsroom/wildfire-map-spotlight-bug-and-stallion-fires-nevada
**Date**: 2026-08-12
**Author**: IQAir Staff Writers
**Keywords**: wildfire mapping, Nevada, real-time fire maps, air quality, emergency management, geographic monitoring

## Elevator pitch
IQAir's wildfire map spotlight tracks the Bug Fire (93,898 acres, 94% contained) and Stallion Fire (40,691 acres, 95% contained) across Nevada's Washoe County, demonstrating how real-time interactive maps support emergency response and public safety.

## Takeaways
- The Bug Fire spans Washoe County (Nevada) and Lassen/Sierra Counties (California), burning 93,898 acres with 94% containment as of August 19, 2026
- The Stallion Fire covers 40,691 acres south of Pyramid Lake in Washoe County, reaching 95% containment
- Interactive wildfire mapping combines geographic boundaries, containment status, and affected community data in real time
- All evacuation orders in Washoe and Lassen counties have been lifted as fire conditions improved
- Residents are advised to continue monitoring official evacuation maps and local emergency notifications as conditions can change rapidly

## Synthesis
IQAir's wildfire map spotlight provides a detailed geographic analysis of two concurrent wildfires in Nevada, showcasing how modern mapping tools serve as critical infrastructure for emergency communication. The Bug Fire originated northwest of Cold Springs along US Route 395 near the Nevada-California state line and merged with the Fred Mountain Fire, extending east toward Winnemucca Ranch Road. The Stallion Fire, a separate incident in Washoe County, spread south of Pyramid Lake near State Routes 445 and 446, affecting communities between Pyramid Lake and the Reno-Sparks area.

The mapping effort identifies 16 specific affected areas, from Antelope Valley to Wadsworth, providing granular geographic data that emergency managers use for evacuation planning and resource allocation. The containment data—94% for Bug Fire and 95% for Stallion Fire as of August 19—illustrates how map-based tracking of fire progression and containment percentages helps both responders and the public understand evolving threat levels.

IQAir integrates wildfire mapping with air quality monitoring, recommending that residents use real-time air quality apps and high-performance air purifiers to filter wildfire smoke. The article references Watch Duty's fire tracking platform (app.watchduty.org) as its source for fire boundaries and containment data, highlighting the growing ecosystem of citizen and professional mapping tools that complement official emergency management systems.
