## Elevator pitch
Belgium's SNCB trip planner can show routes up to 41 minutes slower than Google Maps because it defaults to train-only routing, ignoring metro, tram, and bus connections that would provide faster multimodal journeys.

## Takeaways
- SNCB planner shows up to 41 minutes more than Google Maps for routes like Charleroi–Brussels-Schuman
- The SNCB planner defaults to train and walking only, ignoring metro, tram, and bus options
- Google Maps proposes multimodal routes that combine train with public transit for faster journeys
- A hidden "Personnalisez votre voyage" tab lets users enable other transport modes for better routes
- Example: Ostend–Delta takes 2h16 via SNCB vs 1h45 via Google Maps
- Even seasoned commuters were unaware of the hidden multimodal option in the SNCB planner

## Synthesis
A significant discrepancy between Belgium's national railway trip planner and Google Maps has come to light, with the SNCB planner showing journeys up to 41 minutes longer than Google Maps for the same routes. Investigation by La DH reveals that the problem stems not from incorrect calculations but from a default limitation: the SNCB planner only considers train and walking segments, whereas Google Maps automatically proposes multimodal routes combining train, metro, tram, and bus.

For example, planning a trip from Ostend to Delta (Brussels), the SNCB planner routes passengers through Vilvorde with a 19-minute wait at Brussels-Midi, totaling 2h16. Google Maps proposes taking the metro from Brussels-Midi, reaching Delta in just 1h45. Similarly, Waterloo to Delta takes 1h07 on the SNCB planner versus 37 minutes on Google Maps. The pattern repeats across multiple routes: Ottignies–Mérode takes 54 minutes (SNCB) vs 34 minutes (Google Maps), and Charleroi–Brussels-Schuman shows a 41-minute gap.

The SNCB confirmed that a simple solution exists: users must click the relatively inconspicuous "Personnalisez votre voyage" (Customize your journey) tab and explicitly enable other modes of transport. However, this option is so discreet that even seasoned commuters taking the train four times a month had never noticed it. The situation illustrates how default settings in trip planners can significantly affect journey quality, and how Google Maps' automatic multimodal routing provides a better user experience than transit-specific planners that require manual configuration.
