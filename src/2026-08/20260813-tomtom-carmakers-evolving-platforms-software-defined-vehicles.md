# From Fixed Machines to Evolving Platforms: The New Challenge for Carmakers
**Source**: https://www.tomtom.com/newsroom/explainers-and-insights/a-defining-era-for-carmakers-when-disruption-became-more-than-a-phase
**Date**: 2026-08-13
**Author**: Maya Barakova
**Keywords**: TomTom, software-defined vehicles, carmakers, AI, connected services, electrification, ADAS, centralized architecture, automotive software

## Elevator pitch
TomTom argues that cars are shifting from fixed machines to evolving software platforms, where the real challenge for carmakers is building foundational software and data layers that let every vehicle capability interact, improve, and scale over time.

## Takeaways
- Cars are increasingly defined by software rather than hardware, driven by AI, electrification, and connected services
- Drivers expect continuous improvement post-purchase, similar to consumer technology
- Centralized architectures, unified in-vehicle experiences, and zonal computers are reshaping vehicle development
- The challenge is not adding more features but building the software foundation for all capabilities to interact
- AI assistants, intelligent ADAS, and automated driving must work as part of a single trusted system
- Software and data must sit at the center of the vehicle experience, not as an add-on layer

## Synthesis
TomTom's analysis highlights a fundamental shift in the automotive industry: cars are no longer just machines that get people from A to B, but evolving software platforms defined less by what sits under the hood and more by the software that shapes the experience behind the wheel. As AI, electrification, connected services, and driver expectations advance, carmakers face the challenge of building vehicles that keep improving long after they leave the factory.

The transition is pushing the industry toward AI-focused, software-led production. Centralized architectures, unified in-vehicle experiences, and zonal computers are becoming central to vehicle development, redefining the car by the software it runs as much as by the hardware it is built on. This creates a closer relationship between vehicle, driver, and road, shaped by continuous updates and shared intelligence.

TomTom emphasizes that the real challenge is not simply adding more features like AI assistants, entertainment apps, or ADAS alerts, but creating the foundational software and data layers that allow every aspect of the vehicle to interact and improve the driving experience. Features such as AI assistants, intelligent ADAS alerts, and automated driving must work together as part of a single trusted system. For carmakers, software must sit at the center of the experience rather than act as a layer added on top of disparate hardware, requiring a fundamental rethink of vehicle architecture and development processes.
