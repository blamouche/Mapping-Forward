# Belo Horizonte Drivers Lose 130 Hours Per Year in Traffic, TomTom Traffic Index 2025

## Synthesis
The TomTom Traffic Index 2025 has revealed that drivers in Belo Horizonte, Brazil, lost 130 hours to traffic congestion over the course of the year — equivalent to more than five full days stationary on asphalt. The annual index, which monitors congestion in hundreds of cities with populations exceeding 800,000, places Belo Horizonte in the 33rd position globally with a congestion rate of 58.6%, narrowly surpassing São Paulo's 58.5%.

Among Brazilian capitals, Belo Horizonte occupies the third position alongside Recife, both at 130 hours lost annually per driver. They trail Curitiba (135 hours) and São Paulo (132 hours), placing all four cities among the country's worst in urban mobility. The most critical congestion occurs on Tuesdays and Wednesdays, with morning peak congestion reaching 97% and evening congestion exceeding 103%, meaning travel times during peak hours are more than double what they would be in free-flowing conditions.

The data was presented by Fernanda Rezende, executive director of the National Transport Confederation (CNT), at the NTU Seminar in São Paulo. CNT used the TomTom Traffic Index to contextualize the mobility scenario in Brazilian capitals, underscoring how GPS-derived traffic data has become a key resource for transport policy discussions. The findings highlight the growing mobility challenges in Brazilian metropolitan areas and the value of continuous, data-driven traffic monitoring for urban planning.
