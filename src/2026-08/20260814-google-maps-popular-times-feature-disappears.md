# Google Maps Loses Popular Times Feature and Nobody Knows Why
**Source**: https://www.lesnumeriques.com/appli-logiciel/google-maps-perd-l-une-de-ses-fonctionnalites-les-plus-utiles-et-personne-ne-sait-pourquoi-n260410.html
**Date**: 2026-08-14
**Author**: Nassim Chentouf
**Keywords**: Google Maps, popular times, affluence, bug, feature removal, mobile app, Android, crowd avoidance

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps' popular times graph — a crowd-avoidance feature used by millions since 2015 — has disappeared from the mobile app for some users without explanation, with evidence pointing to a server-side bug rather than intentional removal.

## Takeaways
- The popular times (affluence) graph has disappeared from the Google Maps mobile app for some users
- The feature, available since 2015, shows hourly crowd levels for restaurants, gyms, and other venues
- The web version of Google Maps still displays the graph for affected accounts
- Multiple Reddit users report the feature vanished without any app update
- Google has not communicated about the removal in release notes or help pages
- Evidence suggests a server-side bug rather than deliberate feature removal

## Synthesis
One of Google Maps' most practical features — the popular times graph that shows how busy a location is at different hours of the day — has quietly disappeared from the mobile application for a subset of users. The feature, introduced in 2015 and enhanced with real-time crowd data in late 2016, relies on anonymized location data from users who have enabled Location History. It has become an essential tool for millions of people looking to avoid crowds at restaurants, gyms, cinemas, and tourist spots.

Reports on Reddit and confirmed by Android Authority indicate that the disappearance occurred without any app update, suggesting a server-side issue rather than a client-side change. Notably, affected users who lose the graph on mobile can still see it on the web version of Google Maps using the same account, further supporting the bug hypothesis. Google has remained silent on the matter, with no mention in release notes or help pages.

The situation echoes a similar incident in March 2025 when the precipitation map in Google's Pixel Weather app vanished overnight without explanation. If this follows the same pattern, the feature is likely to return silently. However, the lack of communication from Google has frustrated users who depend on the crowd-level data for daily planning, and the incident highlights the fragility of cloud-dependent features that can disappear at any time without user recourse.
