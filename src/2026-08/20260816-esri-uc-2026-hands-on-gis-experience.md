## Elevator pitch

- The 2026 Esri User Conference in San Diego featured hands-on labs where participants worked directly with GIS and mapping tools

## Synthesis
At the 2026 Esri User Conference in San Diego, reporter Tasbiha Fatima Hasan participated in hands-on GIS labs that transformed her understanding of the mapping process. Beyond watching demonstrations, working directly with GIS tools created a different kind of understanding—reinforcing that the finished map is only one part of a much larger workflow. Before information becomes a map, data must be collected, organized, and analyzed. Researchers must decide which variables matter, how they should be represented spatially, and what geographic patterns they are trying to understand.

A key topic at the conference was the integration of R and ArcGIS. R is commonly used for statistical computing and data analysis, while GIS adds the geographic dimension. This connection allows analysts to move from asking "What pattern exists in my data?" to "Where does this pattern occur?"—a distinction particularly relevant to environmental research. For example, a spreadsheet can show water quality measurements across samples, but GIS connects those measurements to lakes, rivers, watersheds, and communities. Similar approaches apply to vegetation, land use, and climate datasets. The author's major takeaway was summarized as four steps: Data → Analysis → Map → Understanding. For students beginning to explore GIS, the most important lesson is that a map represents decisions about data, analysis, and place—not simply an attractive visualization at the end of a project.
