# ETH Zurich Student Creates 3D GIS Web App to Map Bird Migration
**Source**: https://www.esri.com/en-us/industries/blog/articles/3d-gis-web-app-for-bird-migration
**Date**: 2026-08-14
**Author**: Esri
**Keywords**: GIS, 3D web mapping, bird migration, ArcGIS, spatial analysis, ETH Zurich, ecology

## Elevator pitch
A former ETH Zurich geomatics student built an interactive 3D GIS web application using ArcGIS Maps SDK to visualize bird migration patterns, combining spatial analysis with ecology to reveal new insights into avian behavior.

## Takeaways
- Dagmara Pasiak, a former ETH Zurich geomatics master's student, created an award-winning 3D GIS web app for visualizing bird migration
- The application was built with ArcGIS Maps SDK for JavaScript, Calcite Design System, and ArcGIS Living Atlas layers
- An "Explore mode" lets users generate and interpret migration data through multidimensional visualizations
- The app helps behavioral ecologists study bird movement across space and time, with charts for speed, altitude, and terrain analysis
- Pasiak transitioned from student to product engineer at the Esri R&D Center in Zurich, turning her passion into a career

## Synthesis
Dagmara Pasiak's project "Investigating Bird Movement with an Interactive 3D Web Map" demonstrates the power of modern GIS tools for ecological research. Built during her master's studies at ETH Zurich, the application blends spatial analysis, 3D web design, GPS tracking data, and ecology to reveal patterns in avian migration and behavior. Rather than building a mapping framework from scratch, Pasiak leveraged the ArcGIS Maps SDK for JavaScript as her foundation, paired with Calcite Design System components for the interface and ArcGIS Living Atlas of the World layers for geographic context.

The application's standout feature is its Explore mode, which allows users to generate and interpret migration data through visualizations that show speed, altitude, and terrain together. This multidimensional approach gives behavioral ecologists a new way to examine bird movement across both space and time. The SDK's Charts components and Elevation Profile component enable researchers to analyze tracking datasets alongside geographic features. The app was designed for accessibility, making scientific findings communicable to diverse audiences including conservationists, policymakers, and the public. After completing her master's, Pasiak joined the Esri Research & Development Center in Zurich, transforming her academic project into a professional career at the intersection of 3D mapping and visualization.
