## Elevator pitch
Google Maps and other navigation apps are sending tourists from Perpignan toward Spain via a narrow, non-carrossable road in Maureillas-las-Illas, transforming what should be a straightforward drive into a nightmare for unsuspecting travelers.

## Takeaways
- Navigation apps including Google Maps direct tourists from Perpignan to Spain via a narrow, non-carrossable road in Maureillas-las-Illas (Pyrénées-Orientales)
- Tourists report "Google Maps nous a dit que c'était la route la plus directe" but find themselves on an impassable path

## Synthesis
In the Pyrénées-Orientales region near Perpignan, Google Maps and other navigation applications are directing tourists heading toward Spain onto a narrow, non-carrossable road through Maureillas-las-Illas. Tourists report being told "Google Maps said it was the most direct route" only to find themselves on an impassable path unsuitable for vehicles. The situation creates dangerous scenarios for drivers unfamiliar with the area, particularly during the busy summer tourist season when many travelers cross the Franco-Spanish border.

The incident highlights a recurring problem with GPS navigation in rural and mountainous border areas, where apps may not accurately distinguish between proper roads and impassable paths. Local authorities have expressed concern about the safety risks, as vehicles attempting to navigate the false départementale can become stuck or damaged. This is not an isolated incident—navigation apps have been criticized elsewhere for sending drivers on dangerous routes, including incidents on hiking trails and in remote areas. The case underscores the need for better verification of road data by mapping providers and for drivers to exercise caution when following GPS directions in unfamiliar terrain, especially when the suggested route seems unusual or too good to be true.
