## Elevator pitch
TomTom's 2025 Traffic Index reveals India's most congested cities, with Bengaluru at 74.4% congestion and Pune at 71.1% ranking among the global top five, costing commuters hundreds of hours per year in rush-hour traffic.

## Takeaways
- Bengaluru ranks as India's most congested city at 74.4% congestion, with average speeds of just 16.6 km/h and commuters losing 168 hours/year in rush hour
- Pune holds second place at 71.1% congestion, with a 5.4 percentage point increase from 2024—the largest jump in the ranking
- Mumbai saw a 3.3 percentage point improvement, dropping to 63.2% congestion, while New Delhi worsened to 60.2%
- Ahmedabad showed the biggest improvement with a 3.8 percentage point drop, reaching 49% congestion
- Two Indian cities (Bengaluru and Pune) rank in the global top five most congested cities worldwide for 2025

## Synthesis
The TomTom 2025 Traffic Index provides a detailed picture of urban congestion across India's major cities. Bengaluru, India's IT capital, leads with 74.4% congestion—up 1.7 points from 2024—where average speeds of 16.6 km/h mean commuters can only travel 4.2 km in 15 minutes and lose approximately 168 hours annually to rush-hour traffic. Pune follows at 71.1% congestion with the steepest year-over-year deterioration (5.4 points), costing commuters 152 hours per year. Both cities rank in the global top five most congested cities worldwide.

The data reveals mixed trends across the country. Mumbai improved by 3.3 points to 63.2%, while New Delhi worsened by 3.5 points to 60.2%. Ahmedabad showed the most dramatic improvement with a 3.8-point drop to 49%, suggesting effective traffic management interventions. Other cities like Kolkata (58.9%), Jaipur (58.7%), Chennai (58.6%), Hyderabad (55.5%), and Ernakulam (54.5%) remain in the middle range. The index measures congestion as the extra travel time compared to free-flow conditions, using anonymized GPS data from connected devices and fleet vehicles. The findings underscore the urgent need for improved public transit, road infrastructure, and urban planning in India's rapidly growing metropolitan areas.
