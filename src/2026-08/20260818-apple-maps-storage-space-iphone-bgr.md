# Why Apple Maps Is Taking Up So Much Storage — And How to Free Up Space
**Source**: https://www.bgr.com/2235462/why-apple-maps-taking-up-storage-space/
**Date**: 2026-08-18
**Author**: José Adorno (BGR)
**Keywords**: Apple Maps, iPhone storage, offline maps, iOS 26, cache, navigation apps

## Elevator pitch
BGR reports that Apple Maps can consume significant iPhone storage through offline maps and cached data, with iOS 26 bugs exacerbating the issue, and provides step-by-step solutions to reclaim space.

## Takeaways
- Apple Maps can consume significant iPhone storage, particularly on devices with 64GB or 128GB capacity
- Two main causes: accumulated offline map data and a common bug in iOS 26 that inflates storage usage
- Users can delete offline maps by going to Apple Maps > profile icon > Offline Maps > Places to remove personalized routes and guides
- Additional storage savings from disabling Location Services features: Routing & Traffic, Improve Maps, and Significant Locations
- iCloud backup settings for Apple Maps can be toggled off, and as a last resort, a factory reset may resolve persistent storage bugs

## Synthesis
BGR's José Adorno reports that Apple Maps can become an unexpected storage hog on iPhones, particularly for users with 64GB or 128GB devices who need to manage limited space carefully. The issue has two primary causes: legitimate accumulation of offline map data and a storage bug that has persisted across iOS 26 and earlier versions.

The offline maps feature, while useful for navigation without cellular connectivity, can silently consume significant storage as users download regional maps, personalized routes, and guides. Many users are unaware of how much data has accumulated until they check their iPhone storage settings. The solution is straightforward: navigating to Apple Maps' Offline Maps section allows users to review and delete downloaded maps and guides they no longer need.

More problematic is the storage bug, which can cause Apple Maps to report using far more storage than expected, even when offline maps are minimal. BGR recommends a multi-step troubleshooting approach: first, clearing offline maps data; second, disabling non-essential Location Services features that feed data to Apple Maps (Routing & Traffic, Improve Maps, Significant Locations); third, toggling off Apple Maps in iCloud Storage backups; and finally, performing a factory reset if the bug persists.

The article highlights a broader issue with mapping apps on mobile devices: the tension between rich offline functionality and storage constraints. As Apple Maps continues to add features like offline navigation, Look Around, and detailed city experiences, the storage footprint grows accordingly. For users of lower-capacity iPhones, managing this tradeoff becomes an ongoing maintenance task.
