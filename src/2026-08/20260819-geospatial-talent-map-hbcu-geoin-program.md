# A Program That's Redrawing the Geospatial Talent Map: HBCU Immersion in GEOIN
**Source**: https://www.geoweeknews.com/articles/a-program-thats-redrawing-the-geospatial-talent-map/
**Date**: August 19, 2026
**Author**: Abigail Hart, Geo Week News
**Keywords**: HBCU, geospatial intelligence, GEOIN, workforce development, GIS education, Harris-Stowe State University, GeoHornet Lab, diversity

## Elevator pitch
A four-week summer immersion program brings students from Historically Black Universities and Colleges to St. Louis to develop geospatial intelligence skills, combining technical GIS training with community belonging to build the next generation of geospatial professionals.

## Synthesis
The HBCU Immersion in GEOIN (Geospatial Intelligence) program addresses a fundamental question facing the geospatial industry: who is going to fill the jobs of the future, and are they ready? The four-week summer program brings students from Historically Black Universities and Colleges (HBCUs) across the United States to St. Louis, where they live on the Harris-Stowe State University (HSSU) campus and work out of the university's GeoHornet Lab at T-REX, the city's downtown innovation hub.

The program's distinction lies in its approach. It is not solely about teaching GIS software or mapping techniques — it is about developing the people who will carry the industry forward. The curriculum introduces students to the city's culture, restaurants, and neighborhoods, because workforce development is not just about skills but about belonging. The program's philosophy, articulated by Dr. Wills, is people-first: start with the community, look at what's happening from a civic and industry standpoint, and build real partnerships.

The program has demonstrated tangible outcomes, with former participants pursuing graduate studies in GIS at institutions like Northwest Missouri State University. For other institutions looking to build similar talent pipelines, the advice is to start with community engagement, identify local industry partners, and create programs that foster both technical competence and personal connection. The geospatial industry faces a well-documented talent gap, and programs like this represent a pathway to filling it while simultaneously diversifying the workforce.
