## Synthesis
Along the Outer Banks of North Carolina, the pace of coastal erosion has reached alarming new levels. In the village of Buxton — a half-hour drive south of Rodanthe, where house collapses had not previously been typical — 20 homes have been lost to the sea since September 2025. Between September 15 and October 28 alone, 15 homes were lost, with as many as five collapsing in a single day. Four more fell in February 2026, and another in early June 2026.

Kristen Stilson, a geographic information system (GIS) analyst for Dare County, is using a DJI Matrice 350 RTK drone to document the erosion with detailed aerial mapping. The drone captures high-resolution imagery of the retreating coastline, the damaged and collapsing homes, and the shrinking buffer between the ocean and North Carolina Highway 12 — the primary road into and out of Dare County. "This was drastic, even for us," said Stilson, who has been mapping the changes as rising seas, strong waves, fierce winds, and sinking land all contribute to the crisis. In some places, the edge of the beach has shrunk by more than 10 feet (3 meters) annually.

The current mitigation strategy is beach nourishment — replenishing sand to bolster the buffer between the water and Highway 12. However, the coast will inevitably continue to erode, and the beach will need to be renourished every five years. The drone mapping provides critical data for planning these interventions, tracking erosion rates, and documenting the accelerating pace of climate-driven coastal change. This case illustrates how drone-based GIS mapping is becoming an essential tool for coastal communities facing existential threats from sea level rise.
