# Google Maps on Android Automotive Stuck in Infinite Routing Loop
**Source**: https://samsungmagazine.eu/fr/2026/08/20/google-maps-android-automotive-fail/
**Date**: August 20, 2026
**Author**: Samsung Magazine
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Android Automotive, Gemini, Polestar, routing bug, infotainment, navigation, EV charging

## Elevator pitch
A bug in Google Maps on Android Automotive is trapping drivers — particularly Polestar 2 owners — in an infinite routing recalculation loop, rendering the built-in navigation system practically unusable, with the issue likely linked to a recent Gemini assistant integration update.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps on Android Automotive enters an infinite routing loop, recalculating constantly even when the vehicle hasn't changed direction
- The issue is most commonly reported by Polestar 2 owners, with similar reports from Honda drivers
- A standard infotainment system restart does not fix the problem; the navigation immediately re-enters the loop
- The bug appeared shortly after a Gemini assistant integration update
- The only workaround is clearing Google Maps cache and data in system settings, then restarting navigation

## Synthesis
Drivers of vehicles equipped with Android Automotive — the full operating system integrated into cars, not to be confused with Android Auto smartphone mirroring — are encountering a severe navigation bug that renders Google Maps practically unusable. The application enters an infinite loop after launch, constantly recalculating routes even when the vehicle has not changed direction. The problem is most commonly reported by Polestar 2 owners, though similar behaviour has been documented by Honda drivers.

The issue appears unrelated to GPS hardware malfunction: after closing Google Maps or when parked, the system correctly determines the vehicle's position. Many affected drivers report the problem emerged shortly after an update related to the Gemini assistant integration, raising concerns about the trade-offs of embedding AI features into critical automotive systems. For electric vehicles, where navigation is directly tied to range estimation and charging stop planning, an unreliable navigation system is more than an inconvenience — it can undermine the entire driving experience.

Google has not yet offered an official fix, but some users have found a temporary workaround: completely clearing the Google Maps cache and app data in system settings, then restarting navigation. The article argues that Android Automotive is supposed to be a mature, deeply integrated platform with absolute reliability, yet the forced integration of AI features like Gemini appears to be coming at the expense of stability in essential services. The author calls for Google to issue a patch before drivers lose confidence in the built-in system and revert to smartphone-mounted navigation.
