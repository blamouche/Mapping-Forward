# Africans Hope a Fairer Map Can Lead to a Fairer World: The Correct the Map Campaign
**Source**: https://www.theglobeandmail.com/world/article-africa-map-cartography-us-government-togo-correct-the-map/
**Date**: August 21, 2026
**Author**: Geoffrey York, The Globe and Mail
**Keywords**: Africa, cartography, Mercator projection, Equal Earth projection, African Union, Correct the Map, UN General Assembly, map distortion, Togo

## Elevator pitch
An African Union-backed campaign advocating the Equal Earth map projection as a replacement for the centuries-old Mercator projection gained fresh momentum after a US State Department map mislabeled African countries, with Togo set to bring the issue to the UN General Assembly.

## Takeaways
- A Trump administration official presented a map of Africa with wrongly labeled and misshapen countries at a global conference in Rio de Janeiro
- The African Union backs the "Correct the Map" campaign advocating the Equal Earth projection over the Mercator projection

## Synthesis
When a US State Department official presented a map of Africa at a global conference in Rio de Janeiro with every highlighted country wrongly labelled, some misshapen or incorrectly located, the blunder reignited a centuries-old debate about cartographic justice. The State Department apologised, but for activists it was merely the latest example of Western cartographic disregard for the African continent.

The African Union-backed "Correct the Map" campaign is now advocating for the adoption of the Equal Earth map projection, developed in 2018 by an international team of cartographers, as a fairer alternative to the Mercator projection that has been the world's standard since 1569. The Mercator projection, originally designed for maritime navigation, dramatically exaggerates the size of land masses near the poles while diminishing the apparent size of Africa and much of the Global South. In reality, Africa is 30.37 million square kilometres — three times larger than Canada and fourteen times larger than Greenland — yet the maps conceal this.

Moky Makura, executive director of Africa No Filter, calls the Mercator map "the world's longest misinformation campaign," arguing that centuries of cartographic distortion have subconsciously diminished the world's perception of Africa's significance. The campaign could reach the United Nations General Assembly at its annual meeting, where Togo's government has pledged to raise the issue. While there is no guarantee of a vote in favour, campaigners are optimistic that the case for proportional accuracy is, as Makura puts it, "a no-brainer."
