# Contribute to Google Maps with Tell Maps: AI-Powered Local Knowledge Sharing
**Source**: https://support.google.com/maps/answer/17515915?hl=en
**Date**: August 21, 2026
**Author**: Google Maps Help
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Tell Maps, AI, crowdsourcing, local knowledge, business hours, place attributes, community contribution

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps has introduced Tell Maps, an AI-powered conversation-based contribution experience that lets users share local knowledge — reporting business hours, marking places closed, editing attributes, sharing tips, and uploading photos — through a guided dialogue interface.

## Takeaways
- Tell Maps is an AI-powered contribution experience in Google Maps available on Android
- Users can report business hours, mark places as closed, edit place attributes, share local tips, and upload photos
- The feature uses a conversational interface to guide users through contributing local knowledge
- Currently limited to US users only
- Tell Maps represents Google's effort to leverage AI for more structured, accessible community contributions

## Synthesis
Google has introduced Tell Maps, an AI-powered contribution feature in Google Maps that transforms the way users share local knowledge. Instead of navigating complex editing forms, users engage in a guided conversation with the system, making it easier and more intuitive to contribute updates about places they know.

The feature allows users to perform several key actions: reporting inaccurate business hours, marking places that have permanently closed, editing place attributes (such as amenities or accessibility features), sharing local tips about a place, and uploading photos. By wrapping these capabilities in a conversational AI interface, Google lowers the barrier to participation — potentially increasing both the volume and quality of community contributions that keep Maps data accurate.

The feature is currently available only on Android and only in the United States, suggesting a phased rollout. Tell Maps represents a broader trend in the mapping industry: using AI not just for routing and recommendations, but for data collection itself. By making contribution more accessible, Google can tap into the long tail of local knowledge that its 2 billion users possess, maintaining the data freshness that gives Google Maps its competitive edge.
