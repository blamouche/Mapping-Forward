# Alternativas Reales a Google Maps Que No Son Estadounidenses
**Source**: https://www.wikiversus.com/moviles/alternativas-a-google-maps/
**Date**: 2026-08-19
**Author**: Rubén Castro, WikiVersus
**Keywords**: Google Maps alternatives, OpenStreetMap, Mapy.com, HERE WeGo, Organic Maps, OsmAnd, Sygic, TomTom, Petal Maps

## Elevator pitch
A comprehensive comparison of non-US Google Maps alternatives reveals that most share the same OpenStreetMap base data underneath, and the real differentiators are interface, offline capabilities, and business listing quality.

## Takeaways
- Most "alternatives" to Google Maps share the same OpenStreetMap base data, differing mainly in interface and features
- Mapy.com has proprietary cartography only in Czechia; elsewhere it uses OpenStreetMap
- HERE WeGo (Netherlands) has its own cartography, originally Nokia's maps division, now owned by Audi/BMW/Daimler
- Organic Maps offers unlimited offline downloads with no advertising, built on OpenStreetMap
- OSM data quality varies significantly by zone depending on local contributor activity
- OpenStreetMap excels in hiking/mountain trails and pedestrian detail in European cities, but lacks business listings and reviews

## Synthesis
WikiVersus provides a detailed comparison of non-US alternatives to Google Maps, beginning with an important caveat: most alternatives share the same OpenStreetMap base data underneath, meaning the real differentiators are interface, navigation, offline functionality, and business data rather than the map itself. OpenStreetMap, started in 2004, is a collaborative geographic database maintained by volunteers worldwide, similar to Wikipedia for maps.

The article highlights a critical nuance: excluding US-based services removes not only Google Maps and Waze (owned by Google since 2013), but also Apple Maps, Bing Maps, and Mapbox, which underlies many seemingly independent apps. What remains is a diverse ecosystem: Mapy.com (Czechia, proprietary maps only at home, OSM elsewhere), HERE WeGo (Netherlands, fully proprietary cartography originally from Nokia, now owned by Audi/BMW/Daimler), Organic Maps (non-profit, OSM-based, unlimited offline, no ads), OsmAnd (Netherlands, OSM-based, highly customizable), Magic Earth (Netherlands, OSM), Sygic (Slovakia, TomTom-based), TomTom GO (Netherlands, proprietary), and Petal Maps (China/Huawei, TomTom and other sources).

A key insight is that OpenStreetMap quality varies dramatically by zone. In neighborhoods with active contributors, OSM can surpass Google Maps in pedestrian detail, trail mapping, and accuracy. In areas with no contributors, it can be worse. Crucially, anyone can fix errors directly, and changes propagate to all apps using the data. The article's practical conclusion: the difficulty in abandoning Google Maps is not about map quality but about business listings, reviews, and opening hours, where Google's commercial data layer remains unmatched. For hikers and mountain users, OpenStreetMap-based apps are already net better than Google.
