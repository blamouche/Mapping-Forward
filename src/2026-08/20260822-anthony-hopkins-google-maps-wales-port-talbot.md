## Synthesis
Sir Anthony Hopkins, the two-time Academy Award-winning actor born in Port Talbot, Wales, has revealed that he uses Google Maps almost daily to virtually revisit the streets of his childhood. Now 88 and living in Los Angeles, Hopkins says he is "a bit too old" to physically return to walk the streets of Margam, Port Talbot, but finds a deep connection through Google Maps' street-level imagery.

"I go to streets and move up the street, the photographs of my streets in Wales... I'm always going back there," Hopkins said in an interview with ITV News. "Now time is running out. I wish I could just get out and walk around but I'm getting a bit too old for that. But I'm always going back there... most days I'm back there somehow."

The digital exploration of his childhood home has directly inspired his creative work. His first album, released toward the end of his ninth decade, draws on childhood memories. One composition was inspired by a specific memory of living on Bracken Road in Port Talbot, where his mother made him lemon curd sandwiches, and he could see across to the moors and the sea, recalling "the stillness, the sound of flies and distant cattle and distant traffic."

Hopkins describes Wales as "the land of hiraeth (longing), the land of my fathers," crediting his identity to growing up there. The story highlights how Google Maps and Street View have become tools not just for navigation but for preserving personal and emotional connections to places people can no longer physically visit, serving as a digital window to personal history and nostalgia.
