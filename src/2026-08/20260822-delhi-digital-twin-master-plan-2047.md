# Delhi To Get Digital Twin As Master Plan 2047 Takes Urban Planning Into 3D Era

## Elevator pitch
Delhi's Master Plan 2047 introduces a 3D Digital Twin of the city combining GIS data, urban sensors, and real-time government systems to simulate development scenarios before implementation, marking a shift from conventional 2D maps to technology-driven urban governance.

## Takeaways
- The Delhi Development Authority is developing a 3D digital replica of Delhi as part of Master Plan 2047
- The digital twin combines GIS data with real-time inputs from urban sensors to simulate and assess development scenarios
- The GIS-based master plan includes layered infrastructure datasets and geo-referenced maps with regularly updated databases
- The approach is aligned with national initiatives including PM Gati Shakti National Master Plan and AMRUT

## Synthesis
The Master Plan for Delhi-2047, notified on August 21, 2026, represents a paradigm shift in Indian urban planning — from conventional 2D maps and manual data to 3D digital modelling and real-time geospatial systems. The Delhi Development Authority (DDA) is developing a "Digital Twin of the city" that will serve as a spatial planning tool, combining geographic information system (GIS) data with real-time inputs from urban sensors and government systems.

The digital twin will enable planners to visualise the city's urban systems and assess the likely impact of proposed developments before they are implemented on the ground. The GIS-based master plan includes layered infrastructure datasets and geo-referenced maps, with provisions for regularly updated digital databases and integration of data from different agencies. Common data standards and spatial systems are proposed to improve interoperability between agencies and enable better coordination in planning and governance.

The initiative is aligned with national frameworks. The PM Gati Shakti National Master Plan uses a GIS-based platform for integrated infrastructure planning and multimodal connectivity. The Atal Mission for Rejuvenation and Urban Transformation (AMRUT) promotes standardised geospatial databases for urban planning. Technology-based interventions under the Swachh Bharat Mission, including GIS mapping for waste management, have also informed the approach. Delhi's digital twin initiative could serve as a model for other Indian cities seeking to leverage geospatial technology for data-driven urban governance.
