# Google Maps Error Causes Monster Traffic on Pont Jacques-Cartier
**Source**: https://www.journaldemontreal.com/2026/08/22/une-erreur-de-google-maps-et-des-travaux-causent-un-trafic-monstre-sur-le-pont-jacques-cartier
**Date**: 2026-08-22
**Author**: Le Journal de Montréal
**Keywords**: Google Maps, traffic, bridge closure error, Montreal, Jacques-Cartier, Samuel-de-Champlain, navigation misinformation

## Elevator pitch
A Google Maps error incorrectly showing the Samuel-de-Champlain bridge as closed forced all traffic onto the Jacques-Cartier bridge, causing monstrous congestion that turned a 20-minute crossing into a 90-minute ordeal.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps incorrectly indicated the Samuel-de-Champlain bridge was closed in the direction of Montreal, when in fact it was fully open
- The error diverted all traffic to the Jacques-Cartier bridge, creating pare-chocs-à-pare-chocs (bumper-to-bumper) congestion
- A normal 20-minute crossing took 1h30 on Saturday, with Google Maps itself estimating over 50 minutes
- The situation was compounded by real construction closures on other bridges (Victoria bridge southbound, Turcot interchange) and major events on Île Sainte-Hélène
- Radio Circulation spokesperson Brigitte Modor confirmed the Samuel-de-Champlain bridge was actually the best route with no traffic

## Synthesis
A significant traffic disruption occurred on Saturday August 22, 2026 in Montreal when a Google Maps error incorrectly indicated that the Samuel-de-Champlain bridge was closed in the direction of Montreal. In reality, only the northbound direction (Montreal to Brossard) was closed for weekend construction. The southbound lane was completely open and flowing freely.

This misinformation caused all drivers relying on Google Maps to route onto the Jacques-Cartier bridge instead, creating massive congestion. Vehicles were practically immobilized on all approach roads on both the Montreal and South Shore sides. A crossing that normally takes about 20 minutes outside of rush hour took 1 hour 30 minutes. Even Google Maps' own estimate of "over 50 minutes" proved optimistic.

The situation was exacerbated by multiple simultaneous factors: the Turcot interchange was closed, the Victoria bridge was closed southbound until September 11, and major events including the Vans Warped Tour and Beach Pro Tour were taking place on Île Sainte-Hélène. Mobilité Montréal had recommended the Jacques-Cartier bridge, unaware that the Google Maps error would concentrate all traffic there.

Brigitte Modor of Radio Circulation urged drivers to use the Samuel-de-Champlain bridge, noting it was virtually empty and the fastest route. The incident illustrates how a single mapping platform error can create cascading traffic effects when combined with real construction and events, highlighting the outsized influence of navigation apps on real-world traffic patterns.
