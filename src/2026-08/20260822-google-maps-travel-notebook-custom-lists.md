# Google Maps Is More Useful as a Travel Notebook Than a Navigation App
**Source**: https://www.androidpolice.com/google-maps-travel-notebook-custom-lists/
**Date**: 2026-08-19
**Author**: Marvin, Android Police
**Keywords**: Google Maps, custom lists, travel planning, itinerary, emoji labels, timeline, collaboration

## Elevator pitch
An Android Police features author argues that Google Maps' custom lists feature transforms it from a navigation tool into a powerful travel planning notebook, combining itinerary building, note-taking, and geographic visualization in one app.

## Takeaways
- Custom lists in Google Maps allow users to create categorized travel collections with emoji icons
- Users can add notes to each pinned location (e.g., what to order at a restaurant)
- Custom list order can be arranged by priority or chronological itinerary
- Lists can be shared with travel companions as collaborators who can add their own pins and notes
- Zooming out on the map reveals all custom icons, exposing geographically unrealistic itineraries
- Google Maps Timeline preserves a detailed record of trips for future reference

## Synthesis
An Android Police author makes the case that Google Maps' custom lists feature has transformed the app from a pure navigation tool into a comprehensive travel planning notebook. The workflow begins with resisting the temptation to save every destination to Starred Places or Favorites, instead creating purpose-built custom lists that support notes, customizable ordering, and emoji icons for visual categorization.

The custom list creation process is straightforward: users access the You tab, create a new list, name it, choose an emoji icon, and enable custom ordering. Each pinned location can receive notes, such as what food to order at a restaurant or which souvenirs to check out at a shop. Lists can be shared with travel companions, who can be invited as collaborators to add their own locations and notes. The geographic visualization aspect is what makes this approach powerful: zooming out on the map reveals all custom icons and their exact locations, instantly exposing inconvenient walking paths, ill-timed transit routes, or geographically unrealistic itineraries before the trip begins.

The author notes that Google Maps Timeline serves as an archival complement, showing where the user went and what time they arrived for each day, mapping out the chronological order of destinations. While Timeline can occasionally get locations wrong during underground transit, the combination of custom lists, notes, reviews, and Timeline creates a geographic record of trips that no standalone note-taking app can match. The article concludes that while Google Maps is not a total replacement for productivity apps like Calendar, Keep, and Drive, its ability to show how a trip comes together in real geographic space makes it an indispensable travel planning tool.
