# GPS et écologie : pourquoi votre trajet met désormais plus de temps sur Waze ou Google Maps
**Source**: https://android-mt.ouest-france.fr/news/gps-et-ecologie-pourquoi-votre-trajet-met-desormais-plus-de-temps-sur-waze-ou-google-maps/209224/

**Author**: Guillaume P., Android MT (Ouest-France)
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Waze, fuel-efficient routing, ecology, GPS navigation, French decree, less polluting routes, speed limiting, environmental regulation

## Elevator pitch
A French decree now requires navigation apps like Google Maps and Waze to default to the least polluting route rather than the fastest, meaning your GPS may suggest longer but more environmentally friendly journeys.

## Takeaways
- A French decree imposes the least polluting route as the default option in GPS navigation apps
- Google Maps and Waze must now limit suggested speeds and prioritize fuel-efficient routing
- Users may notice their journeys take longer than before as environmental considerations override pure speed optimization
- The regulation reflects a growing policy trend of using digital navigation tools as levers for reducing transportation emissions
- Users can still manually override the default to select the fastest route, but the eco-friendly option is presented first

## Synthesis
On July 25, 2026, Android MT (Ouest-France) reported on a French decree that is changing how GPS navigation applications like Google Maps and Waze calculate and present routes to users. The decree mandates that navigation apps must now default to suggesting the least polluting route rather than the fastest one, marking a significant policy intervention in the intersection of digital mapping and environmental regulation.

The regulation requires two key changes to navigation app behavior. First, the default route suggested to users must be the one with the lowest environmental impact, not the quickest. Second, the apps must limit the suggested speeds presented to drivers, aligning with fuel-efficiency objectives rather than pure time optimization. For users accustomed to GPS apps prioritizing speed above all else, this means journeys may now take longer by default.

This decree is part of a broader French and European policy framework aimed at reducing transportation-related emissions. By leveraging the widespread adoption of navigation apps — which millions of drivers consult daily — regulators can influence driving behavior at scale. The approach treats digital navigation as a public policy lever, not merely a consumer tool.

The change echoes Google Maps' existing fuel-efficient routing feature, which the company introduced globally as an opt-in setting. The French decree effectively makes this behavior mandatory by default, removing the user's choice to always see the fastest route first. Users can still manually override the default to select a faster route, but the eco-friendly option is presented as the primary recommendation.

The regulation raises interesting questions about the role of mapping platforms in environmental governance. While fuel-efficient routing can reduce individual fuel consumption, critics note that longer routes may have unintended consequences, such as increased total vehicle-kilometers traveled if rerouting sends drivers through less optimal paths. The effectiveness of the decree will depend on how navigation apps implement the requirement and whether drivers accept the trade-off between time and environmental impact.
