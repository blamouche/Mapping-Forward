# NGT Directs Bhopal to Use GIS Mapping for Forest Encroachment Removal
**Source**: https://www.livelaw.in/amp/news-updates/ngt-bhopal-remove-encroachments-from-forest-land-roads-546857
**Date**: 2026-08-21
**Author**: Live Law
**Keywords**: NGT, Bhopal, GIS mapping, forest encroachment, geo-tagging, satellite imagery, afforestation, National Green Tribunal, India

## Elevator pitch
India's National Green Tribunal has ordered Bhopal authorities to use GIS and satellite imagery to demarcate forest land and remove illegal encroachments within three months, mandating geo-tagging and a comprehensive tree census.

## Takeaways
- The NGT Central Zone Bench directed Bhopal's Collector and Divisional Forest Officer to conduct a joint GIS/GPS survey to demarcate all forest land within the district
- Illegal encroachments must be removed within three months where there are no lawful impediments
- Bhopal's forest cover is below 12% of district area per ISFR 2023, with a decline of 0.54 sq.km since 2021
- The tribunal mandated GIS mapping of the Bhopal Municipal Corporation area including road verges, along with a tree census
- Compensatory plantation must be monitored for 15 years by a committee of Forest, Municipal, Horticulture, and State PCB members
- The NGT directed rules requiring Miyawaki plots on vacant municipal land, vertical green facades on metro pillars, and green roofs on commercial buildings

## Synthesis
The National Green Tribunal (NGT) Central Zone Bench has issued a sweeping order directing the Madhya Pradesh government and Bhopal Municipal Corporation to use GIS mapping and satellite imagery to identify and remove illegal encroachments on forest land. The bench, comprising judicial member Justice Sheo Kumar Singh and expert member Sudhir Kumar Chaturvedi, issued the directives after noting that Bhopal's forest cover is less than 12% of the district area according to the Indian State Forest Report (ISFR) 2023, with a decline of approximately 0.54 sq.km between 2021 and 2023.

The tribunal directed Bhopal's Collector and Divisional Forest Officer to undertake a joint survey and demarcate all recorded forest land preferably through GIS/GPS and satellite imagery. Where illegal encroachment is found without lawful impediment, authorities must remove it within three months. The NGT also called for GIS mapping of the entire Bhopal Municipal Corporation area, including central and side verges of roads, along with a comprehensive tree census.

Beyond encroachment removal, the tribunal mandated systemic afforestation measures. Compensatory plantation within the municipal area must be monitored for 15 years by a multi-departmental committee. The NGT directed that vacant municipal land parcels over 200 sq. meters allocate at least 25% of their footprint to Miyawaki plots, that the Bhopal Metro Rail Corporation engineer vertical modular green facades on concrete pillars, and that new commercial and residential structures over 500 sq. meters cover 40% of unshaded roof space with living green roofs. The tribunal also recommended using treated wastewater from nearby Sewage Treatment Plants to support plantation growth, and ward-wise public participation to engage citizens in conservation efforts.
