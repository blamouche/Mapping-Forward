# 3366 Hectares Burned in the Hautes Fagnes: Map Visualization Puts the Scale in Perspective
**Source**: https://www.rtbf.be/article/3366-hectares-ont-ete-brules-dans-les-hautes-fagnes-qu-est-ce-que-cela-represente-a-l-echelle-de-liege-ou-namur-11773834
**Date**: 2026-08-22
**Author**: Marie-Laure Mathot (RTBF)
**Keywords**: fire mapping, Copernicus, Sentinel-2, satellite imagery, Belgium, Hautes Fagnes, map visualization, EFFIS

## Elevator pitch
Belgium's RTBF used satellite imagery from Copernicus Sentinel-2 and map overlays to visualize the 3,366 hectares burned in the Hautes Fagnes fire, translating the abstract figure by overlaying it on maps of Brussels, Liège, Namur, and Charleroi for scale.

## Takeaways
- The Service public de Wallonie (SPW) reported 3,366 hectares burned in the Hautes Fagnes fire, based on crisis management data from the Cortex coordination center
- The European Copernicus Sentinel-2 satellite captured the fire on August 16, 2026, using infrared spectral bands to reveal the burn trace through cloud cover, with active fires shown in orange and vegetation in bright green
- The European Forest Fire Information System (EFFIS) counted 2,825 hectares burned, with 86.5% located in Natura 2000 protected areas
- RTBF created map overlays translating the burned area onto major Belgian cities: from Grâce-Hollogne to Herstal in Liège, La Plante to Saint-Marc in Namur, and Gilly to Monceau-sur-Sambre in Charleroi
- The SPW noted the data is evolving as the situation develops, with satellite imagery limited by heavy cloud cover during the fire

## Synthesis
The Hautes Fagnes fire in Belgium has become a case study in how map visualization can make abstract disaster statistics comprehensible to the public. The Service public de Wallonie (SPW) published data indicating 3,366 hectares burned, calculated from information transmitted through the Cortex coordination center and the Department of Nature and Forests. However, the raw number — 3,366 hectares — is difficult for most people to contextualize without geographic reference.

RTBF's approach to solving this communication challenge was twofold. First, they leveraged satellite imagery from the European Copernicus Sentinel-2 mission, which captured the fire area on August 16, 2026. The image was presented in both natural colors and false colors, using infrared spectral bands to highlight the fire's trace through atmospheric haze. Active fires appeared in vivid orange, while vegetation showed as bright green, making the burn scar visible despite heavy cloud cover that prevented a standard satellite photo.

Second, RTBF created comparative map overlays that translated the 3,366-hectare area onto Belgium's major urban areas. When overlaid on Brussels, the burn area covers a significant portion of the capital region. In Liège, it stretches from Grâce-Hollogne to Herstal. In Namur, it extends from La Plante to Saint-Marc. In Charleroi, it spans from Gilly to Monceau-sur-Sambre. These overlays transform an abstract figure into an immediately graspable geographic comparison.

The European Forest Fire Information System (EFFIS) provided additional context, reporting a slightly lower figure of 2,825 hectares but noting that 86.5% of the burned area falls within Natura 2000 protected zones, underscoring the ecological significance of the damage. The discrepancy between SPW and EFFIS figures reflects the evolving nature of the data and different measurement methodologies.
