# After SDVs, Next Is AI: Future Cars' Battleground Is Context Data
**Source**: https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/95011/after-sdv-next-is-ai-future-cars-battleground-context-data
**Date**: August 22, 2026
**Author**: Choi Jae-won, Digital Today
**Keywords**: TomTom, AI-defined vehicles, software-defined vehicles, context data, Leo Sei, autonomous driving, navigation, high-definition maps, energy management

## Elevator pitch
TomTom CPO Leo Sei argues that the auto industry's competitive axis is shifting from software-defined vehicles (SDVs) to AI-defined vehicles (AIDVs), where the ability to understand and respond to roads, drivers, and driving conditions using context data matters more than which software is installed.

## Takeaways
- The competitive focus is moving from SDVs (software-defined vehicles) to AIDVs (AI-defined vehicles) where performance is determined by continuously learning ML models
- Context data — combining sensors with detailed maps — enables vehicles to anticipate unseen risks including lane splits, speed-limit changes, and road closures
- AI is not limited to autonomous driving: it extends to navigation, infotainment, battery and energy management, and climate control
- Qualcomm and Nissan have also referenced the AIDV concept, presenting it as the next technology trend
- AI is also being used to create road data itself, by analyzing sensor data to build and update maps

## Synthesis
TomTom CPO Leo Sei's analysis of the automotive industry's evolution from SDVs to AIDVs positions context data as the decisive competitive battleground. In SDVs, centralized computing and always-on connectivity enable software updates and new services post-factory. AIDVs go further: vehicle performance and behavior are determined by continuously learning machine-learning models rather than prewritten rules, fundamentally changing how vehicles adapt to their environment.

The key differentiator is context data. Vehicles using only sensors respond to what cameras or radar currently detect. Sei argues that combining sensor data with high-definition maps and road information allows vehicles to anticipate situations beyond the driver's view — lane splits, speed-limit changes, road closures, and potential risks. This could reduce sudden braking or steering corrections, enabling more natural driver assistance. The use of AI extends well beyond autonomous driving into navigation, infotainment, battery and energy management, and climate-control systems.

Critically, AI is also being used to create road data itself. By analyzing sensor data collected from connected vehicles, companies can build and update maps more efficiently than traditional surveying methods. This creates a flywheel effect: more connected vehicles generate more data, which improves maps, which in turn enables better AI-driven vehicle behavior. TomTom's positioning in this ecosystem leverages its decades of mapmaking expertise and proprietary data assets, including 2.8 trillion kilometers of global distance data and continuous live traffic feeds.
