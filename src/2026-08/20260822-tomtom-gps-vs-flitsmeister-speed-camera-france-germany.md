# Why TomTom GPS Is Better Than Flitsmeister for Driving in France and Germany
**Source**: https://www.iphoned.nl/nieuws/tomtom-gps-beter-flitsmeister/
**Date**: 2026-08-22
**Author**: iPhoned.nl
**Keywords**: TomTom, Flitsmeister, speed camera warnings, France, Germany, danger zones, navigation apps, legal compliance, traffic law

## Elevator pitch
TomTom GPS handles speed camera warnings legally in France by switching to "danger zones" instead of exact locations, while Flitsmeister shows precise positions — risking fines of up to €1,500 for users who don't disable the feature.

## Takeaways
- France prohibits real-time speed camera location warnings while driving; TomTom complies by showing "danger zones" (300m–4km depending on road type) instead of exact camera positions
- Flitsmeister shows exact camera locations in France, shifting legal responsibility to the user, with potential fines up to €1,500
- Germany also bans speed camera warnings while driving; both apps advise users to disable the feature before crossing the border
- TomTom offers full speed camera warnings in the Netherlands and Luxembourg where it is legal
- The distinction matters most for drivers regularly crossing between countries with different warning legislation

## Synthesis
A key difference between TomTom GPS and Flitsmeister emerges when driving abroad, particularly in France and Germany where speed camera warning regulations differ significantly from the Netherlands. The article highlights how TomTom's compliance approach makes it the safer choice for international drivers.

In France, the law prohibits being warned about the exact location of speed cameras while driving. TomTom adapts to this by switching from precise camera alerts to "danger zones" (zones de danger) — areas where a camera might be located but isn't necessarily present. These zones range from 300 meters in built-up areas to 2 kilometers on secondary roads and 4 kilometers on highways. This approach complies with French law while still providing useful cautionary information.

Flitsmeister, by contrast, shows exact camera locations in France and places the responsibility on the user to disable the feature. This creates legal risk: French fines for using speed camera warning devices can reach €1,500. Flitsmeister's website explicitly states that users are responsible for turning the signaling on or off.

In Germany regulations are similar — speed camera warnings apps are prohibited while driving. Both TomTom and Flitsmeister advise users to disable warnings before entering Germany, though other Flitsmeister alerts remain permitted. For the Netherlands and Luxembourg, TomTom provides full speed camera warnings as the practice is legal. The article concludes that TomTom is particularly advantageous for drivers regularly traveling through France, where its automatic compliance with local legislation eliminates the legal risk that Flitsmeister users face.
