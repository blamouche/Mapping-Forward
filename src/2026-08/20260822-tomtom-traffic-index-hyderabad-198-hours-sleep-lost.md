# Hyderabad Residents Lose 198 Hours of Sleep a Year to Traffic and Heat
**Source**: https://hyderabadmail.com/hyderabad-residents-lose-198-hours-sleep-traffic-heat/
**Date**: 2026-08-22
**Author**: Hyderabad Mail
**Keywords**: TomTom Traffic Index, Hyderabad, traffic congestion, sleep loss, urban heat island, Climate Central, night-time temperature, India

## Elevator pitch
TomTom Traffic Index data reveals Hyderabad residents lose 198 hours of sleep annually — 123 hours from peak-hour traffic congestion and 75 hours from rising night-time temperatures — highlighting how urban mobility failures impact personal health.

## Takeaways
- Hyderabad residents lose 198 hours of sleep per year: 123 hours from traffic congestion and 75 hours from rising night-time temperatures
- The TomTom Traffic Index recorded a 55.5% congestion level for Hyderabad, with evening traffic heavier than morning
- A 10-km journey takes 32.37 minutes normally but rises to 38.5 minutes during evening peaks, with speeds dropping to 10 kmph
- Hyderabad has over 80 lakh (8 million) vehicles with underdeveloped public transport

## Synthesis
A combination of TomTom Traffic Index data and a Climate Central study reveals that Hyderabad residents lose approximately 198 hours of sleep every year due to two urban factors: traffic congestion during peak hours (123 hours) and rising night-time temperatures (75 hours). The findings illustrate how urban infrastructure and climate change directly impact public health.

The TomTom Traffic Index, which covers 500 cities across 62 countries, recorded a 55.5% congestion level for Hyderabad. Evening traffic is worse than morning congestion, with a 10-km journey taking 38.5 minutes during evening peaks compared to 32.37 minutes under normal conditions. Average vehicle speeds in the city run about 15 kmph — half the ideal 25-30 kmph — and drop to around 10 kmph during evening peaks. Hyderabad's 8 million vehicles and underdeveloped public transport system contribute to the problem.

After enduring long commutes, residents face another sleep disruptor: rising night-time temperatures. Climate Central's study of 1,338 cities found that concrete buildings and tar roads absorb heat during the day and release it slowly after sunset, preventing temperatures from falling. Hyderabad loses 75 hours of sleep annually to this effect, while nearby Warangal loses 82 hours.

Among major Indian cities, Bengaluru records the highest traffic-related sleep loss at 168 hours, followed by Kolkata at 132 hours, Chennai at 152 hours, and Mumbai at 126 hours. Chennai faces the highest temperature-related sleep loss at 93 hours. The data underscores how rapidly growing Indian cities need integrated transport and urban cooling strategies to protect residents' health and well-being.
