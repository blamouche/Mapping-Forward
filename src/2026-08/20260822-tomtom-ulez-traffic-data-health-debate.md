## Synthesis
The article highlights how TomTom's traffic data is being used beyond its traditional navigation applications — as evidence in public health debates about urban air quality. A study found that London's Ultra Low Emission Zone (ULEZ) was linked to rapid recovery in children's lung growth, with children whose lung development had been stunted by air pollution showing striking improvement after the zone's implementation.

A TomTom executive emphasized the virtues of traffic data in this debate, positioning the company's mobility data assets as a valuable input for evidence-based urban policy. The ULEZ, which charges vehicles that do not meet emissions standards to drive in central London, has been controversial, and robust traffic and emissions data is critical to evaluating its effectiveness.

This represents a broader trend of navigation and mapping companies finding new value streams for their traffic data assets. TomTom's real-time and historical traffic data, collected from millions of connected vehicles, can quantify changes in vehicle volumes and patterns before and after policy interventions. When combined with public health data, this creates a compelling evidence base for or against urban emission policies. The article suggests TomTom is actively engaging in these policy conversations rather than simply providing data passively.
