# Waze and Google Maps "Shortcut" at Perthus Turns Into Nightmare for Residents
**Source**: https://www.lindependant.fr/2026/08/21/ce-raccourci-conseille-par-waze-et-google-maps-pour-contourner-les-bouchons-du-perthus-continue-de-tourner-au-cauchemar-13517388.php
**Date**: 2026-08-21
**Author**: Driss Chaït, L'Indépendant
**Keywords**: Waze, Google Maps, Perthus, alternative route, Maureillas-las-Illas, traffic diversion, rural roads, navigation apps, municipal ordinance, France

## Elevator pitch
Navigation apps are routing drivers through a narrow dirt track near the Spanish border to avoid Perthus traffic jams, turning a peaceful village into a dangerous bottleneck despite a municipal ban on oversized vehicles.

## Takeaways
- Waze and Google Maps suggest an alternative route through the col de Manrella and Las Illas to avoid Perthus/Boulou traffic jams
- The route includes a narrow unpaved road designed for emergency access, now flooded with cars, camper vans, and trucks
- A municipal ordinance banning camper vans, trailers, and trucks was issued but remains ineffective two weeks later
- Residents report over 100 vehicles on delivery days, with vehicles unable to pass each other on the narrow road
- A 91-year-old local describes the situation as unprecedented: "C'est la première fois que je vois ça"
- A caravan previously ended up in a ditch, highlighting the danger of the diversion

## Synthesis
For over ten days, residents of Las Illas in the commune of Maureillas-las-Illas (Pyrénées-Orientales) have been dealing with a constant stream of vehicles sent by Waze and Google Maps through their small village. The navigation apps suggest the col de Manrella route as a shortcut to avoid the notorious traffic jams at the Perthus border crossing and Le Boulou. However, the route includes a narrow, unpaved road primarily designed for emergency vehicle access.

The situation has become dangerous. A local farmer reports encountering over 100 vehicles on Tuesdays and Fridays when returning from deliveries, with confused tourists following their GPS onto roads they didn't expect. A 91-year-old resident named Claude described being blocked by oncoming vehicles in areas where two cars cannot pass, requiring one to reverse to a passing point on a road with blind corners and no visibility.

The municipality issued an ordinance banning camper vans, trailers, and trucks from the route, but as of August 21, the problem persists nearly two weeks later. A caravan had already ended up in a ditch earlier in the month. Residents and elected officials are at their wits' end, with the situation showing no signs of improvement.

This case exemplifies a growing problem where navigation apps redirect traffic through inappropriate rural roads, creating safety hazards and quality-of-life issues for small communities. It follows similar incidents reported elsewhere in France and highlights the tension between algorithmic traffic optimization and local road infrastructure capacity.
