# Yandex Digitally Added Forest to Maps to Hide Russian Military Sites
**Source**: https://meduza.io/en/news/2026/08/17/yandex-digitally-added-forest-to-maps-to-hide-military-sites
**Date**: 2026-08-17
**Author**: Meduza
**Keywords**: Yandex, satellite imagery, map censorship, military sites, Kronstadt, Russia, digital manipulation

## Elevator pitch
Yandex has been caught digitally pasting copied forest fragments over military installations on its online maps, marking a new method of concealing sensitive sites that goes beyond previous blurring techniques.

## Takeaways
- Yandex digitally added forest cover over a military installation near Kronstadt on its online maps
- Finnish outlet YLE reported that copied fragments of forest appear in satellite images where Russian missile batteries are stationed
- This marks a new concealment method; previously Yandex simply blurred images of military sites
- Foreign mapping services from Google, Apple, and Microsoft show the missile batteries in pre-April 2026 images but not in more recent ones
- Military expert Marko Eklund said the concealment makes no operational sense since Western militaries use their own sources
- Yandex also conceals airfields and Putin's Valdai residence on its maps

## Synthesis
Finnish outlet YLE has discovered that Yandex has moved beyond blurring satellite imagery of sensitive Russian military sites to a new technique: digitally pasting copied forest fragments over the installations. The discovery was made in satellite imagery of the area around Kronstadt, where Russian missile batteries are stationed. Copied fragments of forest appear in the image, covering the military infrastructure in a way that looks superficially natural but is clearly artificial upon close inspection.

This escalation in map manipulation is notable because it represents a shift from obvious blurring (which signals that something is being hidden) to more subtle digital alteration that attempts to make the landscape appear undisturbed. Foreign mapping services from Google, Apple, and Microsoft show the missile batteries near Kronstadt in images taken before April 2026, but the batteries are absent from more recent images, suggesting the mobile missile systems may have been relocated.

Military expert Marko Eklund told YLE that the concealment makes little operational sense. Ukrainian and Western militaries rely on their own intelligence sources rather than Yandex maps, so the digital forest serves primarily as a domestic information control tool rather than a genuine military deception. Eklund also suggested that Russian authorities could be the ones requiring Yandex to hide certain sites. The case highlights the growing tension between commercial mapping platforms and state censorship requirements, particularly in countries where mapping services operate under government pressure. It also underscores the importance of multiple independent satellite imagery sources for maintaining an accurate picture of the world.
