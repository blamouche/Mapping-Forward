# Android Auto perd de nouveau les couleurs du trafic sur Google Maps
**Source**: https://android-mt.ouest-france.fr/news/android-auto-perd-de-nouveau-les-couleurs-du-trafic-sur-google-maps/211882/
**Date**: 2026-08-24
**Author**: Ramza Lefort, Android MT
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Android Auto, traffic colors, bug, Immersive Navigation, workaround, navigation

## Elevator pitch
The Google Maps traffic color bug has resurfaced on Android Auto after being fixed on mobile, with users linking the disappearance of traffic lines to the Immersive Navigation rollout and finding no satisfactory workaround.

## Takeaways
- Traffic colors (green, yellow, orange, red) have disappeared on Android Auto but work on the phone app
- The bug is linked to the deployment of Immersive Navigation, which introduced 3D buildings and visual enhancements
- The issue is server-side rather than tied to a specific app version, making it hard to pinpoint
- Workarounds include switching to overhead view, uninstalling Maps updates, or disabling auto-updates
- An Android Auto v17.5 user with Maps v26.33 still experienced the bug, ruling out a simple version fix
- Google has made no public statement about this dysfunction
- The loss of traffic colors significantly reduces Google Maps' value while driving

## Synthesis
Android MT reports that the Google Maps traffic color bug, recently resolved on smartphones, tablets, and computers, has resurfaced specifically on Android Auto in-car displays. Multiple drivers have reported on the Android Auto community forum and Reddit that the green, yellow, orange, and red traffic lines indicating road conditions no longer appear on their vehicle screens, while the same Google account works normally on the phone app.

The timing is suspicious: several users connect the issue to the rollout of Immersive Navigation, Google Maps' new interface deployed on Android Auto the previous month, which introduced 3D buildings, enhanced highway ramps, and visual adjustments for more intuitive driving. Because this feature relies on server-side deployment rather than a specific app version, users cannot reliably determine if it is the trigger.

Workarounds exist but are unsatisfactory. Switching to overhead view restores traffic display but only on major roads with zoom too large for practical navigation. Uninstalling Google Maps updates reverts to the old interface but loses recent features. One Reddit user reports the bug persists even with Android Auto v17.5 and Maps v26.33, indicating a version-agnostic problem. Google has not publicly addressed the issue, which is particularly frustrating given that colored traffic display is one of the most useful visual cues for anticipating slowdowns without taking eyes off the road.
