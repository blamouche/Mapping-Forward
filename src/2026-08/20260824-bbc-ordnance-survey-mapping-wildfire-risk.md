# Ordnance Survey Mapping Data Helps Identify Future Wildfire Risk
**Source**: https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cp3rvd3ygqvo
**Date**: 2026-08-24
**Author**: Mike Apps, BBC
**Keywords**: Ordnance Survey, wildfire risk, climate change, drone mapping, GIS, emergency services, flood mapping, UK mapping

## Elevator pitch
Britain's national mapping agency Ordnance Survey is using drone, aircraft, and satellite data to help government, emergency services, and businesses predict and prepare for future wildfire risks driven by climate change.

## Takeaways
- Ordnance Survey (OS) provides mapping data to identify areas liable to flooding, wildfires, and other climate impacts
- OS operates a drone team of 21 pilots alongside aircraft and 200 field surveyors, achieving 2cm mapping accuracy
- More than 1.8 million homes are located within 100 metres of urban boundaries where most fires occur
- Firefighters used OS data during the New Forest wildfire on August 9, 2026, to plan routes and check gate widths
- OS monitors extreme heat, subsidence, winter flooding, and coastal erosion in addition to wildfire risk
- The agency checks new housing developments every two months during construction
- OS data helps emergency services decide resource placement, create fire plans, and identify at-risk homes

## Synthesis
The BBC reports that Ordnance Survey (OS), Britain's national mapping service, is leveraging its extensive geographic database to help predict and mitigate climate-related risks, particularly wildfires. The agency has evolved from traditional manual surveying to a modern operation employing 21 drone pilots, aircraft, and 200 field surveyors who achieve mapping accuracy of up to 2 centimeters.

During the New Forest wildfire on August 9, 2026, firefighters used OS mapping data to plan their response, including checking whether gates were wide enough for vehicles to pass through. This real-world application demonstrates the critical role of high-resolution mapping in emergency response. OS analysis has revealed that over 1.8 million homes sit within 100 metres of urban boundaries — areas that have seen the greatest proportion of fires in recent years.

Beyond wildfires, OS monitors extreme heat, subsidence, winter flooding, and coastal erosion. The agency visits new housing developments every two months during construction to ensure emergency services have up-to-date information on the quickest routes to specific houses. Simon Navin, Head of Geospatial Services at OS, emphasizes that combining building composition data with climate projections gives authorities the tools to make informed choices about how to prepare for a changing climate, particularly as extended drought periods create additional wildfire risks.
