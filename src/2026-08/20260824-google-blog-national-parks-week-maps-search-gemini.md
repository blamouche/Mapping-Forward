# Celebrate 110 Years of National Parks with Maps, Search, and Gemini
**Source**: https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/national-parks-week-google-2026/
**Date**: 2026-08-24
**Author**: Claire Herrin, Google Blog
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Google Search, Gemini, national parks, hiking, road trips, Ask Maps, AI Mode, travel planning

## Elevator pitch
Google showcases how its ecosystem of tools — Maps, Search, Gemini, and Ask Maps — can help people plan and enjoy visits to U.S. national parks during the 110th anniversary celebration of the National Park Service.

## Takeaways
- National Parks Week celebrates 110 years of the National Park Service
- Search interest in hiking reached an all-time high, with "hiking for beginners" queries up 165%
- Rocky Mountain National Park is the top trending park searched alongside "beginner hiking"
- AI Mode in Search can help find hiking gear tailored to specific needs
- Ask Maps can suggest scenic stops, restaurants, and hotels for road trips
- Gemini Live can identify plants, rocks, and landmarks in real time via camera
- Acadia National Park was the top trending national park travel itinerary on Search
- Great Sand Dunes National Park is the top trending road trip destination

## Synthesis
As the U.S. celebrates 110 years of the National Park Service, Google has published a comprehensive guide showing how its products can enhance outdoor adventures. The article reveals that interest in hiking has reached an all-time high, with a 165% increase in "hiking for beginners" queries over the past month, indicating a surge of newcomers to outdoor activities.

Google positions its ecosystem as a complete travel planning toolkit. AI Mode in Search helps users find specialized gear like hiking boots with good ankle support, providing tailored product listings. Ask Maps serves as a road trip planning assistant, suggesting scenic stops, dining options, and hotels along the way, while also providing traffic delay alerts. The Gemini app offers real-time nature identification through Gemini Live — users can point their camera at wildflowers, rock formations, or trail markers to get instant information.

The article also highlights the United Parks of America collection on Google Arts & Culture, which offers interactive experiences like virtual trips through the Everglades and wildlife discovery in Yellowstone. This initiative demonstrates Google's strategy of integrating AI across its product suite to capture the growing outdoor recreation market.
