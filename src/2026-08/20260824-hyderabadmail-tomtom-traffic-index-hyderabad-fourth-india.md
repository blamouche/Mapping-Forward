# Hyderabad Ranks Fourth in India for Slow Traffic According to TomTom Traffic Index
**Source**: https://hyderabadmail.com/hyderabad-fourth-india-slowest-traffic-tomtom-index/
**Date**: 2026-08-24
**Author**: Hyderabad Mail
**Keywords**: TomTom, TomTom Traffic Index, Hyderabad, India, traffic congestion, urban mobility, GIS, smart cities

## Elevator pitch
Hyderabad ranks fourth among Indian cities with the slowest traffic, with a 10-km journey taking over 32 minutes on average, as vehicle growth far outpaces road network expansion according to the TomTom Traffic Index.

## Takeaways
- A 10-km journey in Hyderabad takes an average of 32 minutes 37 seconds
- Bengaluru is slowest at 36 minutes, followed by Kolkata (35 min) and Pune (33 min); Mumbai is faster at 28 min
- Average vehicle speed in Hyderabad is 23 kmph, dropping to 5-10 kmph during peak hours and rain
- Commuters lose an average of 123 hours per year to traffic congestion
- Over 3,000 new vehicles are added to Hyderabad's roads daily
- Road infrastructure expansion has not kept pace with vehicle population growth
- The IT corridor (Gachibowli, Raidurg, Kondapur) sees heavy congestion from 9am to 11:30am and evenings until 9pm

## Synthesis
The TomTom Traffic Index reveals that Hyderabad ranks fourth among Indian cities with the slowest traffic, with a 10-kilometer journey taking an average of 32 minutes and 37 seconds. Bengaluru leads the slowest cities at 36 minutes for the same distance, followed by Kolkata at 35 minutes and Pune at 33 minutes, while Mumbai performs better at 28 minutes. The average vehicle speed in Hyderabad is approximately 23 kmph, but this drops dramatically to between 5 and 10 kmph during peak hours and rainfall.

The report highlights a structural mismatch: over 3,000 new vehicles are added to Hyderabad's roads every day, while road infrastructure has not expanded at a corresponding pace. This imbalance, combined with unplanned one-way restrictions, traffic rule violations, and an inadequate public transport system, has complicated traffic management. The city's rapid expansion as a global IT hub and continuing migration have increased traffic volumes significantly.

Congestion patterns vary by corridor. The western IT corridor experiences heavy traffic during morning (9-11:30am) and evening commute hours. Central commercial areas see peak traffic from noon to 3pm and again in the evening. Eastern and northern corridors face heavy traffic between 8-10:30am and 6-9:30pm. Commuters lose an estimated 123 hours per year to congestion, underscoring the widening gap between vehicle population growth and road network capacity.
