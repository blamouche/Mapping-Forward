# Google Maps : corrigé sur smartphone, ce bug handicapant touche maintenant Android Auto
**Source**: https://www.phonandroid.com/google-maps-corrige-sur-smartphone-ce-bug-handicapant-touche-maintenant-android-auto.html
**Date**: 2026-08-24
**Author**: Céline Demoineret, PhonAndroid
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Android Auto, traffic colors, bug, Immersive Navigation, navigation, traffic display

## Elevator pitch
After being fixed on smartphones and desktop, the Google Maps traffic color display bug has migrated to Android Auto, with users reporting that the Immersive Navigation update is the likely culprit behind disappearing traffic lines.

## Takeaways
- The traffic color display bug has been fixed on iOS, Android, and desktop but now affects Android Auto
- Users report colored traffic lines (green, orange, red) disappearing on Android Auto screens
- The bug is confined to Android Auto — the same account works normally on the phone app
- The issue appeared after the Immersive Navigation update that redesigned Google Maps' trip display with 3D views
- No fix is currently available from Google for the Android Auto variant of the bug
- Users have tried clearing cache, reinstalling updates, and changing settings without success

## Synthesis
PhonAndroid reports that a Google Maps bug causing traffic color lines to disappear has shifted platforms. Previously affecting iOS, Android, and desktop users — where it was recently resolved — the dysfunction now targets Android Auto users. The traffic feature, which displays green, orange, and red lines to indicate traffic density, is a crucial navigation aid that helps drivers anticipate slowdowns.

Multiple user reports on Reddit and the Android Auto community forum point to a common origin: the bug appeared after the Google Maps update that introduced Immersive Navigation, a redesigned trip display featuring detailed 3D views. The problem is specifically confined to the Android Auto interface — the same Google account displays traffic colors normally when using the mobile app directly. This confinement to the in-car interface makes it harder for Google's technical teams to identify the exact cause.

Users have attempted various fixes including clearing cache and storage, uninstalling and reinstalling updates, but none have proven successful. The traffic color option does not appear in Android Auto settings, leaving users without a manual toggle. The article highlights the irony that Google Maps is investing heavily in a more immersive and readable navigation experience while simultaneously losing one of its most useful visual features for drivers.
