# Ahmedabad Launches ₹240 Crore GIS Mapping Project for 3D City View Ahead of Commonwealth Games 2030
**Source**: https://www.bhaskarenglish.in/local/gujarat/ahmedabad/news/ahmedabad-gis-mapping-project-commonwealth-games-2030-properties-138829646.html
**Date**: 2026-08-25

## Elevator pitch
Ahmedabad's Municipal Corporation has approved a ₹240 crore citywide GIS mapping project to create a 3D digital model, geo-code 25 lakh properties, and map underground utilities — preparing the city for the 2030 Commonwealth Games while modernizing urban governance.

## Takeaways
- The e-Governance Committee approved a modern GIS database for all of Ahmedabad at a cost of ₹240 crore
- Around 25 lakh (2.5 million) properties will be geo-coded in two phases: 9 lakh in phase one, 16 lakh in phase two
- The project covers roughly 625 sq km and will create a three-dimensional digital representation of the city
- Aerial surveys using drones and aircraft with high-resolution cameras will create the 3D city map
- Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR) will capture precise depth and location of underground drainage, water, and storm water lines
- Every property will receive a unique "Digital Door ID" via door-to-door mobile app survey
- 360-degree street-level recording using vehicle-mounted sensors, similar to Google Street View
- The project will be executed by Genesis International Corporation Limited over two and a half years, with 5 years of O&M

## Synthesis
Ahmedabad, India's largest city in Gujarat, is undertaking one of the most ambitious municipal GIS mapping projects in the country as part of its preparations for hosting the 2030 Commonwealth Games. The Municipal Corporation's e-Governance Committee has approved a ₹240 crore ($28.7 million) contract to create a comprehensive GIS database covering the entire city, to be executed by Genesis International Corporation Limited over two and a half years, followed by five years of operation and maintenance.

The project is divided into two phases: the first will geo-code 9 lakh (900,000) properties, with the remaining 16 lakh (1.6 million) following in phase two. The scope extends well beyond property mapping. Aerial surveys using drones and aircraft equipped with high-resolution sensors and cameras will produce a 3D city model. Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR) will map the precise depth and location of decades-old underground drainage, drinking water, and storm water networks — a critical capability for a city where repeated excavation for utility work has long been a source of disruption.

Each property will receive a unique "Digital Door ID" through door-to-door surveys conducted via mobile app, with property tax ID, zone, owner name, address, building height, number of floors, property type, and age all accessible on a single platform. Vehicle-mounted sensors will record roads, buildings, and traffic signals in 360-degree imagery similar to Google Street View. The resulting 'Enterprise GIS Platform' will integrate with the municipality's other software systems.

According to Urban Acres reporting, the project builds on earlier fragmented GIS exercises for individual networks. The new effort aims to bring all data onto one integrated platform, covering roughly 625 square kilometers. Urban planners emphasize that data governance — clear standards for accuracy, updating, and inter-departmental access — will determine whether the investment delivers lasting value. The project also has direct fiscal implications: identifying unregistered properties could significantly boost property tax revenue, though experts caution that transparent data practices must accompany this expanded administrative capability.
