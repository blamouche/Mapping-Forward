## Synthesis
Geo Week News published its weekly industry roundup highlighting three compelling stories from the geospatial, 3D, and AEC (Architecture, Engineering, Construction) sectors, showcasing the remarkable range of applications for mapping and spatial technology.

The first story, reported by GIM International, covers a stunning archaeological discovery in the Amazon rainforest. Airborne lidar pierced the dense canopy to reveal a previously unknown civilization — now called the Aquiry civilization — that built between 24,000 and 30,000 monumental earthworks across what is now Brazil's Acre state. The discovery, published in Nature, suggests this single region was once more densely populated than it is today, with researchers estimating between 1.25 and 3 million people lived there in the first centuries AD. The finding fundamentally reshapes understanding of pre-Columbian Amazonian societies and demonstrates the power of lidar to reveal human history hidden beneath vegetation.

The second story, from Spatial Source, details how engineering firm Hatch completed a first-of-its-kind digital mapping assessment of 5,000 kilometres of Australian coastline for flooding and erosion risk. Using statistical and process-based modelling, Hatch provided a state government agency with a consistent, region-wide picture of hazard exposure. The work is particularly significant given that 1.4 million Australian properties are already at risk of flooding and annual event costs approach $400 million, making the case for systematic, large-scale coastal hazard mapping.

The third story explores how 3D printing technology is transforming the creation of physical models from digital twin data in the AEC industry, bridging the gap between virtual and tangible representations of built assets. Together, these stories illustrate the remarkable versatility of geospatial technology — from uncovering ancient civilizations to protecting modern coastlines to advancing construction practices.
