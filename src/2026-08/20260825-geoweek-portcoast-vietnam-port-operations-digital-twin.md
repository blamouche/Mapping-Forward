# How Portcoast Is Rewriting Port Operations in Vietnam with Digital Twins
**Source**: https://www.geoweeknews.com/articles/how-portcoast-is-rewriting-port-operations-in-vietnam/
**Date**: 2026-08-24
**Author**: Abigail Hart, Geo Week News
**Keywords**: Portcoast, Vietnam, digital twin, port operations, ArcGIS, bathymetry, reality capture, lidar, drone surveying, accident simulation, grain port, Ho Chi Minh City

## Elevator pitch
Vietnam-based engineering firm Portcoast is using an integrated digital twin platform combining bathymetric data, drone reality models, and live vessel tracking on ArcGIS to revolutionize port design, accident analysis, and 100-year planning across Vietnam's port sector.

## Takeaways
- Portcoast built a digital twin of a port near Ho Chi Minh City that enabled accident simulation using captured footage
- The platform integrates bathymetric data, drone-captured reality models, and live vessel tracking on a single ArcGIS system
- Portcoast's engineering pedigree gives it mastery of reality capture: drones, laser scanners, lidar, and unmanned surface vessels
- At a grain port in Vung Tau, the digital twin simulated grain conveyor belt flows from vessel to warehouse
- The company covers the entire built asset lifecycle: surveying, feasibility, design, engineering, and construction supervision
- Environmental data, underwater surveys, and years of studies feed into the single ArcGIS platform for government presentations
- The digital twin approach goes beyond 3D models to include live operational feeds, IoT sensors, and camera systems

## Synthesis
Portcoast, a Vietnamese engineering and design firm specializing in ports, waterways, dams, and offshore structures, is demonstrating how digital twins can go far beyond static 3D models to transform maritime infrastructure management. The company's approach was vividly illustrated when a vessel plowed into a berth at a port near Ho Chi Minh City: rather than relying on scattered camera feeds and crew reports, Portcoast could simulate the entire lead-up to the accident using its fully realized digital twin of the port, which combined bathymetric data, drone-captured reality models, and live vessel tracking on a single ArcGIS platform.

Khoi Tran, an AI and computer science specialist at Portcoast, describes the digital twin initiative as "a natural extension" of the company's desire to cover the entire built asset lifecycle. The firm's deep roots as a design and engineering firm give it a critical edge in reality capture — deploying drones, laser scanners, lidar, and unmanned surface vessels to capture existing conditions. When this geospatial data is layered with live operational feeds, IoT sensors, and camera systems, the result is a digital twin with depth that off-the-shelf platforms cannot match.

At a grain port in Vung Tau handling corn, beans, and soy imports, Portcoast loaded the BIM model of a new warehouse into the digital twin and simulated every conceivable way grain could flow from vessel to warehouse, accounting for berth operations, existing infrastructure, and physical site constraints. This allowed operators to optimize conveyor belt system design before construction began. The platform also aggregates years of environmental data and underwater surveys, enabling Portcoast to present comprehensive findings to government authorities on a single integrated system — from accident analysis to 100-year port planning.
