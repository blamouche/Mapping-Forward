# Google Builds Korea Team for Full-Functioning Maps Service with Turn-by-Turn Navigation
**Source**: https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10851560
**Date**: 2026-08-25
**Author**: Moon Joon-hyun, The Korea Herald
**Keywords**: Google Maps, South Korea, turn-by-turn navigation, map data export, Naver Map, Kakao Map, partnerships, 1:5000 scale maps, Google Korea

## Elevator pitch
Google is recruiting a senior partnerships manager in Seoul to build out a full-functioning Google Maps service in South Korea — including turn-by-turn navigation — following the government's February 2026 conditional approval to export high-precision map data.

## Takeaways
- Google is officially recruiting a Seoul-based Strategic Partnerships Development Manager to build its Korea Maps team
- Google confirmed the plan includes turn-by-turn driving and walking navigation, currently unavailable in Korea
- The hiring follows South Korea's February 27, 2026 conditional approval of Google's export of 1:5,000-scale map data
- Korea had previously rejected Google's map data export requests in 2007 and 2016 over national security concerns
- Google Maps in Korea currently offers place listings, reviews, and transit directions but no turn-by-turn navigation
- Naver Map leads the Korean market with 29.25 million monthly active users vs Google Maps' 9.08 million as of April 2026
- The new partnerships role will negotiate "first-of-a-kind" agreements with Korean companies and public entities
- Experts say Google needs better base-map data, POI/address data, and traffic/parking/transit information from local providers

## Synthesis
Google is accelerating its push into the South Korean mapping market by recruiting a Seoul-based Strategic Partnerships Development Manager, marking what the company internally describes as a "monumental, historical turning point" for Google Maps in Korea. The job posting, first reported exclusively by The Korea Herald, reveals that Google is "building a team" to launch "full-functioning Google Maps capabilities" in the country, with the company explicitly confirming that turn-by-turn navigation is part of the plan.

The move follows South Korea's February 27, 2026 decision to conditionally approve Google's export of 1:5,000-scale high-precision map data — a reversal of earlier rejections in 2007 and 2016 that were driven by national security concerns. At the time of approval, Google Vice President Cris Turner said the company looked forward to bringing "a fully functioning Google Maps to Korea" but did not specify which features would be included. Google's current Korean service offers place listings, reviews, and public transit directions, but walking segments within transit routes appear only as dotted lines rather than guided navigation.

The expansion will pit Google against entrenched local competitors. According to mobile-app analytics firm Mobile Index, Naver Map had 29.25 million monthly active users in April 2026, compared with 12.77 million for Kakao Map and just 9.08 million for Google Maps. The new partnerships role will involve negotiating "first-of-a-kind" agreements and building relationships with Korean companies and public entities. Choi Bo-kyung, a geospatial consultant and former TomTom executive, noted that Google will need to source better base-map data, POI/address data, and traffic/parking/transit information from multiple local providers — a complex process that requires navigating Korean data regulations. Google has not yet announced a launch date.
