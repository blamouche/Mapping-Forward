# New Floodplain Maps Take Effect This November in Larimer County

**Author**: Larimer County Engineering

## Synthesis
Larimer County, Colorado has announced that its updated Flood Insurance Rate Maps (FIRMs) and Flood Insurance Study (FIS) will become effective on November 27, 2026. The updated maps apply to Unincorporated Larimer County and represent the culmination of a process initiated in response to the devastating September 2013 flood that swept through the region. The revision is described as a proactive effort to better understand and communicate flood risks across the county.

The updated floodplain maps incorporate new topography and hydrology data that was not available in previous iterations. This means that some properties previously not considered at risk may now fall within designated flood zones, while others may see their risk classification change. For property owners, these changes can have significant implications for flood insurance requirements — federally backed or regulated lenders typically require flood insurance for properties in high-risk areas.

Larimer County must officially adopt the updated FIRMs and FIS by the November effective date to maintain its participation in the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), which makes federally backed flood insurance available to property owners. The county has made resources available for residents to determine whether their properties are affected, including public hearing dates and information about flood insurance impacts. A Spanish-language version of the announcement has also been published, reflecting the county's commitment to broad community outreach. The update underscores the ongoing importance of accurate flood mapping in a state that has experienced increasingly severe weather events.
