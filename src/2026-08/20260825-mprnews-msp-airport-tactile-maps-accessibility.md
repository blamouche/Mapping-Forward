# Tactile Maps Installed at MSP Airport Ease Travel for More Than Just Blind Passengers
**Source**: https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/08/25/msp-airports-new-maps-make-traveling-for-blind-and-others-easier
**Date**: 2026-08-25
**Author**: Matt Mikus, MPR News

## Elevator pitch
Minneapolis-St. Paul International Airport has become the first airport in North America to install custom 3D-printed tactile maps with braille and textures outside every bathroom, designed in collaboration with blind travelers to improve navigation for passengers with disabilities and everyone else.

## Synthesis
Minneapolis-St. Paul International Airport (MSP) has rolled out an innovative accessibility initiative: tactile maps with braille and textured surfaces installed outside every bathroom throughout the facility. The project, developed over two years in collaboration with the airport's Travelers with Disabilities Advisory Committee, makes MSP the first airport in North America to implement such a system. The maps outline every fixture and path to sinks, toilets, urinals, and changing tables through touch alone.

The scale of the undertaking is significant. The airport's two terminals contain 146 bathrooms, and only two share identical floor plans, meaning nearly every map had to be custom designed. Phil Kuktan, the airport commission's project manager, noted that the team started from scratch with no existing Americans with Disabilities Act standards to follow. They created their own standards for symbology and design, working closely with blind committee members at the design table from the very beginning. The team used 3D printing with a local company in Bloomington, Minnesota, which enabled rapid prototyping and iteration at lower cost than the sheet metal traditionally used for tactile maps.

Ken Rogers, president of the American Council of the Blind of Minnesota and a completely blind traveler, described the maps as "a game changer" that provide independence and freedom. The initiative demonstrates a broader principle of universal design: the tactile maps also help sighted travelers navigate MSP's notoriously complex restrooms, some of which have multiple entrances or branching paths. Airport staff now hope to share what they learned with other airports across the country, with Portland already expressing interest, and envision their approach eventually becoming a standard that travelers can expect wherever they go.
