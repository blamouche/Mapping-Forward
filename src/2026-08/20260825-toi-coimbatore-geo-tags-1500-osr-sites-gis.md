## Synthesis
The Coimbatore city municipal corporation in Tamil Nadu, India, has made significant progress in creating a comprehensive digital inventory of its open space reservation (OSR) lands through GIS mapping. According to The Times of India, the corporation has geo-tagged approximately 1,507 of the city's 2,702 total OSR sites, with the title-change process completed for 1,771 sites. The initiative is nearing completion and is expected to be integrated with the corporation's official website, making land records accessible to the public.

The GIS mapping exercise began in 2025 with an initial batch of approximately 500 parcels. The database was designed to include ward and zone details, survey and patta numbers, approved plot numbers, layout plans, extent of OSR land, and details of encroachments. Drone imagery and field verification by licensed surveyors were also proposed as part of the methodology, ensuring accuracy and completeness of the spatial data.

Once the online system is fully integrated, residents and prospective property buyers will be able to access key details of individual OSR sites, including survey numbers, ward numbers, and other official land records. This transparency is expected to make it easier for citizens to verify whether land in layouts has been earmarked as OSR and belongs to the civic body. The corporation's website already hosts ward-wise reserve-site details including survey numbers, layout approval details, and the present condition of several sites. Officials emphasized that the digitization will provide a consolidated database of OSR properties and help the corporation monitor and protect these public assets more effectively against encroachment — a persistent challenge in rapidly growing Indian cities.
