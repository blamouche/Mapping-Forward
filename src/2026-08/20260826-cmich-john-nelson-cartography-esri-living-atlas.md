# Making Maps Cool: John Nelson's Cartographic Journey from CMU to Esri
**Source**: https://www.cmich.edu/news/details/making-maps-cool
**Date**: 2026-08-26
**Author**: Heather Smith (Central Michigan University)
**Keywords**: cartography, Esri, ArcGIS Living Atlas, John Nelson, map design, education, GIS career, YouTube

## Elevator pitch
CMU alumnus John Nelson has turned cartography into a creative public craft, building Esri's Living Atlas by day and teaching nearly 40,000 YouTube followers how to make compelling maps by night.

## Takeaways
- John Nelson works as a cartographer and UX designer on Esri's ArcGIS Living Atlas team, building what he describes as "a high-resolution, constantly living and growing model of the world."
- His maps support multiple industries: national and local governments, construction and engineering businesses, and medical systems that use his maps for predictive health analysis.
- He creates educational content for almost 40,000 YouTube followers, 13,000 on Instagram, and 26,000 on Twitter, driven by the principle that "nothing teaches like teaching."
- Nelson majored in geography, earth science, and social science with a minor in art at Central Michigan University (BA 2000, MS 2004), combining analytical and creative training.
- His father Burton Nelson taught geography at CMU for 81 semesters (1966–2006), and the family's connection to the institution is multigenerational.

## Synthesis
John Nelson's career illustrates how cartography can sit at the intersection of data science, visual design, and public education. As a cartographer and UX designer on Esri's ArcGIS Living Atlas team — described as "the foremost collection of geographic information from around the globe" — Nelson builds and maintains a continuously updated digital model of the world that users can interrogate from multiple angles. The Living Atlas serves as the curated, ready-to-use geographic data layer underpinning Esri's ArcGIS platform, supporting government, engineering, and healthcare applications. Medical systems, for instance, use his maps to discover patterns and conduct predictive analysis for future health outcomes.

Nelson's educational philosophy is rooted in his college experience: "I learned in college that I never truly understood something unless I explained it to someone. Nothing teaches like teaching." This drives his prolific content creation across YouTube (nearly 40,000 followers), Instagram (13,000), and Twitter (26,000), where he shares the process of making maps — not just the finished products. His tagline: "It's exciting to make maps, but it's even more exciting to share the process of that making."

His academic path was deliberately interdisciplinary: a triple major in geography, earth science, and social science, with a minor in art, followed by a master's in geoscience. This combination of analytical rigor and aesthetic sensibility informs his approach to cartography, which emphasizes clarity, storytelling, and accessibility. He travels internationally to deliver keynotes at geography events and lead cartography workshops.

Nelson works remotely from a custom-built pine woodshed in his Michigan backyard — sourced from locally harvested timber milled by his brother-in-law — where he both performs his Esri duties and films his educational videos. He is also working with Esri to make satellite imagery easier to find and use for students, addressing the gap between the availability of free imagery and its usability for non-experts. His volunteer work on CMU's College of Science and Engineering Advisory Board ensures that the next generation of students has industry-connected programs and hiring pathways.

The profile is a case study in how individual passion, combined with the right platform and an instinct for teaching, can elevate a technical discipline into something broadly accessible and culturally resonant.
