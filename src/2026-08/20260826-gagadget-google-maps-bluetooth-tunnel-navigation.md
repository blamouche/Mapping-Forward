# Google Maps sur Android : activez cette option pour ne plus rater de sorties en tunnel

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps on Android can now use Bluetooth tunnel beacons to maintain positioning when GPS signal disappears underground, matching a capability Waze has offered since 2016.

## Takeaways
- A hidden setting in Google Maps for Android enables scanning of Bluetooth beacons installed in tunnel walls, allowing the app to maintain accurate positioning when GPS is unavailable.
- The beacons use Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), consuming minimal battery, and once activated the setting persists for all future trips without further manipulation.
- Waze, also owned by Google, has used this beacon technology since 2016, giving it a long-standing advantage in urban tunnels over Google Maps.
- The beacon deployment, started in early 2024, covers a limited set of cities: New York, Chicago, Paris, Rio de Janeiro, Brussels, Oslo, Sydney, Boston, and Mexico.
- No timeline has been announced for iOS; the capability remains Android-only for now.

## Synthesis
Tunnels have long been a blind spot for satellite navigation apps: without a GPS fix, the device loses its position and continues issuing instructions based on a theoretical trajectory, risking missed exits or delayed guidance. Google Maps on Android now offers a solution borrowed from its sister app Waze — Bluetooth tunnel beacons.

These small, low-power transmitters are fixed to tunnel walls and continuously broadcast a Bluetooth signal that the phone picks up to recalculate its position independently of satellites. Google Maps uses this signal to correct its location and adjust voice instructions in real time. The activation is a one-time action buried in the app's privacy settings: users authorize Bluetooth beacon scanning once, and the option stays active for all subsequent journeys. Because the beacons use Bluetooth Low Energy, the battery impact is negligible — the radio operates in short background bursts.

Waze has exploited this technology since 2016, which gave it a notable edge in tunnel-heavy urban environments where Google Maps displayed the same limitations as any conventional GPS app. Google has also recently added 3D navigation on Android and iOS to better render interchanges, elevated roadways, and tunnel entrances, complementing the beacon-based positioning.

The beacon infrastructure is not universal. Deployment began in early 2024 and currently covers a limited list of metropolitan areas: New York, Chicago, Paris, Rio de Janeiro, Brussels, Oslo, Sydney, Boston, and Mexico City. Only Android users traveling through one of these equipped cities can currently benefit from the feature. Apple's iOS, while technically capable of background BLE scanning, has no announced timeline for support. For everyone else, tunnels remain a navigation gap that requires driver attention to signage and anticipation.
