## Synthesis
TomTom's 2025 Traffic Index has revealed a surprising finding: Arequipa, Peru's second-largest city, now has a higher congestion level than Lima, the capital. According to the index — which monitors traffic in 500 cities worldwide using anonymised GPS data from drivers across the complete road network — Arequipa registered a 69.5% congestion level in 2025, ranking 8th globally, compared with Lima's 69.3%. Drivers in Arequipa lost an average of 154 hours in traffic during the year, equivalent to 6 days and 10 hours, representing an increase of 6 hours and 23 minutes compared to 2024.

The data raises important questions about urban mobility in rapidly growing secondary cities. While Lima's larger road network means drivers there lose more total hours (195 vs 154), Arequipa's higher congestion percentage indicates that its road infrastructure is under proportionally greater strain. This is a pattern seen in many developing-world secondary cities where population growth and vehicle ownership have outpaced infrastructure investment. The TomTom index distinguishes between metro and city areas, measuring congestion across the complete road network including highways, which provides a more nuanced picture than simple average speed metrics.

The cartographic and geospatial methodology behind the ranking is significant. TomTom's data is based on trip data spanning more than 3.65 trillion kilometres, anonymously collected from drivers within both the metropolitan area and central city. In Arequipa, the average speed during rush hour drops to 16.3 km/h, with congestion levels reaching 114.7% during the worst periods. This level of granular, city-wide traffic mapping enables urban planners to identify specific corridors and time windows where interventions — from public transit improvements to road network expansion — could have the greatest impact.
