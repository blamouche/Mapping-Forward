# TomTom Expert: Robotaxi Rollout Reveals Challenge of London as a Testing Environment

**Date**: 2026-08-27
**Author**: Kevin Borras, Highways News
**Keywords**: TomTom, robotaxi, London, autonomous vehicles, HD mapping, traffic data, location intelligence, Andy Marchant, Waymo, self-driving cars

## Elevator pitch
TomTom traffic expert Andy Marchant says London's complex street layout and evolving infrastructure make it an exceptionally challenging environment for robotaxi deployment, with high-precision mapping and live traffic data forming core infrastructure for safe autonomous driving at scale.

## Takeaways
- TomTom's Andy Marchant identifies London's narrow roads, changing speed limits, and evolving street layouts as key challenges for robotaxi rollout
- Regulatory frameworks, liability models, and operating standards are still catching up to autonomous vehicle technology
- High-precision mapping combined with live traffic and hazard information is "core infrastructure" for automated driving, working alongside vehicle cameras and radar
- Location intelligence provides context "beyond the immediate line of sight" of vehicle sensors, making robotaxis "street smart"
- The same mapping data will be critical for UK government and transport authorities to plan and govern safe, scalable robotaxi deployments

## Synthesis
The postponement of robotaxi launches in London has highlighted the unique challenges of the city as a testing environment for autonomous vehicles, according to Andy Marchant, a Traffic Expert at TomTom. In comments to Highways News, Marchant explained that London's combination of narrow roads, changing speed limits, and evolving street layouts creates a particularly complex operational design domain for self-driving systems. The delays have shone a spotlight on the gap between the promise of autonomous mobility and the reality of deploying safely at scale in a dense, historic urban environment.

Marchant's analysis emphasises the central role of mapping and location intelligence in bridging this gap. He argues that "dependable, continuously updated, high-precision mapping, combined with live traffic and hazard information, is core infrastructure for automated driving." This intelligence works alongside a vehicle's onboard sensors — cameras, radar, and other detectors — providing vital context beyond their immediate line of sight. The integration of these data sources is what makes robotaxis "street smart," offering a richer, more contextual understanding of the road network and changing conditions. This is not merely about navigation: it is about giving autonomous systems the situational awareness needed to handle edge cases in a city where street configurations can change overnight due to roadworks, events, or temporary restrictions.

The broader implication is that mapping companies like TomTom are positioning themselves as essential infrastructure providers for the autonomous vehicle era. Marchant concluded that the same location intelligence will be "critical in helping the UK Government and transport authorities plan safe, scalable robotaxi deployments, giving them the visibility and oversight needed to govern these services effectively and build public trust." This frames high-definition mapping not just as a commercial product but as a public safety necessity for the governance of autonomous mobility.
