# München: Landshuter Allee Sperrung und ihre Auswirkungen auf den Verkehr
**Source**: https://www.sueddeutsche.de/muenchen/muenchen-verkehr-landshuter-allee-ausweichverkehr-tomtom-li.3537127
**Date**: 2026-08-27
**Author**: Martin Mühlfenzl, Süddeutsche Zeitung
**Keywords**: TomTom, Munich, Landshuter Allee, road closure, traffic analysis, congestion level, Neuhausen, Mittlerer Ring, Ausweichverkehr, urban traffic mapping

## Elevator pitch
TomTom traffic data analysis reveals how the closure of Munich's Landshuter Allee for road maintenance sends ripple effects through surrounding neighbourhoods, with congestion levels rising dramatically in Neuhausen, Schwabing-West, Moosach, and Milbertshofen.

## Takeaways
- The Landshuter Allee, one of Munich's busiest roads with over 70,000 vehicles daily, was closed for maintenance in both directions on consecutive weekends
- TomTom compared traffic data from August 13–16 (no closure) with August 20–23 (northern carriageway closed) to measure impact
- Neuhausen-Nymphenburg was most affected, but Schwabing-West, Moosach, and Milbertshofen-Am Hart also saw significant congestion increases
- TomTom's "Congestion Level" metric quantifies how much travel times increase in affected neighbourhoods compared to baseline
- The analysis identifies which alternative routes absorbed the additional traffic, demonstrating the value of real-time traffic mapping for urban planning

## Synthesis
The Süddeutsche Zeitung obtained exclusive TomTom traffic data analysing the impact of closing one of Munich's most heavily travelled roads — the Landshuter Allee on the Mittlerer Ring, which carries over 70,000 vehicles per day. The road was closed in both directions on consecutive weekends for road maintenance, and TomTom compared two reference periods: August 13–16 without closure and August 20–23 with the northern carriageway closed. The analysis focused on two questions: how do speeds develop on central alternative routes, and how much additional time must drivers in different neighbourhoods allocate?

TomTom's data provided clear spatial answers. The neighbourhood most affected was Neuhausen-Nymphenburg, through which the Landshuter Allee runs, but the impact extended well beyond: Schwabing-West, Moosach, and Milbertshofen-Am Hart all experienced noticeably increased congestion. The analysis used TomTom's Congestion Level metric, which measures the percentage increase in travel times compared to free-flow conditions. A screenshot provided to the SZ shows a heat map of affected neighbourhoods, with darker shading indicating greater traffic impact — a compelling example of how congestion data can be visualised geographically to communicate complex urban dynamics to the public.

The analysis demonstrates the broader value of commercial traffic data for urban management. By comparing equivalent periods with and without the closure, TomTom isolated the specific effect of the roadworks from normal traffic variation. This methodology — essentially a natural experiment enabled by continuous GPS-based traffic monitoring — allows city planners to predict the impact of future closures and design better diversion strategies. The Landshuter Allee case also illustrates how mapping the cascading effects of infrastructure disruptions across a city's road network can inform more responsive urban governance, particularly as cities face increasing maintenance backlogs on ageing infrastructure.
