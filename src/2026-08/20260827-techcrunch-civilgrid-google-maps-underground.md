# This Former PG&E Engineer Is Building a 'Google Maps for the Underground'
**Source**: https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/27/this-former-pge-engineer-is-building-a-google-maps-for-the-underground/
**Date**: 2026-08-27
**Author**: TechCrunch
**Keywords**: CivilGrid, underground utilities, subsurface mapping, utility strikes, PG&E, Spark Capital, infrastructure mapping, geospatial startup, Series A

## Elevator pitch
CivilGrid, founded by former PG&E engineer Josh Mackanic, has raised $26 million to build a unified subsurface mapping platform that aggregates data on buried utility assets — solving a problem that causes 200,000 utility strikes per year in the US alone.

## Takeaways
- CivilGrid aggregates disparate data on underground utility assets, property ownership, and environmental regulations into a single mapping platform
- The startup raised a $26 million Series A led by Spark Capital, with participation from Afore, A*, Ford Street Ventures, SNR, and Energy Impact Partners
- A PG&E case study identified $60 million in avoidable paving costs across 1,600 planned gas distribution projects using CivilGrid
- The US experiences approximately 200,000 "utility strikes" annually because utilities only have visibility into their own lines, not competitors' infrastructure
- Mackanic envisions evolving the platform from data provision to automated permitting and infrastructure planning

## Synthesis
Josh Mackanic spent ten years as an engineer at Pacific Gas and Electric, where he experienced firsthand the consequences of incomplete subsurface mapping. After a job was shut down for three days because of an unknown pipe in an excavation — costing $60,000 in delays — he realised that even the largest US utility only has visibility into its own electric and gas lines. Pipes carrying sewage or water belong to other organisations, and so does the information about their exact locations. This fragmentation leads to approximately 200,000 "utility strikes" every year in the United States.

In 2020, Mackanic founded CivilGrid to solve this problem from the outside. The startup gathers disparate data regarding utility assets, property ownership, and environmental regulations and bundles it into what he calls "Google Maps for what's underground." The platform serves governments, civil engineering firms, and utilities — including his former employer PG&E. The company has now raised a $26 million Series A led by Spark Capital, with additional investment from Afore, A*, Ford Street Ventures, SNR, and Energy Impact Partners, a fund whose utility company LPs Mackanic sees as a vote of confidence from the industry.

The impact is measurable. A PG&E case study identified $60 million in avoidable paving costs across 1,600 planned gas distribution projects by using CivilGrid. Mackanic's vision extends beyond data aggregation: he envisions the platform eventually recommending optimal infrastructure placement and automating the permitting process. The subsurface mapping problem is not new, but Mackanic argues that nobody had previously solved the challenge of collecting, sorting, and securing the data while building relationships with the partners most likely to pay for it. CivilGrid represents a significant application of geospatial mapping technology to one of infrastructure's most persistent and costly blind spots.
