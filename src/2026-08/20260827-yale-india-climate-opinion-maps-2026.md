# Updated India Climate Opinion Maps for 2026
**Source**: https://climatecommunication.yale.edu/publications/updated-india-climate-opinion-maps-for-2026/
**Date**: 2026-08-27
**Author**: Yale Program on Climate Change Communication
**Keywords**: climate opinion maps, India, MRP, survey data, cartography, climate change perception, district-level mapping, Yale, CVoter

## Elevator pitch
Yale's updated India Climate Opinion Maps tool visualises climate change beliefs, risk perceptions, and policy support across 34 Indian states and 634 districts, revealing sharp north–south divides in personal experience and climate literacy despite overwhelming national concern.

## Takeaways
- The interactive tool maps climate opinions across 34 of India's 36 states/UTs and 634 districts using 25,000+ survey responses (2022–2026)
- After hearing a definition of climate change, over 75% of Indians in every state think global warming is happening, with rates from 80% (Andhra Pradesh) to 92% (Delhi)
- A stark north–south divide exists: northern states report higher personal experience with heat waves and perceived harm, while central/eastern states show low climate literacy (fewer than 50% in Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, and West Bengal understand global warming is human-caused)
- Despite regional differences, 94% of Indians support national programs to teach about global warming and train people for renewable energy jobs
- The estimates use multilevel regression with post-stratification (MRP) combined with demographic, geographic, and climate vulnerability data, accurate to approximately ±7 percentage points at state level

## Synthesis
The Yale Program on Climate Change Communication has released its 2026 update to the India Climate Opinion Maps, an interactive cartographic tool that visualises climate change experience, beliefs, policy support, and risk perceptions across 34 of India's 36 states and Union territories and 634 districts. The update incorporates new nationally representative survey data through 2026, collected in partnership with CVoter, and represents one of the most granular geospatial analyses of climate opinion in any country.

The maps reveal a complex geographic pattern. Knowledge of global warming is low — fewer than half of Indians in most central, western, and eastern states say they know about global warming — yet when provided a definition, the vast majority say it is happening. The cartographic visualisation makes immediately visible the north–south divide in personal experience: 94–95% of people in Himachal Pradesh and Chandigarh report personally experiencing the effects of global warming, compared with only 59% in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. Similarly, 80% of people in Haryana report experiencing severe heat waves, versus 52% in Kerala. These spatial patterns correlate with regional climate exposure — northern India faces more extreme heat — and the maps make this relationship visually explicit through choropleth and difference-from-average visualisations.

The methodological approach is notable for the cartographic community. The estimates are derived using multilevel regression with post-stratification (MRP), combining survey data from over 25,000 respondents with demographic and geographic population characteristics including gender, age, caste, urbanicity, education, and climate vulnerability, weighted using Indian census counts. The tool provides district-level resolution, enabling exploration of fine-grained geographic differences that national averages obscure. As India is both the seventh most climate-vulnerable country and the third-largest greenhouse gas emitter, these opinion maps can directly inform State Action Plans on Climate Change, demonstrating how cartographic tools can bridge the gap between public opinion data and policy action.
