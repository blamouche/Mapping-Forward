## Elevator pitch
At State of the Map 2026 in Paris, T-Kartor and Jungle Bus present their massive OpenStreetMap-based project to produce and maintain location-specific area maps ("plans de proximité") for 35,000+ bus and tram stops across the Paris region — Europe's most extensive public transport network.

## Takeaways
- T-Kartor, the Swedish cartographic agency behind Legible London, WalkNYC, and Toronto TO360, has partnered with Île-de-France Mobilités to create "plans de proximité" — stop-specific area maps for the entire Paris region
- The project covers approximately 35,000 individual bus and tram stops, each requiring a unique map with high quality and consistency standards
- OpenStreetMap data is the backbone: the process goes from editing OSM data to delivering print-ready files to transport operators, with dozens of intermediate steps
- Jungle Bus, a local OSM community organization, has been a key partner throughout the effort
- The talk at State of the Map 2026 covers the full production pipeline: context, build, deployment, maintenance, and lessons learned

## Synthesis
This State of the Map 2026 presentation represents one of the most ambitious OpenStreetMap-based cartographic production systems ever undertaken. The "plans de proximité" concept — location-specific area maps displayed at each bus and tram stop — is deceptively simple in description but extraordinarily complex in execution. Each of the 35,000+ stops requires a unique map showing the surrounding area at a scale useful for pedestrians, with consistent styling, accurate data, and regular updates. The sheer volume makes manual cartography impractical, necessitating an automated production pipeline rooted in OpenStreetMap data.

The involvement of T-Kartor is significant. As the agency behind three of the world's most influential pedestrian mapping systems — Legible London, WalkNYC, and Toronto TO360 — T-Kartor brings deep expertise in designing maps specifically for pedestrian wayfinding. Their approach differs from typical navigation app maps: printed maps at transit stops must be immediately readable, orientationally intuitive (heads-up), and show only the information a arriving passenger needs to navigate the immediate area on foot. The Paris project applies this philosophy at unprecedented scale.

The choice of OpenStreetMap as the data source reflects the maturity of the OSM ecosystem for transit applications. By using OSM, the project benefits from continuous community updates, open licensing, and the ability to contribute back to the shared dataset. The partnership with Jungle Bus — a French OSM community group specializing in transit data — ensures local knowledge and quality control. The project's emphasis on printed maps, in an era of digital navigation dominance, is a notable counterpoint: physical maps at transit stops remain essential for accessibility, quick reference, and users without smartphones or data connectivity. This project demonstrates that OSM can serve not just as a database but as the foundation for a sophisticated, large-scale cartographic production system.
