# Tamil Nadu Mandates GIS-Based Master Plans for Urban Local Bodies

**Author**: Rudhran Baraasu, The New Indian Express

## Synthesis
The Tamil Nadu government has issued a landmark Government Order (G.O.) mandating the preparation, approval, and adoption of legally valid Geographic Information System (GIS)-based Master Plans, Zonal Plans, and Land Use Plans across all Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) and urban development authorities in the state. The order, dated August 11, 2026, was issued by the housing and urban development department and directs all planning authorities to implement standardised geospatial methodologies and uniform data structures.

The directive addresses a significant gap: Tamil Nadu's existing 123 Master Plans, which govern 130 towns, were built entirely using conventional methods and lack a digital, data-driven foundation. The new framework mandates a comprehensive spatial database that includes administrative boundaries, cadastral parcels (where available), transportation networks, existing and proposed land use, building footprints, water bodies, drainage, contours and terrain, utilities (water supply, sewerage, storm water, electricity, gas, telecommunications), environmental features, heritage assets, public and community facilities, green and open spaces, disaster-prone areas, infrastructure corridors, and demographic and socio-economic attributes linked through spatial identifiers.

A comprehensive regulatory framework will govern the entire planning lifecycle, from baseline benchmarking and base map preparation through existing land use mapping to the formulation of final proposals via a strict standard operating procedure. All approved GIS-based plans must be uploaded online, enabling transparency and inter-departmental data sharing. An urban development expert who assisted the Chennai Metropolitan Development Authority as senior planning advisor noted that the GIS layers can be used by other departments when implementing projects, maximising the value of the spatial database across government functions.

This mandate positions Tamil Nadu as a leader in digital urban governance in India, following similar initiatives in Maharashtra and Karnataka.
