# You Can Now 'Ask Maps' For Hyper-Specific Travel Advice. But Should You?

**Source**: https://www.cntraveler.com/story/you-can-now-ask-maps-for-hyper-specific-travel-advice-but-should-you
**Date**: 2026-08-28
**Author**: Ross Kenneth Urken, Condé Nast Traveler
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Ask Maps, Gemini AI, travel advice, navigation, AI itinerary, serendipity

## Elevator pitch
Condé Nast Traveler tests Google's new Gemini-powered "Ask Maps" feature that lets travellers ask hyper-specific questions without leaving the Maps app, while exploring what we lose when AI replaces the spontaneity of asking locals for directions.

## Takeaways
- Google's "Ask Maps" is a conversational AI feature powered by Gemini that lets users ask hyper-specific travel queries directly within Google Maps
- Users can ask questions like "My phone is dying—where can I charge it without waiting in a long line?" or "Is there a public tennis court with lights I can play at tonight?"
- The feature charts travel itineraries based on crowd-sourced rankings and efficient routes with waypoints matching specified preferences
- Experts warn that over-reliance on AI may erode the serendipity and human connection of traditional travel
- Travel industry professionals note AI can help you "get to the door" but cannot replace what happens once you walk through it

## Synthesis
Condé Nast Traveler's Ross Kenneth Urken takes a thoughtful, first-person approach to testing Google's new "Ask Maps" feature, a Gemini-powered conversational AI integrated directly into Google Maps. The feature allows travellers to ask highly specific questions—such as where to charge a dying phone without a queue, or where to find a public tennis court with lights—without ever leaving the Maps app. It represents the first integration of its kind, combining crowd-sourced rankings, efficient routing, and AI-generated waypoints.

The article frames Ask Maps within the broader trend of travellers turning to AI chatbots like ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity AI for itinerary planning. However, Ask Maps is distinct because it operates within the mapping ecosystem, leveraging Google's massive geographic database alongside Gemini's reasoning capabilities. The author tests the feature on a family trip to New Jersey and reflects on the tension between convenience and serendipity.

Several experts weigh in on what travellers gain and lose. Vicente Abarca Del Rio of Muletek asks: "Why do we travel? Simply to follow the little line traced by our map, oblivious to the life unfolding around us?" Andrey Meshcheryakov of Recombinators notes that Ask Maps "effectively de-risks the travel experience by eliminating the friction of human error and language barriers" but "threatens the serendipity engine that traditional travel provided." University of South Florida's Seden Dogan observes a shift "from organic, human-driven moments to more personalized, algorithm-driven ones." Outdoorsy co-founder Jen Young summarises: "we are trading a bit of soul for a smoother interface."

The author ultimately concludes that AI can help you arrive more easily, but the human experience—being curious, open, and willing to engage—remains irreplaceable.
