# Copernicus Emergency Management Service Maps Flood Damages in Northern Nepal

## Elevator pitch
The Copernicus Emergency Management Service activated its Rapid Mapping component to assess flood damage in northern Nepal after a glacier collapse triggered a devastating debris avalanche and flash flood on August 26, 2026.

## Synthesis
On August 26, 2026, a severe flooding event struck northern Nepal and Tibet. Initial assessments indicate that the partial collapse of a high-altitude glacier released a large avalanche of ice and rock, which descended approximately 1,200 metres into the Lhende River basin within seconds. This generated a powerful flow of water, mud, and debris downstream, devastating communities along the Nepal-China border.

The Copernicus Emergency Management Service (CEMS) Rapid Mapping component was activated as EMSR927 to map the flood extent and assess damage across four Areas of Interest. The data visualisation focused on the area around the village of Syapru Besi, Nepal, revealing the impact on built-up areas and roads. CEMS analysis indicated that within the mapped area, more than 240 buildings were destroyed and 32 were damaged.

This activation demonstrates the critical role of satellite-based emergency mapping in disaster response. The Copernicus programme provides rapid, standardized damage assessment products that help emergency responders prioritise interventions and allocate resources. The EMSR927 activation maps are publicly available through the Copernicus EMS portal, enabling coordination between Nepalese authorities, international aid organisations, and humanitarian responders.

The event is part of a broader pattern of increasing glacial hazards in the Himalayan region, where climate change is destabilising high-altitude ice masses. The speed and severity of the avalanche—descending over a kilometre in seconds—highlights the destructive potential of glacial lake outburst floods and debris avalanches in mountainous terrain.
