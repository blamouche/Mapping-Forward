## Synthesis
NBC News has published a detailed data-graphics piece using maps and satellite imagery to reconstruct the glacier collapse that triggered deadly flash flooding along the Nepal-Tibet border on August 26, 2026. The visualisation traces the sequence of events: a high-altitude glacier partially collapsed, releasing a massive avalanche of ice and rock that descended into river systems, generating catastrophic floodwaters that swept through downstream communities.

The human toll is staggering: over 380 people have died and hundreds more remain missing. The Nepal Tourism Board reported that over 600 foreigners are missing, including 65 Americans, highlighting the international dimension of the disaster in a region popular with trekkers and mountaineers. The remoteness of the affected areas has complicated rescue and recovery efforts.

The NBC News mapping approach demonstrates how satellite imagery combined with geographic data can help the public understand complex cascade hazards. By tracing the glacier collapse point, the avalanche path, and the downstream flood inundation zone, the maps provide a clear narrative of how a single glacial event propagated through the landscape to devastating effect. This kind of visual storytelling is increasingly important as climate-driven glacial hazards intensify across the Hindu Kush-Himalaya region, where an estimated 1.9 billion people depend on water originating from glacier-fed river systems.
