# GIS-Based Application Launched for Rural Water Asset Management in Karnataka

**Source**: https://www.newindianexpress.com/states/karnataka/2026/Aug/28/gis-based-application-launched-for-rural-water-asset-management-in-karnataka
**Date**: 2026-08-28
**Author**: Express News Service, The New Indian Express
**Keywords**: GIS, geo-tagging, Karnataka, rural water assets, water management, fraud prevention, mobile app, India

## Elevator pitch
Karnataka's RDPR Minister launched a GIS-based mobile and web application to geo-tag rural water supply assets, improve transparency, and prevent fraudulent billing across the state's more than 3 lakh rural water assets.

## Takeaways
- Karnataka launched a GIS-based mobile and web app for scientific management of rural water assets
- The application will geo-tag rural water supply assets with up to 5-metre accuracy, recording their physical condition and maintenance status
- More than 70,000 assets have already been mapped and documented during the first phase of verification
- All 3 lakh rural water assets will be digitised by the end of September 2026
- The app also directs officials to remove pump sets and electricity connections from non-functional borewells to eliminate unnecessary power bills

## Synthesis
Karnataka Rural Development and Panchayat Raj (RDPR) Minister Eshwar B Khandre launched a GIS-based mobile and web application on August 28, 2026, to ensure scientific management of rural water supply assets across the state. The application, which can geo-tag assets with an accuracy of up to five metres, is designed to improve transparency and prevent fraudulent billing in rural water supply schemes.

The app covers a wide range of water infrastructure assets including borewells, overhead tanks, open wells, pump houses, water treatment plants, and distribution networks. Each asset is geo-tagged and its details—physical condition, operational status, and maintenance records—are documented in the system. This creates a comprehensive digital inventory that enables data-driven decision-making for maintenance and investment.

The scale of the initiative is significant: Karnataka has more than 3 lakh (300,000) rural water assets, all of which are targeted for digitisation by the end of September 2026. During the first phase of verification, more than 70,000 assets have already been mapped and documented. The minister also directed officials to remove pump sets and electricity connections from non-functional borewells, highlighting how the geo-tagging data can identify waste and drive cost savings.

The initiative reflects a broader trend across Indian states of leveraging GIS technology for governance and asset management. By creating an authoritative spatial database of water infrastructure, Karnataka aims to curb fake bills, reduce operational costs, and ensure that public investment in rural water supply is properly tracked and maintained.
