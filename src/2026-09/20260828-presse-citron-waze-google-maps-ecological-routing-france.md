# Waze and Google Maps No Longer Give the Fastest Routes in France

**Source**: https://www.presse-citron.net/cest-dommage-pourquoi-waze-et-google-maps-ne-donnent-plus-les-trajets-les-plus-rapides-en-france/
**Date**: 2026-08-28
**Author**: Jean-Yves Alric, Presse-citron
**Keywords**: Waze, Google Maps, ecological routing, France, decree 2022-1199, emissions, navigation, regulation

## Elevator pitch
Presse-citron explains why Waze and Google Maps now prioritize ecological routes over fastest routes in France, following a government decree that mandates navigation apps to highlight the lowest-emission itineraries.

## Takeaways
- French decree n° 2022-1199 obliges digital mobility platforms to prioritize routes with the lowest greenhouse gas emissions
- Both Waze and Google Maps now prominently feature ecological routes, which are often longer than the fastest alternatives
- On highways with speed limits of 110 km/h or above, apps must also propose an alternative with a 20 km/h speed reduction
- Users can still manually switch back to fastest routes via app settings, though many are unaware of the option
- The Île-de-France region previously negotiated with Google Maps before the Paris 2024 Olympics to favor alternative routes and reduce congestion

## Synthesis
Presse-citron's Jean-Yves Alric documents a significant change in how Waze and Google Maps operate in France: the applications now prioritize ecological routes—those with the lowest greenhouse gas emissions—over the fastest routes. This shift is the direct consequence of French decree n° 2022-1199, which mandates that digital mobility assistance platforms "highlight route proposals with the lowest impact in terms of greenhouse gas emissions."

The decree includes a specific provision: if a route includes a section where the maximum speed limit is 110 km/h or above, the applications must propose an alternative with a 20 km/h speed reduction. This means drivers on French highways are now presented with slower, longer routes as the default recommendation, a change that has generated significant user frustration in online comments.

While the ecological routes allow users to visualize their carbon footprint and make environmentally conscious choices, they typically add time to journeys. The applications do not block users from switching back to fastest routes—the setting is accessible in the app's preferences—but many users remain unaware of this option.

The article draws a parallel with the Île-de-France region's pre-Olympics approach, when authorities negotiated directly with Google Maps to modify routing and favor alternative paths to reduce traffic congestion. Laurent Probst, director general of the Île-de-France transport syndicate, had warned that if platforms did not comply voluntarily, the state would intervene, potentially even requiring them to shut down their applications during critical periods.

The change raises broader questions about the balance between environmental policy and user experience, and whether regulatory mandates on routing algorithms represent an effective tool for emissions reduction or an overreach that alienates users without meaningful environmental impact.
