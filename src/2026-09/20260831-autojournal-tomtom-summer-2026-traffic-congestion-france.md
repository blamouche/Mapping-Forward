# Summer 2026: Why Traffic Was More Fluid on French Roads
**Source**: https://www.autojournal.fr/actu/ete-2026-pourquoi-a-t-il-ete-plus-fluide-sur-les-routes-cette-annee-423695.html
**Date**: 2026-08-31
**Author**: L'Auto-Journal
**Keywords**: TomTom, traffic congestion, France, summer traffic, Île-de-France, driving behavior, fuel consumption, traffic data

## Elevator pitch
TomTom traffic data reveals that French roads were significantly less congested during summer 2026 compared to 2025, with the maximum congestion peak dropping from 35.6% to 29.4% and the number of high-congestion days in Île-de-France halving from 20 to 10.

## Takeaways
- Maximum congestion peak in France dropped to 29.4% on July 7, 2026, compared to 35.6% in summer 2025
- France experienced zero days above 30% congestion in summer 2026, versus 7 days in summer 2025
- Île-de-France saw congestion peaks above 40% drop from 20 days (2025) to 10 days (2026) — a 50% reduction
- Key factors: shorter summer holidays, better anticipation of departure times, and trips spread across the entire season
- Declining purchasing power in 2026 also contributed to fewer and shorter road trips
- Gasoline consumption dropped significantly in May 2026, reducing state tax revenue

## Synthesis
TomTom's traffic data analysis for summer 2026 reveals a marked improvement in traffic fluidity across French roads compared to the previous year. The data, published by the Dutch navigation specialist, shows that the maximum congestion peak in France reached only 29.4% on July 7, 2026 — meaning a journey took 29.4% longer than free-flow conditions. This is a significant drop from summer 2025's peak of 35.6%, and critically, France experienced zero days exceeding 30% congestion in 2026 versus seven such days in 2025.

The Île-de-France region followed the same trend even more dramatically. The number of days with congestion above 40% fell from 20 in summer 2025 to just 10 in summer 2026 — a 50% reduction. This means that even in the most densely populated region of France, travelers experienced significantly fewer severe traffic days.

TomTom specialists attribute this shift to changing driver behavior patterns. Summer holidays became shorter in 2026, but more importantly, motorists anticipated their departures better and spread them across the entire season rather than concentrating them on peak weekends. Declining purchasing power among French households in 2026 also played a role, as fewer and shorter trips meant fewer vehicles on the road. The broader context of reduced gasoline consumption — which dropped sharply in May 2026 — confirms a structural decrease in driving activity, creating fiscal challenges for the state through reduced fuel tax revenue while easing congestion for those who do travel.
