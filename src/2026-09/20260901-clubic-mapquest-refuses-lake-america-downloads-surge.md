# MapQuest Refuses to Rename Lake Ontario, Sees Downloads Surge
**Source**: https://www.clubic.com/actualite-627568-cette-alternative-a-google-maps-n-a-pas-cede-aux-caprices-de-trump-et-explose-les-telechargements.html
**Date**: 2026-09-01
**Author**: Guillaume Belfiore (Clubic)
**Keywords**: MapQuest, Google Maps, Lake Ontario, Lake America, Trump, App Store, mapping alternatives

## Elevator pitch
MapQuest's refusal to rename Lake Ontario to "Lake America" following Trump's executive order propelled it to the top of the US App Store navigation category, with downloads surging 10x above normal levels.

## Takeaways
- MapQuest reached #1 in free navigation apps on the US App Store, with usage multiplied 50x compared to a normal week
- Sensor Tower estimates 184,000 global downloads for the week of August 24, including 162,000 in the US — 10x the usual volume
- Daily US downloads jumped 128% between August 26-30, and MapQuest also reached #1 in Canada
- MapQuest adopted the same approach as with the Gulf of Mexico renaming, which it also refused to implement
- The service, launched in 1996, was acquired by System1 in 2019 after passing through AOL and Verizon

## Synthesis
MapQuest's decision to refuse the Trump administration's executive order renaming Lake Ontario to "Lake America" has proven to be a remarkable commercial success. The mapping service, one of the oldest online cartography platforms, surged to the first position in the navigation category on the US App Store and the eighth position overall (including games). The company reports hundreds of thousands of new downloads since the controversy began.

Doug Berger, MapQuest's general manager, explained the company's approach mirrors its stance during the Gulf of Mexico renaming controversy, which it also refused to implement. The service even launched a satirical tool allowing users to rename the lake at will. This consistent positioning as a mapping provider independent of US political pressure has clearly resonated with users seeking alternatives to Google and Apple Maps.

The episode highlights a competitive dynamic in the mapping market: when dominant players like Google and Apple comply with political naming directives, smaller competitors can differentiate themselves through editorial independence. MapQuest's 24.4 million downloads in 2026 (97% from the US) may be dwarfed by Google Maps, but the surge demonstrates meaningful demand for mapping services that maintain independent geographic naming conventions.
