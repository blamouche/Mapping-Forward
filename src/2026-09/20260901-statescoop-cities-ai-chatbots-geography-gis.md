# Cities Are Giving Their AI Chatbots a New Dimension: Geography
**Source**: https://statescoop.com/cities-ai-chatbots-dimension-geography/
**Date**: 2026-09-01
**Author**: StateScoop
**Keywords**: AI chatbots, GIS, Maricopa County, IBM Watson, Esri, RAG, municipal government, geography

## Elevator pitch
Maricopa County and Washington D.C. are connecting AI chatbots to GIS systems, enabling residents to ask location-based questions like "What construction projects are within a mile of my house?" or "Is my property in a flood zone?"

## Takeaways
- Maricopa County launched Mari (public) and Marco (internal) AI agents built on IBM Watson with Esri GIS tools
- The system uses RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) for text search and Esri tools for spatial queries
- After testing 11,000 prompts, the system launched in June 2026 and averages 706 users per month with 12,000+ questions asked
- "Nearest neighbor" searches convert user addresses to map points and calculate distances to relevant services
- Privacy safeguards include policy warnings, access controls, 13-month retention limits, and guardrails against legal advice

## Synthesis
Maricopa County's deployment of AI chatbots with geographic capabilities represents an emerging trend in municipal technology. Kacie Baker, the county's geographic information officer, and Aaron Judy, chief of innovation and emerging technologies, developed two twin agents: Mari for public constituents and Marco for internal employees. Both are built on IBM Watson conversational models with RAG search and Esri GIS tools handling spatial queries.

The system's "nearest neighbor" search capability is particularly innovative: when a resident asks for the nearest dog shelter or construction project, the system converts their address to a map point, queries relevant datasets, and calculates distances to return the closest results. After testing 11,000 prompts, the system formally launched in June 2026 and now averages 706 users per month. Of the 12,000+ questions asked, 14% concerned property taxes, 8% pet adoption, and 3% county jobs.

Privacy and data quality are key challenges. The county implements policy warnings, access controls, and 13-month retention schedules. Conversations are accessible only to designated staff through an internal curation dashboard. Guardrails keep Mari grounded in official county data and prevent it from providing legal advice. Washington D.C. has launched a similar tool, suggesting this pattern may spread across local governments seeking to make their GIS data more accessible to residents through conversational interfaces.
