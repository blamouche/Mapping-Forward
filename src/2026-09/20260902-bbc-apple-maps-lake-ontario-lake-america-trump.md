# Apple Maps Renames Lake Ontario as "Lake America" After Trump Order
**Source**: https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cddvyyz0e47o
**Date**: 2026-09-02
**Author**: BBC News
**Keywords**: Apple Maps, Google Maps, Lake Ontario, Lake America, Trump, mapping sovereignty, geographic naming

## Elevator pitch
Apple Maps followed Google Maps in renaming Lake Ontario to "Lake America" for US users after a Trump executive order, while MapQuest refused and Canada pushed back — highlighting how mapping platforms have become geopolitical instruments.

## Takeaways
- Apple Maps renamed Lake Ontario to "Lake America" on its web version following a Trump executive order, days after Google made the same change
- Not all map providers complied: MapQuest explicitly refused to rename the lake and saw downloads surge 128% as a result
- Canadian government websites using Google Maps data inadvertently displayed "Lake America" and had to correct their maps back to "lac Ontario"
- The Globe and Mail published an opinion piece calling Google Maps "a tool of digital imperialism"
- Quebec government sites (Quebec511, SAQ, SQDC) and Ontario agencies (Metrolinx, HydroOne, LCBO) were all affected by third-party mapping dependencies

## Synthesis
The renaming of Lake Ontario to "Lake America" across major mapping platforms illustrates the growing tension between political authority and cartographic sovereignty. After President Trump signed an executive order to rename the lake, Google Maps implemented the change for US users, followed by Apple Maps. However, the geographic naming dispute exposed how deeply government services depend on commercial mapping providers: Canadian provincial websites using Google Maps data inadvertently displayed the new name, forcing agencies to issue corrections.

The backlash was swift and multifaceted. MapQuest, one of the oldest online mapping services, refused to comply with the executive order and experienced a dramatic surge in downloads — occupying the first place in navigation apps on the US App Store with usage multiplied 50-fold. The Globe and Mail published an opinion piece framing Google Maps as "a tool of digital imperialism," while Quebec and Ontario government agencies scrambled to restore "lac Ontario" on their interactive maps.

The incident reveals a structural vulnerability: when government mapping services rely on commercial platforms that respond to political pressure from a single country, geographic naming conventions can shift overnight for millions of users worldwide. It also demonstrates how mapping has moved from a neutral technical service to a contested geopolitical domain where naming choices carry significant symbolic weight.
