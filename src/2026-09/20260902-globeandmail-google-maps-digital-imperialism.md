# Google Maps Has Become a Tool of Digital Imperialism
**Source**: https://www.theglobeandmail.com/opinion/article-google-maps-has-become-a-tool-of-digital-imperialism/
**Date**: 2026-09-02
**Author**: The Globe and Mail
**Keywords**: Google Maps, digital imperialism, Lake Ontario, MapQuest, geographic naming, mapping sovereignty

## Elevator pitch
The Globe and Mail argues that Google Maps' compliance with Trump's Lake Ontario renaming order exemplifies how dominant mapping platforms have become instruments of digital imperialism, shaping how millions perceive geography.

## Synthesis
The Globe and Mail's opinion piece frames the Lake Ontario renaming controversy as symptomatic of a broader issue: the concentration of mapping power in the hands of a few technology giants who can be influenced by political pressure from individual governments. Two decades after Yahoo Maps shut down and MapQuest was sold multiple times, Google Maps has achieved near-universal dominance in consumer mapping, making its naming decisions effectively global.

The editorial perspective highlights how mapping platforms, once neutral technical utilities, have become geopolitical actors. When Google Maps renames a body of water at the request of a single government, the change propagates worldwide through embedded maps on government websites, store locators, transit applications, and countless third-party services. This dependency was laid bare when Canadian provincial websites inadvertently displayed "Lake America" because they consumed Google Maps data.

The piece also notes the cultural dimension: mapping references in popular culture, from characters using "Google Maps" as a generic verb, reveal how thoroughly the platform has become synonymous with digital cartography. The result is that a single company's compliance with a political directive can reshape geographic perception for hundreds of millions of users, making the case for mapping diversity and sovereign cartographic infrastructure.
