# TomTom Sees Breakthrough in Autonomous Driving Accelerating

**Author**: De Telegraaf

## Elevator pitch
TomTom expects partially self-driving cars to break through rapidly, predicting that by 2030 approximately 60% of new cars sold in Europe will feature systems capable of largely autonomous steering, acceleration, and braking on highways.

## Synthesis
TomTom's leadership sees the autonomous driving breakthrough accelerating faster than previously anticipated. The navigation specialist predicts that by 2030, approximately 60% of newly sold cars in Europe will be equipped with systems capable of largely autonomous steering, acceleration, and braking on highways. The prediction reflects accelerating adoption of Level 3+ ADAS (Advanced Driver Assistance Systems) across European automotive manufacturers.

CEO Mike Schoofs characterized 2026 as a transition year before the company returns to revenue growth, with the automotive sector's shift toward autonomous driving creating sustained demand for TomTom's core assets: high-definition map data and real-time traffic information. Without accurate map data and current traffic information, autonomous driving systems cannot function safely — positioning TomTom as a critical infrastructure provider for the autonomous vehicle ecosystem.

This prediction aligns with TomTom's strategic positioning at CES 2026, where the company showcased how its automotive navigation combines predictive logic, a proactive AI agent, and integrated EV routing to power the next era of driving. The company's evolution from a consumer navigation device maker to a B2B automotive data provider reflects the broader industry transformation, where map data shifts from being a consumer product to essential infrastructure for autonomous mobility. The 60% figure for 2030 European market penetration represents a significant acceleration from current adoption rates and underscores how quickly ADAS technology is maturing.
