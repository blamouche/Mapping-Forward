# Warum Man in Simmerath Dem Navi Nicht Trauen Soll

**Author**: Aachener Zeitung

## Elevator pitch
The small German municipality of Simmerath has erected official signs telling drivers to ignore their GPS navigation systems, highlighting the tension between algorithmic routing and local traffic management.

## Takeaways
- Simmerath erected blue-arrow signs telling drivers to turn off navigation apps
- Apple, Google, TomTom and other nav systems route through quiet residential areas
- The issue affects a new housing development (Meisenbruch) with ~90 plots
- Heavy construction traffic is being directed through residential streets
- Local officials want drivers to use higher-classified roads (L266, L246) instead

## Synthesis
The small Eifel municipality of Simmerath in Germany has taken the unusual step of erecting official road signs telling drivers not to trust their navigation systems. The signs, featuring a blue arrow, instruct drivers heading to the new Meisenbruch housing development to ignore Apple Maps, Google Maps, TomTom and other navigation apps and follow the official signage instead.

The problem is a familiar one: navigation algorithms optimise for the shortest route without considering the nature of the roads they send traffic down. In Simmerath's case, drivers approaching from certain directions are routed through the quietest residential streets — Heustraße and "In der Mahr" — rather than the higher-classified L266 and L246 roads that the municipality prefers. The routes are identical in length, but the residential routing creates noise and safety issues.

Beigeordnete (deputy mayor) Frank Prömpeler explained that the concern extends beyond private cars to heavy goods vehicles serving the new development. With approximately 90 building plots available, significant construction traffic is expected for years to come. The municipality has hung signs at multiple approach routes to redirect traffic.

The Simmerath case illustrates a growing tension between algorithmic navigation and local traffic management. While navigation providers generally follow public roads, they do not account for municipal preferences about which roads should carry through-traffic. The new development's streets, named after native birds (Dohlen, Drossel, Finken, Lerchen and Meisenweg), will eventually see the signs become less relevant — but for now, Simmerath is one of many communities worldwide pushing back against navigation apps that route traffic through inappropriate areas.
