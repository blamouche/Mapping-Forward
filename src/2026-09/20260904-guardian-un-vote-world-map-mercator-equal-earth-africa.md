# UN to Vote on Adopting New World Map That Shows Africa's True Scale

**Source**: https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/04/un-vote-world-map-mercator-equal-earth-africa
**Date**: 2026-09-04
**Author**: The Guardian
**Keywords**: Mercator projection, Equal Earth, Correct the Map, UN General Assembly, cartography, Africa, Togo

## Elevator pitch
The UN General Assembly voted on a Togo-sponsored resolution to phase out the Mercator projection in favour of the Equal Earth map, which accurately depicts Africa's true size relative to other continents.

## Takeaways
- Togo's foreign minister Robert Dussey sponsored the resolution on behalf of the African Union's 55 member states
- The Mercator projection, created in 1569, makes Africa appear similar in size to Greenland despite being 14 times larger
- The campaign promotes the 2018 Equal Earth projection as a more accurate alternative
- Critics call the Mercator distortion "cartographic colonialism" for visually diminishing the global south
- The resolution is nonbinding but could influence classroom curricula and technology platforms

## Synthesis
The UN General Assembly's vote on the "Correct the Map" resolution marks a historic moment in global cartography. Sponsored by Togo and backed by all 55 African Union member states, the resolution calls for phasing out the 16th-century Mercator projection in favour of the 2018 Equal Earth map, which more accurately represents the true relative sizes of continents.

The Mercator projection, designed by Flemish cartographer Gerardus Mercator in 1569, was originally created for maritime navigation — its key property being that straight lines on the map correspond to constant compass bearings. However, this comes at a cost: regions near the equator appear significantly smaller than they are, while high-latitude areas appear much larger. The most striking distortion is Africa appearing roughly the size of Greenland, when the continent is in fact 14 times larger.

Togo's foreign minister Robert Dussey framed the initiative as a scientific rather than political correction, noting that "there is scientific proof so we all have to accept the reality." The campaign has gathered over 11,000 signatures on Change.org and draws on decades of criticism from historians and geographers who argue the Mercator map reinforces Western biases by minimising the physical presence of the global south.

The Equal Earth projection, developed by an international team of cartographers in 2018, preserves area accuracy while maintaining a visually pleasing continental shape. The resolution, while nonbinding, could lead to updates in classroom curricula, government maps, and technology platforms including Google Maps, which still uses Mercator for its web interface.
