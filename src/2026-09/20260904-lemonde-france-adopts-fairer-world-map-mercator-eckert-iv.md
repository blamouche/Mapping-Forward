## Synthesis
On September 4, 2026, France made a significant cartographic shift by officially abandoning the Mercator projection in favour of Eckert IV for its diplomatic world maps. The announcement came from French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot at the opening of La Fabrique de la Diplomatie, the largest annual event of the French diplomatic corps, in Paris.

The change means that France, the United States, and Russia — all high-latitude nations inflated by the Mercator projection — will appear noticeably smaller on official French maps. Barrot framed the decision as both a matter of accuracy and diplomatic narrative: "Our diplomacy must fight on two fronts: to put forward our own narrative and to always ensure we represent the world accurately. These distortions end up shaping perceptions. The time has come for us to correct that."

France's choice of Eckert IV rather than the Equal Earth projection endorsed by the UN is notable — Eckert IV is an equal-area projection that maintains a compromise between area accuracy and shape fidelity, developed by Max Eckert in 1906. France also co-sponsored the African-led "Correct the Map" resolution at the UN General Assembly, aligning its cartographic policy with the broader diplomatic push to decolonise global map representations.

The move signals that the Mercator correction is not merely an African concern but a shift embraced by Western powers willing to see their own territorial presence visually reduced in the name of accuracy.
