# UN Approves Resolution in Support of Map That Shows Africa's True Size

**Source**: https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/un-approves-resolution-support-map-that-shows-africas-true-size-2026-09-04/
**Date**: 2026-09-04
**Author**: Reuters
**Keywords**: UN General Assembly, Correct the Map, Equal Earth, Mercator, Africa, Togo, resolution

## Elevator pitch
Reuters confirms the UN General Assembly approved the "Correct the Map" resolution, with Togo leading the initiative and announcing immediate changes to school maps.

## Takeaways
- The resolution was adopted with 164 votes in favour, 1 against, and 6 abstentions
- Togo led the UN initiative as part of the African Union campaign
- The 16th-century Mercator map diminishes Africa's size; the Equal Earth projection corrects this
- Togo's minister announced the country would change school maps this year
- The vote was largely symbolic but could influence global mapping standards

## Synthesis
Reuters reported the UN General Assembly's approval of the "Correct the Map" resolution on September 4, 2026, confirming the lopsided vote of 164 to 1 (United States) with 6 abstentions. The resolution, led by Togo on behalf of the African Union, calls for replacing the 16th-century Mercator projection with one that more accurately shows Africa's true size.

The news agency noted that Togo's foreign minister announced the country would change maps in schools starting this year, making it one of the first concrete implementations of the resolution. The Mercator projection, created by Flemish cartographer Gerardus Mercator in 1569, has been the de facto standard for world maps for over 450 years, but its well-known distortion makes Africa appear similar in size to Greenland, when the continent is actually 14 times larger.

Reuters highlighted that the resolution, while nonbinding, represents a significant milestone in the years-long campaign by African nations to correct what they see as a cartographic legacy of colonialism. The Equal Earth projection, developed in 2018, is the primary alternative promoted by the campaign.

The United States was the sole nation voting against the resolution, while traditional Western allies including France co-sponsored it, signalling that the cartographic correction has become a broad international consensus rather than a purely African initiative.
