## Synthesis
Dutch business radio BNR Nieuwsradio published an analysis examining whether TomTom can recapture the glory of its consumer-navigation peak years (the "jaren nul" or 2000s). After a series of unsuccessful consumer product launches, TomTom has pivoted its strategy toward serving enterprises and governments with its mapping and location intelligence technology.

The article reflects on TomTom's transformation from a consumer GPS device manufacturer — once ubiquitous on European dashboards — to a B2B location data and technology provider. This strategic shift follows years of decline in the standalone GPS market, made obsolete by smartphone navigation apps like Google Maps and Waze.

TomTom's core assets remain its high-quality map database (now branded as Orbis Maps) and its traffic intelligence capabilities. The company has been positioning these assets for autonomous driving, fleet management, and smart city applications. The BNR piece asks whether this enterprise-focused strategy can deliver growth comparable to the consumer boom years, or whether TomTom will remain a smaller but more stable niche player in the geospatial ecosystem.

The question is timely: TomTom's recent partnerships with Microsoft, Cedar Electronics, and transit technology firms suggest the B2B pivot is gaining traction, but whether it can translate into the scale of its consumer heyday remains an open question for investors and industry observers.
