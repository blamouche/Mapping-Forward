# Waze contre Google Maps : 4 Points Forts de Waze

**Source**: https://samsungmagazine.eu/fr/2026/09/05/waze-vs-google-mapy/
**Date**: 2026-09-05
**Author**: Martin Duchoslav, Samsung Magazine
**Keywords**: Waze, Google Maps, navigation, community reporting, EV charging, speed cameras, comparison

## Elevator pitch
Despite Google owning Waze for over a decade, the community-driven navigation app still outperforms Google Maps in four key areas: real-time incident reporting, volunteer-managed local data, EV charging routing, and speed camera alerts.

## Takeaways
- Waze's community-driven incident reporting remains superior to Google Maps
- Volunteer contributors update local data that algorithms cannot capture
- Waze offers EV-specific connector type and charging network preferences
- Google Maps excels at finding businesses, reviews, and offline resources
- The two platforms have not merged despite a decade of shared ownership

## Synthesis
Samsung Magazine published a comparison arguing that despite Google's acquisition of Waze more than ten years ago and the gradual adoption of Waze features into Google Maps, the two platforms remain distinct — and Waze still holds advantages in core navigation functionality.

The article identifies four areas where Waze outperforms Google Maps. First, real-time incident reporting: while Google Maps allows users to report accidents and obstacles, Waze's active community provides more precise and localised information, including potholes, dangerous curves, construction zones, and police patrols in real-time. Second, volunteer-managed local details: Waze's network of volunteer contributors updates data that Google's automated algorithms cannot capture, such as school zones with limited hours, bus lanes, and municipality-specific restrictions.

Third, EV charging support: Waze lets users specify their vehicle's connector type and preferred charging networks, then adapts routes based on battery compatibility and available charging stations — avoiding the risk of arriving at an incompatible charger. Fourth, speed camera alerts (implied by the broader reporting context): Waze's community-driven approach to hazard reporting extends to speed camera locations, which remains a key differentiator.

The author acknowledges that Google Maps excels at finding businesses, reading reviews, and accessing offline resources, but argues that for pure navigation — especially for drivers who encounter dynamic road conditions — Waze remains the stronger choice. The persistence of this gap, despite a decade of shared ownership, raises questions about whether the two platforms will ever fully merge or whether Google is deliberately maintaining them as complementary products.
