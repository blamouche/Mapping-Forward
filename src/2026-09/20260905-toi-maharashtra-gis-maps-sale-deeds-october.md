# GIS Maps to Be Attached to Sale Deeds from October in Maharashtra

**Source**: https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/pune/gis-maps-to-be-attached-to-sale-deeds-from-oct-in-maharashtra-rollout-in-10-districts-in-first-phase/articleshow/133775829.cms
**Date**: 2026-09-05
**Author**: Nisha Nambiar, The Times of India
**Keywords**: GIS, Maharashtra, property registration, sale deeds, land records, mojini, K-Prat, digital mapping, India

## Elevator pitch
Maharashtra will attach GIS maps to property sale deeds starting October 2026, replacing decades-old boundary descriptions with precise digital maps in an initial 10-district rollout.

## Takeaways
- GIS-linked sale deeds will replace textual boundary descriptions in Maharashtra
- Initial rollout covers 10 districts, with statewide expansion to follow
- The system integrates land records, building footprints, and satellite imagery
- The 'mojini' (land measurement) system will be implemented before registration
- Benefits include precise identification, fewer boundary disputes, and fraud deterrence

## Synthesis
Maharashtra is set to become one of the first Indian states to attach Geographic Information System (GIS) maps to property sale deeds, with a rollout expected to begin in October 2026 across 10 districts. The initiative replaces the decades-old practice of describing plot boundaries through cardinal directions and landmarks with precise digital maps linked to each transaction.

The Department of Land Records has submitted a proposal to the state government for implementing 'mojini' (land measurement) before property registration. Once approved, the department will prepare a standard operating procedure and integrate the K-Prat digital land measurement system with the registration department. The system will link every property mentioned in a sale deed to its precise location on a digital map.

Currently, sale deeds describe boundaries by referencing east, west, north, and south sides along with adjoining survey numbers, roads, or buildings. Officials note that such descriptions can be ambiguous and have contributed to disputes, particularly where land records are fragmented or claims overlap. The GIS-linked system will integrate land ownership records, building footprints, and data from multiple government agencies.

Initially, high-resolution satellite imagery will provide the mapping baseline until ground-level resurvey and mapping is completed. The central government has been working with states on this initiative and has sought inputs from the GIS industry. Officials emphasise that GIS-linked sale deeds could help with precise identification, fewer boundary disputes, greater transparency, overlap detection, fraud deterrence, better records, and easier future transactions. The rollout complements other reforms including Aadhaar-linked registration and mandatory prior land measurement.
