# UN Tells the World: Stop Making Africa Look Small

**Source**: https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/09/1168284
**Date**: 2026-09-05
**Author**: UN News
**Keywords**: UN General Assembly, Correct the Map, Equal Earth, Mercator, Africa, Togo, cartography

## Elevator pitch
The UN General Assembly voted overwhelmingly to adopt the "Correct the Map" resolution, urging governments, schools, and tech companies to stop using maps that make Africa look smaller than it is.

## Takeaways
- The resolution passed with 164 votes in favour, 1 against (United States), and 6 abstentions
- The vote encourages governments, schools, and tech companies to adopt equal-area map projections
- Togo spearheaded the resolution on behalf of the African Union

- The resolution is nonbinding but carries significant symbolic and practical weight

## Synthesis
The United Nations General Assembly voted overwhelmingly on September 4, 2026, to adopt resolution A/80/L.104, "Correct the Map," with 164 nations in favour, only the United States voting against, and six abstentions. The resolution urges governments, schools, and technology companies worldwide to phase out maps that use the Mercator projection, which distorts Africa's true size.

The measure, sponsored by Togo on behalf of the African Union, is nonbinding — Mercator is not disappearing overnight. But the near-unanimous vote gives international backing to an argument that many African governments have pressed with growing urgency: that the choices made over map projections shape how generations understand the world and each continent's place in it.

Togo's foreign minister announced that the country would begin changing maps in its schools starting this year, making it one of the first nations to implement the resolution at the practical level. The campaign promotes the Equal Earth projection, developed in 2018, which preserves area accuracy and shows Africa at its true proportional size — 14 times larger than Greenland, as opposed to appearing similar in size under Mercator.

The vote builds on a March 2026 Ghana-sponsored resolution that described the slave trade as "the gravest crime against humanity," suggesting a broader African Union strategy to reshape international representations of the continent's history and geography. The lopsided vote — with even traditional allies of the US joining — underscores how the cartographic correction has transcended its origins as an African initiative to become a broadly shared global consensus.
