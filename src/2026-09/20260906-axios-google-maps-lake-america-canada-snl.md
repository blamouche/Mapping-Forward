# Google Maps Switches to "Lake America" as Canada Compares Change to SNL Skit

**Source**: https://www.axios.com/2026/08/30/lake-america-lake-ontario-maps-trump
**Date**: 2026-08-30
**Author**: Donica Phifer, Axios
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Apple Maps, MapQuest, Lake Ontario, Lake America, Trump, Canada, Doug Ford, GNIS, geographic names

## Elevator pitch
As Google Maps implements the "Lake America" name change following Trump's executive order, MapQuest refuses and surges in popularity while Canada's Ontario Premier Doug Ford calls the change "completely absurd."

## Takeaways
- Google Maps now shows "Lake America" on mobile and desktop for US users, while Apple Maps initially kept "Lake Ontario"
- MapQuest publicly refused the name change and its tweet went viral, boosting the app to #1 on the US App Store
- "Lake America" appeared on Ontario government agency websites pulling data from Google Maps, despite Google saying Canadian users would see "Lake Ontario"
- Ontario Premier Doug Ford compared the change to a "Saturday Night Live" skit and installed a "Lake Ontario, today and forever" sign
- Google's region-localization means developers embedding Maps can set a Canada region to display "Lake Ontario"

## Synthesis
President Trump's executive order renaming Lake Ontario to "Lake America" has created a split among digital map providers, with Google complying, Apple initially resisting, and MapQuest publicly refusing the change. An Axios review on August 30 showed Google Maps displaying "Lake America" on both mobile and desktop, while Apple Maps continued showing "Lake Ontario."

Google stated it changed the maps in accordance with updates from the US Geographic Names Information System (GNIS), following its long-standing policy of reflecting name changes from official government sources. However, "Lake America" appeared on some Ontario government agency websites that pull data from Google Maps, despite Google's statement that Canadian users would continue to see "Lake Ontario." A Google spokesperson explained that website developers who embed Google Maps can select a region to localize the map, which determines how place names are displayed.

MapQuest, the internet mapping pioneer launched in 1996, refused the change outright. Its tweet — "We're not changing the name of Lake Ontario. Name it whatever you want at your leisure" — went viral, and the app surged to #1 on the US App Store's free downloads chart, surpassing TikTok and ChatGPT. MapQuest had previously refused to rename the Gulf of Mexico to "Gulf of America" in 2025, and added a tool letting users rename geographic features themselves.

The backlash was swift. Ontario Premier Doug Ford compared the move to a "Saturday Night Live" skit on ABC's "This Week," saying: "No one's going to call it Lake of America. It's been Lake Ontario for hundreds of years." Ford installed a sign reading "Lake Ontario, today and forever" at the waterfront. The split among tech companies highlights how digital mapping has become a politically contested space, with naming decisions carrying diplomatic weight.
