# How the GNIS Lake Ontario/Lake America Name Change Will Appear in Google Maps

**Source**: https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/gnis-lake-ontario-lake-america-name-change/
**Date**: 2026-08-30
**Author**: Google Blog
**Keywords**: Google Maps, GNIS, Lake Ontario, Lake America, Trump executive order, geographic names, place names, Canada, United States

## Elevator pitch
Google's official blog explains that Maps will show "Lake America" to US users, "Lake Ontario" to Canadian users, and both names to everyone else, following the GNIS name change under Trump's executive order.

## Takeaways
- The US Geographic Names Information System (GNIS) formally changed "Lake Ontario" to "Lake America" in the United States
- Google Maps follows official government sources (GNIS for the US) for name changes
- US users see "Lake America," Canadian users see "Lake Ontario," and users outside both countries see both names
- The update follows Google's long-standing policy for bodies of water with country-specific names
- Changes were rolling out as of August 30, 2026

## Synthesis
Google's official blog post details how the company will implement the name change from "Lake Ontario" to "Lake America" in Google Maps, following the US Geographic Names Information System (GNIS) formally updating its records. GNIS is the official federal database of geographic names for the United States, and Google Maps reflects name changes from this source.

The implementation follows Google's established policy for bodies of water that have different names across countries. Users in the United States will see "Lake America," users in Canada will continue to see "Lake Ontario," and users outside both countries will see both names displayed together. This approach mirrors Google's earlier handling of the Gulf of Mexico/Gulf of America name change.

The change stems from an executive order signed by President Trump on August 27, 2026, renaming the lake. Google's policy of deferring to official government naming sources means the company implements such changes when GNIS updates its records, regardless of political controversy. The update began rolling out on August 30, 2026.

Notably, Google's blog post frames the change as a routine administrative update following long-standing policy, rather than a political decision. However, the move has drawn significant criticism from Canadian officials, including Ontario Premier Doug Ford, who called the change "completely absurd" and compared it to a Saturday Night Live skit. Competitor MapQuest publicly refused to make the change, while Apple Maps initially maintained "Lake Ontario" before updating its display.
