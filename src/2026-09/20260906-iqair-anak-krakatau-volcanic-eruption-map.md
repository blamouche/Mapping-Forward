## Synthesis
As of September 6, 2026, Anak Krakatau — whose name means "child of Krakatau" — is erupting in Indonesia's Sunda Strait between the islands of Java and Sumatra. The volcano emerged within the caldera left by the catastrophic 1883 eruption of Krakatau, one of the most powerful volcanic events in recorded history. The current eruptive activity began late on September 4 and has produced lava fountains, booming sounds, and ash plumes reaching approximately 50,000 feet on the Sumatra side.

The eruption has caused significant disruption across western Indonesia. Volcanic ash has spread over communities, transportation networks, schools, and fishing activities. Airport operations were disrupted in Jakarta, Bandung, and Lampung, with flights serving Bali, Medan, Surabaya, and several international destinations experiencing cancellations, delays, or diversions. Over 300 flights were affected in total.

The volcano remains at Alert Level III, the second-highest level on Indonesia's four-level volcanic warning scale. Repeated eruptions indicate an ongoing supply of magma and volcanic gases, and officials continue to monitor seismic activity, ash emissions, and changes around the crater. Authorities have reported no immediate tsunami threat, but the nearest settlement is more than 16 kilometers from the volcano. A 3-kilometer exclusion zone has been established around the active crater.

Indonesia's disaster mitigation agency has announced plans for weather modification measures, including cloud seeding, to encourage rainfall that may help remove ash from affected areas. People affected by ashfall have been advised to wear masks and eye protection, limit outdoor activity, cover water sources, and drive carefully due to reduced visibility.
