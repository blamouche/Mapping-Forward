# Google Maps Bluetooth Beacons Bring Precise Navigation to Tunnels

**Source**: https://www.larepubliquedespyrenees.fr/sciences-et-technologie/web-et-internet/comment-activer-la-nouvelle-fonctionnalite-de-google-maps-qui-rend-la-navigation-encore-plus-precise-30400557.php
**Date**: 2026-08-29
**Author**: Maxime Lacaze, La République des Pyrénées
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Bluetooth beacons, tunnels, GPS, navigation, Android, driving, Waze, precision

## Elevator pitch
Google Maps now uses Bluetooth beacons installed in tunnels to maintain navigation continuity when GPS signals drop, an Android-only feature that Waze has offered for years.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps uses Bluetooth beacons installed in tunnels to maintain navigation when GPS signal is lost
- The feature is activated via Settings > Navigation > Driving options > "Tunnel Bluetooth beacon"
- Currently Android-only because Apple limits background Bluetooth usage on iPhone
- Waze has offered a similar beacon-based tunnel navigation service for several years
- The feature provides real-time positioning information inside tunnels where GPS is unavailable

## Synthesis
Losing GPS signal inside tunnels and missing the correct exit is a common problem for drivers. Google Maps has introduced a new feature that uses Bluetooth beacons — small transmitters installed in many tunnels — to maintain navigation continuity when satellite signals cannot penetrate underground infrastructure.

The feature, called "Tunnel Bluetooth beacon," relies on beacons that transmit positioning data to the phone's Bluetooth receiver, providing real-time navigation information even in the absence of GPS. This ensures that the driver continues to receive turn-by-turn directions and exit notifications while passing through tunnels, where GPS signal loss can last for several kilometers on major highways.

To activate the feature, users need an up-to-date Google Maps app on Android. The setting is found in the app's parameters under the "Navigation" tab, then "Driving options," where the "Tunnel Bluetooth beacon" toggle can be enabled. Once the required Bluetooth permissions are granted, the system becomes operational.

A significant limitation is that the feature is exclusively available on Android smartphones. Apple restricts background Bluetooth usage on iOS, preventing Google Maps from offering the same functionality on iPhone. This platform divide means iPhone users continue to experience GPS signal loss in tunnels without the beacon workaround.

Google is not innovating in this space — Waze, the competing navigation app also owned by Google, has offered beacon-based tunnel navigation for several years. The feature's arrival in Google Maps represents a convergence of capabilities between the two Google-owned navigation platforms.
