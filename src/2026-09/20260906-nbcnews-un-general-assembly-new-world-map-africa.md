# UN General Assembly Endorses New World Map Showing Africa's True Size

**Source**: https://www.nbcnews.com/world/africa/un-general-assembly-endorses-new-world-map-shows-africas-size-accurate-rcna596305
**Date**: 2026-09-05
**Author**: Associated Press / NBC News
**Keywords**: UN General Assembly, Equal Earth, Mercator, Africa, Togo, African Union, Correct the Map, cartography, vote

## Elevator pitch
The UN General Assembly endorsed the Equal Earth projection by 164-1, encouraging governments and institutions worldwide to replace the Mercator map that has distorted Africa's size for over four centuries.

## Takeaways
- 164 member states voted in favor, only the US voted against, and 6 countries abstained
- The resolution encourages replacing the Mercator projection with Equal Earth but does not ban Mercator or impose a replacement
- Africa is 14 times larger than Greenland, but Mercator makes them appear similar in size
- French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot called the correction "an act of truth"
- Advocacy group Africa No Filter wants accurate maps in schools, textbooks, newsrooms, and digital platforms

## Synthesis
The United Nations General Assembly voted on September 4, 2026, to endorse a new world map that represents Africa more accurately, marking the culmination of a campaign led by African countries and advocacy groups. The resolution, sponsored by Togo and backed by the African Union, passed with 164 member states voting in favor. Only the United States voted against it, while Serbia, Estonia, Georgia, Lithuania, Moldova, and Ukraine abstained.

The resolution encourages governments and institutions worldwide to replace the traditional Mercator map — the standard for over four centuries — with the Equal Earth projection, which shows continents in their true proportions. The distortion is significant: on a Mercator map, Greenland and Africa appear roughly the same size, whereas Africa is actually 14 times larger than Greenland. The Mercator projection enlarges regions near the poles (North America, Greenland, Europe) while shrinking Africa and South America.

The resolution does not ban the Mercator projection or impose a replacement. It merely encourages wider use of the Equal Earth projection, developed in 2018, which follows the Earth's curvature and preserves true area. Togo's Foreign Minister Robert Dussey told the Assembly that "maps shape our understanding of the world" and "guide education, nourish the imagination, and influence collective perceptions."

The advocacy group Africa No Filter, one of the lead campaigners, hailed the resolution and called for accurate maps "in schools, textbooks, newsrooms, businesses and on the digital platforms billions of people use every day." French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot said on X: "Changing the map obviously doesn't change the world. But correcting a representation that distorts it is already an act of truth." Syracuse University geography professor Mark Monmonier noted that outside of narrow navigation applications, "there is no point in using" the Mercator projection.
