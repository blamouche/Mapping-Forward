## Synthesis
The United Nations General Assembly has voted to promote the use of world map projections that more accurately represent the relative size of continents. The resolution was presented during a session at UN headquarters in New York on September 4, 2026, and received 164 votes in favor, with one country voting against and six abstaining.

Sponsored by Togo, the resolution encourages governments, educational institutions, international organizations, and technology companies to use equal-area projections when geographic scale is relevant. The resolution does not formally replace or prohibit the use of the Mercator projection, which has been widely used since the 16th century. Instead, it calls for greater use of projections that preserve the relative area of landmasses and encourages education about the limitations of representing the Earth's curved surface on a two-dimensional map.

The 2018 Equal Earth projection is identified as one example of a map that more accurately represents continental scale. Unlike the Mercator projection, which exaggerates the size of landmasses at higher latitudes — making Greenland appear comparable to Africa despite Africa being 14 times larger — Equal Earth preserves the relative area of all landmasses.

The resolution's impact could extend beyond cartography into architecture and urban planning, where accurate geographic representation informs design decisions and spatial understanding. For the design and architecture community, the shift toward equal-area projections affects how global projects are contextualized and how the relationship between built environments and their geographic contexts is understood.
