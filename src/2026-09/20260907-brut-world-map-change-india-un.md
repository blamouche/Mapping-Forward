## Synthesis
On September 4, 2026, the UN General Assembly adopted a resolution titled "Correct the Map: Rebalancing Global Cartographic Representation and Promoting Equitable Representation of the World's Regions, Particularly Africa." Introduced by Togo on behalf of African countries, it passed with 164 countries voting in favor, the United States voting against, and six countries abstaining — Serbia, Estonia, Georgia, Lithuania, Moldova, and Ukraine.

The resolution calls for wider use and consideration of equal-area map projections, including the Equal Earth projection developed in 2018. Unlike the Mercator projection (1569), which was designed for navigation and exaggerates the size of regions farther from the Equator, Equal Earth preserves the relative size of landmasses. On an Equal Earth map, Africa appears significantly larger, Europe and North America appear smaller, and India appears slightly larger. However, no flat map is completely distortion-free — every projection must compromise between area, shape, distance, and direction.

The resolution does not ban the Mercator projection or legally force countries to adopt Equal Earth. It encourages governments, educational institutions, international organizations, and technology companies to use equal-area projections when geographic scale is relevant.

India's position has two components. It voted in favor, supporting the broader use of equal-area projections, but clarified this should not be interpreted as endorsing one specific map projection. India also raised a concern about territorial representation: the Ministry of External Affairs stated that any UN-backed map must accurately depict India's sovereign territory, including Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh, in accordance with India's official map. Any representation that is inaccurate or misleading would not be acceptable.

The resolution does not redraw international borders or require countries to replace existing maps. It could lead to greater use of equal-area maps in schools, public information, and digital mapping, while the Mercator projection will continue to be used for navigation purposes.
