# Wildfire Map Spotlight: Konitsa Fire, Greece

**Source**: https://www.iqair.com/ca-fr/newsroom/wildfire-map-spotlight-konitsa-wildfire-greece
**Date**: 2026-09-07
**Author**: IQAir Staff Writers
**Keywords**: wildfire, Greece, Konitsa, Epirus, mapping, air quality, disaster response, interactive maps, Vikos-Aoos National Park, firefighting

## Elevator pitch
A major wildfire in Greece's Konitsa region is burning in rugged mountain terrain near Vikos-Aoos National Park, with 170 firefighters deployed and emergency alerts issued to residents amid heavy smoke and difficult access conditions.

## Takeaways
- The Konitsa wildfire began August 31, 2026 and intensified through September 7 in the Trapezitsa area near Konitsa, Epirus, northwestern Greece
- About 170 firefighters, 45 vehicles, 20 support vehicles, aircraft, helicopters, and drone teams are involved in suppression efforts
- A 112 emergency alert was sent to Konitsa residents urging alertness due to the wildfire and heavy smoke
- The fire threatens Vikos-Aoos National Park, the Aoos Gorge, and the Tymfi mountain area
- Steep terrain, dense vegetation, and strong flare-ups continue to complicate firefighting operations
- The trail toward Stomio Monastery was preventively closed due to fire and dense smoke

## Synthesis
As of September 7, 2026, the Konitsa wildfire is actively burning in the mountainous Trapezitsa area near Konitsa in Epirus, northwestern Greece. The fire began on August 31 and has intensified following stronger flare-ups in recent days. Steep, difficult-to-access terrain and dense vegetation have significantly complicated firefighting efforts, with crews working to prevent the fire from moving toward Konitsa or crossing the Aoos River into the Tymfi mountain area.

The affected areas include Konitsa and the Vikos-Aoos National Park, with heavy smoke covering the town and the fire threatening forests and protected areas around the Aoos Gorge and Tymfi. About 170 firefighters, 45 firefighting vehicles, 20 support vehicles, aircraft, helicopters, volunteers, and drone teams are involved in the operation.

A 112 emergency message was sent to residents in the Konitsa area urging them to remain alert and follow authorities' instructions. Authorities advised people with respiratory or heart conditions to stay in sealed, air-conditioned spaces or leave the area if necessary. The trail toward Stomio Monastery was also closed preventively due to the fire and dense smoke.
