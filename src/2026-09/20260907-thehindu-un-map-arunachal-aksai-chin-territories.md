## Synthesis
Two days after India supported the "Correct the Map" resolution in the UN General Assembly on September 4, 2026, a new UN world map has raised concerns by depicting Arunachal Pradesh and Aksai Chin as territories caught between Indian and Chinese claim lines. The map, published on July 1, 2026, labels Arunachal's southern border with Assam as the "Chinese Line" while showing the state's northern border with China's Tibet as the "Indian Line." Similarly, Aksai Chin's eastern borders are labeled "Indian Line," with a "Chinese line" serving as demarcation between Ladakh and Aksai Chin — territory India claims as part of Ladakh.

The map does include a note explaining the dotted Line of Control in Jammu and Kashmir as an India-Pakistan agreement with final status not yet settled. However, no explanatory note accompanies the separate Indian and Chinese claim lines around Arunachal Pradesh and Aksai Chin, leaving the territorial disputes visually ambiguous.

India's Ministry of External Affairs reiterated that the vote supported the underlying principle of promoting equal-area cartographic representation, not endorsement of any specific map or boundary depiction. Official Spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal stated that India's sovereign territory, including the Union Territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh, must be depicted in accordance with India's official map, and that any inaccurate or misleading depiction is unacceptable. The MEA has not yet responded to queries about the specific demarcation of Arunachal Pradesh and Aksai Chin with contesting claim lines.

The map appears to favor India on the Kalapani border dispute with Nepal, reflecting Indian control over the Kalapani-Lipulekh-Limpiyadhura region claimed by Nepal. The non-binding resolution map is expected to be used by multilateral institutions worldwide despite its advisory nature.
