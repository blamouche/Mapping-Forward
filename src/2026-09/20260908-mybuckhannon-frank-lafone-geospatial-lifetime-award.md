## Synthesis
Fairmont State University geography faculty member Dr. Frank LaFone has been honored with the West Virginia Association of Geospatial Professionals (WVAGP) Lifetime Achievement Award, recognizing his career-long contributions to the advancement of geographic information systems (GIS) and geospatial technology throughout West Virginia. The award was presented at WV GEOCON, the state's annual geospatial conference.

Over the course of more than two decades, LaFone has played a pivotal role in advancing the geospatial profession in West Virginia. He was a founding member and key architect of WVAGP, helping establish and grow the organization into a leading advocate for GIS professionals across the state. His early GISP (Geographic Information Systems Professional) certification — obtained early in the program's history — demonstrated his commitment to professional standards, and he has served multiple terms on the WVAGP Board of Directors, including as president and vice president.

Beyond organizational leadership, LaFone co-founded VerySpatial, one of the longest-running geography and geospatial podcasts. This venture has contributed to professional education and engagement on a national and international scale, extending his influence far beyond West Virginia's borders. The podcast represents an early and sustained effort to build community among geospatial professionals through digital media.

The WVAGP Lifetime Achievement Award recognizes individuals whose dedication, leadership, and service have helped shape the future of geospatial technology in West Virginia. LaFone was nominated by WVAGP members, with the nomination approved by the Board of Directors, Board President Meryl Friedrich, and Taryn Moser, West Virginia state GIS coordinator at the West Virginia Geological and Economic Survey. The award is presented by the state GIS coordinator each year.

In his current role as a faculty member at Fairmont State University, LaFone continues to champion GIS education by connecting students with opportunities to apply geospatial technologies to address community needs throughout West Virginia. "You always work hard to do your best for your community, your state, your country, and your profession as good work is its own reward," LaFone said. "However, it is always humbling and a privilege when your peers recognize your work and seek to celebrate you for it."

The award highlights the importance of state-level geospatial professional organizations in advancing GIS adoption, education, and standards — and the role of dedicated individuals in building the infrastructure that supports modern mapping and spatial analysis capabilities.
