# TomTom Expands EV Charger Data Past 2 Million Points

**Source**: https://theevreport.com/tomtom-ev-charging-data-2-million-chargers
**Date**: 2026-09-08
**Author**: The EV Report Staff
**Keywords**: TomTom, EV charging, 2 million chargers, availability profiles, live pricing, AI range prediction, super aggregator, Drew Meehan, EV POI data, charging infrastructure

## Elevator pitch
TomTom now covers more than two million EV charging points globally and plans to ship an AI-based battery range prediction model by the end of 2026, as the company reports delivering on its two-year EV roadmap with overhauled POI data, availability profiles, and live pricing.

- TomTom's EV charging data now covers over 2 million charging points globally

## Synthesis
TomTom has crossed a significant milestone in its EV charging data coverage, now encompassing more than two million EV charging points globally. The figures come from a TomTom newsroom update in which Drew Meehan, who leads the company's Destination and Waypoint Management product group, reviews progress against the EV roadmap TomTom published two years ago.

The company reports it has rebuilt its EV charging point-of-interest (POI) data, introduced more granular driver preferences for routing and charging, launched availability profiles and live pricing for charging stations, and unified its search and routing stack for EV-specific navigation. A user feedback mechanism for correcting charger data is in development, and charger ratings and reviews are due soon.

TomTom attributes its coverage growth to what Meehan calls a "super aggregator" model: working with regional data partners rather than connecting to every charge point operator individually. This approach lets the company extend coverage into new markets more quickly. According to Meehan, "In the places that matter most to our customers, we're often better than the competition. Across the whole world, I would feel confident saying that the global leaders in EV POI data are now at parity with each other, TomTom included."

Beyond raw coverage, TomTom is emphasizing data depth. Availability profiles draw on six weeks of rolling usage data, applying the methodology the company uses for historic traffic flow, to show drivers how busy a charging point is likely to be at their expected arrival time. Reliability scoring, amenity information and pricing round out the station data. This approach builds on the EV routing and charging-stop capabilities TomTom co-developed with CARIAD for Volkswagen Group's next-generation navigation system.

The shift in driver expectations is notable. TomTom says drivers now ask for prices, payment integration, nearby amenities, security lighting, and covered parking. "Now that we've satisfied that base level expectation; drivers are now applying personal preferences over simply finding a place to charge," Meehan said. "They're choosing a lower price. They're choosing stops where they can get a coffee or eat or go to the toilet. They're looking for stops that have WiFi, or where there are lights over the chargers for their personal safety."

TomTom's next step is an AI-based service that learns a driver's habits, anticipates charging needs and manages the vehicle day to day. The range prediction upgrade moves from multi-variate equations to learning models trained on actual usage data, accounting for weather, roof boxes, towing, tire pressure, open windows and driving style. The update is on track for release by the end of the year in a new and revised API. Meehan also expects vehicle-to-grid and vehicle-to-load functions to grow in importance, with the caveat that the driver experience must be reliable — a driver who wakes to find the car at 15% charge because it fed the grid overnight will not use the feature again.
