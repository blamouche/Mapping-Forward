# The Next Chapter in Electric Driving Isn't About EVs

**Source**: https://www.tomtom.com/newsroom/behind-the-map/tomtoms-2026-ev-vision-and-future
**Date**: 2026-09-08
**Author**: Matthew Beedham, TomTom Newsroom
**Keywords**: TomTom, EV, electric vehicle, charging infrastructure, EV charging data, range anxiety, AI range prediction, Drew Meehan, Destination and Waypoint Management, Orbis Maps

## Elevator pitch
TomTom reviews its two-year EV roadmap, revealing that EV charging data now covers over 2 million points globally, with AI-based range prediction and background intelligence features planned by year-end as the company shifts focus from simply finding chargers to helping drivers choose the right one.

## Takeaways
- EVs now account for close to 30% of new car sales across Europe on average, with battery EVs at 20%; Norway leads at over 97%
- TomTom has overhauled its EV charging POI data, added availability profiles, live pricing, and unified its search and routing stack for EV-specific navigation
- The company uses a "super aggregator" model, working with regional data partners rather than connecting to every charge point operator individually
- Driver demand has shifted from finding a charger to choosing one based on price, amenities, security, and covered parking
- An AI-based range prediction API is on track for release by end of 2026, moving from multi-variate equations to learning models trained on actual usage data
- The EV team has been reorganized as "Destination and Waypoint Management," expanding scope to POI search, fuel prices, parking, and in-car transactions

## Synthesis
TomTom's 2026 EV vision update marks a significant inflection point in the company's approach to electric mobility. Drew Meehan, who now leads the Destination and Waypoint Management product group (expanded from an EV-only remit), reflects on how the market has transformed since the company first published its EV roadmap two years ago.

The numbers tell a story of rapid maturation: across Europe, EVs now account for close to 30% of new car sales (BEV and PHEV combined), with battery EVs alone at 20%. The Netherlands has surpassed 40%, and Norway operates in a category of its own with over 97% of new vehicles sold being electric. Public charging infrastructure has doubled over the past four years, making range and charging anxiety increasingly a thing of the past for early adopters.

TomTom's response has been to deliver systematically on its 2024 roadmap promises. The company has rebuilt its EV charging point-of-interest data from the ground up, introduced availability profiles that use six weeks of rolling usage data to predict how busy a charging point will be at a given arrival time, added live pricing, and unified its search and routing stack for EV-specific navigation. Charger ratings and reviews are due soon, and a user feedback mechanism for correcting charger data is in development.

The coverage growth strategy relies on what Meehan calls a "super aggregator" model: rather than connecting to every charge point operator individually, TomTom works with regional data partners. This approach allows faster expansion into new markets. Meehan claims that in the places that matter most to customers, TomTom is often better than the competition, and that globally the leaders in EV POI data are now at parity with each other.

Perhaps the most significant shift is in driver expectations. TomTom says the conversation has moved from "where can I charge?" to "which charger should I choose?" Drivers now ask for pricing information, payment integration, nearby amenities (coffee, food, restrooms), security lighting, covered parking for weather protection, and WiFi. This evolution in demand is pushing TomTom to build richer location context layers around each charging point.

The next frontier is AI-powered background intelligence. TomTom is developing an AI-based range prediction model that moves from multi-variate equations to learning models trained on actual usage data, accounting for weather, roof boxes, towing, tire pressure, open windows, and driving style. The company envisions a background intelligence layer that learns driver habits, anticipates charging needs, and manages the vehicle day to day — ensuring the car is charged before a habitual 8 a.m. commute, or charging from solar on days the driver typically doesn't drive. This is distinct from TomTom AI Assistant (TAIA), which handles conversational prompts.

The reorganization of Meehan's group from EV-focused to Destination and Waypoint Management signals that TomTom views EV features as a proving ground for broader software-defined vehicle capabilities. Charger selection, amenity search, in-car payments, and multi-stop routing were built for electric driving, but the underlying tooling now extends across vehicle types and use cases.
