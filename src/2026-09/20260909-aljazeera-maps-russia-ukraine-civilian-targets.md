# In Maps and Charts: Russian and Ukrainian Attacks on Civilian Targets
**Source**: https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/9/in-maps-and-charts-russian-and-ukrainian-attacks-on-civilian-targets
**Date**: 2026-09-09
**Author**: Hanna Duggal, Marium Ali (Al Jazeera)
**Keywords**: Russia-Ukraine war, civilian infrastructure, maps, drones, ACLED, CSIS, ballistic missiles, Patriot

## Elevator pitch
Al Jazeera's data visualization maps how both sides in the Russia-Ukraine war have increasingly targeted civilian infrastructure, with drone attacks more than doubling and interception rates falling.

## Takeaways
- Russian attacks on Ukrainian civilian infrastructure rose from 5% of all attacks in 2022 to 13% by August 2026 (7,170 incidents), per ACLED data
- Ukrainian attacks on Russian civilian infrastructure rose from 10% in 2022 to 27% by August 2026 (3,560 incidents)
- Donetsk (3,652 attacks) and Belgorod (9,549 attacks) are the most targeted regions on each side
- Drones now dominate the war, replacing artillery as the primary attack method, with Ukraine planning 1,000 drone launches per day
- Missile interception rates fell from 24% in 2025 to 10.2% in 2026 due to dwindling Patriot and IRIS-T interceptor supplies

## Synthesis
Al Jazeera's interactive data visualization piece maps the escalation of attacks on civilian infrastructure in the Russia-Ukraine war, now in its fifth year. Using data from the Armed Conflict Location and Event Data (ACLED) project and the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS), the piece traces how both sides have increasingly relied on drones over artillery, with attacks reaching further beyond the front line.

The data is stark. Russian attacks on Ukrainian civilian infrastructure grew from 1,635 incidents (5% of all attacks) in 2022 to 7,170 (13%) by August 2026. Ukrainian attacks on Russian civilian infrastructure grew from 24 (10%) to 3,560 (27%) over the same period. While a larger share of Ukrainian attacks targeted civilian infrastructure, Russia carried out far more attacks in absolute terms. Residential structures bore the brunt: 68% of Russian strikes and 69% of Ukrainian strikes on civilian infrastructure hit residential buildings. Energy facilities accounted for 19% and 30% respectively.

Geographically, the front-line region of Donetsk faced the most Russian attacks (3,652), followed by Kharkiv, Sumy, Dnipropetrovsk, and Kherson. On the Russian side, the western border region of Belgorod absorbed the most Ukrainian attacks (9,549), followed by Kursk, Bryansk, Krasnodar, and Rostov. Kyiv region recorded 742 attacks despite being far from the front line.

The nature of warfare has shifted fundamentally. Drones and air strikes now constitute the majority of attacks, replacing the artillery-dominated early years. Russia is on track to launch 840 ballistic missiles in 2026, 47% more than 2025, including the first combat use of the longer-range Iskander-1000. Meanwhile, Ukraine's interception rates have plummeted from 24% to 10.2% as Patriot and IRIS-T interceptor supplies have dwindled. Ukraine announced plans to launch 1,000 drones per day against Russia, up from the previous daily average of 300. The EU has committed 3.9 billion euros toward a 6 billion euro drone procurement package, and the UK announced a 752 million pound package for 150,000 drones and 350 air defense missiles.
