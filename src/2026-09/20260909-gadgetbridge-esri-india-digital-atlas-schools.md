## Synthesis
Esri India, the country's leading GIS and mapping software company, has launched the Digital Atlas Program — a free initiative that provides schools across India with interactive digital mapping tools and curriculum-relevant content. Built on Esri's ArcGIS Online cloud platform, the program replaces static printed maps with interactive layers of real-world data, allowing students to explore population patterns, weather systems, land use, and other geographic phenomena within lesson plans aligned to the curriculum.

The initiative represents a fundamental shift in how geography is taught. Rather than tracing state boundaries and memorizing river names, students are encouraged to investigate real-world events. Agendra Kumar, Managing Director of Esri India, framed the shift as moving from asking "where is it" to "why is it there." Using flooding as an example, students might trace where flooding occurred, why it happened, how many people it affected, and what could prevent it from recurring.

The program builds on education research showing that spatial thinking — the ability to interpret distance, direction, and geographic patterns — is linked to stronger performance in STEM subjects and sharper critical thinking. Multiple academic studies on GIS in classrooms have found measurable gains in analytical skills among students using mapping tools compared to those relying on textbooks alone. Esri India's bet is that Indian classrooms are ready for this shift. The program builds on existing work with schools, colleges, and universities, but the new ambition is making GIS a normal part of school-level geography rather than something encountered only through specialization.
