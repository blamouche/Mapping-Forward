## Synthesis
KFF (Kaiser Family Foundation) published a descriptive mapping of 14 key global health and related international institutions, analyzing their work to address health challenges in low- and middle-income countries. The report comes at a time of intense reform activity in the global health architecture, with multiple efforts underway including the Lusaka Agenda, the Accra Reset, and the WHO's Joint Process on Reform of the Global Health Architecture.

The global health ecosystem has grown increasingly complex over the past 25 years, with new institutions emerging to address growing health challenges. Today, diverse international organizations with different mandates, governance models, financing structures, and operational approaches support the same countries, address similar health issues, and rely on overlapping revenue sources. This has raised questions about duplication, opportunities for synergies, and better coordination across entities — questions made more acute by tightening fiscal environments that have forced many institutions to scale back operations.

KFF's mapping looks across a range of variables to provide a structured comparison of these 14 institutions. The Multilateral Organization Performance Assessment Network (MOPAN) is conducting similar efforts. The KFF report is explicitly descriptive rather than evaluative: it does not assess organizational performance or effectiveness. Instead, it serves as an input into ongoing reform processes, offering a snapshot of the rapidly changing global health institutional ecosystem.
