# Geo Week Announces Strategic Partnerships for 2027 Event
**Source**: https://amerisurv.com/2026/09/09/geo-week-announces-strategic-partnerships-for-2027-event/
**Date**: 2026-09-09
**Author**: The American Surveyor
**Keywords**: Geo Week, ASPRS, RCN, GISCI, ISPRS, Women in GIS, geospatial, conference, Salt Lake City, partnerships

## Elevator pitch
Geo Week 2027 has announced its strategic partnerships including event partners ASPRS and Reality Capture Network, industry partners including GISCI, ISPRS, and NSPS, and 185+ supporting partner organizations spanning six continents.

## Takeaways
- Geo Week 2027 will take place February 23-25, 2027, in Salt Lake City, Utah
- Event Partners: ASPRS (American Society for Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing) and Reality Capture Network (RCN)
- Industry Partners include GISCI, ISPRS, MAPPS, NSPS, OGC, USGIF, USIBD, and WGIC
- New partnerships with Utah-based organizations UGIC and SLUG reflect local community engagement
- Women in GIS will participate, strengthening diversity initiatives
- 185+ supporting partner organizations span six continents
- Registration is open with Super Saver pricing

## Synthesis
Geo Week's 2027 partnership announcement reveals the scale and diversity of the geospatial industry ecosystem. With 185+ supporting organizations spanning six continents, the event positions itself as the premier gathering for geospatial and mapping professionals. The inclusion of Women in GIS as a partner reflects growing attention to diversity in the field.

The partnership structure — Event Partners providing session content, Industry Partners advancing sector collaboration, and Supporting Partners amplifying reach — demonstrates how the geospatial community organizes itself. The presence of standards bodies (OGC), professional organizations (NSPS, ASPRS), and intelligence-focused groups (USGIF) under one roof highlights geospatial's role as a convergence point for commercial, academic, and government interests. The event's move to Salt Lake City also aligns with the growing geospatial corridor in the Mountain West, near the NGA's western headquarters.
