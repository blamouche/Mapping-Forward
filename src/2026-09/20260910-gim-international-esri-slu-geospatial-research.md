# Esri and Saint Louis University to Advance Geospatial Research and Innovation
**Source**: https://www.gim-international.com/news/esri-and-saint-louis-university-to-advance-geospatial-research-and-innovation
**Date**: 2026-09-10
**Author**: GIM International
**Keywords**: Esri, Saint Louis University, GeoSLU, GIS, NGA, geospatial research, GEOINT, ArcGIS Online, partnership

## Elevator pitch
Esri has entered into an official partnership with Saint Louis University's Geospatial Institute (GeoSLU) to advance GIS research addressing global challenges including climate change, food security, and economic stability, with both organizations affiliated with the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency.

## Takeaways
- Saint Louis University recently founded the Geospatial Institute (GeoSLU)
- Both SLU and Esri are affiliated with the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA)
- Esri has collaborated with NGA for 30 years; SLU signed a CRADA with NGA last year
- Projects include an "Ideathon" called "Saving the Herd with AI" using ArcGIS Online to counter wildlife trafficking
- Jack Dangermond, Esri founder, will participate at Geo-Resolution, a geospatial collaboration event
- Research focus areas include climate change, access to food and clean water, and economic stability

## Synthesis
The Esri-SLU partnership consolidates St. Louis's growing reputation as a geospatial industry hub, anchored by the NGA's presence. The partnership goes beyond academic collaboration: both organizations bring NGA affiliations, creating a triple partnership between academia, industry, and the intelligence community. The "Saving the Herd with AI" Ideathon exemplifies how GIS technology is being applied to non-traditional domains like wildlife conservation, using ArcGIS Online as a platform for student innovation.

Jack Dangermond's participation at Geo-Resolution signals the strategic importance of this partnership for Esri. The focus on global challenges — climate, food security, water access — positions geospatial technology as essential infrastructure for addressing societal problems, rather than just a mapping tool. The NGA affiliation also highlights the dual-use nature of geospatial intelligence, where defense capabilities increasingly overlap with civilian applications.
