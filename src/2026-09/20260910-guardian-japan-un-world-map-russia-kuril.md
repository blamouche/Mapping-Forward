# Japan Seeks Change to New UN World Map Over Russia 'Misconception'
**Source**: https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/10/japan-seeks-change-to-new-un-world-map-over-russia-misconception
**Date**: 2026-09-10
**Author**: The Guardian
**Keywords**: UN world map, Japan, Russia, Kuril Islands, Equal Earth, true-scale map, territorial dispute

## Elevator pitch
Japan has formally requested changes to the new UN "true-scale" world map, arguing that it depicts the disputed Kuril Islands as ruled by Moscow — a depiction Tokyo considers a "misconception" of its territorial sovereignty.

## Takeaways
- The new 'true-scale' map shrinks Europe and North America while enlarging Africa
- Japan objects to the map showing the disputed Kuril Islands as Russian territory
- The Kuril Islands have been disputed between Japan and Russia since WWII
- Japan's objection highlights how map representations carry political and territorial implications beyond mere cartographic accuracy
- The UN adopted the Equal Earth-type map with 164 votes for and 1 against

## Synthesis
Japan's challenge to the newly adopted UN world map illustrates that even "corrected" cartographic representations remain politically contested. While the UN resolution focused on replacing the Mercator projection with the Equal Earth projection to better represent the relative size of continents, Japan's objection shifts the focus from area accuracy to territorial sovereignty.

The Kuril Islands, seized by the Soviet Union at the end of World War II and claimed by Japan as its "Northern Territories," remain one of the most intractable territorial disputes between the two countries. By depicting them as Russian territory, the new UN map — however unintentionally — takes a position on this decades-old conflict. This case underscores that no map is ever purely neutral: choices about borders, labels, and colors all carry political weight, even when the primary goal is achieving fairer area representation.
