# Japan, India, Ukraine: Why Some Countries Are Uneasy About the New UN Map
**Source**: https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/11/japan-india-ukraine-why-some-countries-are-uneasy-about-new-un-map
**Date**: 2026-09-11
**Author**: Al Jazeera
**Keywords**: UN world map, Equal Earth, Japan, Kuril Islands, India, Kashmir, Ukraine, Crimea, cartographic sovereignty, territorial disputes

## Elevator pitch
Following the UN's adoption of the Equal Earth projection, Japan, India, and Ukraine have raised concerns — not about the projection itself, but about how the new map depicts their disputed territories through colour shading and border delineation.

## Takeaways
- Japan objects to the Equal Earth map using the same colour for the disputed Kuril Islands as for Russian territory
- India demands that Kashmir be depicted "in accordance with India's official map," rejecting any "inaccurate or misleading depiction"
- Ukraine abstained from the vote, arguing the map's shading of Crimea legitimizes Russia's 2014 annexation
- None of the three countries oppose the Equal Earth projection itself — they contest how their disputed territories are rendered
- Japan has formally asked Equal Earth's creators to change the map through its embassy in the US
- The six abstentions (Estonia, Georgia, Moldova, Serbia, Lithuania, Ukraine) all voted in solidarity with Ukraine over Crimea

## Synthesis
The UN's adoption of the Equal Earth projection was hailed as a victory for cartographic justice, but the aftermath reveals that no map — however scientifically sound — can escape political contestation. The concerns raised by Japan, India, and Ukraine shift the debate from area accuracy to territorial sovereignty, demonstrating that colour choices and border delineations carry geopolitical weight even in "corrected" projections.

Japan's objection centres on the Kuril Islands (Northern Territories), seized by the Soviet Union at the end of WWII and claimed by Tokyo. The Equal Earth map uses similar shading for the islands and mainland Russia, which Japan considers a "misconception" of its sovereignty. Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara stated Japan has asked the map's creators to make corrections. India's concern involves Kashmir, with the Ministry of External Affairs insisting its sovereign territory must be depicted per India's official map. Ukraine's abstention reflects alarm that Crimea's yellowish tint on the new map appears closer to Russia's shade than Ukraine's, which Kyiv argues could legitimize the illegal annexation.

These objections underscore a fundamental tension: the UN resolution addresses area distortion but cannot resolve the representational politics of disputed territories. Every choice about colour, border, and label remains a political act, even in a map designed to correct colonial-era distortions.
