# Waze Dethroned? Drivers Now Prefer Google Maps for Navigation
**Source**: https://www.journaldugeek.com/2026/09/11/waze-detrone-les-automobilistes-preferent-une-autre-application-gps/
**Date**: 2026-09-11
**Author**: Thomas Estimbre (Journal du Geek)
**Keywords**: Waze, Google Maps, Apple Maps, navigation apps, GPS, radars, Android Auto, TomTom AmiGo, traffic, MarketWatch

## Elevator pitch
A MarketWatch study shows Google Maps has overtaken Waze as the most-used GPS app among US drivers (70%), driven by its intuitive interface, real-time traffic prediction, and Google's ongoing integration of Waze's best community features.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps is used by 70% of US drivers for radar avoidance, compared to 27% for Waze and 25% for Apple Maps
- Even iPhone users in the US prefer Google Maps over Apple's native Maps app
- Google has been gradually merging Waze's best features into Google Maps, including community alerts for police controls and incidents
- In France, Google Maps cannot display radar warnings due to legal restrictions (Code de la route Article R. 413-15), giving Waze and TomTom AmiGo an advantage
- Waze has faced criticism for connectivity issues and less relevant routing over the past year
- Google plans to add more incident types (accidents, road hazards, construction) to Google Maps community alerts

## Synthesis
The navigation app landscape is shifting as Google Maps consolidates its dominance over Waze, its sister app. A MarketWatch study found that 70% of US drivers now use Google Maps for radar avoidance, far outpacing Waze (27%) and Apple Maps (25%). The trend reflects Google's strategy of integrating Waze's most popular community features — real-time alerts, incident reporting — into Google Maps, making the standalone Waze app increasingly redundant for many users.

The picture differs by market, however. In France, where radar warning features are prohibited by law, Google Maps does not offer the feature natively, giving Waze (with "zones de contrôle" workarounds) and TomTom AmiGo a competitive edge. But as Google continues to merge Waze's community alert capabilities into Maps — including police control confirmations and upcoming incident types — Waze's differentiation is narrowing. The consolidation raises questions about the future of Waze as a standalone product and about the broader trend of large platforms absorbing community-driven mapping features.
