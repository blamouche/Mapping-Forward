# TomTom Adds Knowledge Management and AI to Orbis Maps
**Source**: https://www.techcentral.ie/tomtom-adds-knowledge-management-and-ai-to-orbis-maps/
**Date**: 2026-09-08
**Author**: TechCentral.ie (via Emerce)
**Keywords**: TomTom, Orbis Maps, AI agents, location intelligence, MCP server, OpenStreetMap, Overture Maps, spatial knowledge, autonomous driving, geomarketing, TAIA

## Elevator pitch
TomTom is transforming its Orbis Maps platform into a spatial knowledge layer for AI agents, adding contextual data, AI assistant capabilities, and an MCP server that lets AI models query location intelligence for complex spatial reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways
- TomTom Orbis Maps combines OpenStreetMap, Overture Maps, TomTom's own data, and vehicle sensor data — over 5 billion data points
- TomTom has stored 2.8 trillion kilometres of worldwide distance data and trillions of GPS points
- Orbis is being positioned as a "spatial knowledge layer" for AI agents, enabling natural-language spatial queries
- TomTom has released Orbis APIs and an MCP server for AI agent integration
- The TomTom AI Assistant (TAIA) works at the front end, while the Agent Toolkit enables third-party AI agents to access spatial intelligence
- Use cases include insurers querying flood-damage proximity, city planners analyzing travel-time changes, and fleet managers rerouting vehicles
- Orbis Lane Model Maps provide lane-level geometry for autonomous driving applications

## Synthesis
TomTom is evolving its Orbis Maps platform beyond a digital road map into a comprehensive spatial knowledge layer designed for AI agents. Orbis combines data from OpenStreetMap, Overture Maps, TomTom's proprietary sources, and real-time vehicle sensor data — over 5 billion data points that are continuously updated. The platform stores 2.8 trillion kilometres of distance data and collects anonymous driving data from connected vehicles daily.

The key innovation is TomTom's positioning of Orbis as an AI-accessible spatial intelligence layer. The company has released APIs and an MCP (Model Context Protocol) server that allow AI agents to query the map data for complex spatial reasoning. This enables natural-language interactions where an insurer could ask which damage claims fall within 500 meters of a flood zone, or a city planner could analyze how road closures have changed travel times around a bridge over six months. The distinction between AI assistants (TAIA, operating at the application front end) and AI agents (integrated deeper into architecture, capable of complex multi-step queries) reflects TomTom's vision of maps as a queryable knowledge base rather than just a visual display tool. This positions TomTom at the intersection of mapping, AI, and enterprise spatial intelligence.
