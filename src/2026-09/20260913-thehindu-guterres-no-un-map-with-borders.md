# There Is No Such Thing as a "UN Map With Borders": Guterres

## Elevator pitch
UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres clarified that the UN has never published a map with borders, after India raised "anomalies" about a map used during the "Correct the Map" resolution that omitted Indian and Chinese claim lines in Arunachal Pradesh and Aksai Chin.

## Synthesis
Days after India pointed out "anomalies" in a world map used during the "Correct the Map" resolution at the UN General Assembly, Secretary-General Antonio Guterres gave an exclusive interview to The Hindu to clarify the UN's position on maps and borders. His message was unequivocal: "There is no such thing as a 'UN map with borders'. The resolution that was approved in the General Assembly is not a map. It is a resolution that says — quite correctly, I think — that there are distortions in the landmasses that correspond to real sizes. These come from power relations of the past. And those distortions should be corrected."

The controversy centered on a map titled "Map of the World," published on July 1 by UN Geospatial and used during consultation for the resolution. It depicted Arunachal Pradesh and Aksai Chin without the specified Indian and Chinese "claim lines" that previous UN maps had included. The map showed the Line of Control in Jammu & Kashmir as a dotted line with an explanatory note about its provisional status, but provided no similar explanation for the omitted claim lines in the northeastern sector.

Guterres further distanced the UN from the map, stating it was "not an official UN map" but rather "the contribution of an NGO" and that "the map is indicative, but there is no definition by the UN of what borders should be." India's MEA spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal confirmed India voted for the resolution in support of the "equal area representation" principle, and that the anomaly regarding claim lines was being taken up with the United Nations. The episode highlights the geopolitical sensitivity that accompanies any UN cartographic product, even when the underlying resolution is about projection fairness rather than borders.
