## Elevator pitch
The UN General Assembly adopted the "Correct The Map" resolution spearheaded by Togo on behalf of the African Group, a diplomatic milestone that seeks to replace Mercator projection world maps with fairer representations of Africa's true size.

## Takeaways
- The UN General Assembly adopted the "Correct The Map" resolution on September 4, 2026, in New York
- The initiative was carried by Togo on behalf of the African Group
- The resolution aims to promote more accurate, balanced, and equitable cartographic representations of Africa
- The initiative challenges the Mercator projection, which distorts the true size of African countries

## Synthesis
The "Correct The Map" initiative, spearheaded by Togo on behalf of the African Group, was adopted by the UN General Assembly on September 4, 2026, in New York. The resolution promotes a more accurate, balanced, and equitable cartographic representation of the world, particularly addressing how Africa is depicted on world maps.

The initiative challenges the dominance of the Mercator projection, which distorts the relative sizes of landmasses, making Africa appear much smaller than it actually is. While the resolution is non-binding, it carries significant symbolic and diplomatic weight, potentially influencing how international institutions, educational bodies, and media organizations choose their world map projections.

Togo's Foreign Minister Robert Dussey emphasized the historic nature of the adoption, stating that the Togo and the African Union will work together to implement the decision. For the African Union, the adoption represents a diplomatic victory that goes beyond cartography — it addresses the political and perceptual dimensions of how Africa is represented globally. The initiative's promoters are calling on international institutions to translate the resolution into practice by adopting fairer map projections in their communications and educational materials.
