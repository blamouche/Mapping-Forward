## Synthesis
Barranquilla, Colombia, emerged as one of the world's slowest and most congested cities in the TomTom Traffic Index 2025. The capital of the Atlántico department ranked first as the slowest city globally and 19th for congestion, while ranking fourth within Colombia behind Bogotá, Medellín, and Cali.

The data shows that a 10-kilometer trip in Barranquilla took an average of 36 minutes and 35 seconds, escalating to 46 minutes and 9 seconds during evening rush hour, with average congestion reaching 103.4%. Congestion increased 1.7 percentage points from 61.1% to 62.8% compared to the previous year.

Mobility expert Isidro Ruiz disputed the severity of the ranking, arguing that Barranquilla's congestion is "localized" rather than city-wide, driven by road works, canalizations, and intersection improvements that temporarily disrupt traffic. He contended that travel times in Barranquilla are not longer than in Cali, Medellín, or Bogotá. However, urban planning specialist Porfirio Ospino attributed the congestion to exponential city growth and vehicle ownership increases against a static road network, citing developments like Alameda del Río where the city had to absorb infrastructure costs.

Proposed solutions include strengthening public transit, limiting building heights based on road capacity, implementing parking fees in tourist areas like the Gran Malecón, staggered schedules for schools and businesses, better-calibrated peak-hour restrictions, and requiring proportional road infrastructure for new real estate developments before approval.
