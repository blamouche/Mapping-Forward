# BMW Dates Urban Hands-Free Driving: 2027, Germany First
**Source**: https://www.autonext.co/fr/news/bmw-maps-3d-aide-conduite-octobre-2026-ville-mains-libres-2027
**Date**: 2026-09-15
**Author**: Beau Ackx, AutoNext
**Keywords**: BMW, hands-free driving, urban autonomous driving, Mapbox, Operating System X, Germany, 2027

## Elevator pitch
BMW will launch its unified 3D navigation and driver-assistance view in October 2026, with the next step being semi-automated urban driving guided by navigation — hands-free city driving arriving in Germany from 2027.

## Takeaways
- From October 2026, BMW Maps on Operating System X combines navigation, environmental perception, and driver-assistance status into a single 3D view on the central screen
- The system places the car in its own lane at the correct road level on multi-level interchanges, drawing traffic from sensors rather than the map
- BMW's next milestone is semi-automated city driving guided by navigation, starting in Germany in 2027
- Mapbox provides the mapping and rendering layer while HERE supplies the certified high-definition map for redundant lane detection
- The visualization was developed in-house by BMW, running on Mapbox data since Operating System 9 in 2023

## Synthesis
BMW has set a timeline for the next phase of its driving automation strategy, with urban hands-free driving targeted for Germany in 2027. The announcement comes alongside the October 2026 launch of BMW Maps' unified 3D view on Operating System X, which merges navigation, the car's perception of its environment, and driver-assistance status into a single image on the central screen.

The new interface represents a significant departure from the traditional split between instrument cluster and infotainment display. The vehicle is placed in a depth-mapped virtual reconstruction of the road, positioned in its own lane at the correct level on complex multi-level interchanges. Traffic is drawn from the car's own sensors rather than from the map database, and the active driving assistant appears within the same 3D scene, allowing the driver to verify at a glance that the car's AI vision matches the real world.

BMW's dual mapping strategy is particularly noteworthy. The company is a co-owner of HERE since the 2015 acquisition from Nokia, yet its navigation has run on Mapbox since the arrival of Operating System 9 on the X1 and Mini in 2023. The new 3D assistance view also uses Mapbox for rendering. HERE provides the certified high-definition map layer that supports redundant lane detection for the driving assistant, while Mapbox provides the visual interface. This division of labor between two competing mapping providers gives BMW flexibility and reduces single-vendor dependency. The semi-automated urban driving planned for 2027 will depend on this combined mapping infrastructure to navigate complex city environments.
