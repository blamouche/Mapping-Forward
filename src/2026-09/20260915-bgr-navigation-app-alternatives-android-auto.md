# Not Google Maps, Not Waze: 5 Navigation App Alternatives for Android Auto
**Source**: https://www.bgr.com/2254995/google-maps-waze-alternatives-android-auto-apps/
**Date**: 2026-09-15
**Author**: Gaurav Shukla, BGR
**Keywords**: Android Auto, navigation apps, TomTom, Sygic, OsmAnd, Calimoto, MapQuest, Google Maps alternatives

## Elevator pitch
BGR surveys five navigation apps that work with Android Auto as alternatives to the dominant Google Maps and Waze, covering TomTom, Sygic, OsmAnd, Calimoto, and a resurgent MapQuest.

## Takeaways
- TomTom offers free turn-by-turn navigation with Android Auto support, offline maps, EV routing, and an ad-free, privacy-respecting interface
- Sygic provides offline navigation, live traffic, and speed camera alerts, but requires a Premium+ subscription ($20/year) for Android Auto support
- OsmAnd excels at off-road and rural navigation with custom map layers and topographic maps, with Android Auto as a paid feature ($15/year Maps+)
- Calimoto is designed for motorcycle riders with scenic routing, offline maps, and curved-road preference, starting at $13/week
- MapQuest relaunched in March 2026, went viral for refusing to rename Lake Ontario as Lake America, and now supports Android Auto in beta

## Synthesis
While Google Maps and Waze dominate the Android Auto navigation landscape, BGR has identified five alternatives that offer different approaches to in-car navigation. The selection ranges from established players to niche specialists, each addressing shortcomings in Google's offerings around privacy, data collection, and feature gaps.

TomTom stands out as the most direct competitor, offering a free app with turn-by-turn navigation, real-time traffic, and speed restriction data. It distinguishes itself with an ad-free, distraction-free interface and does not track user data. The app includes EV-specific routing with charging station information and has a 4.3-star rating on the Play Store. Its main weakness is occasional data inaccuracy, particularly for business listings.

MapQuest's resurgence is the most notable story. The pioneer of online mapping (1996) has returned with a proper app in March 2026 and gained viral attention by refusing to comply with President Trump's executive order to rename Lake Ontario as Lake America. This stance helped it briefly dethrone Google Maps as the top travel app. Its Android Auto support is in beta, with users reporting latency issues and an overly zoomed-out default perspective, but the 4.6-star Play Store rating suggests strong user satisfaction.

The niche apps serve specific audiences well. OsmAnd is the choice for off-road and rural navigation, with support for custom map layers, contour lines, satellite overlays, and GPX file import — features no other app in this list offers. Calimoto targets motorcycle riders specifically, with routing that prefers curvy backroads over highways. Sygic rounds out the list with a feature-rich offering that includes fuel prices and lane assistance, though its constant upselling and subscription requirements limit its appeal.
