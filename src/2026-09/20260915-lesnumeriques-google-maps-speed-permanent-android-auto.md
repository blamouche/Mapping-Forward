# Google Maps: Speed Limits and Speedometer Now Display Permanently on Android Auto
**Source**: https://www.lesnumeriques.com/gps/google-maps-limitations-et-compteur-de-vitesse-s-affichent-en-permanence-sur-android-auto-mais-les-bugs-persistent-n261870.html
**Date**: 2026-09-15
**Author**: Les Numériques
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Android Auto, speedometer, speed limits, always-on display, bug fix, deployment

## Elevator pitch
After disappearing for a week, Google Maps' speed limits and speedometer are returning to Android Auto with a new always-on mode that displays even without an active navigation route, though bugs persist for some users.

## Takeaways
- Speed limits and the speedometer disappeared from Google Maps on Android Auto for many users, prompting fears of a feature removal
- Google confirmed the disappearance was a temporary bug, not a deliberate rollback of the feature launched in summer 2026
- A new always-on mode is now rolling out, displaying the speedometer, speed limits, stop signs, and traffic lights without requiring a destination to be set
- The always-on display is particularly useful for daily commutes on familiar routes where full navigation is unnecessary
- Deployment is gradual and not yet available to all users, with Google not specifying a timeline

## Synthesis
Google has addressed user concerns about the disappearance of speed-related features in Google Maps on Android Auto, confirming the removal was a temporary technical issue rather than a deliberate feature rollback. The speedometer, which launched in July 2026, and speed limit displays vanished for many users in early September, prompting complaints on Reddit and social media. Google's Android Auto team confirmed they are actively investigating the bug and that the features remain part of the product roadmap.

More significantly, Google is rolling out an always-on display mode for speed information on Android Auto. A Google employee confirmed on Reddit that "the ability to keep the speedometer, speed limits, stop signs, and traffic lights displayed without a set destination is being deployed." This means drivers will no longer need to start a navigation route to see their current speed and the posted speed limit on the car's display — a significant improvement for daily commutes on familiar roads where full turn-by-turn navigation is unnecessary.

The always-on mode addresses a common complaint from Android Auto users who found it redundant to launch a full navigation session just to monitor their speed. On Apple CarPlay, speed information has been available without active navigation for some time, and the gap with Android Auto has been a source of frustration. The gradual deployment means not all users have access yet, and those still missing the speedometer are advised to set a destination as a workaround until the always-on mode reaches their account. The persistent bugs suggest the rollout is encountering technical challenges, but the direction is clear: permanent speed display is coming to all Android Auto users.
