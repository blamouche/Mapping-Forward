# Rhodes College Launches New GIS Undergraduate Certificate Program
**Source**: https://news.rhodes.edu/stories/career-booster-rhodes-college-offers-new-certificate-geographic-information-science
**Date**: 2026-09-15
**Author**: Rhodes College
**Keywords**: GIS, geographic information science, undergraduate certificate, liberal arts, spatial data, cartography, education

## Elevator pitch
Rhodes College has launched a Geographic Information Science undergraduate certificate, claiming to be the only liberal arts college offering an interdisciplinary GIS credential that bridges sciences, humanities, and arts.

## Takeaways
- Rhodes College now offers a 12-credit GIS Undergraduate Certificate, the first interdisciplinary GIS certificate at an independent liberal arts college
- The program includes a foundational course plus electives like Critical Cartographies, Remote Sensing, Mapping Population Health, and Mapping Oral Histories
- GIS skills are increasingly valued across fields including environmental science, urban planning, public health, data analytics, and historic preservation
- The certificate bridges technical skill-building with critical thinking and writing, leveraging the liberal arts context
- Alumni already working in GIS roles report that the combination of hard GIS skills and soft skills from liberal arts training is highly valuable in the job market

## Synthesis
Rhodes College has introduced a Geographic Information Science (GIS) Undergraduate Certificate, responding to growing demand for spatial data skills across multiple industries. The 12-credit program requires a foundational course (ENVS/URBN 225) and offers electives ranging from Critical Cartographies and Remote Sensing to Mapping Population Health and Mapping Oral Histories and Geographies — a course selection that reflects the interdisciplinary ambition of the program.

The certificate is notable for its institutional context. According to Dr. Shaolu Yu, the faculty member who led its development, Rhodes is the only independent liberal arts college offering an interdisciplinary GIS certificate that bridges sciences, humanities, and arts. While many state universities and larger institutions offer GIS credentials, the liberal arts framing distinguishes this program by pairing technical training with critical thinking, writing, and relationship-building skills.

The program responds to clear market demand. GIS professionals work across government, environmental conservation, urban planning, emergency management, and public health, using specialized software to analyze spatial patterns and guide decision-making. Roberta Moore, a Rhodes environmental science alumna now serving as senior field representative for The Conservation Fund, noted that the certificate gives "official standing to a skillset Rhodes students have quietly been building for years." She emphasized that learning GIS at a liberal arts institution provided a combination of hard science, technical mapping skills, and soft skills that she relies on daily in her land conservation work across Georgia and Alabama.

The program also highlights GIS as a storytelling medium, making it increasingly popular among students in art, history, and media studies. This positions GIS not merely as a technical tool but as a bridge between quantitative analysis and narrative — a framing that aligns with broader trends in the geospatial industry toward community-engaged and participatory mapping.
