# World Maps Might Soon Get an Upgrade: The UN Votes to Endorse Equal Earth Projection
**Source**: https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/world-maps-might-soon-get-an-upgrade-the-united-nationas-votes-to-endorse-global-charts-that-better-represent-africas-true-size-180989495/
**Date**: 2026-09-15
**Author**: Smithsonian Magazine
**Keywords**: UN resolution, Equal Earth projection, Mercator projection, Africa, map distortion, Correct the Map, cartography, cognitive justice

## Elevator pitch
The UN General Assembly voted 164-1 to endorse equal-area maps like the Equal Earth projection over the 500-year-old Mercator, which drastically distorts Africa's true size — with only the United States voting against.

## Takeaways
- The UN General Assembly passed a resolution on September 4 urging institutions to adopt maps that better represent countries' relative sizes, with 164 votes in favor and only the United States against
- The resolution specifically recommends the Equal Earth projection, developed in 2018, as a fairer alternative to the 1569 Mercator projection
- The #CorrectTheMap campaign, spearheaded by Togo and endorsed by the African Union, argues that the Mercator map diminishes Africa's significance
- The resolution does not ban the Mercator map but urges governments, schools, and tech companies to use equal-area maps where relative landmass size matters
- Togo will be the first country to update its geography textbooks, aiming to do so before the end of 2026

## Synthesis
The United Nations General Assembly has voted overwhelmingly to endorse a shift away from the Mercator projection, the world's most widely used map for nearly 500 years, toward equal-area projections that more accurately represent the true relative sizes of continents. The resolution, passed on September 4 by 164 votes to one, represents a significant moment in the global cartographic debate that has intensified over the past year.

The Smithsonian provides essential context: the Mercator projection, drawn in 1569 by Flemish cartographer Gerardus Mercator, was revolutionary for navigation because it maintains compass bearing accuracy. However, it severely distorts landmass sizes — Greenland appears roughly the same size as Africa on a Mercator map, when Africa is actually about 14 times larger. This distortion systematically understates the significance of equatorial regions, particularly Africa, while exaggerating the prominence of northern countries.

The #CorrectTheMap campaign, which gained momentum after endorsement by the African Union in August 2025, argues that this is not merely a technical issue but one of cognitive justice. Kenyan geographer Kioko Muendo told the BBC that maps shape how regions are perceived politically and economically, and that the Mercator map has understated Africa's resources, population, and investment prospects. The resolution frames the shift as an act of "cognitive justice and memorial reparation."

The vote was not unanimous. The United States voted against, with deputy representative Yaryna Ferencevych calling the resolution a distraction from genuine international problems and describing the ideological agenda as "barnacles on our work." Several Eastern European countries — Estonia, Georgia, Lithuania, Moldova, Serbia, and Ukraine — abstained, with Ukraine objecting to how some Equal Earth maps portray Russian-occupied Ukrainian territory. The resolution does not legally obligate anyone to change maps, but its moral weight and the specific recommendation of the Equal Earth projection give the campaign institutional backing that will pressure mapping companies and educational institutions worldwide.
