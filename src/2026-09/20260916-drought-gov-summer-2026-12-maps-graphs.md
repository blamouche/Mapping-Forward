# Drought in Summer 2026 in 12 Maps and Graphs
**Source**: https://www.drought.gov/news/drought-summer-2026-12-maps-and-graphs-2026-09-16
**Date**: 2026-09-16
**Author**: NOAA/NIDIS
**Keywords**: drought, maps, NOAA, NIDIS, climate, temperature, precipitation, wildfire, reservoirs, El Niño, United States

## Elevator pitch
NOAA's National Integrated Drought Information System presents 12 maps and graphs showing that the contiguous US experienced its warmest June-August on record in 2026, with drought covering nearly half the country, record-low reservoir levels, and a historically strong El Niño developing.

## Takeaways
- The contiguous US experienced its warmest June-August on record in 2026, exceeding previous records from 1936 and 2021 by 0.4°F
- Drought covered nearly half the country, sustained by below-normal precipitation and above-normal temperatures
- Major western reservoirs Lake Mead and Lake Powell hit all-time record lows
- The extreme conditions fueled a destructive wildfire season with unhealthy smoke levels across large parts of the US
- A potentially historically strong El Niño is projected to continue developing through fall and winter
- Flash drought is worsening in the Southern Plains and Lower Mississippi River Basin
- High evaporative demand was observed across almost every CONUS state, especially Colorado

## Synthesis
The NOAA National Integrated Drought Information System (NIDIS) has published a comprehensive visual summary of drought conditions during the summer of 2026, presented through 12 maps and graphs. The data reveals that the contiguous United States experienced its warmest meteorological summer (June-August) on record in the 132-year historical record, surpassing previous records set in 1936 and 2021 by 0.4°F.

The maps show that above-normal temperatures were widespread across most of the country, with extreme heat concentrated in the Southwest and Four Corners region (Utah, Colorado, Arizona, New Mexico) extending into Texas. Southern Florida also recorded warmest-on-record temperatures. Precipitation was normal to below normal for most of CONUS, with the nation receiving 8.11 inches — 0.21 inch below the 20th-century average.

The Evaporative Demand Drought Index (EDDI) showed high atmospheric thirst across almost every CONUS state between June and August 2026, with Colorado experiencing the highest evaporative demand. Almost none of CONUS had low evaporative demand, even in regions like the Ohio River Basin that received above-normal precipitation.

Drought persisted across nearly half the country, and major western reservoirs Lake Mead and Lake Powell continued declining to all-time record lows. The extreme conditions fueled a destructive wildfire season. Looking ahead, a potentially historically strong El Niño event is projected to continue developing, which typically brings wetter conditions to the Southern US and warmer conditions to the Northern US.
