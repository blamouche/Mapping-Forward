# Google Maps' Misleading Nine-Hour Detour Spells Plummeting Bookings for West Coast Businesses
**Source**: https://www.thepress.co.nz/nz-news/361081290/google-maps-misleading-nine-hour-detour-spells-plummeting-bookings-west-coast-businesses
**Date**: 2026-09-16
**Author**: The Press (New Zealand)
**Keywords**: Google Maps, detour, routing error, New Zealand, West Coast, tourism, SH6, business impact, navigation reliability

## Elevator pitch
Tourism operators on New Zealand's West Coast report cancellations and plummeting bookings because Google Maps is routing drivers on a misleading nine-hour detour, treating a restricted section of State Highway 6 as fully closed when it remains passable.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps is treating a restricted section of New Zealand's State Highway 6 as completely closed, routing drivers on a nine-hour detour
- Tourism operators report visitors cancelling bookings due to the misleading routing information
- The restricted SH6 section remains passable, but Google Maps does not reflect this nuance
- The issue highlights the real-world economic impact of mapping errors on local businesses dependent on accurate navigation data
- This is another example of navigation apps causing problems by not accurately reflecting road conditions

## Synthesis
Tourism operators on New Zealand's West Coast are reporting significant business impacts as Google Maps directs drivers away from a restricted section of State Highway 6, treating it as fully closed and routing travelers on a misleading nine-hour detour. The restricted section of SH6 remains passable, but Google Maps does not accurately reflect this, causing visitors to cancel bookings or avoid the region entirely.

The situation demonstrates the real-world economic consequences of mapping and routing errors. When a major navigation platform like Google Maps inaccurately represents road conditions, the impact cascades through local economies — particularly in tourism-dependent regions like the West Coast, where businesses rely on steady visitor flow. Operators report that the misleading detour is directly causing cancellations and reduced bookings.

This case adds to a growing body of evidence about the limitations of crowdsourced and algorithmic routing systems. While Google Maps uses real-time traffic and road condition data, it appears to lack the nuance to distinguish between fully closed roads and those with restricted access. For communities dependent on accurate navigation information, these distinctions are not merely technical — they are existential.
