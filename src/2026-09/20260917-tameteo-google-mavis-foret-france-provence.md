# D'après les avis laissés sur Google Maps, la plus belle forêt de France se situe en Provence

**Source**: https://www.tameteo.com/actualites/loisirs/d-apres-les-avis-laisses-sur-google-maps-la-plus-belle-foret-de-france-se-situe-en-provence.html

**Author**: Tameteo.com
**Keywords**: Google Maps, reviews, forests, France, Sainte-Baume, Huelgoat, Aïtone, Holidu, rankings

## Elevator pitch
A ranking by vacation rental platform Holidu uses Google Maps user reviews to identify France's most beautiful forests, with the Sainte-Baume forest in Provence taking first place — though the methodology reveals how review-based rankings can skew toward lesser-known locations.

## Takeaways
- The forest of Sainte-Baume in Var, Provence, ranks first with a 4.9/5 score based on 238 Google Maps reviews
- Huelgoat forest in Brittany ranks second with 4.8/5 from 5,534 reviews, while Aïtone forest in Corsica ranks third with 4.8/5 from 116 reviews
- The Bois de Vincennes, despite collecting 41,456 reviews, only ranks 14th with a 4.5/5 score
- The ranking illustrates how Google Maps reviews function as an emergent evaluation system for natural sites, though small sample sizes can inflate scores
- Sainte-Baume is classified as a "forêt d'exception" by the French National Forestry Office (ONF), featuring an ancient beech forest on a Mediterranean massif
- The forest is also a major pilgrimage site, with the grotto of Sainte-Marie-Madeleine accessible through forest trails

## Synthesis
Vacation rental portal Holidu has produced an autumn-focused ranking of France's most beautiful forests based entirely on Google Maps user reviews. The results offer an interesting case study in how crowdsourced geographic reviews create unexpected geographic narratives.

The Sainte-Baume forest, located on the north slope of the massif of the same name straddling the Var and Bouches-du-Rhône departments, earned the top spot with a 4.9 out of 5 rating. However, this score comes from only 238 reviews — a fraction of the 5,534 reviews for second-place Huelgoat in Brittany (4.8/5) or the 41,456 reviews for the Bois de Vincennes in Paris (4.5/5, 14th place). This disparity highlights a known bias in review-based rankings: locations with fewer visitors but highly motivated reviewers tend to score higher.

The article describes Sainte-Baume as an "exceptional forest" classified by ONF, featuring an ancient beech forest that survives at Mediterranean latitudes thanks to the massif's microclimate of shade and humidity. The forest contains natural curiosities including lapiaz, caves, sinkholes, and natural basins in the Castellette valley. The site is also culturally significant as the location of the Sainte-Marie-Madeleine grotto, a pilgrimage destination since the 11th century.

The ranking demonstrates how Google Maps has evolved beyond navigation into a comprehensive review and discovery platform for natural sites, with its review system effectively creating a crowdsourced ranking infrastructure that tourism businesses and media increasingly reference.
