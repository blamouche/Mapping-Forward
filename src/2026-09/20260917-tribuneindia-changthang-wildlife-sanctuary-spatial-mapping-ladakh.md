# Ladakh Orders Spatial Mapping of Changthang Wildlife Sanctuary

**Source**: https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/j-k/ladakh-l-g-orders-settlement-of-wildlife-sanctuary-rights-claims-within-two-months/
**Date**: 2026-09-17
**Author**: PTI / The Tribune
**Keywords**: Ladakh, Changthang, wildlife sanctuary, spatial mapping, GIS, GPS, geo-referencing, satellite imagery, boundary review, defence installations

## Elevator pitch
The Ladakh administration has ordered spatial mapping and field verification of the Changthang Cold Desert Wildlife Sanctuary using GPS/GIS and satellite imagery, in a process that balances community rights, wildlife conservation, and national security concerns in a strategic border region.

## Synthesis
The Ladakh administration has initiated a comprehensive spatial mapping exercise for the Changthang Cold Desert Wildlife Sanctuary and two other protected areas, responding to long-standing concerns about inaccurate boundary mapping and unresolved community rights.

During the 16th meeting of the State Board for Wildlife, Lieutenant Governor Vinai Kumar Saxena directed that the settlement of rights and claims of local communities in Hemis National Park, Changthang Wildlife Sanctuary, and Karakoram Wildlife Sanctuary be completed within two months. The protected areas had been preliminarily notified in 1987 by the erstwhile Jammu and Kashmir government, but their boundaries were never accurately mapped on the ground.

The previous boundary demarcation was conducted through a "table-top" process without proper field verification or adherence to the statutory process mandated under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972. The L-G observed that this rendered the exercise inconsistent with the Act, inequitable to affected communities, and vulnerable to legal challenges. Several areas included in the earlier sub-committee report are also strategically important for national security, but mandatory defence considerations had not been adequately factored in.

The new mapping exercise will use GPS/GIS mapping, geo-referencing, and satellite imagery. Wildlife officials will scientifically map critical habitats and wildlife movement corridors, while spatial mapping and field surveys of defence installations will be conducted in coordination with police and defence authorities, subject to national security protocols.

The Board also ratified 23 projects related to power transmission infrastructure in border regions, defence infrastructure, and other projects in the Karakoram and Changthang areas, forwarding them to the Standing Committee of the National Board for Wildlife for statutory clearance.
