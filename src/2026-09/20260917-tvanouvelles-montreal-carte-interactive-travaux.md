# Tannée du trafic, une étudiante crée une carte interactive des travaux à Montréal

**Source**: https://www.tvanouvelles.ca/2026/09/17/une-ingenieure-creee-une-carte-interactive-des-travaux-routiers-a-montreal
**Date**: 2026-09-17
**Author**: Agence QMI / TVA Nouvelles
**Keywords**: Montreal, road works, interactive map, crowdsourcing, AI, traffic, Google Maps, cestdejalenfer.ca

## Elevator pitch
A computer engineering student in Montreal built an interactive platform that aggregates road construction data from 20 municipalities in the Greater Montreal area, using AI to fill gaps where official sources and Google Maps fall behind.

## Takeaways
- Shelsea Saint-Fleur, a data engineering student, created cestdejalenfer.ca after being stuck in traffic and frustrated by scattered, outdated road work information
- The platform covers Montreal plus 19 other municipalities, consolidating data from Québec 511, municipal interactive maps, and AI-extracted information from public notices
- The site uses AI to read construction notices and automatically extract street names, closures, and work details from sources that lack their own maps
- Users can query works in real time and also search future date ranges to plan trips around upcoming closures
- The platform allows crowdsourced contributions through two forms for citizens to supplement the data
- The creator spent approximately 25 hours building the platform, developed overnight from 10 PM to 4 AM

## Synthesis
Montreal's road construction season has become notorious enough to inspire a citizen-built mapping platform. Shelsea Saint-Fleur, a bachelor's student in computer engineering specializing in data engineering, created cestdejalenfer.ca after what she described as "pété une coche" — snapping while stuck in traffic.

The platform addresses a real gap: Montreal has so many simultaneous construction projects that official city websites are not always updated in time, which means Google Maps also cannot reflect the latest road closures. Rather than relying solely on official feeds, Saint-Fleur's platform uses AI to process sources that lack structured map data. For sites without interactive maps, the system reads public notices and automatically extracts street names, closures, and work details.

The platform goes beyond real-time information by offering temporal queries: users can select a date or a date range to see all planned works and closures for that period, enabling proactive trip planning rather than reactive avoidance.

The project demonstrates how citizen-developed mapping tools can outperform traditional navigation apps in specific local contexts where data freshness and completeness matter more than global coverage. It also illustrates the growing role of AI in geospatial data processing — using natural language processing to convert unstructured municipal announcements into structured map data.
