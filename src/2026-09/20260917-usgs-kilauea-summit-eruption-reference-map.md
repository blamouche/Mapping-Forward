## Synthesis
The USGS Hawaiian Volcano Observatory (HVO) published a reference map documenting the Kīlauea summit eruption that began on September 16, 2026. The map was created using data collected during a helicopter overflight conducted on the morning of September 16, as part of HVO's routine eruption response protocol.

The reference map is part of the observatory's Photo & Video Chronology series, which tracks volcanic activity at Kīlauea through periodic overflights and ground-based observations. The map documents new eruptive features within the Halemaʻumaʻu crater, the active vent site within Kīlauea's summit caldera that has been the focus of repeated eruption episodes in recent years.

Kīlauea is one of the world's most active volcanoes and has been the subject of intensive monitoring by the HVO, which uses a combination of seismic sensors, gas emission measurements, satellite imagery, and direct visual observation via helicopter overflights to produce reference maps during eruptions. These maps serve as critical tools for hazard assessment, public communication, and scientific documentation of eruptive activity.

The September 16 eruption reference map represents the kind of rapid-response cartographic product that volcano observatories produce during active eruptions, translating raw observational data into structured geographic information that can be used by emergency managers, researchers, and the public.
