# 'A map is never neutral': meet the cartographers who took on centuries of western bias

**Source**: https://www.theguardian.com/science/ng-interactive/2026/sep/18/map-cartographers-western-bias-mercator-equal-earth
**Date**: 2026-09-18
**Author**: The Guardian
**Keywords**: Equal Earth projection, Mercator, UN, cartography, western bias, map projections, Tom Patterson, Bojan Šavrič, Bernhard Jenny, Africa, Correct the Map

## Elevator pitch
The three creators of the Equal Earth projection — Tom Patterson, Bojan Šavrič, and Bernhard Jenny — describe their astonishment at the UN's near-universal adoption of their map, which more accurately represents Africa and all continents, and share how diplomatic complaints about borders crept into what was meant as a pure mathematical projection.

## Takeaways
- The UN General Assembly voted 164-1 (US voting no, 6 abstentions) to adopt the Equal Earth projection, replacing Mercator on school walls and government websites
- Tom Patterson, a retired US national parks cartographer, has been inundated with questions from governments and diplomats since the vote
- Japan requested a revision because the map made the disputed Kuril Islands appear to belong to Russia; Ukraine abstained because Russian-occupied Crimea was shown in a different colour
- The cartographers clarified the confusion: the UN resolution adopted a mathematical projection without borders — the border disputes arose from Patterson's annotated website version
- Equal Earth was developed in 2018 by Patterson, Šavrič (software engineer), and Jenny (Monash University associate professor) as an alternative to both Mercator's size distortions and Gall-Peters' elongated appearance
- Šavrič appreciated that the UN resolution does not ban Mercator or mandate a single replacement, instead promoting map literacy
- Jenny noted the power of the name "Equal Earth" versus a hypothetical "Šavrič-Patterson-Jenny projection"

## Synthesis
This Guardian feature follows up on the UN's September 2026 adoption of the Equal Earth projection with interviews of its three creators. The article reveals the unintended diplomatic fallout — border disputes projected onto a mathematical transformation — and underscores Jenny's observation that "a map is never neutral." The piece also traces the history of map projection attempts to fix Mercator's distortions, from Gall-Peters to Goode homolosine, positioning Equal Earth as the first to gain institutional backing at the highest level.
