## Synthesis
The Arctic Institute's Cryosphere Series 2026 includes a striking map of the Arctic Ocean that goes beyond conventional cartography. Rather than depicting a single layer of information, the map is an artistic-scientific tool that brings together traces of environmental change, political power, ecological life, human history, language, and questions of territory and positionality within the same image.

Constructed from scientific data, the map layers multiple dimensions: Arctic ice boundaries showing the retreat of sea ice, territorial claims of Arctic nations, existing and proposed shipping lanes (including the Northern Sea Route, Northwest Passage, and Transpolar Sea Route), oil and gas prospecting areas, and wildlife migration routes for birds and whales. It also includes linguistic regions, connecting human cultural geography to the physical and political landscape.

A cropped section of the map, photographed by Jennifer Rainsford, shows the density of information: ice lines, migration routes, shipping lanes, extraction zones, and linguistic boundaries all coexisting in the same visual space. By assigning each trace an equal graphic value, the map proposes an understanding of the Arctic Ocean as an interconnected system where environmental, political, economic, and cultural forces are entangled rather than separate.

The map functions as a tool for drawing connections between past and future trajectories of transformation — processes that may reshape the world order as Arctic ice retreats, new shipping routes open, and territorial claims are asserted. It is both a scientific document and an artistic statement about the complexity of one of the planet's most rapidly changing regions.
