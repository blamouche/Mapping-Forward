# Traffic chaos in Perthshire villages after Google Maps glitch

**Source**: https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c933k2y1xk53o
**Date**: 2026-09-22
**Author**: BBC Scotland (Claire Thomson, Sarah Farron)
**Keywords**: Google Maps, glitch, traffic diversion, Perthshire, A9, Dunkeld, Pitlochry, single-track road, navigation error

## Synthesis
A Google Maps glitch caused significant disruption in rural Perthshire over the weekend of September 19–21, 2026. The navigation app falsely reported the A9 trunk road as closed between Dunkeld and Pitlochry and redirected drivers onto the U163, an unclassified single-track road with no designated passing places, running through the villages of Ballinluig, Tulliemet and Guay.

The issue was first noticed on Friday. By Saturday, Transport Scotland had posted on X (formerly Twitter) that Google Maps was falsely reporting the A9 as closed and was recommending an "inconvenient alternate route" when the road remained open. The problem was eventually resolved after Laura Hansler of the A9 Dual Action campaign group contacted the Google Maps team in London directly.

Local residents described frightening conditions. Joanne Boyne, who lives in Guay, reported that traffic was travelling in both directions on the narrow road, forcing cars to reverse when they met. She observed campervans, a large coach, and cars with trailers — one towing a boat — all using the road, some at speeds as fast as 40 mph. The winding, narrow nature of the road, combined with overgrown trees and bushes, made it unsafe for such traffic. Boyne's parents, who live three miles away in Tulliemet, were unable to leave their house since Friday, even to go for a walk, because it was too dangerous. A normally 10-minute drive took her 45 minutes.

The bumper-to-bumper traffic meant there would have been no space for an ambulance or fire engine to pass, raising serious safety concerns in the community. Hansler noted that this was not an old diversion that was never cancelled, but rather one of Google Maps' recurring unexplained errors. She added that Google Maps had previously shown incorrect speed limits on sections of the A9.

The incident highlights the real-world consequences of navigation app errors on rural infrastructure and the importance of rapid communication between transport authorities and mapping platforms.
