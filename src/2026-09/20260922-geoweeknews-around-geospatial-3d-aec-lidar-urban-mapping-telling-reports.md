## Synthesis
Geo Week News' weekly round-up covers three stories from the geospatial, 3D, and AEC industries, each highlighting a different dimension of the field's evolution.

**Leveraging LiDAR in a Digital Ecosystem** (American Surveyor) profiles WW Clyde, a Utah-based heavy civil contractor that has made mobile LiDAR the connective tissue of its digital construction workflow. The company uses ground-based scanning not just for pre-construction surveys but for progress monitoring, as-built documentation, and dispute protection across highway corridors stretching 20 to 40 miles. The payoff extends beyond expected use cases: when a 682.5 MW solar project discovered that 148,000 pilings had been installed at incorrect elevations, WW Clyde's mobile scanner captured the top-center 3D coordinates of every pile, giving the general contractor the data needed to decide whether to adjust or redo the work.

**People, not technology, are at the heart of an urban digital twin** (GIM International) features an interview with Markus Mohl, head of Digital Twin Munich, ahead of Intergeo in Munich. Mohl pushes back on the technology-first narrative around urban digital twins, arguing that the hardest part of building one isn't acquiring LiDAR data or integrating sensor feeds, but convincing dozens of city departments to break down data silos and share information even when they have no direct benefit from doing so. From a city-wide building register to a unique lane-level model of Munich's street space, Mohl walks through what seven years of real-world progress looks like — and why the profession's biggest unsolved problem isn't real-time integration or AI, it's communication.

**WGIC report: Trustworthy, decision-grade geospatial data** (Spatial Source) covers a new report from the World Geospatial Industry Council sounding the alarm on a quiet but consequential shift in how geospatial data is produced. As AI increasingly generates and synthesizes spatial data at scale, the sector is moving from measurement-based trust to model-based trust, bringing risks of silent errors, untraceable lineage, and blurred accountability when decisions go wrong. The report lays out six properties of decision-grade data and a phased action plan spanning provenance tracking and metadata standards now, cryptographic verification within two years, and a federated industry-wide governance framework within three. WGIC Executive Director Aaron Addison warns that a vulnerability anywhere along the sensor-to-decision data chain propagates all the way downstream.
