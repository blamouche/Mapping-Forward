## Elevator pitch

## Takeaways
- Dynamic movement data adds a temporal dimension to spatial analysis, revealing how people, wildlife, and other moving elements interact with landscapes over time
- Integrating dynamic movement data with project information in GIS platforms like ES GeoHub helps identify potential spatial interactions requiring further review
- GPS tracking of animal movements combined with biodiversity datasets supports more informed risk analysis and project planning
- For road infrastructure projects, understanding wildlife movement patterns can help identify risks before construction, supporting better route alignment and reducing costly design changes
- The IDB is exploring this approach to reduce environmental and operational risks in development projects

## Synthesis
The Inter-American Development Bank (IDB) is exploring how Global Positioning System (GPS) tracking data and Geographic Information Systems (GIS) can complement biodiversity information to reduce environmental and operational risk in development projects. Traditional geographic analysis relies on static data layers representing conditions at a specific point in time, but GIS can also incorporate dynamic data such as human mobility patterns, transportation movements, and animal movement data.

By integrating animal movement data with project locations, the IDB's approach helps identify potential interactions between wildlife and development projects earlier. This supports better-informed environmental planning and decision-making. For example, for a road infrastructure project, understanding how wildlife movement patterns intersect with a proposed route can help identify potential risks before construction begins, supporting better route alignment decisions and minimizing impacts on ecologically sensitive areas.

The IDB integrates this dynamic movement data through its ES GeoHub web application, combining it with biodiversity and territorial datasets. When key environmental information emerges too late in the project cycle, it can trigger additional studies or design revisions that increase costs and delay the project. Dynamic movement data aims to surface these risks earlier.

The approach also has broader applications beyond wildlife: dynamic movement data can incorporate human mobility, public transportation movements, logistics flows, and route optimization, making it a versatile tool for spatial risk analysis across multiple sectors.
