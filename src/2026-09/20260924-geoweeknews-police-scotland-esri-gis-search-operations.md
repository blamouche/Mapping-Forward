# Police Scotland modernises search operations with Esri GIS

**Source**: https://www.geoweeknews.com/articles/police-scotland-modernises-search-operations-with-esri-gis/
**Date**: 2026-09-24
**Author**: Industry Press Release (Geo Week News)
**Keywords**: Police Scotland, Esri, GIS, search operations, missing persons, mobile mapping, Esri Award, Commonwealth Games, public safety

## Elevator pitch
Police Scotland has won an Esri Special Achievement in GIS Award for transforming search operations with mobile mapping apps and interactive dashboards, replacing paper-based processes to find missing people, secure Royal visits, and protect major events like the Glasgow Commonwealth Games.

## Takeaways
- Police Scotland won an Esri Special Achievement in GIS Award for modernising search operations

- The system was most recently used at the Commonwealth Games in Glasgow in July 2026

## Synthesis
Police Scotland has won an Esri Special Achievement in GIS Award for transforming its search operations for finding missing people, securing Royal visits, and protecting major events. The award was presented at Esri's annual User Conference in San Diego, with winners chosen by Esri President Jack Dangermond for setting new standards in the global GIS community.

The Missing Persons Module helps senior officers plan and allocate resources more efficiently, assigning areas to dog teams, horses, drones, diving and marine units, fingertip search, or mountain rescue. Officers on the ground receive assignments and search boundaries on mobile devices. The system connects field teams and command staff instantly, with everyone seeing the same updates including images of finds such as recovered clothing, enabling real-time search strategy refinement.

For securing venues before major events or Royal visits, officers use mobile devices to log search and seal operations at potential hiding places. Teams use drone imagery and CAD drawings to create accurate operational pictures. Police Scotland has created virtual models of regularly searched locations including Holyrood House and the Royal Mile in Edinburgh. The recent developments evolved from initial operations created for COP26 in 2022, where new geospatial tools meant operations were completed up to 75% faster.
