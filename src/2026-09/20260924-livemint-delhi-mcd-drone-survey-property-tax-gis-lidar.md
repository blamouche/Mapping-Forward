## Elevator pitch
Delhi's Municipal Corporation has launched a pilot drone survey in two wards using GIS, LiDAR, AI, and 3D modelling to identify unassessed and under-assessed properties, building a more accurate digital property database and reducing reliance on manual inspections.

## Synthesis
The Municipal Corporation of Delhi (MCD) has begun a pilot drone survey of properties in two wards—Rohini F Ward of Rohini Zone and Gharoli Ward of Shahdara South Zone—as part of an effort to improve property tax assessment. The civic body plans to combine drone imagery with technologies including GIS, LiDAR-based mapping, geo-tagging, artificial intelligence, and 3D digital modelling to build a more detailed property database.

The technology-based exercise allows the MCD to examine existing property records against aerial and mapped data, identifying incorrectly mapped or duplicate records, as well as additional floors or constructions not appearing in existing records. The system can compare declared built-up area with actual built-up area, enabling assessment based on size, number of floors, and property usage.

The MCD expects the integrated system to reduce reliance on manual property inspections. Ward- and zone-level dashboards will allow officials to monitor discrepancies, issue notices, and track property tax arrears and recovery. For residents, the initiative is expected to bring greater transparency to property records while reducing the need for physical interaction with civic officials. The current survey is a pilot in the two identified wards, forming the first phase of MCD's broader technology-driven approach.
