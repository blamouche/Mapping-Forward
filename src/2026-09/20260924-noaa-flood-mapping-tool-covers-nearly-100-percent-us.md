# NOAA's flood mapping tool now covers nearly 100% of U.S.

**Source**: https://www.noaa.gov/news-release/noaa-flood-mapping-tool-now-covers-nearly-100-of-us
**Date**: 2026-09-24
**Author**: NOAA National Weather Service
**Keywords**: NOAA, flood inundation mapping, FIM, river flooding, emergency management, GIS, National Water Model, disaster response

## Elevator pitch
NOAA's Flood Inundation Mapping (FIM) tool, which predicts river flooding at neighborhood scale, has expanded from 60% to nearly 100% coverage of the U.S. population, providing emergency managers and the public with critical flooding extent visualization along 3.3 million river miles.

## Takeaways
- FIM now covers nearly 100% of the U.S. population, up from 60% in 2025 and 10% at launch in 2023
- The tool depicts flooding potential along 3.3 million river miles throughout the continental U.S., territories, and Hawaii
- New coverage areas include the southeastern U.S. coast, Florida, Great Lakes region, New England, Rocky Mountains, the Dakotas, and western Nebraska
- FIM provides four versions: hourly analysis, 5-day inundation forecast, RFC 5-day forecast, and static categorical FIM
- Real-world success stories include evacuations in Tennessee, Arkansas, Texas, Oklahoma, and Michigan that saved lives
- Maps are publicly available on the National Water Prediction Service website in multiple GIS file types

## Synthesis
NOAA's National Weather Service announced that Flood Inundation Mapping (FIM), a revolutionary tool for predicting river flooding, is now available on an experimental basis to nearly 100% of the U.S. population. This represents a massive expansion from FIM's prior coverage of 60% of the country. FIM produces estimates of land area covered in water based on model forecast river flows, predicting where flooding impacts may occur in the following days.

FIM puts river and stream flooding on the map at a neighborhood scale, depicting flooding potential along 3.3 million river miles throughout the continental U.S., U.S. territories, and Hawaii. The latest expansion adds coverage for the southeastern U.S. coast and Florida, Great Lakes region, New England, the Rocky Mountains, the Dakotas, and western Nebraska.

The tool has already proven its value in real emergencies. NWS Nashville used FIM to safely evacuate a Senior Assisted Living facility in Macon County hours before floodwaters arrived. In Hardy, Arkansas, FIM helped achieve a nearly 100% evacuation success rate. During July 2025 storms in central Texas, FIM enabled road closures and evacuations in Georgetown, TX. FIM services are publicly available on the National Water Prediction Service website in several GIS applications and multiple file types.
