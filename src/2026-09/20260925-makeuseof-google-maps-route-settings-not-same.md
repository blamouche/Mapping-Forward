# Google Maps isn't giving everyone the same route, and these 3 settings are why

**Source**: https://www.makeuseof.com/google-maps-route-settings/
**Date**: 2026-09-25
**Author**: MakeUseOf
**Keywords**: Google Maps, route settings, fuel-efficient routes, navigation, personalization, algorithm

## Elevator pitch
MakeUseOf explains how three Google Maps settings — fuel-efficient routes, eco-friendly routing, and personalized route preferences — cause different users to receive different directions for the same destination.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps does not show identical routes to all users for the same trip — three key settings control the variation
- Fuel-efficient routing is now enabled by default, prioritising greener routes over the fastest option
- Personalized route preferences allow users to customize avoidance criteria (tolls, highways, ferries) which directly affects the suggested itinerary
- The algorithm also factors in real-time traffic, historical data, and user behaviour patterns to personalise recommendations
- Understanding these settings empowers users to override defaults and get routes that match their priorities rather than Google's algorithmic choices

## Synthesis
This MakeUseOf article addresses a common user complaint: two people entering the same destination in Google Maps may receive different routes. The explanation lies in three settings that control how the routing algorithm prioritises options. The article demystifies a behaviour that many users find confusing or attribute to bugs.

The first setting, fuel-efficient routing, is now the default. Google introduced eco-friendly routing to reduce fuel consumption and emissions, sometimes at the cost of travel time. This means the app may suggest a slightly longer but greener route, a choice that not all users are aware of or want. The second setting involves personalised route preferences — users can specify avoidance of tolls, highways, or ferries, and these preferences create route divergence. The third factor is the algorithm's use of real-time traffic and historical data combined with user behaviour patterns, meaning the app adapts based on accumulated signals about what routes work best.

The practical takeaway is that Google Maps' routing is not a neutral, optimised recommendation but a personalised suggestion shaped by both user-configured settings and algorithmic defaults. Users who want the fastest route regardless of fuel efficiency need to explicitly disable the eco-friendly option. The article serves as a useful reminder that navigation apps make choices on our behalf, and understanding those choices is essential for getting the results we actually want.
