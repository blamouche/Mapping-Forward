## Synthesis
Geographic information systems (GIS) help organizations see and explain spatial patterns in public health, from disease prevalence to access to care and health outcomes. However, maps and data alone do not reach people — they need to be translated into effective digital health communication that resonates with target audiences in specific places.

The article argues that "place" is a critical dimension in health communication. Understanding where audiences live, work, and seek care enables health organizations to tailor their messaging and interventions to specific communities. GIS provides the analytical foundation for identifying these spatial patterns, but the communication layer requires additional skills including content strategy, design, and data visualization.

Producing strong digital health content may involve analysts, writers, designers, developers, and video editors. Smaller teams may need to bring in outside specialists for particular projects, such as cleaning a dataset, designing an accessible map, or adapting an article into a short explainer. The production workflow should be built around the work itself, ensuring that GIS insights are effectively translated into actionable health communication that reaches the right audiences in the right places.
