# Accès à l'A8 depuis la promenade des Anglais à Nice : ce que révèlent les chiffres de TomTom

**Source**: https://www.nicematin.com/societe/vie-locale/acces-a-l-a8-depuis-la-promenade-des-anglais-a-nice-ce-que-revelent-les-chiffres-de-tomtom-10730460
**Date**: 2026-09-27
**Author**: Nice-Matin
**Keywords**: TomTom, traffic congestion, Nice, A8, promenade des Anglais, Mercantour, Valéry-Giscard-d'Estaing, urban mobility, real-time traffic data

## Elevator pitch
TomTom traffic data reveals that the reopening of the A8 motorway entrance from Nice's promenade des Anglais via boulevard du Mercantour has worsened congestion by 20 percentage points compared to 2025, with peak congestion exceeding 200% in 2026 versus 165% the previous year.

## Takeaways
- Since August 20, 2026, the Métropole Nice Côte d'Azur reopened the A8 entrance from the promenade des Anglais via boulevard du Mercantour, reversing Christian Estrosi's 2025 closure
- TomTom data shows average congestion in the zone rose from 33.7% (2025) to 54.6% (2026) between August 20 and September 10
- Peak congestion record in 2025 was 165%, while 2026 already exceeds 200%
- The busiest days remain Wednesdays, Thursdays, and Fridays, with the most congested hours between 16:00 and 19:00
- Two-thirds of Nice-Matin readers favor making the access permanent, though many report worse traffic on avenue Valéry-Giscard-d'Estaing
- The experimental reopening runs until October 30, 2026, with the prefecture evaluating effects on traffic flow

## Synthesis
The reopening of the A8 motorway entrance from Nice's promenade des Anglais, initiated by Métropole Nice Côte d'Azur president Éric Ciotti on August 20, 2026, has produced measurable traffic degradation according to TomTom's real-time traffic data. The congestion rate for the zone around the Valéry-Giscard-d'Estaing avenue access jumped from 33.7% in 2025 to 54.6% in 2026 for the same August 20 to September 10 period, a 20-point increase.

TomTom's data, consulted by Nice-Matin, reveals that the peak congestion record has surged from 165% in 2025 to over 200% in 2026. The traffic patterns remain consistent across both years: Wednesdays, Thursdays, and Fridays are the most congested days, with the 16:00-19:00 window being the most tense. The experimental phase, set to end October 30, 2026, reversed the closure implemented by Ciotti's predecessor Christian Estrosi, who had eliminated the entrance to widen the A8 access to two lanes and accommodate the Mathis tunnel opening in May 2025.

A reader consultation by Nice-Matin found two-thirds in favor of permanent retention of the access, though many noted increased traffic on the Valéry-Giscard-d'Estaing avenue and criticized a traffic light excessively unfavorable to vehicles coming from boulevard du Mercantour.
