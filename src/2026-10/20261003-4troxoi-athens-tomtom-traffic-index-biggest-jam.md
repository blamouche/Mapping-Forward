## Synthesis
Greek automotive publication 4troxoi published a retrospective analysis of Athens' worst traffic jam of the year, using TomTom Traffic Index data to quantify the paralysis. On October 2, 2025, congestion levels reached 149%, the highest recorded for the year, meaning drivers needed at least 15 minutes to cover just 2.8 kilometres during peak hours.

The article identifies three converging factors. First, a Robbie Williams concert at the historic Kallimarmaro stadium drew tens of thousands of fans who flooded major arteries precisely when the evening commute was peaking. Second, a large pro-Palestine protest march prompted Greek police to close central avenues including Vasilissis Sofias, Panepistimiou, Stadiou, and Vasilissis Amalias, forcing traffic onto surrounding roads that could not absorb the volume. Third, sudden heavy rainfall caused localised flooding and reduced accessibility on already saturated routes.

The TomTom Traffic Index data provides the quantitative backbone of the analysis, demonstrating how the real-time congestion monitoring platform captures the compounding effect of simultaneous events on urban road networks. The article illustrates the value of traffic data platforms for post-hoc analysis of urban mobility failures.
