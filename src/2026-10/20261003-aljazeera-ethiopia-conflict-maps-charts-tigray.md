# How the fighting in Ethiopia spread beyond Tigray, in maps and charts

**Source**: https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/2/how-the-fighting-in-ethiopia-spread-beyond-tigray-in-maps-and-charts
**Date**: 2026-10-02
**Author**: Marium Ali (Al Jazeera)
**Keywords**: Ethiopia, Tigray, conflict mapping, interactive maps, Amhara, Afar, TPLF, civil war, ACLED, cartographie conflictuelle, cartes interactives

## Elevator pitch
Al Jazeera published a detailed visual explainer using interactive maps and charts to show how fighting has intensified across northern Ethiopia, spreading from Tigray into Amhara and Afar regions, nearly four years after a peace deal ended the devastating 2020-2022 Tigray war.

## Takeaways
- Fighting is concentrated in three regions of northern Ethiopia: Tigray, Amhara, and Afar, with multiple active fronts rather than a single battlefield
- Government-aligned forces captured the strategic town of Alamata in southern Tigray on September 28, while Tigrayan forces pushed into Afar, threatening the Semera-Djibouti import corridor
- Seven Ethiopian armed and political groups announced the "Ethiopian Peoples' Forces Alliance for Survival" on September 20, aiming to overthrow Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's government
- The TPLF, Amhara Fano National Movement, and Ugugumo (Afar armed group) are the three alliance members actively fighting federal forces as of September 26, according to ACLED
- Ethiopia's army chief accused Eritrea, Sudan's army, and Egypt of backing the armed groups, raising fears of a wider regional war
- The interactive maps detail Ethiopia's 12 regional states and ethnic composition (Oromo 35%, Amhara 24%, Tigrayan 6%), providing geographic context for the federal system introduced in 1992

## Synthesis
Al Jazeera's visual explainer combines interactive maps and data charts to document the escalation of conflict in northern Ethiopia in late September 2026. The fighting, which began with clashes near the Sudanese border on August 1, sharply intensified after September 23 when Tigrayan forces seized airports in Mekelle, Axum, and Shire and pushed into neighbouring regions.

The mapping identifies three distinct fronts: southern Tigray around the strategic supply hub of Alamata (captured by government forces on September 28 after drone strikes), western Tigray near the Tekeze River in disputed territory, and northern Amhara around Kobo and Gidan. Tigrayan forces and the Afar armed group Ugugumo have also advanced into Afar, where fighting along the Tigray-Afar border threatens the Semera-Djibouti corridor, Ethiopia's main import route for fuel and goods.

The political mapping is equally detailed, charting the seven-member alliance announced on September 20, its contested membership, and the specific roles of each group. ACLED data shows only three members are actively fighting. The explainer contextualises Ethiopia's federal system, mapping the 12 regional states and the ethnic distribution that underpins them, providing essential geographic context for understanding how territorial disputes drive the conflict.
