## Elevator pitch
French automotive outlet Auto Plus recounts how a Spanish YouTuber spotted what appeared to be two abandoned Ferrari F40s on Google Maps in rural Spain, only to discover on-site that both were cheap replicas built on Pontiac Fiero chassis — illustrating how satellite imagery can deceive.

## Synthesis
French automotive publication Auto Plus reported on a Spanish YouTuber's quest to verify two apparent abandoned Ferrari F40 supercars spotted on Google Maps in rural Spain. The story illustrates the gap between satellite imagery and ground truth.

The first sighting was in a small town near the Portuguese border, where Google Maps showed a coupe resembling a Ferrari F40 in an isolated barn surrounded by old vehicles. The YouTuber, known as Georgesmithgood, traveled to the location and found the building with the car inside. However, upon close inspection, it was not a genuine F40 but a replica built on a Pontiac Fiero chassis — a well-known platform for kit car supercar imitations.

The second sighting near Valencia yielded the same result: another Fiero-based F40 replica. The owner even offered to sell it for $35,000 (approximately €32,000), which the YouTuber declined.

The article explains that Ferrari F40 replicas are widespread, with the Pontiac Fiero being the preferred donor vehicle due to its mid-engine configuration, suitable dimensions, low cost, and availability. Some enthusiasts even sacrifice genuine sports cars like the Acura NSX to create fake F40s. The story serves as a cautionary tale that alluring discoveries on Google Maps can conceal far more mundane realities.
