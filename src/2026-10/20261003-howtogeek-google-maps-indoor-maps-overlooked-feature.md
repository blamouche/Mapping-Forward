# Google Maps' Indoor Maps: The Overlooked Feature for Navigating Buildings

**Source**: https://www.howtogeek.com/this-might-be-one-of-google-maps-most-overlooked-features/
**Date**: 2026-10-03
**Author**: How-To Geek
**Keywords**: Google Maps, Indoor Maps, floor plans, airports, shopping malls, navigation, mobile features, 360-degree photos, travel

## Elevator pitch
How-To Geek highlights Google Maps' Indoor Maps feature, which displays floor plans and points of interest inside supported buildings like airports and shopping malls, arguing it is one of the app's most underappreciated tools for travelers navigating unfamiliar indoor spaces.

## Takeaways
- Google Maps' Indoor Maps shows detailed floor plans, shops, facilities, and points of interest inside supported buildings, going beyond the exterior-only view
- The feature is available on Android, iPhone, iPad, and desktop, but only at supported locations — primarily airports, shopping malls, and high-traffic public buildings
- Users access Indoor Maps by zooming into a supported building; floor plans appear in a corner panel, allowing floor-by-floor exploration
- Google Maps also offers 360-degree interior photos for some buildings, accessible by tapping a place name and swiping through photos to find Street View-symbol entries
- The major limitation is coverage: many popular locations still lack Indoor Maps support, making it a useful supplementary layer rather than a reliable primary navigation tool

## Synthesis
Tech publication How-To Geek published a detailed guide to Google Maps' Indoor Maps feature, calling it one of the app's most overlooked capabilities. Indoor Maps provides detailed floor plans and interior layouts for supported buildings, showing shops, information centers, restrooms, and other points of interest organized by floor.

The feature is accessed by simply zooming into a supported building in Google Maps. The floor plan appears in a corner of the screen — right side on Android and desktop, left side on iOS. Users can then tap through individual floors to see what is located where. Google Maps also complements this with 360-degree interior photos for some buildings, accessible through the photo gallery of places within the building.

The article identifies airports and shopping malls as the two most useful scenarios for Indoor Maps. Airports, with their multiple terminals, gates, lounges, and facilities spread across different wings and floors, benefit particularly from advance indoor layout knowledge — especially for tight connections. Malls, often designed in confusing layouts, become easier to navigate when users can check a store's floor and location before arriving.

The significant caveat is coverage: Indoor Maps is not universally available, and many popular destinations remain unsupported. The author recommends treating it as a useful extra layer rather than a primary navigation tool, particularly valuable when traveling to unfamiliar locations where knowing the layout in advance saves time and reduces stress.
