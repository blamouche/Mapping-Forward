# Live Map: Track the path of Hurricane Rachel

**Source**: https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/live-map-track-the-path-of-hurricane-rachel
**Date**: 2026-10-02
**Author**: Fabiola Sanchez, Jonathan Castellón, Sarah Raza (Associated Press / PBS NewsHour)
**Keywords**: Hurricane Rachel, live map, Category 3, Pacific hurricane, Mexico, National Hurricane Center, NOAA, El Niño, storm tracking, Hurricane Polo, interactive map

## Elevator pitch
PBS NewsHour featured a live interactive map tracking Hurricane Rachel, upgraded to a Category 3 storm off Mexico's Pacific coast, as northern Mexico was still reeling from flooding and destruction caused by the remnants of Hurricane Polo earlier in the week.

## Takeaways
- Hurricane Rachel was upgraded to a Category 3 storm with maximum sustained winds of 120 mph (193 kph), located about 235 miles south of the southern tip of Baja California
- The storm was moving west-northwest at 3 mph and expected to strengthen before weakening over the weekend, according to the National Hurricane Center
- Rachel's swells were expected to generate life-threatening surf and rip-current conditions off Mexico's coast
- Mexican authorities remained on alert for very heavy rain in Baja California Sur, Sinaloa, and Jalisco, with risks of landslides, rising river levels, and flooding
- The Pacific has seen an unusually busy hurricane season attributed to El Niño, which has fueled warmer waters and favorable wind patterns
- Parts of northern Mexico were still recovering from Hurricane Polo, with at least 13 municipalities remaining flooded and hundreds displaced

## Synthesis
PBS NewsHour provided a live interactive tracking map for Hurricane Rachel, which reached Category 3 status on October 2, 2026, off Mexico's Pacific coast. The storm, with 120 mph sustained winds, was located 235 miles south of Baja California and moving slowly west-northwest. The National Hurricane Center warned of life-threatening surf and rip currents along Mexico's coast.

The article contextualises Rachel within an unusually active Pacific hurricane season driven by El Niño, which has produced warmer Pacific waters and wind patterns favorable for hurricane formation. The storm follows Hurricane Polo, which caused extensive flooding in northern Mexico, with at least 13 municipalities still flooded and hundreds of people displaced. The U.S. Embassy in Mexico issued warnings for Cabo San Lucas about beach and port closures.

The live map exemplifies the growing role of interactive mapping tools in emergency communication, allowing the public to track storm paths in near real-time. The National Hurricane Center's data feeds these visualisations, combining satellite imagery, wind speed readings, and projected trajectories to help residents and authorities make informed decisions about evacuations and emergency preparedness.
