# Cal Fire launches AwareCA emergency alerts app with interactive maps

**Source**: https://www.pressdemocrat.com/2026/10/02/awareca-app-cal-fire-emergency-alerts
**Date**: 2026-10-02
**Author**: Martin Espinoza (The Press Democrat), Nathan Chiochios (Chico Enterprise-Record)
**Keywords**: AwareCA, Cal Fire, emergency mapping, Mapbox, wildfire, interactive maps, Intterra, Watch Duty, evacuation zones, push notifications, location-aware alerts, California

## Elevator pitch
Cal Fire, in partnership with software company Intterra, launched AwareCA, a free mobile app providing real-time emergency information with interactive maps built on Mapbox, directly challenging the popular Watch Duty wildfire alert platform.

## Takeaways
- Cal Fire partnered with Intterra to launch AwareCA, a free mobile app available on Apple App Store and Google Play, providing real-time emergency information directly from public safety agencies
- The app features near real-time incident updates, interactive emergency maps, warning and order evacuation zones, shelter and assistance locations, hazard information, road closures, and push notifications with location-aware alerts
- AwareCA covers wildfires, floods, earthquakes, severe weather, hazardous materials incidents, and other large-scale emergencies
- A distinctive feature allows users to track emergency response aircraft and their flight paths in 3D, and access Caltrans traffic cameras and ALERTCalifornia wildfire cameras
- The app competes with Watch Duty, a Bay Area nonprofit that has reached 30 million users and operates in all 50 US states
- Agencies already using AwareCA include Los Angeles City Fire Department, U.S. Forest Service, Ventura County Fire, and San Diego Fire and Rescue

## Synthesis
Cal Fire launched AwareCA on October 2, 2026, a free mobile app built in partnership with Intterra that provides real-time emergency information coordinated directly by public safety agencies. The app's interactive maps, powered by Mapbox, display active incidents across California with evacuation zones, shelter locations, road closures, and hazard information. Users can track emergency response aircraft in 3D and access Caltrans traffic cameras and ALERTCalifornia wildfire cameras through the platform.

The launch positions Cal Fire in competition with Watch Duty, the Santa Rosa-based nonprofit that has become an indispensable wildfire alert tool with 30 million users since its founding five years ago. Watch Duty CEO John Mills welcomed the new app, noting that Watch Duty already draws on Cal Fire's official data alongside U.S. Forest Service data, cross-referenced by trained staff and volunteers.

The key differentiator for AwareCA, according to Cal Fire spokesman Tyree Zander, is that all emergency updates are vetted by public safety agency personnel rather than citizen reporters. The information is simultaneously shared to the app in real time as it updates Cal Fire's website. Intterra states the app will remain free, arguing that Californians already fund emergency response and should not have to pay for life safety alerts.
