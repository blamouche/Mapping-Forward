# Mapping a Greener Future for Louisville: Geography Alumna Leads Brightside, Inc.

**Source**: https://news.louisville.edu/news/mapping-greener-future-louisville-geography-alumna-leads-brightside-inc
**Date**: 2026-10-02
**Author**: Stephanie Godward (UofL News)
**Keywords**: GIS, geography, University of Louisville, Brightside, Angela Richardson, Louisville Metro Government, data analysis, spatial data, community cleanup, sustainability, GIS concentration, career

## Elevator pitch
A University of Louisville geography alumna with a GIS concentration has been named interim director of Brightside, Inc., applying spatial data analysis and mapping technology to lead community cleanup and beautification programs across Louisville.

## Takeaways
- Angela Richardson, a UofL Geography BS graduate with a GIS concentration, was appointed interim director of Brightside, Inc., a public-private partnership for community cleanup and beautification in Louisville
- During her 14 years with Louisville Metro Government, Richardson held leadership roles in GIS analysis, emergency response, performance improvement, and business intelligence
- Brightside community cleanups have mobilized thousands of volunteers, removing more than 300 tons of garbage from Louisville's streets and parks in the past five years
- Richardson credits UofL's Geographic and Environmental Sciences Department for providing the foundation for data-driven decision making, particularly through her undergraduate thesis
- She emphasizes the value of GIS skills in government, noting that few colleagues are familiar with using spatial data to draw conclusions and solve problems
- Richardson advises students to combine GIS skills with their passions and to continuously learn new systems as technology evolves

## Synthesis
The University of Louisville profiled Angela Richardson, a geography alumna with a GIS concentration who has been appointed interim director of Brightside, Inc., a public-private partnership that brings Louisville residents together for community cleanup and beautification programs. The article highlights how GIS skills translate into public sector leadership roles.

Richardson's 14-year career with Louisville Metro Government spanned GIS analysis, emergency response, performance improvement, and business intelligence, demonstrating the versatility of geographic information systems training. At Brightside, she oversees programs that have removed more than 300 tons of garbage from Louisville's streets and parks in five years through volunteer-driven cleanups, with environmental education and planting initiatives.

Richardson credits her UofL education, particularly her undergraduate thesis, for teaching her to approach problems from a data standpoint. She emphasizes that GIS provides a unique perspective in government, where few people are familiar with using spatial data to answer questions about the environment and the community. Her advice to students reflects the interdisciplinary nature of GIS: combine technical mapping skills with personal passions, and keep learning as technology evolves.
