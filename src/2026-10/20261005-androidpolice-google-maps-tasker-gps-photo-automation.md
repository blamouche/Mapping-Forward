## Elevator pitch
An Android Police writer discovered a Google Maps shortcut that uses hidden GPS data from photos to instantly navigate to the location where a photo was taken, saving hours of manual address entry through Tasker automation.

## Takeaways
- GPS coordinates are embedded in photo EXIF metadata by default on most smartphones
- Tasker automation can extract this GPS data and create a Google Maps shortcut to navigate directly to the photo's location
- The shortcut eliminates the need to manually search for and type addresses
- The technique is particularly useful for finding locations from photos taken at unfamiliar places
- The workflow demonstrates how photo metadata can bridge the gap between personal photo libraries and navigation apps

## Synthesis
An Android Police writer shared a productivity shortcut that uses hidden GPS photo data to automate Google Maps navigation. Most smartphones embed GPS coordinates in photo EXIF metadata by default, but this data is rarely accessed by users. Using Tasker, an Android automation app, the writer created a workflow that extracts GPS coordinates from a selected photo and passes them directly to Google Maps as a navigation destination.

This eliminates the tedious process of trying to remember or manually search for an address from a photo. The shortcut is particularly useful for finding one's way back to locations photographed at unfamiliar places — a hiking trailhead, a scenic viewpoint, or a restaurant spotted while traveling. The technique demonstrates how photo metadata, often overlooked, can bridge the gap between personal photo libraries and navigation apps, turning visual memories into actionable navigation inputs.
