## Elevator pitch

## Synthesis
The Harvard Gazette reports on the centuries-long effort to correct the world map, focusing on the shift from the Mercator projection to the Equal Earth projection. Developed in 2018 by cartographers Tom Patterson, Bojan Šavrič, and Bernhard Jenny, the Equal Earth projection shows Africa at its actual size — 30.4 million square kilometers or 11.7 million square miles. As the world's second largest continent, covering about 20 percent of Earth's total land area, Africa could contain the U.S., China, India, and most of Western Europe within its borders.

The Mercator projection, created by Gerardus Mercator in 1569, was originally intended for navigation. By preserving accurate angles and directions, it helped navigators chart a path by drawing a straight line on the map while maintaining a constant compass direction. These same qualities make the Mercator projection useful for displaying streets and navigation routes today — Google Maps uses a modified version called the Web Mercator Projection. However, the projection severely distorts the relative size of landmasses, making countries near the equator appear much smaller than they are.

The article is part of a broader global conversation about decolonizing cartography, following the UN General Assembly's endorsement of the Equal Earth projection for more accurate world maps.
