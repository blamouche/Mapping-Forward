## Synthesis
SWI swissinfo.ch reports that Switzerland's glaciers lost vast amounts of ice in the summer of 2026's baking heat, and the changes are increasingly visible on the country's maps. According to the Swiss Glacier Monitoring Network (GLAMOS), average glacier ice thickness fell by between 2.5 and 4 metres between 2025 and 2026. In some places, little ice will remain and so-called "dead ice" will form — ice that is no longer connected to the active glacier and will not flow.

These physical changes force cartographers to update Switzerland's renowned topographic maps. Glacier boundaries must be redrawn as they retreat, new glacial lakes appear where ice has melted, and the distinctive white areas on maps shrink year by year. The Swiss federal topographical office, which produces the country's official maps, faces an accelerating pace of updates as climate change transforms the Alpine landscape.

The article illustrates how climate change is not just an environmental phenomenon but a cartographic one, directly impacting how one of the world's most precisely mapped countries represents its own territory.
