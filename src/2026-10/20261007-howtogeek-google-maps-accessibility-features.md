# Five Overlooked Google Maps Accessibility Features That Help Everyone

**Source**: https://www.howtogeek.com/useful-google-maps-accessibility-features-overlook-they-help-everyone/
**Date**: 2026-10-07
**Author**: Viraj Gawde, How-To Geek
**Keywords**: Google Maps, accessibility, wheelchair routes, voice guidance, Lens in Maps, Auracast, mobility, vision, hearing

## Elevator pitch
How-To Geek highlights five Google Maps accessibility features — wheelchair-accessible routes, accessible places info, detailed voice guidance, Lens in Maps for visual recognition, and Auracast streaming — that benefit not just users with disabilities but all travelers.

## Takeaways
- Wheelchair-accessible routing filters public transit and walking routes to avoid stairs, useful also for luggage and strollers
- Accessible places information shows ramps, restrooms, seating, parking, and elevators for business listings
- Detailed voice guidance provides step-by-step audio walking directions for low-vision users
- Lens in Maps uses the phone camera to identify nearby places, requiring TalkBack (Android) or VoiceOver (iOS)
- Auracast support lets hearing aid users find venues that broadcast audio via Bluetooth
- The "Emphasize accessibility info" setting can be enabled in Profile > Settings > App and display

## Synthesis
Google Maps offers a robust set of accessibility tools that extend well beyond their intended audience. Wheelchair-accessible routing, which filters transit and walking routes to avoid stairs, benefits anyone carrying luggage or pushing a stroller. The accessible places feature displays detailed information about ramps, restrooms, seating, and elevators at business listings, with an option to emphasize this information in the app's display settings.

For users with visual impairments, detailed voice guidance provides spoken walking directions, while Lens in Maps uses the camera to recognize and announce nearby places like ATMs and restaurants. The newest addition, Auracast support, allows hearing aid users to find venues that stream audio via Bluetooth broadcasting. How-To Geek notes that since much of this information comes from business owners and community contributions, users should verify accessibility details before traveling and contribute corrections after visits.
