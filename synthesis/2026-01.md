# Synthesis 2026-01

## Summary

Artificial intelligence emerges as the common thread of January 2026 in the mapping and geospatial industry. Google extends Gemini AI to walking and cycling navigation in Maps, while CARTO theorizes the concept of "Agentic GIS" enabling natural language queries on spatial data. HERE Technologies dominated CES 2026 with a comprehensive portfolio for software-defined vehicles, including partnerships with Hyundai, Lucid Motors, and Qualcomm. The company introduces a four-phase SDV maturity framework (Connected, Augmented, Adaptive, Agentic) and tools like SceneXtract to generate autonomous test scenarios in minutes rather than weeks. This convergence of AI and location redefines skill expectations: developers must master mapping APIs while understanding AI assistant limitations, as demonstrated by a HERE tutorial revealing that AI-generated code still requires significant corrections due to outdated specifications.

The open mapping data ecosystem continues consolidating around the Overture Maps Foundation, which names William Mortenson as executive director and expands communication initiatives. A notable technical milestone: Protomaps adds Overture support as an alternative data source to OpenStreetMap, illustrating growing interoperability between providers. TomTom joins Precisely's Data Link program, deepening a 20-year partnership to facilitate multi-source data integration. The OpenStreetMap community announces that State of the Map 2026 will be held in Paris, hosted by Geodata Paris with IGN support, marking institutional recognition of the project.

Logistics and supply chain undergo transformation through the convergence of location intelligence, AI-powered optimization, and autonomous robotics. HERE introduces Territory Assignment Optimization (TAO) while autonomous robots from Hyundai and ABB reach production stage. The sector, responsible for nearly a quarter of global CO2 emissions, adopts carbon footprint tracking and reusable packaging with smart tracking. In climate adaptation, Esri demonstrates how GeoAI and digital twins enable cities like Washington DC, Los Angeles, and Prague to strategically deploy urban forestry for heat island mitigation, with US urban forests generating $13.5 billion in annual ecosystem services.

Cartography asserts its role as a geopolitical analysis tool through several notable publications: an Al Jazeera interactive map analyzes the Kushner plan for Gaza, Orient XXI maps the Iranian uprising from Tehran's bazaars, and Toute l'Europe visualizes the 50 US military bases across Europe. The James Webb Space Telescope produces the most detailed dark matter map ever created, with double the resolution of previous observations. In historical cartography, the discovery of a Mercator manuscript hidden for 450 years at El Escorial illuminates the scientific foundations of his methods, while a UCL professor analyzes how Cold War maps can inform current debates on the Arctic and Greenland.
